# MediaPipe and synthetic blemishes make retouching shippable

Every missing piece of `docs/27` has a licence-clean answer, and only two pieces need weights Yellowmoon did not train: Google's MediaPipe **BlazeFace full-range detector (1.08 MB) and Face Mesh V2 (2.55 MB, 478 points)**. Both are Apache-2.0 by their model cards and trained on Google's own consented captures. They port to pure Rust in `ym-ml` the way the crop and bokeh networks did, at an estimated 0.1–0.2 s of single-core time per face. No learned face parser can ship, because every common checkpoint rests on CelebAMask-HQ, LaPa, FaceSynthetics or a non-commercial licence. So `FaceParser` should be built from the mesh's polygons plus MediaPipe's Apache-2.0 **Selfie Multiclass** segmenter (its face-skin class for the hairline, its body-skin class for the neck), with edges pulled into place by the existing guided filter. Beauty marks and the first blemish proposer should be classical. No licence-clean data of moles in portraits exists. And the gain-map design means a proposer only has to repair low-frequency tone inside detected spots and return its input unchanged everywhere else. A trained proposer is affordable: the spec's 379,214-parameter network, trained on synthetic chromophore lesions painted onto Open Images faces (CC BY 2.0 photos), should cost **about $10–12 of Modal time, $20 in the worst case**, well inside the $30. The real risk is its CPU inference, **87–100 GMAC per 450 px face**: roughly 2–10 s, and still untimed on Apple silicon. Quality cannot be judged with PSNR, which already gives an unedited image 44–46 dB. The plan is masked synthetic tests in CI, with the owner's blind A/B as the shipping gate. One researcher also found that the `docs/27` tool set (Blemish, Dark Circles, Even Skin with Amount and Texture, neck toggles, blemish protection, per-face settings) closely matches Capture One's Retouch Faces (May 2025, neck toggles October 2025). That makes the owner's provenance confirmation in `docs/27` §11 the first thing to settle before round 2.

## Two Apache-2.0 Google networks find and mesh every face

The licence chain for MediaPipe's face models is at least as clean as the SAM 2.1 and Depth Anything precedent in `docs/14`. The Face Mesh V2 card (15 September 2022) and the BlazeFace full-range card (19 January 2021) each state **"Licensed under Apache License, Version 2.0"** ([Face Mesh V2 card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20MediaPipe%20Face%20Mesh%20V2.pdf); [BlazeFace card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/MediaPipe%20BlazeFace%20Model%20Card%20%28Full%20Range%29.pdf)). The canonical mesh, with its UVs, and the region index lists for eyes, brows, lips, irises and the face oval live in the Apache-2.0 MediaPipe repository ([LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/LICENSE); [face_mesh_connections.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/python/solutions/face_mesh_connections.py)). The training data is not an academic set. BlazeFace was trained on **"consented images of people using a mobile AR application"**, over 100,000 of them, and Face Mesh V2 on smartphone captures from the same kind of AR app, with z coordinates "obtained from synthetic data" ([BlazeFace card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/MediaPipe%20BlazeFace%20Model%20Card%20%28Full%20Range%29.pdf); [Face Mesh V2 card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20MediaPipe%20Face%20Mesh%20V2.pdf)). Converting the TFLite weights to safetensors changes the work, so the model card and `NOTICE` must say so under Apache-2.0 §4(b), as they do for Depth Anything.

The files are small, and their operators are simple. A researcher downloaded and parsed them. The current `face_landmarker.task` bundle (3,758,596 bytes, last modified 2023-05-03) holds a 128×128 short-range detector, the 256×256 landmark model (2,553,590 bytes, 478 × xyz output) and a blendshapes model that retouching does not need ([face_landmarker.task](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/face_landmarker/face_landmarker/float16/latest/face_landmarker.task)). The separately published **full-range detector** is 1,083,786 bytes, takes 192×192 input, decodes 2,304 anchors and costs 105.7 M multiply-adds ([blaze_face_full_range.tflite](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/face_detector/blaze_face_full_range/float16/latest/blaze_face_full_range.tflite)). Face Mesh V2 costs 112.9 M multiply-adds and uses only CONV_2D, PRELU, DEPTHWISE_CONV_2D, ADD, MAX_POOL_2D, PAD, a single LOGISTIC and RESHAPE. Full range is the right detector because camera photos are the back-facing-camera domain its card targets; the short-range model is for selfie distances. Google's own documents disagree with the files in two places. The Landmarker guide lists the bundled detector as 192×192, but the file is the 128×128 short-range model, and the BlazeFace card says "160x192" while the file's tensor is 192×192 ([Face Landmarker guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_landmarker)). So the exact pre-processing (crop rotation from the eye keypoints, box expansion, input range) must be read from MediaPipe's Apache-2.0 graph configs, not from the guides.

**Importing TFLite at run time is not an option.** tract's TFLite front end is unimplemented ([tract-tflite 0.22.0](https://docs.rs/crate/tract-tflite/0.22.0)). The networks should be hand-ported into `ym-ml` the way `crop_net` and `bokeh_net` were. Every operator either exists there already or is trivial. PReLU needs no `exp`, so the networks stay deterministic without `libm`. The work is a CI-only Python export script (read the flatbuffer, widen f16 to safetensors, reproduce NHWC layout and TF "SAME" padding), SSD anchor decoding, MediaPipe's weighted NMS reimplemented from its Apache-2.0 calculators, and parity fixtures from the MediaPipe Python package on about 20 public photos, all as test tooling. Yellowmoon's crop model runs 263 M multiply-adds in **120 ms on one 2.8 GHz Xeon core**, about 2.2 GMAC/s ([docs/20](../../docs/20-auto-crop.md)). At that rate the detector takes about 50 ms, and each mesh pass about 50 ms. Two mesh passes per face (the second on a crop recomputed from the first, which is how MediaPipe's tracking mode gets its lower error) put a face at roughly 0.1–0.2 s. A 24 MP group photo with ten faces and a tiled detector pass comes to about 1.5 s of single-core work. These are extrapolations; no Apple-silicon timing exists for any Yellowmoon model (M0-H4, [docs/07](../../docs/07-ml-models.md)). Core ML or burn on the GPU would be faster but would break byte-identical exports, and at about 0.1 GMAC per face there is no need for either.

Apple Vision cannot stand in for the mesh, and it should not find the faces either. Revision 3 of `VNDetectFaceRectanglesRequest` (macOS 12+) reports continuous roll, yaw and pitch and handles masked and occluded faces ([WWDC21 10040](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10040/)). But its 76-point landmarks trace the face contour only **"from the left cheek, over the chin, to the right cheek"**, with no forehead ([VNFaceLandmarks2D](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnfacelandmarks2d)). Apple also warns that the default revision moves with new releases. Even a pinned revision fixes the algorithm family, not the bits, and `ym` on Linux has no Vision at all. Face boxes feed every radius in `docs/27` (`D` and `F`), so Vision faces would make the whole tool Mac-only and dependent on the macOS version. Subject and People, by contrast, are one mask kind each, which `docs/23` already tolerates ([docs/23](../../docs/23-nodes.md)). Vision earns a place only as an optional Mac-only second opinion, for masked faces, extreme poses and evaluation, never on the export path.

No other landmarker passes rule 5 of `docs/14` ("Shipped weights only from CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, Apache or our own data", [docs/14](../../docs/14-licensing.md)). dlib's 68-point model was trained on 300-W, whose terms say **"Commercial use (i.e., use in training commercial algorithms) is not allowed"** ([300-W](https://ibug.doc.ic.ac.uk/resources/300-W/); [dlib-models](https://github.com/davisking/dlib-models)). 3DDFA_V2 rests on 300W-LP and the Basel Face Model, which is licensed for "internal, non-commercial research" ([3DDFA_V2](https://github.com/cleardusk/3DDFA_V2); [BFM licence](https://faces.dmi.unibas.ch/bfm/content/basel_face_model/downloads/BFM_NonCommercial_License_Agreement.pdf)). DECA-style regressors inherit FLAME's non-commercial terms; only FLAME 2023 Open is CC BY 4.0, and no image regressor for it was found ([FLAME licence](https://flame.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html)). InsightFace's RetinaFace and SCRFD models are "available for non-commercial research purposes only" ([InsightFace](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface)). The trap is **YuNet**: its weights are MIT and only about 230 KB, but it is trained directly on WIDER FACE, which its Hugging Face card lists as CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 ([YuNet README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv_zoo/main/models/face_detection_yunet/README.md); [WIDER FACE card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/wider_face/blob/main/README.md)). That is not the teacher-chain case the owner accepted for Depth Anything. Here the non-commercial set is the training set itself. Google's new GNM Head (July 2026, Apache-2.0) is a parametric head model with no image-to-mesh fitter, so it is a possible V2 topology for a model we train, not a V1 option ([google/GNM](https://github.com/google/GNM)).

The mesh is accurate enough for soft masks, but not for hard edges or for per-vertex tables. The card reports an IOD-normalised error of **2.62 % in tracking mode and 3.24 % on a single image**, against a human inter-annotator discrepancy of 2.56 % ([Face Mesh V2 card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20MediaPipe%20Face%20Mesh%20V2.pdf)). The canonical mesh puts the IOD at 0.407 of face width ([canonical_face_model.obj](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/modules/face_geometry/data/canonical_face_model.obj)). So on a 4000 px face the error is roughly **43–53 px**: sub-pixel in the 256 px crop, and a larger source image does not shrink it. Against the `docs/27` radii for that face, the error sits well below the dark-circle eye exclusion (+0.03 F = 120 px), the even-skin edge feather (−260 px) and the regional blurs (176–308 px). It is about equal to the even-skin core feather (−44 px). Eyelid margins, lips and the oval against the hairline therefore keep their feathers and the guided filter. The per-vertex tables are a bigger problem. The mean mesh edge is 5.9 % of face width, about 234 px on a 4000 px face, which is too coarse for an under-eye crescent. `UnderEye` and `InvertedSkinUniformity` should be authored as small UV-space textures (256² or 512², u8) sampled through the Apache-2.0 canonical UVs, or rasterised after a fixed subdivision. `docs/27` decision 3 already leaves the tables to the `FaceMesh` implementation, so this needs no trait change, but the tables are tied to MediaPipe's vertex order, so the model choice must be settled first. In the canonical mesh the eyes and mouth are triangulated (the only open boundary is the 36-vertex face oval), so eye and lip masks must come from the contour polygons, not from holes.

The cards' stated limits translate directly into rules the spec should state. BlazeFace needs faces of at least **5 % of the image side** with 70 % of the box inside the frame, handles yaw up to 90° and roll or pitch up to 45°, and caps its detections, for example at 10 ([BlazeFace card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/MediaPipe%20BlazeFace%20Model%20Card%20%28Full%20Range%29.pdf)). One pass on a 6000 px frame therefore misses faces under about 300 px. Group photos need overlapping tiles (1536 px tiles reach about 80 px faces), a fixed-order NMS and our own cap. Capture One uses up to 32 faces of at least 200 px ([Capture One blog](https://www.captureone.com/blog/how-to-use-retouch-faces-portrait-retouching)). Face Mesh V2 needs the eye line within 8° of level and fails beyond 80° look-away or below 50 % visibility ([Face Mesh V2 card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20MediaPipe%20Face%20Mesh%20V2.pdf)). So the crop must be rotated from the detector's eye keypoints, and a yaw cut-off (taken from MediaPipe's 33-point weighted Procrustes fit, in f64) should skip or fade profiles. Three risks have no published numbers. The mesh places the under-eye region under a spectacle frame's lower rim, so Dark circles could lift a dark frame. It covers beards as if they were skin, so Even skin would flatten a beard's low-frequency tone. And it hallucinates a surface under hands and hair. The protection brush covers these today; an automatic guard would need its own spec.

## No shippable face parser exists, so two models assemble the masks

Every widely used face-parsing checkpoint fails rule 5. CelebAMask-HQ is "available for non-commercial research purposes only" ([CelebAMask-HQ](https://github.com/switchablenorms/CelebAMask-HQ)). LaPa is for "non-commercial purposes" ([LaPa](https://github.com/JDAI-CV/lapa-dataset)). Microsoft's FaceSynthetics "can be used for non-commercial research" ([FaceSynthetics](https://github.com/microsoft/FaceSynthetics)). Sapiens is CC BY-NC 4.0 ([Sapiens LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/sapiens/main/LICENSE)), and the popular SegFormer parser on Hugging Face restricts itself to "non-commercial research and educational purposes" ([jonathandinu/face-parsing](https://huggingface.co/jonathandinu/face-parsing)). BiSeNet, SegFace and FaRL have MIT code but are trained on those sets. Using any of them to pseudo-label training data would launder the restriction, so they are evaluation oracles at most. Synthetic data works in principle: a UNet trained only on FaceSynthetics scored **Helen F1 skin 95.1, the same as the network trained on real Helen** ([Wood et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.15102)). But the only labelled synthetic set is non-commercial and its renderer is unreleased. Microsoft's permissive SynthHuman (CDLA-Permissive-2.0) has no part labels ([DAViD](https://github.com/microsoft/DAViD)).

**EasyPortrait** is the one real face-parsing set found that allows commercial use. It has 40,000 images of 13,705 people, collected with signed consent, and a skin class that already excludes **"ears, second chin, and nostrils"**, with eyes, brows, lips and teeth as separate classes; SegFormer-B0 reaches 85.42 mIoU at 1024 px ([EasyPortrait README](https://github.com/hukenovs/easyportrait); [VISAPP 2025 paper](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2025/131353/131353.pdf)). It has no neck class, labels strict-bordered beards as occlusion, comes from webcam selfies with a Caucasian and Russian bias, and is 91.78 GB. Its licence is "not a Creative Commons license": it reworks CC BY-SA 4.0 under Russian law, with ShareAlike terms on adapted material ([licence PDF](https://github.com/hukenovs/easyportrait/blob/main/license/en_us.pdf)). Rule 5 names CC BY-SA, so whether this rework qualifies is the owner's call. It is an upgrade path, not round-2 work.

MediaPipe's **Selfie Multiclass** segmenter fills the gap the mesh leaves. Its card states Apache-2.0. It is a 256×256 Vision Transformer with classes for background, hair, body-skin, face-skin, clothes and accessories, with a mean IoU of 77.23 at 256 px and **68.25 for the darkest skin-tone group (Monk 9–10)** ([Multiclass card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20Multiclass%20Segmentation.pdf)). The card also mentions a 512 px model, but the download page lists only the 256 px one ([Image segmenter guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/image_segmenter)). A local experiment ran it with the Face Landmarker on MediaPipe's two test portraits, which have small faces (about 190–250 px wide); it shows what each class contains, not how accurate it is. The face-skin class covered **100 % of the eye, brow and lip polygons**, the teeth of a smile and 73–89 % of visible ears. A box under the chin was 83–87 % body-skin where the neck showed. And 9–11 % of face-skin lay above the mesh's top oval vertex (landmark 10, mid-forehead), with roughly another 10 % at the temples and ears ([model](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/image_segmenter/selfie_multiclass_256x256/float32/latest/selfie_multiclass_256x256.tflite); [portrait.jpg](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/portrait.jpg); [business-person.png](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/business-person.png)). So face-skin alone is not `SkinWithoutEars`, but it supplies the forehead up to the real hairline that the mesh misses, and body-skin is a cross-platform neck source. The model is 16.4 MB in f32 (about 8 MB at f16). A ViT is more porting work than the two CNNs, and that effort has not been scoped.

The proposed construction works per face in full image coordinates, using the mesh for topology and the segmenter for boundaries. **`SkinWithoutEars`** is face-skin multiplied by the union of the face oval and a forehead band (above the brow line, between the oval's temple vertices), minus slightly dilated polygons for the eyes, brows, lips and nostrils. Ears lie outside the oval below the brows, so they drop out. **`Eyes`** is the union of the two lid polygons, with the iris points available for a pupil exclusion. **`NeckSkin`** is the body-skin component connected to the jaw line (oval vertices 132 → 152 → 361), clipped to a band about 0.8 face heights down and 1.3 oval widths across, minus the face mask. Keeping only that connected component drops hands, arms and chest, which body-skin also contains. At 256 px over a 4000 px face, one model pixel spans 16–24 full-resolution pixels. So every mask goes through the `docs/27` §5.1 feather and then the He–Sun–Tang guided filter already in `docs/algorithms/06` step 5, with a radius scaled to the face ([docs/algorithms/06](../../docs/algorithms/06-masks.md)). The subsampled guided filter gives "a speedup of >10x with almost no visible degradation" ([Fast Guided Filter](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.00996)).

Two helpers are worth keeping, and two candidates should be rejected. Global skin-colour rules are weak: a colour Bayes model averaged F1 0.630 over eight datasets against 0.892 for a CNN ensemble, and scored 0.252 on one of them. The public skin datasets state no licence, and some were scraped from Google Images ([Nanni et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9965146/)). A **per-face** model avoids both problems: a closed-form Gaussian in log-chroma (log R/G, log B/G), fitted to pixels sampled from interior mesh triangles. It adapts to tone and white balance, needs no training data and no EM seed, and works as a soft multiplier at the hairline, beard and glasses and for growing the neck, never on its own. SAM 2.1, already shipped, is a poor parser. With one point prompt, SAM ViT-H scored **skin 71.4, neck 33.9 and upper lip 8.6 IoU** on face parts, because it "predicts the most probable contour containing the prompt" ([arXiv:2403.09199](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.09199v2)). Apple offers no face-part segmentation for arbitrary images: `AVSemanticSegmentationMatte` is auxiliary data captured with the photo, which ARW files do not carry ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avsemanticsegmentationmatte)). The `docs/27` §1 plan of a neck from Vision's People mask therefore makes Linux `ym` differ from the Mac. Body-skin should be the primary neck source, with People at most an optional Mac refinement. If the ViT port stalls, the mesh oval plus the per-face colour model is a working fallback, with the forehead cut at landmark 10.

## Moles and acne share a size range, so colour decides what stays

No licence-clean dataset of moles in ordinary face photographs exists. The dermatology sets with shippable licences, **ISIC 2016 and 2017 (CC-0), ISIC 2024 SLICE-3D Permissive (CC-BY) and PAD-UFES-20 (CC BY 4.0)**, are close-ups of single lesions under dermatoscope or phone light ([ISIC challenge data](https://challenge.isic-archive.com/data/); [PAD-UFES-20](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/zr7vgbcyr2/1)). The bigger sets are non-commercial: HAM10000, ISIC 2019 and 2020, and Fitzpatrick17k, which is CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 ([fitzpatrick17k](https://github.com/mattgroh/fitzpatrick17k)). ACNE04 is "free for academic usage" only ([xpwu95/LDL](https://github.com/xpwu95/LDL)). The domain gap matters as much as the licences. The blemish map works at no more than 450 px, and if 450 px spans about 15 cm of face (an assumption to check against real face boxes), a 3 mm mole is about 9 px across. A network trained on frame-filling close-ups would be guessing at that size, and none of the clean sets labels acne or freckles as the negatives a beauty-mark detector must reject.

Physical size cannot separate moles from acne, but it does separate both from pores:

| Feature | Physical size | Pixels in the 450 px map (≈ 0.33 mm/px) | Share of face size `D` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visible pore | 0.25–0.5 mm ([Flament et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4337418)) | 0.75–1.5 | 0.17–0.33 % |
| Acne papule | under 5 mm ([Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/papules-acne)) | 3–15 | 0.7–3.3 % |
| Common naevus | usually 2–3 mm, under about 6 mm ([GPnotebook](https://gpnotebook.com/en-GB/pages/general-information/clinical-classification/banal-or-acquired-naevi)); mostly round or oval ([DermNet](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/melanocytic-naevus)) | 6–18 | 1.3–4 % |

Pores fall to a pixel or less after the area downscale. Detection should therefore run on the map itself, not on full-resolution crops, and treat anything below about 0.6 % of `D` as texture. The useful spot band is about **0.7–3.5 % of `D`**. `docs/27` states no blemish scale today, and this band should be written into it. The classical face-mole literature agrees on one recipe: restrict to skin, find dark compact blobs with a scale-space LoG or DoG, then prune by contrast, size, shape and isolation. Cho, Freeman and Tsao ran DoG on each RGB channel with an SVM on the patches and reported **84.7 % sensitivity on 28 test images**. Their known failure was that pores and hair roots read as moles at high resolution, fixed by a minimum radius ([Cho et al.](https://people.csail.mit.edu/billf/papers/MMBIA_FinalV7.pdf)). Pierrard and Vetter kept only salient, isolated marks ([CVPR 2007](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/2007/pierrard2007cvpr-skin)), and Park and Jain combined an appearance model that masks facial features with LoG blobs and morphology ([IEEE TIFS 2010](https://nij.ojp.gov/library/publications/face-matching-and-retrieval-using-soft-biometrics)). Colour is the deciding cue. Tsumura's two-pigment model separates melanin from haemoglobin in optical density ([SIGGRAPH 2003](https://www.mi.tj.chiba-u.jp/~tsumura/Tsumura/papers/siggraph2003.htm)). Haemoglobin darkens green and blue and leaves red nearly unchanged, while melanin darkens all three channels. So the ratio `Δlog R / Δlog G` inside a blob, relative to its surroundings, sits near zero for red acne and well above zero for brown moles.

The recommended `ClassicalBeautyMarkDetector` works on the map's own encoding, in logs, inside the skin mask eroded by about 1.5 % of `D`. It finds DoG minima over four to six scales covering diameters of 1.2–4.5 % of `D` and keeps candidates that persist across two adjacent scales. It accepts a candidate whose contrast is below about −15 % linear, whose colour ratio is above about 0.4, whose circularity is above about 0.7 and whose aspect ratio is under about 1.6. It ranks survivors by contrast times isolation, keeps at most about ten, and pads each box by about 30 %. The padding matters because `docs/27` §5.3 zeroes only the ellipse inscribed in the box and ramps out to √2 times it. A box drawn tight to the mole lets that ramp heal the mole's rim, a "shrinking mole". The detector is deterministic (fixed scale list, raster-order ties) and costs roughly 20–40 ms. **Every threshold is a hypothesis**, to be calibrated on public portraits under the V1 data rule, with the limitation recorded in `docs/STATUS.md`. Its expected misses should be documented. Flesh-coloured dome naevi, common on the face, are not dark, but the proposer barely touches them either. Brown post-acne marks look like small flat moles and will often be kept. Deep skin tones, with lower redness contrast and more post-inflammatory pigmentation, are untested.

The commercial tools treat moles as a class of their own and keep them by default. Adobe's **Fade** slider "appears ... when freckles, moles, or beauty marks are detected", and Camera Raw's default of 50 partially fades all permanent marks ([Adobe Learn](https://www.adobe.com/learn/lightroom-cc/web/ai-blemish-removal); [Camera Raw help](https://helpx.adobe.com/camera-raw/desktop/ai-and-generative-features/remove-blemishes-from-photos.html)). Retouch4me Heal added a **Moles** slider and an automatic "Keep Bindi" ([Retouch4me](https://retouch4.me/blog/retouch4me-apex-1-211-update-more-control-in-heal-with-the-new-moles-slider-and-keep-bindi)). Evoto removes moles with one click and restores them with a pen ([Evoto](https://support.evoto.ai/blemish-removal/)). None of these vendors publishes its criteria. `docs/27` keeps detected moles fully (a hole in the mask) and leaves misses to the Brush. A later "Moles" control could scale the hole's depth with no new maths.

## The blemish proposer: classical first, a synthetic-lesion network second

### The gain map limits the proposer's job to low-frequency repair

The proposer's output is never shown. It becomes a per-channel gain `(OUT/IN)²`, clamped to 0.5..2.5, computed on a map of at most 450 px and applied bilinearly at full resolution ([docs/27](../../docs/27-skin-retouch.md) §6). Texture therefore always comes from the original. The proposer has to supply the right **low-frequency tone inside each spot and exactly `IN` everywhere else**. That second condition is strict: above an amount of 0.75 the apply step lerps the strength towards 1 for every pixel, so any tiny correction away from spots turns into a blotchy veil across the face. The chroma guard changes the job as well. It keeps full confidence up to about 1 % of channel spread and drops to its floor beyond about 11 %. A red-spot correction, which lifts G and B more than R, lands at the floor and takes the neighbourhood's chroma from a box of radius `max(w, h)/80`, about 7 px on a 450×600 map. So the proposer's luminance must be right, and its colour matters little. Three consequences follow. A purely achromatic proposer passes the guard untouched and cannot remove redness. Spots wider than about 14 px fall back on the proposer's own chroma. And because the guard runs before the mask, lip red can be pulled into a spot next to the lips, so detection must keep a margin from them. The clamp caps a lift at about +1.3 stops, so very dark marks only half-heal, a second reason to protect moles. A proposer that fully corrects each spot also gives the amount slider its intended order: deep spots (`|log2 g|` of 0.5–1) survive low amounts, and faint ones (0.05–0.15) appear only at high amounts.

### A gated detect-and-fill proposer needs no training

The recommended classical proposer is a multiplicative frequency separation with a harmonic fill. It needs no training and runs in an estimated **10–30 ms single-threaded**. In outline: take `L` as a Gaussian of `IN` with σ ≈ 0.25 % of `D` (1–1.5 px) and `H = IN/L`. Find spots with a per-channel black top-hat in log space, `log closing_r(L) − log L`, using an octagonal closing of radius about 2 % of `D` (van Herk–Gil-Werman, so cost does not grow with radius), or with a red-excess test on `log(R/G)` that requires green to drop too. Drop components smaller than about 0.7 % of `D` (pores and noise), larger than the closing disc, or elongated (hairs and lines), and soften the result to a 0..1 weight `w`. Fill `L` over the spots harmonically from their boundary (red–black Gauss–Seidel with a fixed sweep count). Then return `OUT = IN · (1 + w·(L′/L − 1))`, which is **bit-exactly `IN` wherever `w = 0`**. With this separation the gain is by construction the low-frequency correction `L′/L`. That is what retouchers mean by healing the low layer ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/photography/retouch-images-frequency-separation-5132640)), and what darktable does on a coarse wavelet scale ([darktable manual](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/5.6/en/module-reference/processing-modules/retouch/); GPL, read only). The `docs/27` §1 sketch, a closing plus an edge-aware smoothing, should not ship ungated. A closing alone brightens all dark texture smaller than its disc. Edge-aware smoothers do the reverse of what is wanted: a spot whose contrast exceeds the bilateral's σ_r or the guided filter's ε survives as an "edge", while low-contrast texture is smoothed ([guided filter](https://people.csail.mit.edu/kaiming/eccv10/index.html)). Texture-synthesis inpainting ([Criminisi et al. 2004](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/criminisi_tip2004.pdf); [PatchMatch](https://gfx.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/Barnes_2009_PAR/)) is the wrong fit too. Texture copied at 450 px becomes a texture pattern in the gain, which ghosts over the full-resolution texture and inflates the strength. Telea's fast-marching fill ([Telea 2004](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~telea001/uploads/PAPERS/JGT04/paper.pdf)) works but streaks on holes larger than about 10 px. Threshold fragility, the classic objection to classical acne detection ([Shen et al. 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-24204-6.pdf)), is milder here: thresholds are log-ratios against the same face's local skin, and the amount slider re-thresholds downstream. The repository has box means, guided and bilateral filters, but no grey morphology, connected components, LoG pyramid or inpainting, so those are new but small code. The trait has two gaps. `run` receives only the tile, so the proposer must be built per face with the face size in map pixels. And it cannot see the beauty-mark boxes, so it cannot avoid using a protected mole as the fill boundary for a spot next to it.

### No paired dataset can train shipped weights; Open Images faces can

Every published paired face-retouching set is non-commercial or unreleased. **FFHQR**, 70,000 professionally retouched FFHQ faces, is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([ffhqr-dataset](https://github.com/skylab-tech/ffhqr-dataset)). FFHQ is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 as compiled, even though its individual Flickr images include CC BY 2.0, CC0 and public-domain files ([ffhq-dataset](https://github.com/NVlabs/ffhq-dataset)). CelebA is non-commercial ([CelebA](https://mmlab.ie.cuhk.edu.hk/projects/CelebA.html)). PPR10K is non-commercial and covers tone, not blemishes ([PPR10K](https://github.com/csjliang/PPR10K)). ABPN's own dataset, CRHD-3K, is clothing and research-only ([ABPN](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Lei_ABPN_Adaptive_Blend_Pyramid_Network_for_Real-Time_Local_Retouching_of_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)). DigiFace-1M is non-commercial ([DigiFace1M](https://github.com/microsoft/DigiFace1M)). HGFR (ICCV 2025, 25,000 pairs) is unreleased, and it is built from Civitai checkpoints and Unsplash photos ([Xu et al. 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Xu_Face_Retouching_with_Diffusion_Data_Generation_and_Spectral_Restorement_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf)). FFHQR's value is its 7,000 test pairs, used locally under the owner's 2026-10-24 rule to give literature-comparable numbers. **Open Images V7** is the clean source: its images are listed as CC BY 2.0 and its boxes are CC BY 4.0 ([Open Images](https://storage.googleapis.com/openimages/web/factsfigures_v7.html)), and Yellowmoon already ships crop and bokeh weights trained on it with per-photo credits ([docs/STATUS.md](../../docs/STATUS.md)). A researcher counted the validation split: of 41,620 images, **2,686 have a usable "Human face" box** (neither group-of nor depiction), 1,730 have a face of at least 0.2 of a side, and 685 reach 450 px on the 1024 px mirror ([validation boxes](https://storage.googleapis.com/openimages/v5/validation-annotations-bbox.csv)). In a probe of 60 Flickr originals, 78 % were still downloadable and 62 % of those had faces of at least 450 px. Extrapolated to the training split, that is roughly **29,000–35,000 faces of 450 px or more**. This extrapolation is unverified: the training split was annotated differently. It is still plenty, because every epoch draws new crops and new synthetic lesions. Unsplash's Terms forbid using images "in connection with any machine learning and/or artificial intelligence datasets" ([Unsplash Terms](https://unsplash.com/terms)), and Pexels' API rules forbid bulk collection for training ([Pexels help](https://help.pexels.com/hc/de-de/articles/900005880463)). Wikimedia Commons portraits are mostly CC BY-SA and would make the weights CC BY-SA 4.0 under rule 5, so they are better kept for evaluation. Google's SCIN dermatology photos carry a CC-BY-style licence that bans re-identification ([SCIN LICENSE](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/scin/blob/main/LICENSE)), and could supply real blemish patches if they include facial acne, which no one has checked. Open Images has a documented Western skew ([Shankar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08536)), so sampling should be reweighted by each face's ITA skin-tone angle. Already-smoothed Flickr portraits should be filtered out with a texture measure, or the network learns a plastic target.

### Synthetic chromophore lesions supply the pairs

Synthetic pairs are the route: real clean-licence faces, procedurally generated blemishes added in linear light, and a network trained to remove exactly what was added. The physics has a published basis. CGFR models a blemish as Beer–Lambert absorption by melanin and haemoglobin, shaped as sums of 2-D Gaussians in log space. Fading blemishes this way scored **FID 115.30, against 117.98 for Photoshop inpainting and 144.89 for Stable Diffusion inpainting** ([CGFR, arXiv 2406.13227](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13227v1)). The generator should work on the face crop before the `sqrt` encoding, so training inputs match `docs/27` §6.1 exactly. It should produce haemoglobin papules (1–5 mm), pustules with a pale core, flat brown or red post-inflammatory marks, low-frequency redness patches, comedones, shallow shading scars and thin Bézier-stroke stray hairs. Amplitudes should scale with each face's own skin density, so deep skin gets proportionate lesions rather than grey stains. **Moles and freckles go into input and target alike**, so the network learns that brown, round and sharp is not automatically a blemish, and a share of untouched samples teaches identity. Real faces already carry blemishes, and a target equal to such a source teaches the network to keep them. The fix is a loss mask: give zero weight to pixels the classical proposer flags in the source. The other options fail. Noise2Void-style blind-spot training assumes per-pixel noise and would keep structured blemishes ([Noise2Void](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10980)). Diffusion editors resample the whole face, so their targets are not pixel-aligned. They are also slow: HGFR's LoRA pairs took **45–60 s each on an RTX 4090** ([HGFR supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/supplemental/Xu_Face_Retouching_with_ICCV_2025_supplemental.pdf)). Synthetic-only transfer has been shown only for classification: a ResNet18 trained on StyleGAN-generated acne faces scored above 80 % on real acne images ([PLOS ONE 2024](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0297958)). For retouching it is unmeasured, which is why the A/B decides. The likely result is good removal of isolated papules, marks, redness and stray hairs, and weak handling of severe or clustered acne and textured scars, the cases where AutoRetouch and BPFRe also fail.

### Training fits in about $12, but inference costs seconds per face

The spec's network has **379,214 parameters but 377,856 multiply-adds per output pixel**, 99 % of them in the ten 64→64 convolutions. The recipe should borrow from AutoRetouch, which found that downscaling below half causes "slight pixel displacement and loss of the original texture". From it come training on local windows with cube-root scale sampling and an output that predicts a residual ([AutoRetouch](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf)). Use 256–320 px tiles. The loss combines L1 on encoded pixels, a band-pass L1 outside the lesions to keep texture, a "quiet skin" penalty on any change where no lesion was added (this matters more than PSNR, given the high-amount ramp), and an optional training-only head that predicts the lesion mask, a cheap analogue of BPFRe's blemish-aware attention ([BPFRe](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Xie_Blemish-Aware_and_Progressive_Face_Retouching_With_Limited_Paired_Data_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)). A small RAGAN discriminator, trained from scratch on real faces, should be added only if the gates show over-smoothing. An ImageNet VGG perceptual loss should be avoided, because `docs/14` keeps ImageNet features out of shipped weights. The throughput estimate is ±2×: about 56 GMAC per 256 px sample for forward plus backward, bandwidth-bound, giving **100–250 samples/s on an L4**. At Modal's **$0.80/h for an L4** ([Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)), a million samples costs $0.9–2.2. The programme reuses the bokeh pipeline's safeguards: a ledger that refuses any run whose worst case exceeds the cap, a 1.25 margin, NaN guards, and launches from GitHub Actions, because Modal's gRPC client does not pass the agents' proxy ([training/bokeh/budget.py](../../training/bokeh/budget.py); [docs/STATUS.md](../../docs/STATUS.md)). Two earlier mistakes are now guarded: an orchestrator restart that wasted about $5, and a NaN run that wasted $2.49.

Inference is where the network costs real time. Tile choice alone changes the cost by **4×**. A 450 px crop costs 86.8 GMAC as one 514 px tile, 100.3 GMAC as four tiles with 228 px output, and 344.4 GMAC as four tiles with 448 px output. At the 40–50 GMAC/s on four threads estimated for Depth Anything's packed matrix product ([docs/07](../../docs/07-ml-models.md)), a large face takes about 2–2.5 s. At the crop net's measured 2.2 GMAC/s per core with a plain convolution loop, it takes 10–11 s on four cores. The map is cached per face and does not depend on the amount, so this is a one-off background cost per face and per upstream change. It is still roughly 70 to 1,000 times the classical proposer's cost. If Mac timing proves too slow, the cheapest lever is width: **48 channels cut the cost to 0.57×, and 32 channels to 0.26×**. Either change alters the normative architecture of `docs/27` §4.

## Yellowmoon's tools mirror Capture One's, and PSNR cannot judge them

### The market converged on threshold sliders, mole controls and texture-kept smoothing

Capture One's Retouch Faces (16.6, May 2025) is the closest match. Its **Blemish** "targets larger ones first and reaches smaller ones as the value increases", with a suggested start "between 0 and 40". It has **Dark Circles**, and **Even Skin** with Amount and Texture sliders (80 / −70 for a polished look, 40 / 50 for a natural one), plus Contouring and a global Impact. It handles up to 32 faces of at least 200 px, edited together or per face ([Capture One blog](https://www.captureone.com/blog/how-to-use-retouch-faces-portrait-retouching)). Version 16.7 (October 2025) added an **Include Neck Area** toggle to Remove blemishes and Even skin ([Capture One 16.7](https://www.captureone.com/explore-features/whats-new/16-7)). A Blemish Protection tool followed the blog's admission that Blemish "may smooth out marks you want to keep" ([support article, search extract](https://support.captureone.com/hc/en-us/articles/30277789058461)). Reviewers praised its dark-circle reduction as "probably the best I've seen" and said Even Skin "doesn't affect color", but found that less prominent moles go above about 40–50 ([alexonraw](https://alexonraw.com/capture-one-ai-retouching-all-what-you-need-to-know/)). They also found Even Skin and Contouring artificial ([Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/reviews/capture-one-ai-practical-look-faster-portrait-retouching-704010)). Adobe Camera Raw pairs Amount with Fade and a Restore brush. In a three-portrait test, Photoshop kept rougher skin but left **"uneven color patches"**, while Evoto mistook acne for freckles and the reverse ([Fstoppers 2026](https://fstoppers.com/photoshop/photoshop-capture-one-or-evoto-which-handles-blemishes-best-905014)). Evoto splits Freckle and Acne sliders and adds automatic Beard Protection ([Evoto](https://support.evoto.ai/portrait-retouching-module-blemish-removal/)). Luminar Neo 1.27 (April 2026) moved to four skin sliders, including a Blemish Removal slider ([Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/artificial-intelligence/luminar-spring-update-introduces-effortless-portrait-toolkit-and-new-universal-901468)). Retouch4me ships "one plugin equals one neural network and one layer" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retouch4me)). Only darktable documents where its retouch sits in a raw pipeline: linear, scene-referred RGB, the same choice `docs/27` makes ([darktable manual](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/5.6/en/module-reference/processing-modules/retouch/)). The failures recur across tools: moles and freckles lost, smoothing that looks artificial, colour patches after spot removal. Against this market, Yellowmoon lacks a permanent-mark control, shine reduction and redness evening; Even skin is luminance-only, so only the proposer and its chroma guard ever touch colour. That is defensible for a product of "very few controls". Yellowmoon also has a real differentiator: its proposer produces only a clamped per-pixel gain, so unlike generative tools such as Apple's Clean Up it cannot invent structure ([Apple Photos guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/photos/pht5c38b77c5/mac)).

Professional practice confirms the design. Retouchers heal texture-scale marks, fix blotchiness at low frequency and shape light with low-opacity dodge and burn. They warn that painting on or blurring the low layer makes "shadows and highlights start to blend together" ([Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/photoshop/common-frequency-separation-mistakes-which-will-ruin-your-retouching-results-30913)), and that "a few remaining imperfections" keep a picture believable ([Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/photoshop/five-common-mistakes-make-retouched-skin-look-unnatural-70824)). In FFHQR, **"less than 40% of the pixels are edited"** in most images, and retouched images "must retain moles, freckles, and other distinctive features". AutoRetouch's model even preserved texture better than its professional ground truth, because retouchers "may use large brushes" ([AutoRetouch](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf)). Each `docs/27` tool maps onto a practice. The blemish map is healing at texture scale. Even skin is a frequency separation in which the Texture radius (0.4–2.4 % of the face) plays the separation radius. Dark circles is a gentle dodge. The two classic failures map to two settings: too large a Texture radius looks plastic, and too much Even skin flattens the modelling light. Both must be tested at the slider ends.

### The match with Capture One makes the provenance question urgent

One researcher matched every `docs/27` tool to a documented Capture One counterpart. Blemish has the same larger-first threshold. Dark Circles and Even Skin with Amount and Texture are there. Include Neck toggles sit on exactly the two tools Capture One gave them (`include_neck_blemish`, `include_neck_even_skin`). There is a blemish-protection mask, and settings are per face. Feature parity is common in this market (Adobe's Amount uses the same threshold semantics), so the match proves nothing on its own. But the research cannot explain where the brief's **specific constants** came from: the 450 px map, the 0.5..2.5 gain clamp, the chroma-guard confidence formula, the `(·)^16` darkening suppression, the 0.077 `D` and 0.044 `D` radii, and the exact twelve-convolution architecture. `docs/27` §11 already asks the owner to "confirm the brief is yours to use (not taken from another product's code or binaries)". That confirmation should come before any model port or training money is spent, because every recommendation in this report builds on those constants. Once it is given, Capture One's documented behaviour doubles as test expectations: Blemish 0–40 fixes visible marks only, Even skin 40 / 50 reads as natural, and a minimum face of 200 px.

### Masked synthetic metrics and a blind A/B replace PSNR

Whole-image metrics cannot rank retouching. On FFHQR the **unedited input already scores 45.58 dB and SSIM 0.9938**, and AutoRetouch's model preferred by users scored lower PSNR than plain MSE training. The authors conclude that "neither PSNR nor SSIM are reliable performance indicators" ([AutoRetouch](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf)). BPFRe's table shows raw inputs at 43.89 dB, with methods about 1 dB apart ([BPFRe](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Xie_Blemish-Aware_and_Progressive_Face_Retouching_With_Limited_Paired_Data_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)). Human studies carry the field. AutoRetouch let professionals skip pairs that were "too similar", and undecided shares reached 32–49 % where methods were close. Against professional ground truth on studio data, its model won 76.3 % to 18.7 %. BeautyGRPO (CVPR 2026) adds no-reference quality scores and ArcFace identity, which at 0.971 for ABPN is saturated for local edits ([BeautyGRPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01163v1)). DXOMARK scales pairwise judgements from **more than 30 experts** into JOD units ([DXOMARK EI 2025](https://corp.dxomark.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/EI2025_RealScenes_portraits_generalization_paper.pdf)). For colour, a 90-observer study of skin found lightness the most sensitive: the 50 % acceptability thresholds were **ΔL\* 0.864 against Δa\* 2.037 and Δb\* 2.517** ([Lagouvardos et al. 2018](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpr/62/4/62_503/_article)).

Yellowmoon needs three layers of evaluation and no labelled data of its own. **In CI**, procedural skin patches in ten tones spanning the Monk range, written by our own script so no licence question arises, receive seeded chromophore acne and post-acne marks with known ground truth, plus moles and freckles that must survive. The metrics extend `docs/27` §8: the share of inserted optical density removed, ΔE2000 at each spot, and no change at all outside every mask. Mean skin colour should move by no more than about |ΔL\*| 0.5 and |Δa\*|, |Δb\*| 1. Further gates: a monotonic, strongest-first threshold curve; **mole survival of at least 90 %** at the default amount, plus the amount at which moles start to go; seeded out-of-distribution brush strokes left mostly unchanged (AutoRetouch's robustness test); a pore-scale band-pass energy ratio of at least 0.95; a cheekbone shading gradient that survives Even skin; and dark circles that darken almost no pixels and touch no lashes. For a trained proposer, add the "quiet skin" share, the clean-skin pixels whose `alpha` exceeds 0.05, reported per ITA bin. **On real photos**, 30–40 portraits stratified by skin tone (MST 1–3, 4–7, 8–10), face size, light and marks each get a contact sheet: off, default, Blemish 80, Even skin 80 with Texture at −70 and at +70, and Dark circles 80, at fit and at 100 %. The owner marks failure codes on each. CC0 and CC BY raws come first; evaluation-tier files and FFHQR's test pairs stay local ([docs/13](../../docs/13-testing.md); [test-photo-sources](../../docs/test-photo-sources.md)). No licence-clean, skin-tone-diverse raw portrait set is known, and Google's MST-E may not be used to train models ([MST-E](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09073v3)). This is a genuine gap. **The gate** is the existing `tools/eval/ab` kit with a new `--test skin`. Sides are random. Answers are left, right or "too similar", plus "needs a fix" with reason codes, and decision times are logged. The kit's current form stays: at least 60 % wins (ties count half), a Wilson lower bound above 50 %, no stratum below 45 %, and no more "needs a fix" marks than the reference ([tools/eval/ab](../../tools/eval/ab/README.md)). For the Wilson bound to clear 50 %, a true win share of 60 % needs **97 decisive trials**, 65 % needs 43, 70 % needs 25 and 80 % needs 11. With ties, that means 40–60 photos. The comparisons are default against off, and a learned proposer against the classical one at the same amount; the learned one ships only if it wins. A one-image "retouched or not?" check at fit size tests whether the result looks natural. Choosing among three or more variants should use the Pérez-Ortiz and Mantiuk scaling method, which is built for few observers ([arXiv 1712.03686](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.03686)).

## Conclusion

The binding constraints are not where the brief expected them. Money is ample: the whole learned-proposer programme fits in about a third of the $30. Licences resolve cleanly once MediaPipe's cards are accepted. What remains hard is the provenance confirmation, the CPU cost of a dense 64-channel network on 450 px faces, and the calibration of a dozen hand-set thresholds against real faces when there is one rater and no licence-clean, skin-tone-diverse set of raw portraits. Platform identity also shapes more than it first appears. The reasoning that rules out Apple Vision for faces (exports must match across the app, `ym` and Linux goldens) applies just as much to the neck, which argues for MediaPipe's body-skin over the People mask the spec currently names.

The gain-map architecture also changes what a learned proposer is for. Texture always comes from the original image, so a network's usual advantage over classical methods, plausible texture synthesis, counts for nothing here. What a network can add is recall on low-contrast and large lesions, fewer false fills on facial structure, and better luminance estimates inside spots. A gated classical proposer may well win the owner's first A/B, and that outcome would be a success: it costs milliseconds, needs no data, and stays as the deterministic fallback and the test oracle for the trait. Training should therefore follow, not precede, a working classical round 2 and a timing of the 64-channel network on a Mac.
