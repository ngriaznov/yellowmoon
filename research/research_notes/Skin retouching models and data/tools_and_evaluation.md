# Face skin retouching: what the tools do and how retouching quality is judged (November 2026)

Scope: (a) what commercial and open-source photo tools offer for blemish removal, under-eye (dark circles) and skin smoothing or evening, and how they work where documented; (b) how research and industry evaluate retouching quality; (c) lessons and an evaluation protocol for Yellowmoon's three tools (`docs/27-skin-retouch.md`: blemish removal through a strength threshold, dark circles lifted toward a regional average, Even skin with a Texture control). Researched 2026-11-11. Several vendor help pages (Adobe helpx, Capture One support, PetaPixel) refused automated fetches (HTTP 403); facts from those pages come from search-result extracts of the page and are marked "(search extract)". Reviewer impressions are labelled as such, separate from documented behaviour.

## Q1. What the main tools offer and how they work

### Takeaway
By 2025–2026 the market has converged on one pattern: automatic per-face detection and parsing, a blemish slider whose low values fix only the most prominent marks and whose high values fix everything (Adobe, Capture One, Luminar, Resolve), a separate under-eye lift, and a smoothing or "even skin" control paired with a texture-retention control. Capture One's Retouch Faces (Blemish, Dark Circles, Even Skin Amount + Texture, neck toggles, blemish protection) is functionally almost identical to Yellowmoon's three tools. The recurring weak points in reviews are removed moles and freckles, artificial-looking smoothing, and colour patches left after spot removal; dark-circle reduction is the best-reviewed tool.

### Inferences
- **Yellowmoon's tool set is the Capture One Retouch Faces set.** Blemish with a "larger/stronger first" threshold, Dark Circles, Even Skin with Amount and Texture, Include Neck toggles on Blemish and Even skin, a blemish-protection mask, settings per face: every one has a documented Capture One counterpart. Adobe's Amount has the same threshold semantics. This is ordinary feature parity, but it is relevant to `docs/27` §11 ("confirm the brief is yours to use"): the research cannot tell where the brief's constants came from; the owner should answer that question before round 2. It also means Capture One's documented behaviour gives ready-made test expectations (Blemish 0–40 = visible marks only; Even Skin 40/50 ≈ natural, 80/−70 ≈ polished).
- **What Yellowmoon lacks that the market has:** a separate control for permanent marks (Adobe Fade, Evoto Mole/Freckle split, PortraitPro Details), shine reduction (Capture One reviewers miss it; Luminar, Evoto, Retouch4me Mattifier have it), redness/colour evening (Retouch4me Skin Tone, Resolve Ultra Beauty Color Preservation), contouring, eye and teeth tools. Yellowmoon's Even skin is luminance-only by design, as Capture One's is said to be (alexonraw). That is a defensible minimal set for a "very few controls" product, but "Even skin" will not remove redness; only the blemish proposer and its chroma guard touch colour.
- **The moles-and-freckles problem is the industry's main blemish failure**, documented by Capture One, Evoto and Luminar themselves and seen in every test. With no licence-clean beauty-mark detector (`docs/27` §1), Yellowmoon depends on the threshold and the protection brush; the default Blemish amount should be modest (Capture One's guidance is 0–40) and mole survival must be measured explicitly.
- **Smoothing is where "artificial" verdicts land** (Capture One Even Skin per Meyson, Photoshop Blur, PortraitPro at high settings); dark-circle reduction gets the best reviews. Yellowmoon's asymmetric blend (darkening almost suppressed, brightening rolled off) matches the "lighten under-eyes gently" practice.
- Raw pipeline placement is undocumented for every commercial tool found; only darktable states linear scene-referred RGB. Yellowmoon's choice (linear AWG4 decoded from the node's LogC4) is consistent with darktable's.

## Q2. Techniques professional retouchers describe, and what "natural" means

### Takeaway
Retouchers separate the problem by spatial frequency: heal or clone texture-scale blemishes, fix tone and blotchiness at low frequency (frequency separation) and shape light with low-opacity dodge and burn. "Natural" in their writing means sparse, local edits, pores and identifying marks kept, a few imperfections left, and nothing pushed far enough to blend shadows and highlights into plastic.

### Cited Findings
- Frequency separation splits colour/tone (low) from texture (high); the low-frequency radius should leave only colour in the low layer: too low a radius plus blurring the low layer "sacrifices quality and natural skin" — [Fstoppers, Decaillet 2015 (older)](https://fstoppers.com/photoshop/five-common-mistakes-make-retouched-skin-look-unnatural-70824)
- A 2 px radius "barely picks up any texture"; at 7.8 px "we're starting to see what we don't want" in the high layer (example image, older guide) — [Fstoppers ultimate FS guide](https://fstoppers.com/post-production/ultimate-guide-frequency-separation-technique-8699)
- Painting on or blurring the low-frequency layer is destructive: "shadows and highlights start to blend together" — [Fstoppers FS mistakes (older)](https://fstoppers.com/photoshop/common-frequency-separation-mistakes-which-will-ruin-your-retouching-results-30913)
- Typical pro workflow: fix texture with clone/heal/patch, then several dodge and burn layers for the remaining tonal issues; D&B does most of the skin work — [Fstoppers D&B vs FS](https://fstoppers.com/photoshop/dodge-and-burn-versus-frequency-separation-which-better-581270)
- Dodge and burn on a Soft Light layer above the low-frequency layer, soft brush, ~1 % flow — [DIY Photography](https://www.diyphotography.net/?p=308836); [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/photography/retouch-images-frequency-separation-5132640)
- Work mostly zoomed out, zoom in only for detail; "a few remaining imperfections" keep the picture believable; heavy contouring looks "plastic", especially on small screens; build up with very low opacity and lower the layer opacity afterward to check — [Fstoppers five mistakes](https://fstoppers.com/photoshop/five-common-mistakes-make-retouched-skin-look-unnatural-70824)
- Texture-preserving retouching tutorials go back to at least 2012 — [PetaPixel 2012](https://petapixel.com/2012/11/09/how-to-retouch-portraits-without-losing-skin-texture-with-frequency-separation/)
- Professional retouching is sparse: in FFHQR "less than 40% of the pixels are edited" for most images; retouched images "must retain moles, freckles, and other distinctive features" and "preserve the color tone" (colour correction is a separate job); "blind smoothing" destroys identifying features and texture — [AutoRetouch, WACV 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf)
- Professionals' own ground truth can over-smooth: AutoRetouch's model "preserves the fine texture better than the groundtruth" because retouchers "may use large brushes" that affect areas not needing it — [AutoRetouch](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf)
- Freckles, moles and birthmarks "belong to the subject"; ask clients what to keep; test on your own past clients rather than sample images — [Fstoppers 2026](https://fstoppers.com/photoshop/photoshop-capture-one-or-evoto-which-handles-blemishes-best-905014)
- The darktable approach is the same idea in wavelet form: blotches on a coarse scale, texture on fine scales left alone — [darktable manual](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/5.6/en/module-reference/processing-modules/retouch/)

### Inferences
- Yellowmoon's design maps onto this practice: the blemish map is "healing at texture scale"; Even skin is a frequency separation where the Texture radius (0.4–2.4 % of the face) plays the role of the FS radius, and only the low band is flattened; dark circles is a low-opacity dodge confined to the under-eye. The two classic failure modes map to two parameters: a texture radius set too high pulls pore-scale detail into the flattened band (plastic); an Even-skin amount too high blends the face's modelling light (flat). Both should be tested at the slider ends, not only at defaults.
- Retouchers judge at fit-to-screen first and at 100 % second; a review sheet should show both views.

### Gaps
- Most retoucher sources are 2012–2016 Photoshop tutorials; I found no survey or quantitative definition of "natural" from the retouching trade.
- No published numbers for the typical opacity of under-eye lightening beyond "very low opacity / 1 % flow".

## Q3. How retouching quality is evaluated in research and industry

### Takeaway
Full-reference pixel metrics are close to useless for retouching because the unedited input already scores PSNR ≈ 44–46 dB and SSIM ≈ 0.99 against professional targets. Papers therefore lean on human preference studies (pairwise or ranking, random order, ties allowed), increasingly on no-reference IQA scores and ArcFace identity similarity, and now on learned reward models. Industry (DXOMARK) uses expert pairwise comparisons scaled to JOD units. Texture preservation and skin-tone fairness have no standard metric in the retouching literature; the nearest tools are wavelet energy ratios, Monk Skin Tone stratification, and ΔE thresholds from skin-colour perception studies.

### Cited Findings

**Datasets and full-reference metrics**
- FFHQR: 70,000 FFHQ images (1 MP, face-aligned, Flickr) retouched by hired professionals; split 56,000/7,000/7,000 — [AutoRetouch](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf); licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (search extract of the repository listing) — [ffhqr-dataset listing](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fskylab-tech%2Fffhqr-dataset)
- The "no edit" baseline on FFHQR scores PSNR 45.58 / SSIM 0.9938; AutoRetouch's best user-study model scores lower PSNR (44.82) than plain MSE training; the authors conclude "neither PSNR nor SSIM are reliable performance indicators" and there is "no scalable and objective measure of quality" — [AutoRetouch](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf)
- BPFRe (CVPR 2023) reports PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS and FID on FFHQR, plus a hand-picked ~1.4k-image FFHQR-Hard subset. Raw input PSNR 43.89 (All) / 37.69 (Hard); AutoRetouch 44.18 / 38.01; BPFRe 45.29 / 38.98; LPIPS (All) AutoRetouch 0.0133, BPFRe 0.0092. Differences between methods are about 1 dB — [BPFRe](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Xie_Blemish-Aware_and_Progressive_Face_Retouching_With_Limited_Paired_Data_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)
- RetouchFormer (AAAI 2024) frames retouching as "soft inpainting", predicting imperfection regions and synthesising skin; reported 45.13 dB PSNR at 10 % paired data, 0.56 dB above BPFRe (secondary summary) — [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/28404); [Liner review](https://liner.com/review/retouchformer-semisupervised-highquality-face-retouching-transformer-with-priorbased-selective-selfattention)
- HGFR (ICCV 2025): a synthetic benchmark of 25,000 pairs built by generating portraits with diffusion models and injecting moles, freckles and acne at mild/moderate/severe intensity into segmented face regions with a LoRA model, so the clean image is ground truth by construction; it also counts "face with blemish" with CLIP text similarity; reports PSNR 49.15 / SSIM 0.994 / LPIPS 0.0094 on HGFR+FFHQR — [HGFR supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/supplemental/Xu_Face_Retouching_with_ICCV_2025_supplemental.pdf)
- ABPN (CVPR 2022) is a local-retouching network for ultra-high-resolution photos (low-resolution retouching layer plus a blend pyramid) evaluated on FFHQR and a cloth set — [ABPN](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Lei_ABPN_Adaptive_Blend_Pyramid_Network_for_Real-Time_Local_Retouching_of_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)

**Human studies**
- AutoRetouch: professional retouchers saw two methods' outputs in random order and chose one or **skipped if too similar**; choice and decision time recorded; 508–1008 judgements per pair. Undecided shares were large where methods were close (32 % and 49 % on FFHQR); against the professional ground truth on studio data, the model won 76.3 %, lost 18.7 %, undecided 5 %. Its failure cases (< 2 %): large areas of hard blemishes where "the area remains slightly red" — [AutoRetouch](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf)
- AutoRetouch also tested robustness by adding arbitrary brush strokes of various colours, sizes and opacities; the model left them unchanged, "safer ... to retouch only known blemishes" — [AutoRetouch](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf)
- BPFRe: 50 in-the-wild questions, 80 validated workers rank five methods in random order, preferring "delightful content with realistic details" — [BPFRe](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Xie_Blemish-Aware_and_Progressive_Face_Retouching_With_Limited_Paired_Data_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)
- BeautyGRPO (CVPR 2026, vivo/Sun Yat-sen): argues PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS "do not reliably reflect perceived naturalness"; reports no-reference IQA (NIQE, NIMA, MUSIQ, MANIQA, NRQM, TOPIQ), FID and **ArcFace** identity similarity (ABPN 0.971 on FFHQR); user study with 100 participants on 20 cases: one-of-five preference, plus 5-point ratings on **skin smoothing, blemish removal, texture quality, clarity, identity preservation**; builds FRPref-10K, 10,000 preference pairs annotated by VLMs on five dimensions and verified by humans, to train a reward model — [BeautyGRPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01163v1)

**Industry practice**
- DXOMARK evaluates portrait detail preservation (the texture–noise compromise) from **pairwise comparisons by more than 30 experts** under controlled viewing, scaled to **JOD** (just-objectionable-difference) units, where 1 JOD means the difference is visible; face crops are scaled to the same viewing size; per-scene scales are aligned by a cross-scene annotation and least squares; a Sony A7R IV is the high-quality reference — [DXOMARK EI 2025](https://corp.dxomark.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/EI2025_RealScenes_portraits_generalization_paper.pdf)
- DXOMARK's lab protocol (Camera v6) measures face detail on two realistic mannequins, deep and fair skin tones; selfie tests score skin-tone rendering across deep to light tones — [DXOMARK v6](https://www.dxomark.com/dxomark-smartphone-camera-protocol-v6/); [DXOMARK selfie](https://www.dxomark.com/what-we-test-selfie/)
- Scaling pairwise data: Pérez-Ortiz and Mantiuk give methods and Matlab software for scaling, outlier analysis, confidence intervals, and a prior that improves accuracy with few observers — [arXiv 1712.03686](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.03686); JOD defined in their unified-scale paper — [Pérez-Ortiz & Mantiuk 2019](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/rainbow/projects/unified_quality_scale/perezortiz2019unified_quality_scale-large.pdf)

**Texture, colour and skin-tone measures**
- Wavelet roughness: the ratio of high- to low-frequency wavelet energy per level, averaged; diagonal detail energy for round features such as pores (patent) — [US 10702160](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10702160). A "digital makeup" patent separates high-frequency wrinkle/noise texture from low-frequency blemish and shading structure — [US 9495582](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9495582)
- Skin colour perception (simulated facial skin images, 90 observers, 2018): 50 % thresholds in CIELAB, perceptibility/acceptability ΔL* 0.119/0.864, Δa* 0.993/2.037, Δb* 1.147/2.517, ΔE*ab 1.497/1.808; "changes in L* were more perceptible and less acceptable" than a* or b*. CIEDE2000 not reported; prosthodontic context — [Lagouvardos et al., J Prosthodont Res 62(4)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpr/62/4/62_503/_article)
- Skin-tone diversity: the 10-point Monk Skin Tone scale is used for camera testing with printed faces; darker skin was underexposed relative to lighter skin under the same light in an early Imatest test; a 2026 paper (Borek, Limbocker, Monk) validated wide-gamut printed face charts against real participants spanning the scale, while saying they do not replace testing with real people — [Imatest 2026](https://www.imatest.com/2026/03/ei2026-skin-tone-charts-real-human-data/); [Imatest equity](https://www.imatest.com/equity)
- MST-E (Google): 1,515 images and 31 videos of 19 subjects spanning the MST scale, from TONL; released for research or annotator training only and **may not be used to train ML models** — [Schumann et al., arXiv 2305.09073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09073v3); [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/consensus-and-subjectivity-of-skin-tone-annotation-for-ml-fairness/)
- Physically based skin variation: Tsumura's ICA separation of melanin and haemoglobin in optical density gives a way to synthesise changes in either pigment (e.g. redness vs pigmentation), validated with UV-B and methyl nicotinate — [Tsumura SIGGRAPH 2003](https://www.mi.tj.chiba-u.jp/~tsumura/Tsumura/papers/siggraph2003.htm); [JOSA A 1999](https://www.mi.tj.chiba-u.jp/~tsumura/Tsumura/papers/JOSA99_ICA.htm). A physics-based blemish simulation for retouching exists (NTU, title only seen) — [NTU record](https://libmast.utm.my/Record/ntu-10356%2F173577/Details)

### Inferences
- For Yellowmoon, masked metrics on synthetic ground truth are worth having (they answer "did the blemish go, did everything else stay"), but whole-image PSNR/SSIM is not: the unedited image already wins on them.
- ArcFace similarity is saturated for local retouching (ABPN 0.971) and adds little for three tools that never move geometry; texture energy and mole survival are more informative.
- The 2018 skin-perception thresholds suggest the colour gate should be stricter for lightness than for chroma: roughly ΔL* under 0.5 and Δa*, Δb* under 1 on mean skin colour outside the treated regions would stay below acceptability with margin (an inference from one prosthodontic study, not a standard).
- With one owner as the only rater, AutoRetouch's "skip if too similar" and decision-time logging are directly reusable; DXOMARK's JOD scaling is overkill for two-way tests but useful when choosing among three or more constant sets.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed skin-texture-preservation or pore-visibility metric for retouching was found; the wavelet ratio is from a patent.
- No retouching paper found that reports results by skin tone; skin-tone fairness of blemish detectors (e.g. redness contrast on deep skin) is unmeasured in the literature I saw.
- The licence of ArcFace/InsightFace weights for use as a test oracle was not checked.
- RetouchingFFHQ (retouching detection) was found but not read.

## Q4. Lessons and a recommended evaluation protocol for Yellowmoon

### Takeaway
Position Yellowmoon's retouch as "Capture One-style, minimal and texture-first": three tools, a conservative Blemish default, luminance-only Even skin with texture kept, a gentle under-eye lift, no generative fill. Judge it in three layers: synthetic tests with known truth in CI (blemish removed, everything else unchanged, texture kept, moles kept, colour unshifted, never darker), a skin-tone and face-size stratified visual set from licence-clean or evaluation-tier photos, and the owner's blind A/B through the existing `tools/eval/ab` kit with "too similar" and "needs a fix" reasons.

### Cited Findings
- Yellowmoon's existing blind A/B kit records random-side pairs, counts ties as half a win, and gates on ≥ 60 % wins, a 95 % Wilson lower bound above 50 %, no stratum below 45 %, and no more "needs a fix" marks than the reference; thresholds are provisional and need the owner's agreement — [tools/eval/ab/README.md](../../../tools/eval/ab/README.md)
- `docs/13` specifies the blind protocol on a calibrated P3 display, random order and side, with a binomial interval — [docs/13-testing.md](../../../docs/13-testing.md)
- The repository's photo tiers: CC0 and freely licensed files can be golden images; copyrighted evaluation-tier files are for local visual review and blind A/B only — [docs/13-testing.md](../../../docs/13-testing.md); [docs/test-photo-sources.md](../../../docs/test-photo-sources.md)
- `docs/27` §8 already requires identity, mask locality, threshold, gain clamp, chroma guard, masked-blur fixed point, blend asymmetry, texture (noise amplitude within 5 % after Even skin 100), scale and determinism tests — [docs/27-skin-retouch.md](../../../docs/27-skin-retouch.md)
- Capture One's documented reference points: Blemish 0–40 for visible blemishes only; Even Skin 40/50 natural, 80/−70 polished; minimum face 200 px — [Capture One blog](https://www.captureone.com/blog/how-to-use-retouch-faces-portrait-retouching)
- Trials needed for the Wilson lower bound to clear 50 % at a given true win share (computed here, z = 1.96, ties excluded): 60 % → 97 decisive trials, 65 % → 43, 70 % → 25, 75 % → 16, 80 % → 11 — computed with the Wilson score formula used by [tools/eval/ab/README.md](../../../tools/eval/ab/README.md)

### Inferences

**Lessons for positioning**
1. Threshold semantics are standard and should be stated in the UI copy the same way ("low values fix only the most visible marks"). Default Blemish in the 25–40 range, not 50+: every vendor and reviewer reports moles and freckles going above about 40–50.
2. Without a beauty-mark detector, protection is a brush. Say so plainly; and consider (V2) splitting "temporary" vs "permanent" marks as Adobe's Fade does, which needs a classifier with licence-clean data.
3. Keep Even skin luminance-only and its default low; the "artificial" verdicts in reviews are about smoothing, not blemish removal. Present Texture as "keep more texture" in the positive direction, as Capture One does.
4. Dark circles is the tool most likely to please; the risk is grey or flat under-eyes. The asymmetric blend and the eye-exclusion feather are the right guards; test lower-lid lashes and eye-bag shadows on real faces.
5. Watch for the Photoshop failure "uneven colour patches" after blemish removal; the chroma guard is the defence and needs a test on redness-dominant blemishes, the case AutoRetouch fails on ("remains slightly red").
6. A cinema-look processor gains from not hallucinating: unlike Apple's Clean Up or generative tools, Yellowmoon's proposer only produces a clamped gain (0.5–2.5) per pixel, so it cannot invent structure. That is a real differentiator to state.

**Recommended protocol (no labelled data of Yellowmoon's own)**

*A. Synthetic, in CI (deterministic, licence-clean, seeded)*
1. **Skin patches.** Procedural skin: a base colour from a set spanning light to deep (e.g. ten tones chosen to span the Monk range, defined as linear AWG4 values written by our own script), multiplied by a seeded pore texture (band-limited noise at 0.1–0.5 % of face size) and a gentle low-frequency shading gradient. No photo is needed, so no licence question.
2. **Synthetic blemishes with known truth.** Insert blemishes as optical-density changes in the spirit of Tsumura's two pigments: "acne" = a Gaussian rise in haemoglobin-like absorption (redness, a* up, small L* drop), diameter 0.3–2 % of the face, five contrast levels; "post-acne marks" = melanin-like brown spots; "moles" = small, sharp-edged, dark melanin discs (to be kept); "freckles" = clusters of faint melanin dots (to be kept below a set amount). Ground truth is the patch before insertion.
3. **Metrics per blemish** (masked, at the blemish disc): fraction of inserted optical density removed; ΔE2000 to ground truth; **outside all blemish discs**: maximum change (must be 0 where every mask is 0, already a §8 test), and mean ΔL*, Δa*, Δb* of the skin (gate suggestion: |ΔL*| < 0.5, |Δa*|, |Δb*| < 1, after the default look, from the 2018 thresholds).
4. **Threshold curve.** Fraction of blemishes fixed vs Blemish amount, by contrast level: must rise monotonically and fix strong ones first. **Mole survival**: share of mole contrast kept at the default amount (suggest ≥ 90 %) and the amount at which moles start to go (report it; it is the number reviewers test).
5. **Out-of-distribution marks** (from AutoRetouch): seeded brush strokes of random colour, width and opacity on clean skin; the classical or trained proposer should leave them mostly unchanged (report the mean change).
6. **Texture energy.** Band-pass energy at pore scale (difference of Gaussians or wavelet detail levels, the patent's high/low ratio) inside the skin mask, output over input: at defaults ≥ 0.95; with Texture at −100 vs +100 the ratio moves in the documented direction. **Low-frequency flatness**: standard deviation of blurred luminance in the mask falls with Even skin amount (report the curve). **Shading kept**: a synthetic cheekbone highlight-to-shadow gradient larger than the Even-skin radius must survive (report its contrast ratio), the plastic failure retouchers describe.
7. **Dark circles.** Synthetic under-eye darkening (luminance and a slight blue-purple shift); report the remaining gap to the cheek reference vs amount, the share of pixels darkened by more than 0.5 % (expect ≈ 0), and lash/eye-edge pixels changed (expect 0 inside the feathered eye exclusion).
8. Avoid whole-image PSNR/SSIM; if reported, report masked PSNR at blemish sites only, beside the "no edit" baseline.

*B. Real-photo visual set (local, evaluation tier allowed)*
1. 30–40 portraits stratified by skin tone (MST 1–3, 4–7, 8–10), face size (close-up, half-body, small faces near the minimum), light (soft, hard, mixed/tungsten) and marks (freckles, moles, acne). CC0/CC-BY raws first; evaluation-tier files for local review only. MST-E images are research-only and banned for training; usable at most for local visual review after the owner reads the terms.
2. A contact sheet per photo: off / default / Blemish 80 / Even skin 80 with Texture −70 and +70 / Dark circles 80, each at fit and at a 100 % face crop. The owner marks per cell: moles or freckles lost, colour patch, halo at mask edge, plastic, grey under-eye, missed blemish. This sheet finds failure modes; the A/B below measures preference.

*C. Blind A/B by the owner (the gate)*
1. Reuse `tools/eval/ab` with a new `--test skin`. Trials: default retouch vs off (the main question: does the default improve the photo?), and separately default vs a candidate constant set when tuning. Random side; answers: left, right, "too similar" (as AutoRetouch), plus an optional "needs a fix" with the reason codes from B.2; record decision time.
2. Show the full photo first, then allow a 100 % face crop; a calibrated display as `docs/13` requires.
3. Gate (proposal, same form as the kit's, owner to agree): retouch on wins ≥ 60 % with ties as half; Wilson lower bound > 50 % on decisive trials; no skin-tone stratum below 45 %; "needs a fix" no more often than off. At a true 70 % win share about 25 decisive trials suffice; at 65 % about 43; at 60 % about 97, so plan 40–60 photos (some trials will be "too similar").
4. An optional "is it visible?" check for naturalness: show only one version at fit size and ask "retouched or not?"; detection near chance at the default means the retouch is invisible at normal viewing, which is what "natural" means in the retoucher sources.
5. For choosing among three or more variants (e.g. three Texture radii), use pairwise comparisons scaled with Pérez-Ortiz and Mantiuk's method (their prior is designed for few observers) rather than repeated two-way gates.

### Gaps
- No licence-clean, skin-tone-diverse portrait raw set is known to this research; the stratified set in B depends on what the owner can collect or download for local review.
- The synthetic blemish model (pigment densities, sizes) is unvalidated against real blemish statistics; HGFR's diffusion-injected blemishes are an alternative but its licence chain (Unsplash photos, Civitai models) was not checked for Yellowmoon's use.
- Thresholds suggested above (ΔL* 0.5, texture ratio 0.95, mole survival 90 %, 60 % win share) are proposals for the owner, not derived from a standard.
