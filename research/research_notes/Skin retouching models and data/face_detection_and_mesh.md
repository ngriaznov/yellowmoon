# Face detection and dense face mesh models for Yellowmoon's skin retouching (as of 2026-11-11)

Scope: licence-clean options for (a) finding faces (rectangles, size, pose) and (b) a dense face mesh from which `docs/27` §4–5 rasterises per-vertex tables (`UnderEye`, `InvertedSkinUniformity`) and region masks (face oval, eyes, brows, lips, jawline) at full resolution, run in Rust on Apple silicon with no Python at run time.

Method note: besides web sources, this session downloaded Google's published model files and MediaPipe's mesh data files from the URLs cited below and inspected them locally (HTTP `content-length`, SHA-256, a TFLite flatbuffer parse with the Apache-2.0 `tflite` Python bindings for tensor shapes, operator lists and multiply-add counts, and an OBJ parse of the canonical mesh). Those numbers are this session's own counts from the cited files, not figures Google publishes. Nothing was committed to the repository.

## 1. MediaPipe Face Detector (BlazeFace) and Face Landmarker: licences, files, inputs, accuracy, topology, region lists

### Takeaway
Every piece Yellowmoon would need from MediaPipe is Apache-2.0: the model cards of BlazeFace and Face Mesh V2 say "Licensed under Apache License, Version 2.0", and the canonical mesh (468 vertices, 898 triangles, UVs) and the region index lists live in the Apache-2.0 `google-ai-edge/mediapipe` repository. The models are tiny (BlazeFace full range 1.08 MB, Face Mesh V2 2.55 MB at f16), trained on Google's own consented captures rather than an academic dataset, and have not changed since 2022–2023.

**Files (this session's HEAD requests and parses of the cited files)**

- `blaze_face_full_range.tflite`: 1,083,786 B, published on the Tasks model bucket on 2026-03-11 but byte-identical to the 2023 asset `face_detection_full_range.tflite`; input 1×192×192×3; outputs 2,304 anchors × 16 and 2,304 scores; about 514 k constants; 105.7 M multiply-adds; ops as above plus RESIZE_BILINEAR — [blaze_face_full_range.tflite](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/face_detector/blaze_face_full_range/float16/latest/blaze_face_full_range.tflite); [face_detection_full_range.tflite](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/face_detection_full_range.tflite)
- `face_detection_full_range_sparse.tflite`: 676,746 B — [sparse asset](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/face_detection_full_range_sparse.tflite); the guide calls the sparse model "roughly 60% smaller" — [Face Detector guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_detector)
- `face_landmarks_detector.tflite` (Face Mesh V2): input 1×256×256×3; outputs a 1,434-value tensor (478 × xyz), a 1-value face-presence output and a 1×1 output; about 1.20 M constants (f16); 112.9 M multiply-adds; 471 ops, only CONV_2D (72), PRELU (69), DEPTHWISE_CONV_2D (34), ADD (34), MAX_POOL_2D (6), PAD (3), LOGISTIC (1), RESHAPE (1) plus DEQUANTIZE — [face_landmarker.task](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/face_landmarker/face_landmarker/float16/latest/face_landmarker.task)
- Older meshes: `face_landmark.tflite` (V1, 468 points) 1,242,398 B, input 192×192, about 605 k constants, 35.0 M multiply-adds, outputs 1,404 values (468 × 3); `face_landmark_with_attention.tflite` (478 points) 2,495,952 B — [face_landmark.tflite](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/face_landmark.tflite); [face_landmark_with_attention.tflite](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/face_landmark_with_attention.tflite)
- The blendshapes model (input 1×146×2, 52 scores) uses MEAN, TRANSPOSE, RSQRT and similar ops; skin retouching does not need it — [Face Landmarker guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_landmarker)

**Inputs and outputs as Google documents them**
- Face Landmarker bundle: "BlazeFace short-range", "FaceLandmarker-V2" with "Input 256 x 256" and "478 3D face landmarks", blendshapes "1 x 146 x 2" giving 52 scores; float16; x and y normalised to [0, 1], z "scaled like x relative to the center of the head"; an optional 4×4 "facial transformation matrix" from canonical face coordinates to the detected face; `numFaces` default 1, three confidence thresholds default 0.5 — [Face Landmarker guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_landmarker)
- **Conflict:** the Landmarker guide lists the bundled detector's input as 192×192, but the bundled file is the 128×128 short-range model (identical SHA-256) — [Face Landmarker guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_landmarker) vs the [file itself](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/face_detector/blaze_face_short_range/float16/latest/blaze_face_short_range.tflite). Likewise the BlazeFace full-range card says "resized to 160x192 pixels" while the published full-range file's input tensor is 192×192 — [BlazeFace full-range card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/MediaPipe%20BlazeFace%20Model%20Card%20(Full%20Range).pdf)
- Face Mesh V2 input: "Image of cropped face with 25% margin on each side, and resized to 256x256"; output "478 3D landmarks"; x, y in crop pixels; "z-coordinates are relative to the face center of mass and are scaled proportionally to the face width"; a face flag with default threshold 0.5 — [Face Mesh V2 card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20MediaPipe%20Face%20Mesh%20V2.pdf)
- Face Detector: 6 keypoints (eyes, nose tip, mouth, both ear tragions), a box and a score; the guide's only latency figure is short range on a Pixel 6: 2.94 ms CPU, 7.41 ms GPU — [Face Detector guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_detector)
- BlazeFace full range: "1.1MB", "CenterNet-like with a custom encoder", "~50FPS on Pixel 3 single-core CPU with XNNPACK", "~145FPS on Pixel 4 CPU with XNNPACK" — [BlazeFace full-range card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/MediaPipe%20BlazeFace%20Model%20Card%20(Full%20Range).pdf)

**Accuracy**
- Face Mesh V2: IOD-normalised mean absolute error (2D only) 2.62 % (sd 1.03) in tracking mode and 3.24 % (sd 1.29) in reacquisition mode (first frame, BlazeFace crop), on 1,700 images from 17 regions; regions range 2.06–3.27 % (tracking) and 2.67–3.85 % (reacquisition); skin tones 2.49–2.90 % and 3.09–3.75 %; genders 2.61/2.62 % and 3.24/3.25 %; human inter-annotator discrepancy 2.56 % IOD MAE on 58 samples; version 09/15/2022 improved "2.88 -> 2.76 IOD MAE" over 08/28/2022 "and predicts 10 additional iris landmarks" — [Face Mesh V2 card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20MediaPipe%20Face%20Mesh%20V2.pdf)
- BlazeFace full range: recall 98.9 % average across perceived genders, 99.2 % across skin tones (98–100 %), 99.3 % across subregions (96.7–100 %); average precision 99.8 % across genders, 99.6 % across skin tones, but 89.3 % average (88.1–89.6 %) across subregions — [BlazeFace full-range card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/MediaPipe%20BlazeFace%20Model%20Card%20(Full%20Range).pdf)
- Papers: "Real-time Facial Surface Geometry from Monocular Video on Mobile GPUs" (Kartynnik, Ablavatski, Grishchenko, Grundmann; CVPR workshop 2019) introduces the 468-vertex mesh and reports quality comparable to manual-annotation variance — [arXiv:1907.06724](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06724); "Attention Mesh" (Grishchenko et al., June 2020) "runs at over 50 FPS on a Pixel 2" and is "30 percent faster" than a cascade at the same accuracy — [arXiv:2006.10962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10962)

**Topology and region lists (this session's parse of the cited files)**
- `canonical_face_model.obj`: 468 vertices, 468 UV coordinates, 898 triangles, 1,365 edges; the only open boundary is one 36-edge loop, exactly the 36 vertices of `FACEMESH_FACE_OVAL`, so the eyes and mouth are covered by triangles, not holes; canonical extent about 15.5 × 17.7 units (centimetres) with an IOD of 6.3 (0.407 of face width); mean edge 5.9 % and longest edge 18.5 % of face width — [canonical_face_model.obj](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/modules/face_geometry/data/canonical_face_model.obj)
- A 4096 × 4096 UV visualisation of the mesh is in the same folder — [canonical_face_model_uv_visualization.png](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/modules/face_geometry/data/canonical_face_model_uv_visualization.png)
- Region lists: lips 40 edges / 40 vertices (inner and outer contour), each eye 16 / 16, each brow 8 edges / 10 vertices, each iris 4 / 4 (indices 468–477), face oval 36 / 36, nose 25 / 24, tessellation 2,556 edge entries over 468 vertices — [face_mesh_connections.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/python/solutions/face_mesh_connections.py)
- The face-geometry metadata defines 33 weighted Procrustes landmarks (e.g. landmark 4 weight 0.0709, landmark 33 weight 0.0587) used to fit the canonical model and produce the pose matrix — [geometry_pipeline_metadata_landmarks.pbtxt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/modules/face_geometry/data/geometry_pipeline_metadata_landmarks.pbtxt)

**Currency**
- The bundle was last modified 2023-05-03; no newer Face Landmarker model or announcement was found in 2026 searches; the older Face Mesh solution was superseded by Face Landmarker in May 2023 — [face_landmarker.task](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/face_landmarker/face_landmarker/float16/latest/face_landmarker.task); [MediaPipe legacy Face Mesh docs](https://mediapipe.readthedocs.io/en/latest/solutions/face_mesh.html)

### Inferences
- The licence chain is as clean as SAM 2.1's or cleaner: the weights' own licence is Apache-2.0, and the training data is Google's own consented captures, not a non-commercial academic set. It fits the `docs/14` precedent where third-party weights are judged by their own licence and the card states the data "as Google states it". Converting to f16 safetensors is a change to the work, so the card and `NOTICE` must say so (Apache-2.0 §4(b)), as for Depth Anything.
- Shipping the mesh data (canonical OBJ with UVs, the region lists, the Procrustes weights) is a plain Apache-2.0 redistribution with attribution. The per-vertex tables we author on top are our own data.
- Because eyes and mouth are triangulated, region masks for eyes and lips must be rasterised from the contour polygons in the region lists, not taken from holes in the mesh. `FaceParser::SkinWithoutEars` would be the face-oval polygon (or the mesh's triangles) minus the eye, brow and lip polygons.
- Only Face Mesh V2 and a detector are needed, about 1.2 M + 0.5 M parameters, roughly 3.6 MB at f16 in all: about 1/20 of SAM 2.1 small's 77 MB.

### Gaps
- No licence or terms page specific to the `storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models` bucket was found. The model cards are the licence source.
- The model cards do not say how many training images Face Mesh V2 used or how they were annotated for 478 points (only "11 human annotators").
- I found no published accuracy per region (eyes, lips, face oval, irises) for Face Mesh V2. Only whole-mesh IOD MAE is published.
- The meaning of the third 1×1 output of `face_landmarks_detector.tflite` was not confirmed. MediaPipe's graph config would settle it.

## 2. Running MediaPipe's TFLite graphs in Rust on Apple silicon, and the expected latency for a 24 MP photo

### Takeaway
Do not import TFLite at run time. Hand-port the two networks into `ym-ml`, as `crop_net` and `bokeh_net` were done: they use only plain convolutions, depthwise convolutions, PReLU, add, max-pool, pad and a sigmoid. Extract the f16 weights to safetensors with a CI-only Python script, and check parity against the TFLite interpreter. At Yellowmoon's measured CPU throughput, the detector plus two mesh passes per face come to roughly 0.1–0.2 s of single-core work per face, against SAM's 3.1 s encoder. No Apple-silicon timing exists yet.

### Cited Findings
- `ym-ml` runs every shipped model in its own pure-Rust CPU code, in f32 with f16 storage in safetensors. The rule: "f32 in a fixed order, no fused multiply-add, `libm` for `exp`, `erf`, `tanh` and `log`, every output row computed by one task whatever the pool". burn (wgpu) and ort (`load-dynamic`, optional CoreML) are optional features, off by default — [docs/07](../../../docs/07-ml-models.md); [crates/ym-ml/Cargo.toml](../../../crates/ym-ml/Cargo.toml)
- SAM 2.1 small: 38.5 M parameters, 77.1 MB at f16, encoder "3.1 s at best, 3.6 s median" on a 4-core Xeon; weights converted by `training/sam2/export.py` in CI, parity fixtures from Meta's code; "the Mac numbers are still to be measured" — [docs/07](../../../docs/07-ml-models.md)
- Depth Anything V2-Small: 24.8 M parameters, about 50 MB at f16; about 100.6 GMAC at 518×784, estimated at 2–2.5 s on four Xeon threads, "no Apple-silicon timing exists (M0-H4)" — [docs/07](../../../docs/07-ml-models.md); [research/depth-mask-2026-11-07.md](../../depth-mask-2026-11-07.md)
- The closest shipped analogue is Yellowmoon's crop model: MobileNetV2-style, 256×256 input, "1,101,852 parameters, 263 M multiply-adds", measured at 120 ms "on one core of a 2.8 GHz Xeon"; its parity fixture matches PyTorch within 2.2e-7 — [docs/20](../../../docs/20-auto-crop.md)
- tract's TFLite front end is described as unimplemented ("contributions are welcome"); tract is "designed around ONNX and NNEF", with TF models expected to be converted to ONNX first — [tract-tflite 0.22.0](https://docs.rs/crate/tract-tflite/0.22.0); [tract-api](https://docs.rs/crate/tract-api/0.22.4)
- Third-party ports exist: py-feat's `mp_facemesh_v2` is a PyTorch port (TFLite → ONNX → PyTorch with onnx2torch), tagged Apache-2.0, input 256×256 with a 25 % margin — [py-feat/mp_facemesh_v2](https://huggingface.co/py-feat/mp_facemesh_v2/blob/main/README.md?code=true). ONNX conversions of Face Mesh V2 and BlazeFace are also on Hugging Face — [QtMeshEditor-facemesh-onnx](https://huggingface.co/fernandotonon/QtMeshEditor-facemesh-onnx); [mediapipe-face-onnx](https://huggingface.co/Heliosoph/mediapipe-face-onnx)
- Operator sets, multiply-adds and anchor layouts (this session's parse, §1): short range 30.8 M MAC, full range 105.7 M MAC, Face Mesh V2 112.9 M MAC, legacy 192-px mesh 35.0 M MAC — [face_landmarker.task](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/face_landmarker/face_landmarker/float16/latest/face_landmarker.task); [blaze_face_full_range.tflite](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/face_detector/blaze_face_full_range/float16/latest/blaze_face_full_range.tflite)
- Reference speeds on phones: short-range detection 2.94 ms on a Pixel 6 CPU; full range "~50FPS on Pixel 3 single-core CPU with XNNPACK" — [Face Detector guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_detector); [BlazeFace full-range card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/MediaPipe%20BlazeFace%20Model%20Card%20(Full%20Range).pdf)
- The Vision bridge is accepted because it calls an OS framework, and Core ML's precision "varies based on the hardware and software versions", which breaks byte-identical exports between the app and `ym` — [research/depth-mask-2026-11-07.md](../../depth-mask-2026-11-07.md)

### Inferences
- **Porting cost is small.** Every operator already exists in `crop_net` or `bokeh_net` (convolutions, depthwise convolutions, add, pad) or is trivial (PReLU, max-pool 2×2, sigmoid). PReLU needs no `exp`, so the network is fully deterministic without `libm`. The work is mostly an export script that reads the TFLite flatbuffer, widens the f16 constants and writes safetensors with a self-describing header (the TFLite NHWC layout and TF "SAME" padding must be reproduced exactly), plus the detector's SSD anchor decode and NMS. Anchor counts follow from the tensors: 896 for short range and 2,304 (48×48) for full range. MediaPipe's own graph uses a weighted NMS whose exact rule must be copied from its Apache-2.0 calculators. A judgement, not a measurement: well under SAM's 3,500 lines.
- **Oracle fixtures** can come from the TFLite interpreter or the MediaPipe Python package (Apache-2.0) in CI, as test tooling only, on public photos. That matches how SAM and Depth were done. The f16-storage cost is nil here, because the published weights are already f16.
- **Latency estimate** (scaling from the crop model's 263 M MAC in 120 ms on one Xeon core, about 2.2 GMAC/s; PReLU is cheaper than the crop model's SiLU): full-range detector about 50 ms; Face Mesh V2 about 50 ms per pass; two passes per face (detector crop, then a re-crop from the first mesh) about 0.1 s per face on one core. A 24 MP group photo with a tiled detector pass (about 12 tiles plus one whole-frame pass) and 10 faces would take about 1.5 s of single-core work, and less on parallel rows. Cropping a 256² patch from the full-resolution face (area or bilinear sampling) and rasterising 898 triangles into a mask of up to 4000² px cost tens of milliseconds. These are extrapolations on the documented Linux machine. The Mac number is part of M0-H4.
- ort with CoreML or burn on the GPU could make it faster, but would give up bit-identical exports. At about 0.1 GMAC per face there is no need for either.

### Gaps
- No measured Rust time for these networks on any machine, and no Apple-silicon CPU timing for any Yellowmoon model.
- The exact MediaPipe pre-processing (crop rotation from the eye keypoints, a 1.5× box expansion, letterbox or stretch, the input range [-1, 1] or [0, 1] per model) has to be read from the Apache-2.0 graph configs (`face_detection_*.pbtxt`, `face_landmark_*.pbtxt`). They were not read in this session.

## 3. Apple Vision face requests: what they give, macOS versions, whether they can replace a dense mesh, determinism

### Takeaway
Vision gives good face rectangles with continuous roll, yaw and pitch (revision 3, macOS 12+) and a sparse 76-point constellation with pupils (revision 3, macOS 10.15+). Its face contour runs only "from the left cheek, over the chin, to the right cheek", with no forehead, and 76 points cannot carry per-vertex tables. Answers may change across macOS versions, and Vision does not exist in `ym` on Linux. So Vision can at most be an optional Mac-only face finder. It cannot replace the mesh, and it is a poor basis for exports that must be byte-identical.

### Cited Findings
- `VNDetectFaceLandmarksRequest`: macOS 10.13+. Revisions 1 (deprecated), 2 and 3. A `constellation` property with `constellation65Points`, `constellation76Points` and `constellationNotDefined`. `inputFaceObservations` lets you pass your own boxes — [VNDetectFaceLandmarksRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vndetectfacelandmarksrequest); [VNRequestFaceLandmarksConstellation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnrequestfacelandmarksconstellation)
- `VNDetectFaceLandmarksRequestRevision3`: macOS 10.15+ — [Revision3](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vndetectfacelandmarksrequestrevision3). WWDC21: "the latest revision is revision number three. This revision offers 76-point constellation to better represent major face regions and also provide accurate pupil detection" — [WWDC21 10040](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10040/)
- `VNFaceLandmarks2D` regions: `allPoints`, `faceContour` ("from the left cheek, over the chin, to the right cheek"), `leftEye`, `rightEye`, `leftEyebrow`, `rightEyebrow`, `nose`, `noseCrest`, `medianLine`, `outerLips`, `innerLips`, `leftPupil`, `rightPupil`. Points are normalised to the face's `boundingBox` with a lower-left origin — [VNFaceLandmarks2D](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnfacelandmarks2d)
- `VNFaceLandmarkRegion2D` offers per-point precision estimates — [VNFaceLandmarkRegion2D](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnfacelandmarkregion2d)
- `VNDetectFaceRectanglesRequestRevision3`: macOS 12.0+ — [Revision3](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vndetectfacerectanglesrequestrevision3). WWDC21: revision 3 "can now also detect faces covered by masks"; the detector "can find faces with arbitrary orientation, different sizes, and also partially occluded"; pitch was added and "all three metrics [are] reported in continuous space" (previously roll and yaw "in discrete bins") — [WWDC21 10040](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10040/)
- `VNFaceObservation` carries `roll`, `yaw`, `pitch` (radians), `landmarks` and `faceCaptureQuality` — [VNFaceObservation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnfaceobservation). Face capture quality "is a comparative measure of the same subject" and "not designed to compare faces of different people" — [WWDC21 10040](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10040/)
- The Swift-only API `DetectFaceLandmarksRequest` needs macOS 15.0 and has a `Revision` enum and `supportedRevisions`; its page does not say which revision is the default — [DetectFaceLandmarksRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/detectfacelandmarksrequest)
- On revisions, Apple says: "we always recommend to set explicitly to guarantee deterministic behavior in the future. This is because if new revisions are introduced, the default will also change to represent the latest available revision" — [WWDC21 10040](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10040/)
- Yellowmoon's bridge (`crates/ym-apple`) builds its Vision code for macOS 14+ on both architectures. The spec records that "Apple Vision may answer differently on another macOS version; within one Mac the app and `ym` agree, and the Linux goldens cannot cover it" — [crates/ym-apple/README.md](../../../crates/ym-apple/README.md); [docs/23](../../../docs/23-nodes.md)
- I found no published accuracy figures for Vision's 76 points — [search, WWDC21 10040](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10040/)

### Inferences
- **No stand-in for the mesh.** 76 points with no forehead points cannot define a face oval, an under-eye surface or a uniformity table. The forehead would have to be extrapolated, and the `UnderEye` table would turn into hand-built splines between the lower eyelid and the contour.
- **Determinism.** Even with the revision pinned, a revision number pins Apple's algorithm family, not the bits. Apple gives no promise that the same revision returns the same numbers on a new macOS, and Yellowmoon already treats Vision as able to "answer differently". Face boxes feed every downstream radius (`D`, `F` in `docs/27` §3 and §5), so Vision faces would make the whole skin node Mac-only and dependent on the macOS version. Unlike Subject and People, this would be a whole tool, not one mask kind. MediaPipe in Rust avoids that.
- **Where Vision still helps.** As an optional Mac-only second opinion for detection: masked faces, extreme orientation, yaw and pitch for a "skip profile faces" rule. Or for evaluation on developer Macs. Its People instance mask is already the planned neck source (`docs/27` §1).

### Gaps
- The point counts per region in the 76-point constellation are not in Apple's documentation (the 65 and 76 totals are).
- No documentation states the default revision on macOS 14–27, or whether revision 3 output changed between OS releases. Only measurement on several Macs would tell.
- No published minimum face size for Vision face rectangles.

## 4. Alternatives and their licences (weights, training data, size, speed)

### Takeaway
Outside MediaPipe, every dense or 68-point landmarker found carries non-commercial weights or is trained on research-only data (300-W, 300W-LP, the Basel Face Model, FLAME, InsightFace data). The only other permissive detector is YuNet (MIT weights, about 230 KB), and it is trained on WIDER FACE, which the Hugging Face card lists as CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Apart from MediaPipe, only dlib's public-domain 5-point model has a clean chain, and 5 points are not a mesh. Google's new GNM Head (July 2026, Apache-2.0) is a parametric head model, not an image landmarker.

### Cited Findings
**Detectors**
- **YuNet (OpenCV Zoo)**: "All files in this directory are licensed under MIT License". Versions `face_detection_yunet_2023mar.onnx` (fixed input), `face_detection_yunet_2026may.onnx` (dynamic input, re-exported for OpenCV 5.x) and a block-quantised int8. Detects faces from about 10×10 to 300×300 px. WIDER FACE validation AP 0.884 / 0.866 / 0.750 (easy/medium/hard) in the table, while the introduction says 0.834 / 0.824 / 0.708 (inconsistent on the page) — [YuNet README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv_zoo/main/models/face_detection_yunet/README.md). Git LFS pointers give 232,589 B (2023mar) and 229,738 B (2026may) — [opencv_zoo face_detection_yunet](https://github.com/opencv/opencv_zoo/tree/main/models/face_detection_yunet)
- YuNet's training repository says "YuNet face detection training on WIDER Face" (code BSD-3-Clause) — [libfacedetection.train](https://github.com/ShiqiYu/libfacedetection.train)
- WIDER FACE is tagged CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 on its Hugging Face card; a Kaggle mirror claims CC0, an untrustworthy derivative listing. The official site returned HTTP 503 in this session — [HF wider_face card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/wider_face/blob/main/README.md); [official site](http://shuoyang1213.me/WIDERFACE/)

- **dlib MMOD CNN face detector**: released into the public domain by Davis King; trained on images he gathered from ImageNet, AFLW, Pascal VOC, VGG, WIDER and FaceScrub — [dlib-models](https://github.com/davisking/dlib-models)

**Landmarks and 3D**
- **dlib 68-point** (`shape_predictor_68_face_landmarks.dat`): "trained on the ibug 300-W dataset"; "The license for this dataset excludes commercial use", and the dataset's creator asked for a note that "the trained model therefore can't be used in a commercial product". **dlib 5-point**: trained on dlib's own 7,198-face dataset, released into the public domain — [dlib-models](https://github.com/davisking/dlib-models)
- **300-W**: "The data are provided for research purposes only. Commercial use (i.e., use in training commercial algorithms) is not allowed." — [iBUG 300-W](https://ibug.doc.ic.ac.uk/resources/300-W/)
- **3DDFA_V2**: code MIT; no licence stated for the weights; trained on 300W-LP; dense output 38,365 vertices; MobileNet 3.27 M parameters and 183.5 M MACs, 120×120 input; ONNX at 1.35 ms/face on 4 threads of an i5-8259U (MobileNet ×0.5: 0.85 M parameters, 49.5 M MACs) — [3DDFA_V2 README](https://github.com/cleardusk/3DDFA_V2)
- The Basel Face Model (behind 300W-LP's 3D fits) is licensed "for internal, non-commercial research, evaluation or testing purposes", with commercial licensing through Unitectra — [BFM licence agreement](https://faces.dmi.unibas.ch/bfm/content/basel_face_model/downloads/BFM_NonCommercial_License_Agreement.pdf)

- **GNM Head (Google)**: a "parametric 3D statistical human head and face geometry model" with identity, expression and pose controls and internal anatomy, under Apache-2.0 (technical report arXiv 2607.23687, news of 2026-07-28); the page mentions no image-to-parameter regressor or landmark fitter — [google/GNM](https://github.com/google/GNM); [CG Channel, July 2026](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/07/google-open-sources-gnm-head-its-parametric-human-head-model/)

### Inferences
- **YuNet** fails `docs/14` rule 5 as written ("Shipped weights only from CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, Apache or our own data"; non-commercial data "stay evaluation only"), because its MIT weights were trained directly on WIDER FACE. This is not the teacher-chain case the owner accepted for Depth Anything: there the non-commercial data sat behind an unreleased teacher, here it is the training set itself. Use YuNet for local evaluation only, unless the owner rules otherwise.
- **3DDFA_V2, SPIGA, PIPNet-, HRNet- and dlib-68-style landmarkers** inherit 300-W, 300W-LP, WFLW or BFM terms that exclude commercial training. "Free, open-source" does not change that: rule 5 and the dataset terms are about the data's licence, and Yellowmoon is distributed. Evaluation only.
- **DECA and EMOCA** regress FLAME parameters, so they depend on FLAME 2020's non-commercial licence. FLAME 2023 Open (CC BY 4.0) has no released image regressor that I found. Out.
- **GNM Head** could serve as a future Apache-2.0 topology for a model we train ourselves (V2 work). Today it gives no way to get from a photo to a mesh.
- **dlib 5-point** is clean but gives only eye corners and the nose. It could seed a crop. MediaPipe's detector already gives 6 keypoints.

### Gaps
- DECA's and EMOCA's own repository licences and PIPNet's and HRNet's weight terms were not fetched in this session. The FLAME and 300-W conclusions above are inferences from their dependencies.
- WIDER FACE's official terms page could not be opened (HTTP 503). The CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 reading comes from the Hugging Face card.
- No permissively licensed, permissively trained dense landmarker other than MediaPipe's was found.

## 5. Authoring the per-vertex tables, and how well a mesh fitted on a 256 px crop holds at 24–61 MP (faces up to 4000 px)

### Takeaway
Authoring the two tables on the 468-vertex topology is a small data task (two arrays of 468 bytes). The UV map and the region lists make it tractable, and the result is our own Apache-2.0 data. The mesh's error scales with face size: about 2.6–3.2 % of the interocular distance, so about 40–55 px on a 4000 px face. That is below most of `docs/27`'s feather and blur radii, so the soft masks hold up. Hard eyelid and lip edges at full resolution do not, and the existing feathers and guided refinement must absorb them.

### Cited Findings
- `docs/27` §4: `FaceMesh::render(face, table, size_px)` "Rasterises the face mesh into an 8-bit mask with a per-vertex brightness table"; tables `UnderEye` and `InvertedSkinUniformity`; "the mesh tables belong to the `FaceMesh` implementation (the mock draws an analytic table)" (§9) — [docs/27](../../../docs/27-skin-retouch.md)
- `docs/27` radii, with `F` and `D` the face size in pixels: dark-circle eye exclusion feathered by +0.03 F; even-skin core feathered by −0.011 F and edge by −0.065 F; dark-circle regional blur radius 0.077 D, even-skin 0.044 D. The feather resizes the mask by `f = max(512/w, 512/h)` before dilating and blurring — [docs/27](../../../docs/27-skin-retouch.md)
- Face Mesh V2 input: face crop "with 25% margin on each side, and resized to 256x256"; error 2.62 % IOD (tracking) and 3.24 % (reacquisition); the model "is tolerant to certain level of input inaccuracy: 10% shift and scale ... 8° roll" — [Face Mesh V2 card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20MediaPipe%20Face%20Mesh%20V2.pdf)
- Canonical mesh: IOD 0.407 of face width; mean edge 5.9 %, longest 18.5 % of face width; 468 UVs; a 4096² UV visualisation (this session's parse) — [canonical_face_model.obj](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/modules/face_geometry/data/canonical_face_model.obj); [UV visualisation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/modules/face_geometry/data/canonical_face_model_uv_visualization.png)
- The eye region lists have 16 vertices per eye contour (upper and lower lid), and the irises are separate vertices 468–477 that are not in the canonical OBJ's triangles — [face_mesh_connections.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/python/solutions/face_mesh_connections.py)
- Yellowmoon's "Refine edges" uses the He–Sun–Tang guided filter (48 px radius at 100) to pull a mask edge to image edges — [research/depth-mask-2026-11-07.md](../../depth-mask-2026-11-07.md)

### Inferences
- **Scale arithmetic.** In the 256 px crop a face is about 256 / 1.5 ≈ 171 px wide, so one crop pixel is about 23 px on a 4000 px face. IOD ≈ 0.407 × face width ≈ 1,630 px, so the tracking-mode error of 2.62 % is about 43 px, and the first-pass (reacquisition) error of 3.24 % is about 53 px. In crop pixels that is about 1.8 px: the regression is sub-pixel, and a larger source image does not reduce the error. Running a second mesh pass on a crop recomputed from the first pass's landmarks is how MediaPipe's own "tracking mode" gets its lower error. For stills, two passes should therefore approach 2.6 %. This is an inference: Google measured tracking on video.
- **Against the radii** for a 4000 px face, with F ≈ D ≈ 4000: eye exclusion +120 px, even-skin core −44 px, edge −260 px, regional blurs 176–308 px. The mesh error is below every radius except the even-skin core feather, where it is about equal. The masks are soft, and the feather works at about 512 px across the region (about 8 full-resolution px per feather px), so mesh error is not the limiting factor for dark circles or even skin. It does limit anything hard-edged. Lips, eyelid margins and the face oval against the hairline or background should keep their feathers. They can optionally be refined with the existing guided filter, or intersected with the People mask for the oval against the background.
- **Table resolution.** Per-vertex values interpolated across triangles are piecewise linear. With a mean edge of about 6 % of face width, about 234 px on a 4000 px face, the under-eye gradient can only change about every 230 px, which is coarse for a crescent below the lower lid. Two remedies, both deterministic: (a) store the tables as small UV-space textures (for example 256² or 512² u8) sampled through the canonical UVs, rather than strictly per vertex; (b) subdivide the triangles once or twice with a fixed rule before rasterising. Option (a) also makes authoring easier.
- **Authoring.** Paint each table in UV space over the Apache-2.0 UV visualisation, or derive it from the region lists: `UnderEye` as a falloff from the lower-lid arc of `FACEMESH_*_EYE` towards the cheek vertices; `InvertedSkinUniformity` as high on structural non-uniformities (nostrils and alar creases, nasolabial folds, lip borders, brows, eye sockets, the jawline shadow), so that `core = mul_inv(skin, u)` leaves them alone. A Python script under `training/` (offline, allowed) can write the 468-entry tables or textures and render them on public test photos (`docs/test-photo-sources.md`) for review. Judging the look is a [HUMAN] review. The tables are tied to MediaPipe's vertex order. Any change of model or topology loses them, so the model choice should be settled first.
- **Rasterising at full resolution** means mapping the 478 crop-space points back through the crop's rotation and scale into full-resolution coordinates in f64, then scan-converting 898 triangles with pixel-centre sampling and integer rounding. That is cheap and fully deterministic, and it matches §9's "mesh and parser regions may extend outside the image and are clipped".

### Gaps
- No published per-region accuracy (under-eye, oval) for Face Mesh V2, and no study of it on high-resolution stills or 4000 px faces.
- Whether the face-oval loop reaches the hairline on high foreheads is not documented. The canonical mesh's top vertex is a fixed forehead point, so foreheads may be cut short. Checking needs public portrait photos.
- Whether the second pass on stills reaches the tracking-mode figure needs our own check on public data, for example a public landmark set used for evaluation only.

## 6. Edge cases: profile faces, glasses, beards, small faces in groups, many faces, occlusion

### Takeaway
The cards set clear limits. BlazeFace handles yaw up to 90° and roll or pitch up to 45°, but needs faces at least 5 % of the image side and at least 70 % inside the frame, and caps detections (for example at 10). Face Mesh V2 needs the eye line rotated level (8° roll tolerance), fails beyond 80° of look-away and below 50 % visibility, and degrades on crops that cannot fill its input without upscaling. Glasses and beards have no published numbers. The mesh covers them as if they were skin, so the masks must be guarded.

### Cited Findings
- BlazeFace full range: "Face roll and pitch (tilt) angles should be not more than 45 degrees away from the straight orientation. The yaw (pan) angle should not exceed 90 degrees"; "Face bounding box sides should be at least 5% of the corresponding image sides"; "At least 70% of the face bounding box should lie inside the input image"; "Produces only up to a given limit (e.g. 10) of detections even if more people are present"; out of scope: "Counting the number of people in a crowd", faces "significantly inclined from the vertical orientation", and people "further than 5 meters" — [BlazeFace full-range card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/MediaPipe%20BlazeFace%20Model%20Card%20(Full%20Range).pdf)
- Face Mesh V2 is "not suitable for detecting faces: looking away from the camera (more than 80°), inclined from the vertical orientation (more than 8°), only partially visible (less than 50% of the face), located too far away from the camera (cropped face can't be rescaled to model input of 192x192 without quality degradation)"; the crop "must be rotated in a way that a horizontal line can connect the two centers of the eyes"; degradation is expected with poor light, noise, motion or "face overlapping" — [Face Mesh V2 card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20MediaPipe%20Face%20Mesh%20V2.pdf)
- 3DDFA_V2 notes that few training images show closed eyes, so eye landmarks are less accurate with eyes closed (an example of the dataset-coverage problem) — [3DDFA_V2 README](https://github.com/cleardusk/3DDFA_V2)
- Apple Vision revision 3 detects faces "with arbitrary orientation, different sizes, and also partially occluded", including faces covered by masks — [WWDC21 10040](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10040/)
- YuNet's range is about 10–300 px faces at its input size — [YuNet README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv_zoo/main/models/face_detection_yunet/README.md)

### Inferences
- **Small faces and groups.** One full-range pass on a 6000 × 4000 frame letterboxed to 192 px only finds faces of at least about 300 px (5 % of 6000). Group photos need a tiled pass, for example overlapping 1536 px tiles that find faces from about 80 px, merged with a fixed-order NMS, and our own cap well above 10 (for example 32 faces, settled by the owner). Faces below about 192 px at full resolution would be upscaled into the mesh crop. The card warns of degradation there, but the retouch is barely visible at that size anyway. A rule such as "skip faces under N px" belongs in the spec.
- **Profiles.** Use yaw from the mesh, via MediaPipe's 33-point weighted Procrustes fit to the canonical model, in f64 and deterministic, or from the detector's keypoints. Above roughly 60–70° yaw, skip the face or fade the strength, and drop back-facing triangles using the 3D landmarks' normals, so the hidden side's hallucinated surface is not rasterised. The thresholds are a design choice still to be tuned on public photos.
- **Roll.** The crop must be rotated from the detector's eye keypoints, as MediaPipe does, or tilted heads fall outside the 8° tolerance.
- **Glasses.** The mesh puts the under-eye region under the frame's lower rim. Dark circles (a colour lift towards the regional average) could brighten dark frames or lens reflections, and the eye exclusion (+0.03 F around the eye contour) does not cover a rim 10–20 mm below the eye. Mitigations: the blend's darkening suppression does not help a lift; a person-painted protect mask (`docs/27` §6.2) does; an automatic guard (for example excluding pixels far darker or more saturated than the regional skin average) would need its own spec.
- **Beards and stubble.** The face oval and jaw are part of the mesh, so Even skin would flatten the beard's low-frequency luminance, while Texture keeps its fine detail. No licence-clean beard or hair segmenter was found here. Brush protection, or a darkness and chroma guard, are the routes.
- **Occlusion.** Hands, hair strands, microphones and masks over the face get a hallucinated mesh surface, and the skin mask will include them. Intersecting with the People mask removes background, but not the person's own hand. This is the main residual risk, and it argues for keeping the `FaceParser` trait open for a licence-clean face parser later.
- **Many faces.** Cost is linear in faces at about 0.1 s each (§2), and per-face caches already exist in `docs/27` §6.1.

### Gaps
- No published Face Mesh V2 or BlazeFace numbers for glasses, sunglasses, beards, heavy makeup, hats or hands over the face.
- The behaviour of the per-vertex masks on profile faces is untested. Only evaluation on public photos can set the yaw cut-off.

## 7. Recommended choice and why

### Takeaway
Ship MediaPipe in pure Rust for both jobs: the **BlazeFace full-range** detector (1.08 MB, 192×192, Apache-2.0), tiled for group photos, and **Face Mesh V2** (`face_landmarks_detector.tflite` from the `face_landmarker.task` bundle, 2.55 MB, 256×256, 478 points, Apache-2.0), run twice per face. Author our own `UnderEye` and `InvertedSkinUniformity` tables on MediaPipe's Apache-2.0 canonical topology, preferably as small UV textures. Keep Apple Vision as an optional Mac-only second opinion, never on the export path.

### Cited Findings
- Licences: both model cards say Apache-2.0, and the mesh data and region lists are Apache-2.0 — [Face Mesh V2 card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20MediaPipe%20Face%20Mesh%20V2.pdf); [BlazeFace full-range card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/MediaPipe%20BlazeFace%20Model%20Card%20(Full%20Range).pdf); [MediaPipe LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/LICENSE)
- Training data: Google's own consented smartphone captures — [BlazeFace full-range card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/MediaPipe%20BlazeFace%20Model%20Card%20(Full%20Range).pdf); [Face Mesh V2 card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20MediaPipe%20Face%20Mesh%20V2.pdf)
- Alternatives are non-commercial or trained on non-commercial data: InsightFace, 300-W, BFM, FLAME (except 2023 Open), WIDER FACE for YuNet — [InsightFace](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface); [300-W](https://ibug.doc.ic.ac.uk/resources/300-W/); [BFM](https://faces.dmi.unibas.ch/bfm/content/basel_face_model/downloads/BFM_NonCommercial_License_Agreement.pdf); [FLAME](https://flame.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html); [HF wider_face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/wider_face/blob/main/README.md)
- Vision's contour stops at the cheeks, and its answers may change across macOS versions — [VNFaceLandmarks2D](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnfacelandmarks2d); [docs/23](../../../docs/23-nodes.md); [WWDC21 10040](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10040/)
- `docs/27` §1 already names these routes ("MediaPipe face detection (Apache-2.0 weights) run in Rust", "MediaPipe Face Landmarker, 468 vertices (Apache-2.0 weights)") — [docs/27](../../../docs/27-skin-retouch.md)

### Inferences
- **Why MediaPipe over Vision for faces:** it keeps hard rules 6 and 8 (the app and `ym` give the same bytes on macOS and Linux; same input gives the same output). It needs one detector for the whole tool rather than a Mac-only tool. And the detector's 6 keypoints give the eye line the mesh crop needs.
- **Why V2 (478 points, 256 px) over V1 (468 points, 192 px):** lower error (the card's 2.88 → 2.76 IOD MAE between V2 builds; no V1 card was read), and iris points for an eye or pupil exclusion. The extra cost, 113 against 35 M MAC, is negligible.
- **Why full range over short range:** the short-range model targets selfie distances at 128 px. Camera photos are "back-facing camera" style, which is the full-range model's stated domain. Sparse full range (677 KB) is a size option, but has no published accuracy here.
- **Delivery:** about 3.6 MB for both models at f16, small enough for `data/models/<name>/` to be committed per AGENTS.md's "a few MB" exception, or a release asset like SAM. That is an owner choice. The model card states the data "as Google states it". `NOTICE` carries the Apache-2.0 attribution and the conversion note. The blendshapes model is not shipped.
- **Plan:** (1) an export script that reads the TFLite flatbuffers, writes f16 safetensors with a header, pins the SHA-256 of `face_landmarker.task` and `blaze_face_full_range.tflite`, and produces oracle fixtures from the MediaPipe Python package on about 20 public photos (CI only); (2) `ym_ml::face` with the detector, anchors, a deterministic weighted NMS, the rotated crop, the mesh and the Procrustes pose, with parity and thread-count tests; (3) a `FaceMesh` implementation with authored tables, plus a `FaceParser` from region polygons and the People mask for the neck; (4) evaluation sheets on public portraits and groups for glasses, beards, profiles and occlusion, a [HUMAN] review.
- **Owner decisions this raises:** whether YuNet (MIT weights on WIDER FACE) may ever ship (recommended no); whether Vision faces may serve as an optional Mac-only preview helper (recommended evaluation-only); and the face-size and yaw cut-offs.

### Gaps
- No Rust or Apple-silicon timings yet (M0-H4), and no parity numbers. Both come from the port.
- The accuracy of the tables and masks on real high-resolution portraits can only come from our own evaluation on public photos and an owner review.
