# Training (or obtaining) the blemish-removal proposer network within a $30 Modal budget

Research date 2026-11-11. Scope: the `BlemishProposer` of `docs/27` (sections 1, 4, 6): a fully convolutional, residual, unpadded CNN (12 plain 3x3 convolutions 3->64, ten 64->64, 64->3; ten depthwise 3x3 dilation-2 convolutions; learned-slope LeakyReLU; border 32; input `sqrt` of linear RGB normalised so that mean (R+2G+B)/4 = 0.5; the output-to-input ratio becomes a gain map clamped to 0.5..2.5 and applied through a strength threshold). Method: papers read as PDF where the CVF server allowed a direct download (AutoRetouch, BPFRe, Xu et al. ICCV 2025 and its supplement); dataset licence files read on GitHub or the dataset page; Modal's pricing page fetched today; an own count of Open Images' face boxes on the validation CSVs and a probe of 60 Flickr originals (method in Q2). Repository facts are cited by repo path. Anything not read at the source is marked **(unverified)**.

## Q1. Paired face-retouching / blemish datasets and their licences: may any train shipped weights?

### Takeaway
**No published paired face-retouching dataset can train shipped weights.** FFHQR (70,000 professionally retouched FFHQ faces) is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; FFHQ itself is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 as a compiled dataset; CelebA(-HQ), PPR10K, CRHD-3K (ABPN), ACNE04, Fitzpatrick17k, FaceSynthetics and DigiFace-1M are non-commercial or academic-only; HGFR (ICCV 2025, diffusion-made pairs) is unreleased and built on Unsplash photos whose Terms forbid ML use. These may only be used for **local evaluation** (the owner's rule of 2026-10-24 for research-only sets), never for shipped weights. The only clean paths are unpaired licence-clean photos plus synthetic blemishes (Q2, Q3).

### Cited Findings
- **FFHQR** ("Flickr-Faces-HQ-Retouching", AutoRetouch, Shafaei, Little, Schmidt, WACV 2021): 70,000 retouched 1024x1024 PNGs in seven parts of 10,000, each matching an FFHQ original that must be downloaded separately; "We professionally retouched FFHQ to create FFHQR"; "The retouching dataset is made available under Creative Commons BY-NC-SA 4.0 license by Skylab Technologies Incorporated" — [skylab-tech/ffhqr-dataset](https://github.com/skylab-tech/ffhqr-dataset). The paper: "we hired a team of professional image editors to retouch the images"; split 56,000 / 7,000 / 7,000; a private studio set of 203,725 pairs (DSLR, controlled light) was not released — [AutoRetouch paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf).
- FFHQR edits are sparse: "In the majority of images, less than 40% of the pixels are edited. Furthermore, most pixel value changes are in the [100, 200] range" (mean absolute update on 0..255), and "Professionally retouched images must retain moles, freckles, and other distinctive features" — [AutoRetouch paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf).
- Many FFHQR faces have nothing to remove: by a zero-shot CLIP classifier, 16 % of FFHQ / FFHQR images show blemishes, against 83 % in HGFR and 5 % in PPR10K — [Xu et al., ICCV 2025, Table 2](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Xu_Face_Retouching_with_Diffusion_Data_Generation_and_Spectral_Restorement_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf).
- **FFHQ**: 70,000 aligned 1024x1024 PNGs. "The individual images were published in Flickr by their respective authors under either Creative Commons BY 2.0, Creative Commons BY-NC 2.0, Public Domain Mark 1.0, Public Domain CC0 1.0, or U.S. Government Works license"; "The dataset itself (including JSON metadata, download script, and documentation) is made available under Creative Commons BY-NC-SA 4.0 license by NVIDIA Corporation". The metadata gives per image the Flickr `photo_url`, `author`, `license`, `license_url`, and the `in_the_wild` original's `pixel_size` and `face_rect`. Removal requests are honoured; the dataset "is not intended for ... facial recognition" — [NVlabs/ffhq-dataset](https://github.com/NVlabs/ffhq-dataset).
- **ABPN** (Lei et al., CVPR 2022, "Adaptive Blend Pyramid Network for Real-Time Local Retouching of Ultra High-Resolution Photo"): its own dataset is **CRHD-3K**, a *cloth* retouching set of 3,022 raw/retouched pairs (raw photos from Unsplash, retouched by professional editors), "for non-commercial research purposes only"; face retouching appears only in the supplement, evaluated on FFHQR; the GitHub page said the code "will be released soon" — [ABPN paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Lei_ABPN_Adaptive_Blend_Pyramid_Network_for_Real-Time_Local_Retouching_of_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf), [supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/supplemental/Lei_ABPN_Adaptive_Blend_CVPR_2022_supplemental.pdf), [CRHD-3K (mirror read via search)](https://github.com/youngLBW/CRHD-3K). So there is no separate "ABPN face dataset".
- **BPFRe** (Xie et al., CVPR 2023): trained and tested on FFHQR; "we collect 1,000 images of faces with large blemishes in the wild" for testing, without ground truth (not stated as released); a hand-picked FFHQR-Hard subset of about 1.4k — [BPFRe paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Xie_Blemish-Aware_and_Progressive_Face_Retouching_With_Limited_Paired_Data_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf).
- **HGFR** (Xu, Zhang, Lu, ICCV 2025): 25,000 pairs at 1024x1024, 23,760 synthesised + 1,240 natural; clean portraits from three Civitai Stable Diffusion checkpoints (epiCRealism, Civitai model 4201, Juggernaut XL) driven by 7,920 attribute prompts, then a LoRA injects moles, freckles and acne into segmented cheeks/forehead/nose at mild/moderate/severe intensity; 45–60 s per pair on an RTX 4090; the 1,240 natural photos come from Unsplash, citing the Unsplash licence; "HGFR will be publicly released" (no licence stated) — [paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Xu_Face_Retouching_with_Diffusion_Data_Generation_and_Spectral_Restorement_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf), [supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/supplemental/Xu_Face_Retouching_with_ICCV_2025_supplemental.pdf).
- **PPR10K**: "All files in the PPR10K dataset are available for non-commercial research purposes only"; it is portrait colour/tone retouching (11,161 photos at 4K–8K per Xu et al. Table 2), not blemish removal; code Apache-2.0 — [csjliang/PPR10K README](https://github.com/csjliang/PPR10K), [Xu et al. Table 2](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Xu_Face_Retouching_with_Diffusion_Data_Generation_and_Spectral_Restorement_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf). `docs/14` rule 5 already lists PPR10K as evaluation only — [docs/14-licensing.md](../../../docs/14-licensing.md).
- **CelebA**: "available for non-commercial research purposes only"; no further copying or distribution; CelebA-HQ is not mentioned on the page (it is derived from CelebA) — [CelebA page](https://mmlab.ie.cuhk.edu.hk/projects/CelebA.html).
- **ACNE04** (Wu et al., ICCV 2019, acne photos with severity grades and lesion counts): "This work is free for academic usage. For other purposes, please contact Xiaoping Wu" — [xpwu95/LDL README](https://github.com/xpwu95/LDL). Roboflow re-uploads tag it CC BY 4.0, but they are third-party uploads, not the authors' grant — [Roboflow acne04](https://universe.roboflow.com/andrei-dore-5lz05/acne04).
- **Fitzpatrick17k**: 16,577 clinical images from DermaAmin and Atlas Dermatologico, 114 conditions; "licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 3.0 Unported License" — [mattgroh/fitzpatrick17k](https://github.com/mattgroh/fitzpatrick17k).
- **SCIN** (Google, crowdsourced dermatology photos): 10,000+ images from 5,000+ contributions with self-reported and dermatologist-estimated Fitzpatrick types and Monk skin tones; released under the "SCIN Data Use License", which I read: a CC-BY-style grant to reproduce and share the material and adapted material, with attribution, and a restriction "You may not make any attempt to re-identify or re-link any of the individual data subjects"; I found no non-commercial clause in it — [scin repo](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/scin), [LICENSE](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/scin/blob/main/LICENSE).
- **Microsoft FaceSynthetics** (100,000 renders at 512x512, 70 landmarks, 19-class segmentation): "can be used for non-commercial research" — [microsoft/FaceSynthetics](https://github.com/microsoft/FaceSynthetics). **DigiFace-1M** (about 1.22 M images): "can be used for non-commercial research" — [microsoft/DigiFace1M](https://github.com/microsoft/DigiFace1M).
- **SFHQ** (about 425,000 synthetic 1024x1024 faces): made by encoding inspiration images (paintings, drawings, 3D renders, Stable Diffusion 1.4/2.1 outputs) with e4e into the latent space of StyleGAN2 trained on FFHQ; the repository carries an MIT LICENSE, the README states no image terms — [SelfishGene/SFHQ-dataset](https://github.com/SelfishGene/SFHQ-dataset). SFHQ-T2I: 122,726 Flux-generated faces, README says "no privacy issues or license issues" — [SFHQ-T2I](https://github.com/SelfishGene/SFHQ-T2I-dataset).

### Inferences
- Verdict per dataset for **shipped weights** (rule 5 of `docs/14`): FFHQR no; FFHQ (as distributed) no; CelebA / CelebA-HQ / CelebAMask-HQ no; PPR10K no; CRHD-3K no; HGFR no (unreleased, Unsplash Terms, SD-checkpoint chains); ACNE04 no; Fitzpatrick17k no; FaceSynthetics no; DigiFace-1M no; SFHQ no (its images pass through StyleGAN2 trained on FFHQ, an NC chain whose generator licence I did not read; the MIT tag covers the repository, not clearly the images); SFHQ-T2I no (Flux generator terms unverified). **SCIN: possibly yes** as a source of real blemish *patches* with attribution, if its images include facial acne (unverified) — an owner decision.

- FFHQ's per-image licences include CC BY 2.0, PDM and CC0, so a subset of the *Flickr originals* is clean in itself; but finding them uses NVIDIA's CC BY-NC-SA metadata as the index. Whether that taints anything is a legal call for the owner. It is unnecessary: Open Images gives the same kind of faces with a clean index (Q2).

### Takeaway
**Open Images V7 faces are the clean, sufficient source**: photos listed CC BY 2.0, boxes CC BY 4.0, already handled by Yellowmoon's crop and bokeh pipelines. My count on the validation split extrapolates to roughly 30,000–35,000 training-split faces of at least 450 px (unverified extrapolation), enough for 20k–100k crops. ### Inferences
- **Scale (extrapolation, unverified):** the validation split has 2,686 usable face images in 41,620 (6.5 %). If the training split's face-box density is similar (its annotation protocol differs, so it may not be), 1.74 M images give about 110,000 face images, about 72,000 with a face >= 0.2 of a side, about 29,000 with a face >= 450 px already on the 1024-px mirror, and about 35,000 with >= 450 px from Flickr originals (1,730 x 0.78 x 0.62 x 41.9). Enough for 20k–100k crops, since every epoch draws new crops and new synthetic blemishes.
- **Prefer the 1024-px mirror first** (reliable S3 download, the same code as `training/crop/openimages.py`), add Flickr originals for faces that reach 450 px only at full size, and always *downscale* to the working face size (the spec never upscales). Downscaling a 700 px JPEG face to 450 px also shrinks its JPEG blocks and sharpening halos below the pixel scale.
- **Training faces should span the sizes the app will see**: `docs/27` §6.1 downsizes only when the face is larger than 450 px, so faces of 120–450 px reach the network in group photos; sample face sizes accordingly.
- **Prior retouching in sources is a real risk**: Flickr portraits are often already smoothed; a "clean" source that is plastic teaches the network a plastic target. Filter sources by a texture measure (band-pass energy on the cheek region relative to the face size) before use.
- **Skin tone**: compute ITA on each training face's cheek patch, report the histogram, and reweight sampling to flatten it; Open Images' known Western skew makes this necessary, not optional.
- Commons portraits (CC BY-SA mostly) and Play Raw raws (CC BY-SA 4.0) are better kept for *evaluation*, so the weights stay Apache-2.0.

### Gaps
- The training split's face-box count was not measured (the training box CSV was not downloaded); the extrapolation above is the only number.
- The face-size estimate uses the box fraction of its own axis times the long side, an overestimate for portrait-orientation photos.
- No count of Wikimedia Commons or Openverse portrait photos by licence was made.
- Whether Modal charges for volume storage (the 19 GB on the volume) is still unchecked (as `docs/STATUS.md` question 23 notes).

## Q3. Synthetic blemishes on clean skin, pseudo-labels and self-supervised alternatives

### Takeaway
Synthetic pairs are the route: take real licence-clean faces, **add procedurally generated blemishes in linear light** (a melanin/haemoglobin absorption model with Gaussian-sum shapes, after CGFR), train the network to remove exactly what was added, and **mask the loss where the source already has candidate real blemishes**, so the network neither learns to keep nor to invent their removal. Diffusion-made pairs (HGFR) and diffusion teachers cost too much for $30 and are not pixel-aligned; blind-spot self-supervision does not fit structured blemishes. Generalisation from synthetic to real blemishes is plausible but unmeasured in the literature for retouching.

### Cited Findings
- **Physics-based blemish model (CGFR, arXiv 2406.13227, 2024)**: Beer–Lambert absorption by two chromophores, melanin and haemoglobin, plus a residual term, across R, G, B; the image is taken to linear RGB, then log space, and the extinction matrix is estimated with FastICA; a blemish is a sum of N 2-D Gaussians per chromophore (amplitude, centre, rotation θ, scales σx, σy), fitted against a linear model of the surrounding skin; a gain α per chromophore edits it, α = −1 removes it. No neural network; data from Visia CR4 and OLE clinical systems, self-collected; no code release stated. FID at fading 100 % / 80 % / 60 % / 40 % / 20 %: CGFR 115.30 / 118.12 / 122.64 / 127.09 / 131.60, Photoshop inpainting 117.98 / 120.37 / 125.15 / 129.26 / 129.96, Stable Diffusion inpainting 144.89 / 133.53 / 134.16 / 160.10 / 159.54 — [arXiv 2406.13227](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13227v1).
- **Diffusion-made pairs (HGFR)**: LoRA-injected moles, freckles and acne in segmented regions at three intensities; 45–60 s per pair on an RTX 4090 — [Xu et al. supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/supplemental/Xu_Face_Retouching_with_ICCV_2025_supplemental.pdf).
- **Lesion sizes**: the 1990 AAD consensus defines papules as inflammatory lesions < 5 mm in diameter and nodules as >= 5 mm; comedones have no size cutoff in the sources found — [AAD 1990 consensus summary](https://www.medicalalgorithms.com/aad-1990-consensus-on-acne-classification).
- **Synthetic-only acne learning transfers in classification**: StyleGAN2-generated acneic faces trained a ResNet18 that scored above 80 % accuracy on real acne images not in training — [PLOS ONE 2024](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0297958).
- **Unpaired real blemish images as a regulariser**: BPFRe uses unpaired blemish images so that the discriminator focuses on blemish regions and pushes the generator to remove them — [BPFRe paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Xie_Blemish-Aware_and_Progressive_Face_Retouching_With_Limited_Paired_Data_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf).
- **Locality and scale augmentation**: AutoRetouch treats retouching as "mostly a local operation", trains on w x w windows (w = 150 px) with mirror augmentation and colour perturbation, and draws the downscale as s^(1/3) with s ~ U(s_min, 1) so that windows of all scales are equally likely — [AutoRetouch paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf).
- **Blind-spot self-supervision**: Noise2Void "does not require noisy image pairs, nor clean target images", and "cannot be expected to outperform methods that have more information available during training" — [arXiv 1811.10980](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10980).
- The classical proposer (morphological closing plus edge-aware smoothing at blemish scale) is the round-2 stand-in that implements the same trait — [docs/27-skin-retouch.md](../../../docs/27-skin-retouch.md) §1.

### Inferences
- **Generator design (my proposal, not from a paper).** Work on the face crop in linear light before the `sqrt` encoding, so that the training input matches `docs/27` §6.1 exactly. Per lesion, in optical density (−log of linear RGB) space, add `a_mel · e_mel + a_hb · e_hb` times a Gaussian-sum footprint, with fixed melanin and haemoglobin directions (estimated once from many cheek patches by ICA, as CGFR does, or taken from published absorption spectra). Types and parameters, with sizes tied to the face size `D` (if 450 px spans roughly 15–20 cm of face, 1 mm is about 2.2–3 px; an assumption, not a measurement):
  - inflammatory papule: haemoglobin up, 1–5 mm (about 2–14 px), soft edge, a slight elevation shading (a luminance dipole oriented to the face's dominant light);
  - pustule: the papule plus a small desaturated light core;
  - post-inflammatory marks: melanin up (brown) or haemoglobin up (red-purple), flat, 2–8 mm;
  - redness patches: haemoglobin, low frequency, 0.05–0.15 D, low amplitude;
  - open comedones: dark dots of 1–3 px;
  - small atrophic scars: a shallow shading ring, no chromophore change;
  - stray hairs: 1–2 px wide dark curves (Bézier strokes) of 0.05–0.2 D with alpha falloff and a melanin tint;
  - **distractors that are kept** (added to input and target alike): moles and freckles (round, sharp, melanin), so that the network learns that "brown, round and sharp" is not automatically a blemish.
  Counts per face from 0 (a share of clean samples, so the network learns identity) to severe; amplitudes scaled by the local skin's own density so that dark skin gets proportionate lesions, not grey stains.
- **The "clean source is not clean" problem.** Real faces already contain blemishes; a target equal to the source teaches the network to keep them. Two mitigations: (a) **loss masks**: run the classical proposer on the source and give zero loss weight to its detected candidate pixels (they are neither "keep" nor "remove" examples); (b) optionally a teacher target there: the classical proposer's own healed pixels, at low weight. (a) is safer; (b) only copies the classical proposer's errors.
- **Poisson-blended real patches**: blending real blemish patches cut from licence-clean faces (Open Images; SCIN if allowed) transfers real appearance; harvesting needs a detector and a review pass. Worth a second stage, not the first.
- **Diffusion editors as teachers** (Qwen-Image-Edit, Apache-2.0, was considered for crop outpainting at roughly 10–20 s per image on a GPU — [research/crop-models-2026-10-23.md](../../../research/crop-models-2026-10-23.md)): they resample the whole face, so targets are not pixel-aligned with the input, and the gain map would pick up global changes; at 10–20 s per image even 2,000 pairs take 6–11 GPU-hours on a large GPU (out of budget at H100 rates). Not recommended.
- **Blind-spot / Noise2Void** assumes noise independent per pixel (from the paper's method, not its abstract); blemishes are spatially correlated structures, so a blind-spot net would keep them. Not applicable. A masked-inpainting pretext (predict skin under random discs) is the same idea as the synthetic-blemish objective with a cruder corruption; it may help as warm-up only.
- **Expected transfer**: synthetic papules, PIH spots, redness and hairs on smooth skin should transfer well because their appearance is simple absorption; severe acne, textured scars and clustered lesions transfer worst (both AutoRetouch and BPFRe report severe blemishes as failure cases, Q4).

### Gaps
- No paper was found that measures synthetic-to-real transfer for retouching (only for acne classification).
- Real chromophore amplitudes and size distributions of facial lesions in pixels relative to face size were not found; the ranges above are design assumptions to be checked by eye on real faces.
- CGFR's code and parameter values are not published.

## Q4. Published methods 2020–2026, reported metrics, and what fits a ~0.4 M-parameter CNN

### Takeaway
The literature reports near-identical PSNR for every method on FFHQR (no-edit baselines already score 44–46 dB), so **perceptual user studies, not PSNR, separate methods**. The reference architecture is close in spirit to **AutoRetouch** (full resolution, little or no downscaling, residual, local window), which beat its own professional ground truth in a user study on studio data. Ideas that fit a 0.4 M CNN: residual output (already), training on local windows with cube-root scale sampling, an MSE/L1 core with an adversarial term trained from scratch, an auxiliary blemish-mask head used only in training, and unpaired real faces as a regulariser.

### Cited Findings
- **AutoRetouch** (WACV 2021): downscaling "to a factor smaller than half leads to slight pixel displacement and loss of the original texture", so the network limits downscaling; additive skip connections reduce jitter; loss `α·RAGAN + β·perceptual + γ·MSE` with α = 1e-3, β = 6e-3 (VGG-19 layer 14), γ = 0.5; Adam 2e-4, batch 75, w = 150 px, 500 epochs; five days on FFHQR and two weeks on studio data on three RTX 2080 Ti and one GTX 1080 Ti; "neither PSNR nor SSIM are reliable performance indicators"; FFHQR test: no edit 45.58 dB / 0.9938, MSE 45.88 / 0.9949, MSE+Perc.+RAGAN 44.92 / 0.9952, Pix2Pix 33.45 / 0.9585; "While MSE yields impressive results ... it is easy to see the output ... is smoothed, and sometimes fine textures (e.g. freckles) are removed"; user study on studio data: the full model chosen over the ground truth 76.3 % vs 18.7 %; failures (< 2 %) on severe blemishes; "less than two seconds" per image — [AutoRetouch paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf).
- **BPFRe** (CVPR 2023): two stages (encoder–decoder coarse removal, then a generator) with blemish-aware attention; Adam 0.002, 120k iterations, batch 2, one V100. FFHQR (All / Hard): raw images 43.89 / 37.69 dB; Pix2PixHD 29.38 / 30.10; GPEN 43.12 / 37.88; AutoRetouch 44.18 / 38.01 (SSIM 0.9910, LPIPS 0.0133); MPRNet 44.35 / 38.67; BPFRe 45.29 / 38.98 (SSIM 0.9935, LPIPS 0.0092 / 0.0205). User study on 1,000 in-the-wild faces: BPFRe ranked first 85.2 %; "AutoRetouch over-smoothes the content", "MPRNet ... fails to remove severe acne" — [BPFRe paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Xie_Blemish-Aware_and_Progressive_Face_Retouching_With_Limited_Paired_Data_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf). (Its table prints the raw images' LPIPS on All as 0.9906, apparently a typo.)
- **RetouchFormer** (AAAI 2024): semi-supervised transformer that treats retouching as "soft inpainting"; a review reports 44.44 dB at γ = 0.01 of paired data (0.71 dB over BPFRe) and LPIPS 0.0093 against BPFRe's 0.0106 at γ = 0.1 — [AAAI page](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/28404), [third-party review](https://liner.com/review/retouchformer-semisupervised-highquality-face-retouching-transformer-with-priorbased-selective-selfattention) (paper PDF not readable here).
- **Xu et al., ICCV 2025** (frequency selection and restoration, multi-resolution Laplacian fusion, soft mask branch): FFHQR PSNR / SSIM / LPIPS: ABPN 44.26 / 0.992 / 0.0176, BPFRe 45.13 / 0.993 / 0.0092, RetouchFormer 45.24 / 0.993 / 0.0090, theirs 45.48 / 0.993 / 0.0082; trained on 4 RTX 4090 — [paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Xu_Face_Retouching_with_Diffusion_Data_Generation_and_Spectral_Restorement_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf). **Conflict:** their AutoRetouch row (29.38 / 0.918 / 0.0766) equals BPFRe's Pix2PixHD row (29.38 / 0.9181 / 0.0766), while BPFRe gives AutoRetouch 44.18 dB; one of the two tables appears to have swapped rows.
- **StyleRetoucher** (arXiv 2312.14389): StyleGAN priors blended with the input's features, with "blemish-aware feature selection" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14389). **MoFRR** (ICCV 2025) is retouching *restoration* (undoing retouching) — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/iccv/2025/liu2025iccv-mofrr/). A 2026 Neurocomputing paper proposes a diffusion-based blemish prior — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925231226021119) (abstract only, via search).
- **Retouch4me Heal** (commercial): the vendor says it "automatically detects problem areas of the skin and repairs them while preserving the original skin texture", targets blemishes, pimples, post-acne marks and enlarged pores, works on skin only, and was trained on professionally retouched portraits (vendor history page, via search); no independent quantitative test found — [Retouch4me blog](https://blog.retouch4.me/blog/new-plugin-retouch4me-heal-was-just-released), [Retouch4me history](https://blog.retouch4.me/blog/how-retouch4mes-first-ai-plugin-was-born), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retouch4me), [AI retouching guide](https://retouch4.me/api_uploads/ai-retouching-guide.pdf).

### Inferences
- **What to borrow for 0.4 M parameters**: (1) the residual form and no downscaling (already in the spec; AutoRetouch's evidence that downscaling loses texture supports it); (2) local windows of 256–320 px input with s^(1/3) scale sampling; (3) a loss of L1 on the `sqrt`-encoded image, plus an L1 on a band-pass (Laplacian-pyramid) of the image outside lesion masks (texture keeping), plus a "quiet skin" term that penalises any change where no lesion was added; (4) an optional auxiliary head (a 1x1 conv on the last hidden layer predicting the synthetic lesion mask) used only in training, a cheap stand-in for BPFRe's blemish-aware attention; (5) a second stage with a small discriminator trained from scratch (RAGAN, AutoRetouch's α) on real Open Images faces, as BPFRe uses unpaired data.
- **Avoid an ImageNet VGG perceptual loss**: it imports ImageNet-trained features into the weights' training. `docs/14` excludes ImageNet backbones from shipped weights, and whether a loss network counts is an owner question; the band-pass L1 and the from-scratch discriminator replace it.
- **The downstream threshold changes the objective**: `docs/27` §6.2 applies corrections where `alpha` (max |log2 g|, max-filtered and blurred) exceeds `1 − a`; a network that makes tiny changes everywhere lights up all skin at high amounts. So "quiet skin" matters more than PSNR; measure the distribution of `alpha` on clean skin as a gate.
- **Expected quality (judgement)**: a 0.4 M network trained on synthetic lesions should handle isolated red papules, brown/red marks, redness patches and stray hairs on cheeks and forehead well, and be weaker than FFHQR-trained models on severe or clustered acne and textured scars; it should beat the classical proposer on texture near lesions. Whether it beats it overall is what the owner's blind A/B decides.

### Gaps
- The ABPN face results and RetouchFormer's full tables could not be read at the source.
- No 2025–2026 method was found that trains face blemish removal only on synthetic lesions on real faces and reports real-photo results; HGFR mixes synthetic faces with real ones.
- No parameter counts were extracted for AutoRetouch, BPFRe or RetouchFormer.

## Q5. Cost: parameters, FLOPs, Modal training time and price, data preparation, CPU inference

### Takeaway
The network has **379,214 parameters** but costs **377,856 multiply-adds per output pixel**, about **87–100 GMAC (175–200 GFLOP) per 450 px face** with a sensible tile size (up to 344 GMAC with a bad one). Training is cheap: about **1–3 L4-hours per million 256 px samples, $1–3 at Modal's $0.80/h** (estimate). With data preparation, a pilot, ablations and a main run, the programme is **about $10–12 expected, $20 worst case, inside $30 with a $10 margin**. **CPU inference is the real cost**: about 2–2.5 s per face on 4 threads with a packed-GEMM kernel at the rate the depth model is estimated at, and tens of seconds with the simpler convolution loop the small models use; Apple silicon is untimed.

## Q6. Risks and how to evaluate

### Takeaway
The main risks are **over-smoothing / texture loss**, **removing beauty marks and freckles**, **skin-tone bias** from Western-skewed sources and lesion models tuned on light skin, and **noise everywhere** that the threshold of §6.2 would amplify at high amounts. Evaluate with held-out synthetic pairs (inside versus outside lesion masks, per skin-tone bin), FFHQR's test pairs for literature comparability (local evaluation only), the classical proposer as the baseline, and the owner's blind A/B as the gate.

### Cited Findings
- MSE-trained retouching "sometimes fine textures (e.g. freckles) are removed"; adding perceptual and RAGAN terms raised user preference — [AutoRetouch paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf).
- Distinguishing blemishes from moles is "the main difficulty" BPFRe targets — [CVPR 2023 page](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Xie_Blemish-Aware_and_Progressive_Face_Retouching_With_Limited_Paired_Data_CVPR_2023_paper.html); professional retouching "must retain moles, freckles" — [AutoRetouch paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf).
- PSNR and SSIM barely separate methods (no-edit baselines 45.58 dB and 43.89 dB on FFHQR in two papers' protocols) — [AutoRetouch](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Shafaei_AutoRetouch_Automatic_Professional_Face_Retouching_WACV_2021_paper.pdf), [BPFRe](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Xie_Blemish-Aware_and_Progressive_Face_Retouching_With_Limited_Paired_Data_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf).
- Representation bias in large public image sets — [Shankar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08536). SCIN carries Fitzpatrick and Monk skin-tone labels — [scin](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/scin). Fitzpatrick17k is CC BY-NC-SA (local evaluation only) — [fitzpatrick17k](https://github.com/mattgroh/fitzpatrick17k).
- The library already limits damage: gains clamped to 0.5..2.5, a chroma guard, a strength threshold, beauty-mark holes and a painted protection mask — [docs/27-skin-retouch.md](../../../docs/27-skin-retouch.md) §5.3, §6. The owner's A/B kit exists for model gates (`tools/eval/ab`) — [research/crop-models-2026-10-23.md](../../../research/crop-models-2026-10-23.md).
- Research-only data may be used for local evaluation, never committed or trained on (the owner, 2026-10-24) — [docs/STATUS.md](../../../docs/STATUS.md).

### Inferences
- **Gates I would set before shipping** (thresholds are proposals for the owner):
  1. Held-out synthetic set (faces never trained on, by a hash of the photo id): lesion removal inside masks (L1 and ΔE in linear light, against the clean source), and **outside masks** the share of skin pixels with `alpha` above 0.05 (the "quiet skin" number), and band-pass energy kept (texture ratio within 5 % of the input, echoing the texture test of `docs/27` §8).
  2. The same numbers per ITA bin (at least four bins from light to dark); no bin worse than the best by more than an agreed factor.
  3. Distractor moles and freckles: share left unchanged (gain within ±2 % in linear light).
  4. FFHQR test pairs (local evaluation only): PSNR and LPIPS against the professional targets next to the literature's numbers, read with the caveat that our model was never shown FFHQR's style; LPIPS uses an ImageNet network, acceptable for evaluation only.
  5. Real Sony faces from public CC0 / CC BY-SA raws and Play Raw portraits (evaluation only): contact sheets of the gain map and `alpha`.
  6. **Owner blind A/B** against the classical proposer at the same Blemish amount, on faces neither was tuned on; the learned proposer ships only if it wins.
- **Beauty-mark risk** is reduced by the kept-distractor class in training, but the BeautyMarkDetector has no clean model (`docs/27` §1); until one exists, the protection brush remains the user's control.
- **Identity**: the gain-map design cannot move features (only per-pixel gains), so geometry is safe; the identity risk is reduced to colour and texture, covered by gates 1–3.

### Gaps
- No public, licence-clean face set with balanced Fitzpatrick types was found for evaluation; SCIN's face content is unverified.
- No agreed numeric thresholds exist yet; they are the owner's to set.

## Q7. Recommended plan with costs

### Takeaway
**Train our own proposer on synthetic lesions added to Open Images faces (CC BY 2.0 photos, CC BY 4.0 boxes), keep the weights Apache-2.0, and gate it against the classical proposer in the owner's blind A/B.** Expected spend about $10–12 of the $30, worst case about $20. No published model or paired dataset can be shipped or trained on; FFHQR is used only to measure locally.

### Cited Findings
- No licence-clean training data is known for a blemish proposer and training on Modal needs the owner's yes; a classical proposer implements the same trait first — [docs/27-skin-retouch.md](../../../docs/27-skin-retouch.md) §1, §11.
- The house pattern for small models: our own training on Open Images, Modal L4 started from GitHub Actions, a ledger with a worst-case refusal and a 1.25 margin, NaN guards, an EMA of the weights, a time-based cosine schedule, export to safetensors with a parity test against PyTorch in `ym-ml`, a model card, credits in `CREDITS.tsv.gz` and `NOTICE` — [training/bokeh/README.md](../../../training/bokeh/README.md), [training/bokeh/budget.py](../../../training/bokeh/budget.py), [data/models/bokeh/MODEL_CARD.md](../../../data/models/bokeh/MODEL_CARD.md), [docs/14-licensing.md](../../../docs/14-licensing.md).
- Modal L4 at $0.80/h and CPU at $0.047 per core-hour (computed from per-second rates) — [modal.com/pricing](https://modal.com/pricing).

### Inferences
**Plan (each step's cost from the Q5 table):**
1. **Stage 0 (no Modal cost).** Build the classical proposer of `docs/27` round 2; it is the baseline, the source of loss masks, and the fallback when the weights are missing.
2. **Data (about $1–2).** Select Open Images "Human face" boxes (not group-of, not depiction); fetch the 1024-px mirror images (and Flickr originals for faces that only reach 450 px at full size), crop faces with margin, downscale so the face is 120–450 px, drop faces whose cheek texture is too smooth (already retouched) or too small, compute ITA per face, write shards and the per-photo credit file. Target 30,000–40,000 faces; hold out 5 % by photo-id hash.
3. **Generator (no Modal cost to write; runs on the GPU in each batch).** The linear-light chromophore lesion model of Q3 with kept distractors, sensor noise from the repository's Sony noise priors, small white-balance and exposure jitter, then the exact `docs/27` §6.1 encoding (`sqrt`, mean (R+2G+B)/4 = 0.5).
4. **Training (pilot $1, ablations $2.5, main $3–4).** The reference architecture unchanged; 256–320 px input tiles; loss = L1 on encoded pixels + band-pass L1 outside lesions + "quiet skin" penalty + optional auxiliary mask head; loss weight zero on the classical proposer's candidate pixels in the source; AdamW, bf16, EMA, time-based cosine, NaN guards, ledger. Optional stage 2 ($1.5): a from-scratch RAGAN discriminator on real faces at a small weight, only if the gates show over-smoothing.
5. **Export and Rust (no Modal cost).** safetensors f32 with the architecture in the header; `ym-ml` runtime with a fixed-order kernel and a parity test; choose `output_size_px` to cover 450 px with little waste; time a 450 px face on the reference Xeon and list the Mac timing under M0-H4.
6. **Gates ($0.5).** The six of Q6; ship to `data/models/blemish/` with a model card only if the owner's A/B prefers it to the classical proposer.

**Owner decisions this needs**: (a) the Modal cap for this model (suggest $15 for the programme, $20 worst case, inside the $30); (b) FFHQR (and Fitzpatrick17k) for local evaluation only; (c) no ImageNet perceptual loss (suggested), or allow it as a training-only loss; (d) whether SCIN patches may be used later (it would need attribution in the model card and `NOTICE`); (e) whether to accept a narrower network (48 channels) if the Mac timing of the 64-channel one is too slow.

**Honest expectation**: good removal of small red and brown spots, redness patches and stray hairs on smooth skin with texture kept; weaker than FFHQR-trained research models on severe acne and scars; the clamp, chroma guard and threshold of `docs/27` keep its failures mild. A trained proposer is about 0.4 M parameters (1.5 MB of f32 weights) but costs 2–10 s of CPU per large face until timed and optimised; that, not the training money, is the risk to watch.

### Gaps
- None of the costs or speeds above is measured for this architecture; the GPU smoke and a Rust timing on the reference Xeon would turn them into numbers for under $0.50.
- Whether synthetic-only training beats the classical proposer on real Sony faces is unknown until the A/B.
