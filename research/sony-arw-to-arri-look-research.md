# One Unlicensed ALEXA Curve Gates Everything

Nearly every part of a Sony-ARW-to-ALEXA raw processor can be built from public, permissively licensed material. The two exceptions are the two things that define "ALEXA": the sensor's spectral sensitivities and ARRI's display rendering. The only public ALEXA spectral curve is HdM Stuttgart's 2018 ALEXA SXT measurement. It carries **no license**, and the HdM page still shows a live notice that the curve is wrong because of "a wrong linearization". ARRI's display transforms and its Look Library, "Film" group included, ship only as 3D LUTs under an all-rights-reserved notice. The material between those two endpoints is in good shape. ARRI publishes LogC3, LogC4, AWG3 and AWG4 as closed-form math, and its own colour-temperature-dependent ALEXA matrices for **17 CCTs** sit in the Academy's aces-dev repository under a permissive license. The camera-matching literature agrees on a design: white-balance first, then an illuminant-conditioned, structurally exposure-invariant map whose few coefficients a network of about 1.5 KB can predict. Camera metamerism, not model capacity, sets the floor on its error. On the raw side, "Capture One–level" now means a learned joint demosaic-denoise network, since Adobe, DxO and (from May 2026) Capture One all ship learned denoising. Such networks can be small: a 0.19 M-parameter model comes within about 1–1.6 dB of one 25 times larger, so a 0.2–1 M-parameter network is a realistic target, provided it is trained with calibrated Sony noise and, for the lossy-only A7R, the cRAW quantization map. A permissive Rust stack covers the rest (wgpu 30, burn 0.22 or ONNX Runtime, egui), with the raw decoder as the only LGPL seam. The development plan should therefore start with two gating tasks: get written permission from HdM, and test the ALEXA curve numerically against ARRI's published matrices. ARRI's LUTs should be loaded from a path the user supplies and never bundled.

## The only public ALEXA curve is unlicensed and flagged as wrong

HdM's Open Film Tools database is the one place where an ALEXA and the MVP's Sony body were measured by the same lab with the same method. The Sony A7r was measured through a Sony/Zeiss SEL-55F18Z, and the ALEXA SXT through a Zeiss UltraPrime 40 mm/1.9 with "Nuke/Arri-SDK demosaicing". Both come from a single-shot diffraction-grating spectroscope mounted **in front of the lens**, so each curve is a camera-plus-lens system response, not a bare-sensor curve ([HdM camera responsivities](https://www.hdm-stuttgart.de/open-film-tools/en/camera_responsivities); [OFT camera characterization guide](https://www.hdm-stuttgart.de/open-film-tools/en/publications/OFT-CameraCharacterization.pdf)). Karge et al.'s CIC 26 paper (2018) describes the method, including a correction step that minimises chromaticity error against a chart shot under known illumination and cuts that error by about an order of magnitude ([IS&T CIC 26](https://library.imaging.org/cic/articles/26/1/art00012)). A third party who processed the data records the sampling as **1 nm over 401–770 nm for the A7R and 401–784 nm for the ALEXA** ([ssf-data A7R README](https://github.com/butcherg/ssf-data/blob/master/Sony/A7R/openfilmtools/README.md); [ssf-data ALEXA README](https://github.com/butcherg/ssf-data/blob/master/Arri/Alexa_nuke-arriraw-sdk/openfilmtools/README.md)).

Two problems sit on top of this asset. First, the page still reads, verbatim, "The ARRI ALEXA responsivity is incorrent. We did a wrong linearization with ARRI RAW Converter… the correct response will be available next days", and no date of a fix appears anywhere ([HdM camera responsivities](https://www.hdm-stuttgart.de/open-film-tools/en/camera_responsivities); [legacy page](https://hdm-stuttgart.de/open-film-tools/english/camera_responsivities/)). The "Nuke/Arri-SDK demosaicing" label suggests the published package is the reprocessed one, and Butcher's ICC profiles built from it are internally plausible, with a worst ColorChecker patch of ΔE 3.22 for the ALEXA and 3.71 for the A7R ([ssf-data](https://github.com/butcherg/ssf-data)). Even so, the warning was never withdrawn, and the research could not open `camera_responses.zip` to check file dates or format. Second, **no license, Creative Commons statement or terms of use** appears on any HdM OFT page; the only rights text is "© Hochschule der Medien 2023". Butcher declined to redistribute the curves because the "license could not be determined" ([ssf-data ALEXA README](https://github.com/butcherg/ssf-data/blob/master/Arri/Alexa_nuke-arriraw-sdk/openfilmtools/README.md)). Under default copyright the data is therefore all rights reserved. Using it offline only to generate training data, and shipping only trained weights, is lower risk, but it is not clearly permitted.

No substitute exists. The Academy's rawtoaces-data holds one ARRI curve, the CCD-era **ARRI D21**, not an ALEV sensor ([rawtoaces-data cameras](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/rawtoaces-data/tree/main/data/camera)). Weta's physlight set lists no ARRI camera ([Zenodo 6590768](https://zenodo.org/records/6590768)). Solomatov and Akkaynak could not predict an ALEXA curve because their method relies on Adobe DNG matrices, and Adobe does not support ARRIRAW ([Spectral-sensitivity-estimation](https://github.com/COLOR-Lab-Eilat/Spectral-sensitivity-estimation)). ARRI co-authored research used ALEXA sensitivities modelled from five cameras, but the curves were not published ([Leonhardt & Brendel, CIC 2015](https://community.acescentral.com/uploads/short-url/zQoSiPuY57okAWV5vYUlHWHKs7N.pdf)). The ALEXA 35 uses ALEV 4, a different sensor that ARRI's chief colour scientist says "wasn't developed by ARRI" ([YMCinema](https://ymcinema.com/2022/07/18/arri-chief-color-scientist-alev-4-wasnt-developed-by-arri)). The SXT curve can therefore stand for the ALEV III generation (Classic through Mini LF) and nothing newer.

The Sony side is better covered but still lacks the exact bodies. Weta's "lightsaber" measurements, now in rawtoaces-data under **Apache-2.0**, cover 11 Sony bodies (RX100M4, ILCE-6400, 7CM2, 7M3, 7M4, 7RM2, 7RM3, 7RM4, 7SM2, 7SM3 and ILCE-9) at 380–780 nm in 5 nm steps, normalised to the global peak so the white-balance ratios survive ([rawtoaces-data](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/rawtoaces-data)). **No measured curve exists for the original ILCE-7R other than HdM's, and none at all for the ILCE-1.** The research compared the Weta curves directly: recent full-frame Sony bodies differ from one another by a mean absolute 0.005–0.015 of peak, while Solomatov's model-predicted curves miss the measured ones by 0.036–0.040, three to six times the spread between bodies. A measured sibling is therefore a better A1 proxy than the predicted ILCE-1 curve, which in any case carries no license ([Zenodo 8314702](https://zenodo.org/records/8314702)). Adobe's DNG matrices put the ILCE-1 closest to the **ILCE-7RM4** (distance 0.098) and the 7RM5, and put the A7R far from the Nikon D800 (0.378), weak evidence that the two bodies do not share a filter stack even if they share silicon ([Spectral-sensitivity-estimation](https://github.com/COLOR-Lab-Eilat/Spectral-sensitivity-estimation)).

This picture explains the MVP camera choice and also its cost. The HdM A7r-to-ALEXA pair is the only pair consistent in method, because one lab used one spectroscope and one chart correction for both cameras. Combining a Weta-measured Sony curve with the HdM ALEXA curve would add an inter-lab bias. That argues for computing the Sony-to-ALEXA mapping from HdM pairs and using Weta curves only to model how Sony bodies differ from one another. The datasets also need harmonising before any pairing, because they disagree on grid, normalisation and optical path:

| Source | Grid | Normalisation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| HdM | 1 nm, 401–770/784 nm | Not stated | Lens included in the measurement |
| Weta (rawtoaces-data) | 5 nm, 380–780 nm | Global peak | Method details undocumented |
| Jiang camspec | 10 nm, 400–720 nm | Per channel | Destroys white-balance ratios; never use for camera-to-camera mapping |
| Solomatov (predicted) | 10 nm, 400–700 nm | Not stated | Aliases against spiky fluorescent and HMI spectra unless smoothly upsampled |

Sources: [Zenodo 3245883](https://zenodo.org/records/3245883); [Spectral-sensitivity-estimation](https://github.com/COLOR-Lab-Eilat/Spectral-sensitivity-estimation).

Training spectra and test files are less constrained. The reflectance sets that can ship cleanly are the CIE 224:2017 99 colour-evaluation samples under CC BY-SA 4.0 ([CIE metadata](https://files.cie.co.at/Publications-datasets/CIE_srf_cfi_1nm.csv_metadata_v2.json)), NIST's 100 skin spectra under the NIST open license ([data.gov](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/reference-data-set-of-human-skin-reflectance)) and rawtoaces' 190-patch set under Apache-2.0 ([rawtoaces-data training](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/rawtoaces-data/tree/main/data/training)). Synthetic spectra can extend them, while Munsell/UEF, CAVE, KAIST, Harvard and ICVL are unlicensed or non-commercial. CIE illuminants (CC BY-SA 4.0) and colour-science's bundled lamp sets cover daylight, Planckian, fluorescent and LED sources ([CIE D75 metadata](https://files.cie.co.at/CIE_illum_D75.csv_metadata_v2.json); [colour-science](https://github.com/colour-science/colour)). Real cine fixtures (ARRI tungsten and HMI, Kino Flo, ARRI LED) exist only in HdM's equally unlicensed cine-light database ([HdM cine light spectra](https://www.hdm-stuttgart.de/open-film-tools/en/cinelight_spectra)). For test files, raw.pixls.us licenses new uploads CC0, though some legacy files are CC BY-NC-SA ([PIXLS.US](https://pixls.us/blog/2017/01/new-year-new-raw-samples-website)), and dnglab's corpus holds one ILCE-7R file (ISO 50, compressed) and three ILCE-1 files, one per compression mode ([dnglab rawdb](https://github.com/dnglab/dnglab/tree/main/rawler/data/testdata/rawdb/Sony)). No public CC0 A7R ColorChecker shot was found, and dnglab explicitly asks contributors not to shoot charts ([CONTRIBUTE_SAMPLES](https://github.com/dnglab/dnglab/blob/main/CONTRIBUTE_SAMPLES.md)).

## Illuminant conditioning, not network capacity, sets the colour error

Every primary source on camera-to-camera colour says the mapping depends on the illuminant. In Nguyen, Prasad and Brown's raw-to-raw study, illuminant-specific transforms beat global ones by roughly 25–40%: a per-illuminant 3×3 reaches an RMSE of 0.77 against 1.07 for one global 3×3, and a 3×4 reaches 0.63 against 0.99, with grey and white patches mapping more accurately than chromatic ones ([Nguyen et al., CVPR 2014](https://www.eecs.yorku.ca/~mbrown/pdf/cvpr2014_raw_to_raw.pdf)). Adding a third calibration illuminant to the standard two-point in-camera blend improved reproduction by up to 19% on DSLRs ([Karaimer & Brown, CVPR 2018](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/papers/Karaimer_Improving_Color_Reproduction_CVPR_2018_paper.pdf)). For light off the Planckian locus, typically LEDs, "CCT-based interpolation works poorly", and a **1.54 KB** MLP that predicts the 3×3 from the 2D white-point chromaticity lowered Sony ΔE2000 from 7.21 to 6.60 across 790 illuminants ([Tedla et al., arXiv 2511.17133](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17133v2)). ARRI treats the mapping the same way: its ALEXA raw-to-ACES transforms are 17 CCT-dependent 3×3 matrices interpolated in mired, with a separate table for the ND 1.3 filter ([aces-dev v3_IDT_maker.py](https://github.com/ampas/aces-dev/blob/v1.3.1/transforms/ctl/idt/vendorSupplied/arri/alexa/v3_IDT_maker.py)). The structure is therefore fixed: white-balance the Sony data, apply a chroma map conditioned on the illuminant, then re-apply the target camera's white, which is Nguyen's "un-white-balance" step.

The choice of function family matters less than its structural properties. Kucuk et al. fitted camera RGB to XYZ on 1,995 reflectances under D65 ([Kucuk et al. 2023](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/95300/1/jimaging_09_00214.pdf)):

| Method | Mean ΔE2000 | 95th pct. | Max ΔE2000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3×3 matrix | 0.94 | 2.62 | 7.71 |
| Plain MLP (ΔE2000 loss) | 0.85 | 2.15 | 4.18 |
| Perceptually optimised 2nd-order root-polynomial | **0.69** | **1.96** | **3.81** |

The decisive result is the paper's exposure sweep, scored in CIELAB ΔE. The root-polynomial map holds **1.10 at every exposure from ×0.2 to ×5**. A free MLP drifts from 1.40 at training exposure to 3.77 at five times that exposure; augmentation only partly repairs it (1.38 at ×5 but 2.25 at ×0.2), and a network built to be exactly invariant is stable but less accurate at 1.53. Linear, root-polynomial and hue-plane-preserving corrections are exposure-invariant by construction, while plain polynomials are not ([Colour Correction Toolbox](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/65098/4/Colour_Correction_Toolbox.pdf)). Invariance should therefore be designed into the structure, never learned. The brief's "no static LUTs" constraint is consistent with this, and "small ML model for sensor mapping" is best read as a network that predicts the coefficients of a physically constrained per-pixel operation, with no network applied per pixel.

Below every fitted map lies a floor that no RGB-to-RGB function can remove. Logvinenko, Funt and Godau show that "one colour can become many" across sensors, so no unique mapping exists ([TIP 2013](https://www2.cs.sfu.ca/~funt/LogvinenkoFuntGodauMetamerMismatchingTIP2013.pdf)). Zhang, Funt and Mirzaei found that real metamer-mismatch volumes are far smaller than theory predicts, depend more on the *type* of light than its chromaticity (fluorescent F4 and F11 are largest), and that predicting a colour as the centroid of the mismatch body gives ΔE2000 of about 0.3–1.7 ([JOSA A 2016](https://www2.cs.sfu.ca/~funt/Zhang+Funt+Mirzaei_MetamerMismatchingInPractice_JOSA2016.pdf)). For the ALEXA specifically, ARRI's Harald Brendel and co-author found that **19.2% of spectra under 3200 K blackbody, 29.1% under D56 and 20–28% under an ARRI L7-C LED** are "critical": rendered alike by the camera but seen as different by a human beyond a 10-step MacAdam ellipse. They cluster in green–blue hues, plus orange under tungsten, and the authors state that no non-linear characterisation can resolve them ([Leonhardt & Brendel](https://community.acescentral.com/uploads/short-url/zQoSiPuY57okAWV5vYUlHWHKs7N.pdf)). ARRI's own 2011 note warns that a chart match "does not guarantee" a match off-chart, "or even for the chart colours if the illumination changes" ([ARRI CDM chart note](https://www.Arri.com/resource/blob/178024/838b860c5eb4000eb3ffa1c1cd304437/usage-of-cdm-test-charts-with-the-alexa-camera-data.pdf)). The encouraging counterweight comes from the Academy's Alex Forsythe: "cameras match each other more closely than they match scene colorimetry", and 3×3 matrices performed "very respectably" in Dyer's IDT evaluation ([ACES Central](https://community.acescentral.com/t/results-from-an-idt-evaluation/2229)).

The closest prior art is Huawei's 2025 Neural Physical Model. In about 2.7K parameters it holds learnable spectral sensitivities and reflectances, simulates a chart through both cameras under an estimated illuminant SPD, and fits an illumination-specific 3×3. It beat Rawformer on NUS-8 (ΔE 1.76 vs 2.53), and on BeyondRGB its error fell as the illuminant estimate improved, from 3.37 with gray world to 3.29 with C5 and **2.94 with a spectrometer** reading ([Grönquist et al., arXiv 2506.08650](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08650v2)). That gradient is the practical lesson: once the map is physically constrained, **illuminant-estimation quality, not regression capacity, dominates the real error**.

Full spectral reconstruction (Sony RGB to spectrum, then re-render through the ALEXA curves) is attractive on paper and wrong at inference time. Learned reconstructors overfit to exposure: Lin and Finlayson measured MRAE rising from 1.76 at the training exposure to 15.68 at half exposure, and even with their physically plausible fix, re-rendering through a different camera left worst-case ΔE of about 14–15 ([Lin & Finlayson, CVPRW 2020](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPRW_2020/papers/w31/Lin_Physically_Plausible_Spectral_Reconstruction_From_RGB_Images_CVPRW_2020_paper.pdf)). Parametric upsamplers such as Jakob–Hanika (BSD-3 code) pick the smoothest metamer ([rgb2spec](https://github.com/mitsuba-renderer/rgb2spec)), which is systematically wrong for dyes, LEDs and foliage, the very cases where Sony and ALEXA disagree. Spectral upsampling belongs in the offline data generator, where it can fill gamut regions that measured sets cover poorly.

The research supports a concrete architecture, which is its own synthesis rather than a published result. The map receives white-balanced Sony linear RGB ρ, split into its sum s and chromaticity c = ρ/s, and outputs s·G(c; illuminant). G is a root-polynomial, a hue-plane-preserving piecewise matrix, or a 2D lattice fitted with a Hessian regulariser ([lattice regression](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~raman/Publications_files/Lattice.Regression.TIP.2012.pdf)), constrained so that G(white) = white, and a tiny MLP produces its coefficients from the illuminant chromaticity and an illuminant-class posterior. Negative or out-of-gamut values from saturated LEDs pass through unbounded and are compressed only at rendering, as the ACES Reference Gamut Compression does ([ACES RGC](https://docs.acescentral.com/rgc/specification/)); Brendel has observed that ALEXA's camera error pushes some LED colours outside AP0 ([ACES Central](https://community.acescentral.com/t/spectral-images-generation-and-processing/2590)). Pixels close to clipping carry corrupted chromaticity, so they blend toward the illuminant neutral and are excluded from training.

Estimating the illuminant itself has good, small, permissively licensed options. FFCC (Apache-2.0, about 0.22 MB, about 1–1.4 ms) outputs a von Mises posterior with mean and covariance, can use EXIF exposure data, and reaches a geometric-mean error of 1.07–1.20° on Gehler-Shi ([FFCC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.07596); [google/ffcc](https://github.com/google/ffcc)). C5 (Apache-2.0, about 2 MB) generalises to unseen cameras from a few other images by the same camera ([C5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.11890)), and DMCC, an MLP of about 800 parameters, comes within about 0.5° of C5 ([DMCC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.11773)). CCMNet is CC BY-NC, so its weights cannot ship ([CCMNet](https://github.com/DY112/CCMNet)). The open gap is the illuminant's *spectral class*. No peer-reviewed RGB-only classifier for daylight, tungsten, fluorescent or LED light with a reported accuracy was found; published SPD estimators either need multispectral sensors or report synthetic results only ([Koskinen et al., IJCV 2024](https://www.link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11263-023-01867-x); [Gao & Mantiuk 2025](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rkm38/pdfs/gao2025_spectal_SPD_reflectance_estimation.pdf)). The tractable formulation is a posterior over a discrete set of SPD prototypes, extending colour-by-correlation ([Tominaga & Wandell](https://acorn.stanford.edu/psych221/projects/2010/JasonSu/Papers/IlluminantEstimationIEEE.pdf)), with the final map as the posterior-weighted blend of per-prototype maps so that it degrades gracefully when the class is ambiguous. Training data is the bottleneck: no labelled A7R colour-constancy dataset exists, Cube++ (4,890 raws, **CC BY 4.0**) is the cleanest real source but needs its source camera's curve to be remapped into Sony space ([Zenodo 4153431](https://zenodo.org/records/4153431)), and NUS-8 includes a Sony SLT-A57 for sanity checks but states no license ([NUS-8](https://yorkucvil.github.io/projects/public_html/illuminant/illuminant.html)).

Evaluation without an ALEXA has to be synthetic first. Paired Sony and ALEXA RGB are rendered from reflectance × illuminant × spectral sensitivity, holding out both reflectance sets and illuminant families (for instance, training on blackbody, daylight and some LEDs, then testing on F-series, unseen LEDs and HMI) and injecting realistic illuminant-estimation error. Scores should be ΔE2000 and ΔE_ITP, the latter more reliable for HDR and wide-gamut content ([ITU-R BT.2124 summary](https://www.portrait.com/resource-center/eitp-is-now-itu-r-bt-2124-is-the-industry-ready-to-move-on-from-e2000/)), stratified by hue sector and material and compared against two baselines. The metamer-mismatch centroid gives the floor; a global 3×3 is the strongest documented competitor, since Juan Melara's commercial P6K-to-ALEXA grade is a single linear 3×3 plus hue curves ([EOSHD](https://www.eoshd.com/comments/topic/43271-p6k-to-arri-alexa-resolve-powergrade/)). No paper reports camera-to-camera ΔE for 3×3 vs root-polynomial vs MLP with held-out illuminants, so the project must produce that table itself. Extrapolating from Kucuk and Zhang/Funt, a reasonable expectation under a known illuminant is a mean ΔE2000 of about 0.5–1 and a 95th percentile of about 2, with a multi-ΔE tail that cannot be removed.

## ARRI publishes the math to AWG and keeps the rendering in LUTs

Both ARRI log encodings and both gamuts are fully specified in closed form. LogC3 is EI-dependent, with one parameter set per EI from 160 to 1600; every EI maps 18% grey to 0.391 (400/1023), the sensor clips at LogC 0.9539 at EI 800 (about 7.6 stops above grey when decoded through the curve; ARRI's own latitude figure, cited below, is 7.4), and the Hermite shoulder above EI 1600 exists only in ARRI's ACES IDT maker ([ARRI LogC3 VFX PDF](https://www.Arri.com/resource/blob/31918/66f56e6abb6e5b6553929edf9aa7483e/2017-03-alexa-logc-curve-in-vfx-data.pdf); [aces-dev v3_IDT_maker.py](https://github.com/ampas/aces-dev/blob/v1.3.1/transforms/ctl/idt/vendorSupplied/arri/alexa/v3_IDT_maker.py)). LogC4 is a single EI-independent curve that maps 0.18 to 0.2784 and reserves about **11.35 stops above grey**; ARRI designed it for 12-bit encoding at minimum, so 10-bit LogC4 is unsuitable for interchange ([ARRI LogC4 spec](https://www.arri.com/resource/blob/278790/bea879ac0d041a925bed27a096ab3ec2/2022-05-arri-logc4-specification-data.pdf)). AWG3 and AWG4 primaries are published with D65 white, along with their matrices to XYZ and to ACES AP0 (CAT02). ARRI prints no AWG3-to-AWG4 matrix, but one follows exactly from the two XYZ matrices because both gamuts share a white point. colour-science (BSD-3), OpenColorIO (BSD-3) and ACES 2's input transforms (Apache-2.0) already implement all of this ([colour arri.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/colour-science/colour/develop/colour/models/rgb/transfer_functions/arri.py); [OCIO ArriCameras.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/OpenColorIO/main/src/OpenColorIO/transforms/builtins/ArriCameras.cpp); [aces-input-and-colorspaces](https://github.com/aces-aswf/aces-input-and-colorspaces/tree/main/arri)). The ARRI PDFs carry no license, but the constants are functional facts, so a Rust implementation can be derived from the specs with the BSD or Apache implementations cited as references.

The most valuable public ARRI asset is the camera-native colour step for ALEV III. `Alexa_aces_matrix.txt`, dated 13 November 2013, holds native-to-AP0 matrices at **17 CCTs from 2000 to 11000 K**, which ARRI's script interpolates linearly in reciprocal CCT ([Alexa_aces_matrix.txt](https://github.com/ampas/aces-dev/blob/v1.3.1/transforms/ctl/idt/vendorSupplied/arri/alexa/AlexaParameters-2013-Nov-13/Alexa_aces_matrix.txt)). The matrices expect "white-balanced, black-subtracted linear values"; the per-channel white-balance gains are applied upstream and are not published ([raw IDT EI800 CCT6500](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ampas/aces-dev/v1.3.1/transforms/ctl/idt/vendorSupplied/arri/alexa/v3/EI800/IDT.ARRI.Alexa-v3-raw-EI800-CCT6500.ctl)). Removing the published AWG3-to-AP0 matrix yields native-to-AWG3 at each CCT, with rows summing to 1 as balanced input requires; this derivation is the research's own computation, not an ARRI publication. ARRI's 2011 white paper explains why the matrix changes with CCT: since SUP 3.0 the aim has been to keep images consistent across light sources "without removing the character of the light", so reds stay more pronounced under tungsten ([ALEXA Color Processing white paper](https://www.samys.com/images/pdf/ALEXA-Color-Processing-White-Paper.pdf)). The table is covered by the AMPAS license, which allows copying and derivatives provided the notice is retained ([aces-dev LICENSE](https://github.com/ampas/aces-dev/blob/v1.3.1/LICENSE.md)), and it was **not carried into the ACES 2 repository**. What stays unpublished is the REVEAL/ACE4 native-to-AWG4 matrices, the white-balance gain tables, the green–magenta CC-to-gain mapping and ARRI's ARRIRAW highlight treatment. ALEXA's tint control is known only qualitatively: it adjusts green, in integer steps reaching ±12 on newer cameras ([Foundry ID 218316](https://supportsandbox.foundry.com/hc/en-us/articles/32696796372754-ID-218316-expand-green-magenta-tint-adjustment-in-arriReader-from-8-8-to-12-12-to-conform-to-newer-ARRI-cameras); [ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/28-days-cinematography-insights-redux-day-29-manually-white-balancing-camera-light-source/)).

### The illuminant plays two separate roles

The emulator should separate two jobs that a single "white balance" control usually merges. This follows from ARRI's design and from Nguyen's un-white-balance step, but it is the research's inference. The **actual scene illuminant**, as estimated, decides how the scene's spectra land on the ALEXA sensor; that is the job of the illuminant-conditioned Sony-to-ALEXA-native map, followed by re-applying ALEXA's native white for that illuminant, computed from the ALEXA curve and the illuminant's SPD. The **CCT the user dials in** plays the role of the ALEXA's white-balance setting: it selects ALEXA white-balance gains (computable from the ALEXA curve and a Planckian or daylight SPD at that CCT) and the mired-interpolated ARRI matrix. When the two agree, the result is a neutral ALEXA rendering. When they differ, the image shows the cast an ALEXA would have shown under that mismatch, which is the behaviour a cinematographer expects from "3200 K under daylight".

### Display rendering and looks exist only as LUTs

Display rendering is where the public record stops. ARRI's current DRTs ship **only as .cube LUTs**: for LogC4 (REVEAL), files such as `ARRI_LogC4-to-Gamma24_Rec709-D65_v1_65.cube` plus HLG and PQ variants, and for LogC3, "Classic" K1S1 and a newer v1. No analytic description is published ([ARRI LUT naming guideline](https://www.arri.com/resource/blob/294602/99cf69084be7f6638181660483d3e39d/arri-lut-naming-convention-logc4-and-logc3-guideline-en-data.pdf); [ARRI REVEAL page](https://www.arri.com/en/learn/camera-systems/image-science/reveal-color-science)). For K1S1 only fragments are public: a tone curve applied to Log C that "compresses shadows and highlights", and a "tone-mapped AWG→Rec.709" matrix made deliberately less saturated to offset the curve's saturation boost ([LogC3 VFX PDF](https://www.Arri.com/resource/blob/31918/66f56e6abb6e5b6553929edf9aa7483e/2017-03-alexa-logc-curve-in-vfx-data.pdf)). Community OCIO configs confirm that the REVEAL DRT is used as a baked LUT ([ACES Central](https://community.acescentral.com/t/arri-and-red-drts-in-ocio/4641)).

ARRI's look architecture is strictly ordered: an ASC CDL in LogC4, then a log-to-log "CMT" 3D LUT, then the fixed DRT, with look intensity adjustable from 0 to 100% in 10% steps ([ALF4 guide](https://www.arri.com/resource/blob/361314/f351daead9760d6fc759f3c0e2d65a61/arri-how-to-create-an-arri-look-file-alf4-data.pdf)). The Look Library holds **87 looks in nine groups, group 3000 being "Film"**, distributed as LogC4 log-to-log LUTs, LogC3 log-to-log LUTs and LogC3-to-Rec.709 LUTs ([Look Files page](https://www.arri.com/en/learn-help/learn-help-camera-system/image-science/look-files); [Look Library app page](https://arri.com/en/learn-help/learn-help-camera-system/tools/arri-look-library-app)). ARRI says LogC3 and LogC4 looks are designed separately and "cannot be converted", and that a Rec.709-baked ALF2 look cannot be split from its rendering ([ARRI Color FAQ](https://www.arri.com/en/learn/camera-systems/image-science/color-faq)). Which version of "Film" the developer grades with therefore decides both the working encoding (LogC3 at EI 800 or LogC4) and whether the DRT can be selected separately at all. The research could not determine which look numbers sit inside group 3000.

Licensing settles how these LUTs can be used. ARRI's legal notice reserves all rights and forbids duplication "without the express consent of the owner", and no LUT package, Look Library page or spec PDF carries a grant of rights ([ARRI Legal](https://www.arri.com/en/legal)). The clean design follows: implement the published math, load the official ARRI DRT and Film-look `.cube` files from a path the user configures after downloading them from arri.com, and ship a permissive default DRT. **ACES 2.0** (Apache-2.0, April 2025) is the obvious default; it tone-maps only the J (lightness) channel of a Hellwig-2022-based JMh space, so hue carries through to the display ([ACES output transforms](https://docs.acescentral.com/system-components/output-transforms/); [aces-output LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/aces-aswf/aces-output/main/LICENSE)). OpenDRT is GPL-3.0 and AgX shows no license ([open-display-transform](https://github.com/jedypod/open-display-transform); [sobotka/AgX](https://github.com/sobotka/AgX)). Fitting a parametric approximation to ARRI's LUT and shipping only the fitted parameters is legally unsettled and needs a lawyer's opinion, not an engineering decision.

### ALEV III data can reach AWG4 by two routes

One more nuance affects what "ALEXA look" means. ARRI states that ALEV III ARRIRAW from the LF and Mini LF can be developed through REVEAL to AWG4/LogC4 ([ARRI Log C page](https://arri.com/en/learn-help/learn-help-camera-system/image-science/log-c)), and REVEAL's ACE4 uses "local optimisation" with unpublished matrices ([REVEAL page](https://www.arri.com/en/learn/camera-systems/image-science/reveal-color-science)). The public route (SUP 3.x matrices to AWG3, the computed AWG3-to-AWG4 matrix, then LogC4) produces ALEV III colour in a LogC4 container, a different thing from REVEAL-processed ALEV III. The size of that difference can be measured offline. ARRI's "Helen & John" set for the Mini LF provides the ARRIRAW frame together with ARRI-rendered AWG4 scene-linear EXR, LogC3 TIFF and Rec.709 TIFF ([ARRI sample footage](https://www.arri.com/samplefootage)), and the free ARRI Reference Tool CLI runs on Linux and renders legacy ARRIRAW to AWG3 or AWG4, linear or log, with CCT and tint overrides ([ART CMD manual](https://www.arri.com/resource/blob/402608/20ac446709713dfa4271aa47f633c439/arri-user-manual-art-cmd-v1-0-0-data.pdf)). ART's EULA ships inside the package and was not read, the sample footage carries no redistribution grant, and the ARRI Image SDK is closed and gated behind the Partner Program ([ARRI Partner Program](https://www.arri.com/en/company/the-arri-philosophy/arri-partner-program)), so all three are offline developer tools and never dependencies. On the sensor side, ALEV III's dual-gain readout gives about 14.5 stops, with **7.4 stops above mid-grey at EI 800** ([ARRI VFX FAQ](https://www.arri.com/en/learn-help/learn-help-camera-system/image-science/vfx-faq); [ARRI ALEV sensors](https://arri.com/en/learn-help/technology/alev-sensors)). A single-gain Sony clips earlier at matched mid-grey, so ALEXA-like highlight roll-off has to be a rendering decision, outside the sensor map.

## Capture One parity now requires a learned joint demosaic-denoise

The commercial bar moved in 2023–2026. Adobe has said since 2023 that its Denoise models "perform both demosaicing and denoising in a single step", trained on millions of paired patches with dark frames used to learn pattern noise ([Adobe](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2023/04/18/denoise-demystified)). DxO has run joint demosaic+denoise since 2020, folded chromatic-aberration correction into the same network in 2025, and calibrates "600+ camera bodies, ~20 ISO settings each" with dark frames ([DxO](https://www.dxo.com/news/deepprime-xd3-fourth-generation/)). Capture One 16.8 (May 2026) added AI "Enhanced Denoise" but documents nothing about where in its pipeline it runs ([Capture One 16.8](https://www.captureone.com/explore-features/whats-new/16-8)). At base ISO, a good classical demosaic plus capture sharpening gets close; beating Capture One visibly at ISO 800 and above effectively requires a learned joint network.

Classical algorithms remain necessary as a CPU fallback and for previews, but their best implementations cannot be copied. RCD is darktable's default, AMaZE retains the most detail but overshoots colour more, and LMMSE handles high ISO best ([darktable demosaic](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/5.6/en/module-reference/processing-modules/demosaic/)), yet every widely used implementation of them in RawTherapee, librtprocess and darktable is **GPL-3** ([librtprocess](https://github.com/CarVac/librtprocess); [RCD](https://github.com/LuisSR/RCD-Demosaicing)). The permissive starting points are Getreuer's Zhang–Wu LMMSE (BSD-2) ([IPOL](https://www.ipol.im/pub/art/2011/g_zwld/)), colour-demosaicing's Menon and Malvar (BSD-3) ([colour-demosaicing](https://github.com/colour-science/colour-demosaicing)) and the papers themselves.

Learned joint networks are small, fast and openly reproducible. Gharbi et al.'s 2016 network has **559,776 parameters** and beat the best classical interpolators of its era by about 3 dB on Kodak (41.2 vs 38.1 dB for Getreuer 2011 and 36.1 for AHD), and the optimisation-based FlexISP by 1.2 dB. Its PyTorch code and weights are MIT, but they were trained on gamma-encoded, white-balanced sRGB with Gaussian noise, so they serve as an architectural baseline and need retraining for linear Sony raw ([Gharbi et al.](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/1721.1/134672/2/demosaic.pdf); [demosaicnet](https://github.com/mgharbi/demosaicnet)). In NTIRE 2025's efficient track, capped at 200K parameters, Samsung's 0.19 M-parameter entry trails its own 4.97 M model by only 0.9–1.6 dB ([NTIRE 2025 RAW report](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02197v1)), and JD3Net-S at 25 GFLOPs per 256² tile matches a 408-GFLOP model ([JD3Net](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00703v1)). vkdt's `jddcnn`, an Open-Image-Denoise-style U-Net, trains "in an hour or two" and processes a **100 MP Bayer frame in 45 ms on an RTX 4080S** ([jddcnn readme](https://github.com/hanatos/vkdt/blob/master/src/pipe/modules/jddcnn/readme.md); [pixls.us](https://discuss.pixls.us/t/neural-demosaicing-denoising-for-intel-and-amd/58586)), though its weights state no license ([vkdt-nn](https://codeberg.org/hanatos/vkdt-nn)); Open Image Denoise code and weights are Apache-2.0 ([oidn-weights](https://github.com/OpenImageDenoise/oidn-weights)). At Gharbi-class cost, about 214 kFLOP per pixel by JD3Net's figures, a frame needs about **7.7 TFLOP for the 36 MP A7R and 10.6 TFLOP for the 50 MP A1**: sub-second on a modern GPU, tens of seconds on a CPU. Previews must therefore run the classical path, with the network reserved for 1:1 view and export.

Noise must be calibrated per body, ISO and CFA channel. The reference recipes are permissive: Brooks et al.'s unprocessing (Apache-2.0) synthesises raw training data from processed images ([Brooks et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11127)), and Wei et al.'s ELD (MIT) models Poisson shot noise, Tukey-lambda read noise, per-row Gaussian noise and quantization, calibrated from flat fields and lens-capped bias frames, beating plain Poisson–Gaussian by 2.2–4.4 dB on Sony low-light data ([ELD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.12751); [Vandermode/ELD](https://github.com/Vandermode/ELD)). Sony adds two quirks. The a7R II shows a read-noise discontinuity between ISO 500 and 640 attributed to dual conversion gain ([Kasson](https://blog.kasson.com/?p=13723)); the A1's equivalent point near ISO 500 rests only on forum citations ([DPReview](https://www.dpreview.com/forums/thread/4573531)), and whether the original A7R has dual gain at all is unverified. The A7R also spatially filters Bulb exposures in-camera with no way to disable it ([Lonely Speck](https://www.lonelyspeck.com/?p=87913)), so calibration frames must avoid Bulb. Calibration needs the physical body: the A1 can be characterised directly, while the ILCE-7R MVP needs access to an A7R for dark and flat frames.

The original A7R is the hardest decode case in the lineup because it writes **only lossy cRAW**. Each block of 16 same-colour pixels stores an 11-bit maximum, an 11-bit minimum, two 4-bit indices and fourteen 7-bit deltas shifted by bitlen(max − min) − 7, and a five-segment curve from tag 0x7010 then expands the values to about 14 bits. The result is 32-pixel "blondie" stripes beside high-contrast edges, worst at low ISO on the AA-filter-less A7R ([rawler arw.rs](https://github.com/dnglab/dnglab/blob/main/rawler/src/decoders/arw.rs); [Dietz EI 2016](https://aggregate.org/DIT/EI2016/ei2016ARW.pdf); [KARWY](https://aggregate.org/DIT/KARWY)). Dietz showed that each pixel's quantization interval is exactly recoverable from the block structure, but his KARWY repair uses slow texture synthesis and its source license is unknown. The design implication, an inference, is for the decoder to emit a **per-pixel quantization-step map** that feeds the joint network, and for training to pass synthetic mosaics through a reimplemented ARW2 encoder and decoder so the network learns to suppress the stripes within the known intervals. rawler dithers its curve output ([rawler arw.rs](https://github.com/dnglab/dnglab/blob/main/rawler/src/decoders/arw.rs)), which is wrong for this purpose.

The A1 is easier on paper. It offers Uncompressed, Lossless Compressed L/M/S (firmware 1.30+) and lossy Compressed ([Sony A1 Help Guide](https://helpguide.sony.net/ilc/2040/v1/en/contents/TP1000426670.html)). Lossless L is tiled LJPEG-92, while **M and S are YCbCr pseudo-raw**, "already white balanced" and not Bayer, so no demosaic network applies to them ([LibRaw Changelog](https://github.com/LibRaw/LibRaw/blob/master/Changelog.txt)). Black level, white level and white-balance multipliers live in the encrypted SR2 block (0x7300/0x7310, 0x787f, 0x7313) ([LibRaw sony.cpp](https://github.com/LibRaw/LibRaw/blob/master/src/metadata/sony.cpp)).

Sony embeds radial lens-correction splines with 16 knots in the raw SubIFD: 0x7032 for vignetting, 0x7035 for chromatic aberration and 0x7037 for distortion ([rawler tags.rs](https://github.com/dnglab/dnglab/blob/main/rawler/src/tags.rs)). darktable's GPL source documents the decoding math, which a reimplementation can follow without copying code: a distortion factor of `d·2^-14 + 1`, red and blue CA factors of `ca·2^-21 + 1`, and a vignetting gain of `2^(0.5 − 2^(v·2^-13 − 1))`, squared ([darktable lens.cc](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/blob/master/src/iop/lens.cc)). An independent reverse-engineering of Imaging Edge agrees on the scale factors ([pixls.us](https://discuss.pixls.us/t/sony-raw-chromatic-aberration-correction-model/21153)). Whether the 2013 A7R writes these tags is unverified. Lensfun's database is CC BY-SA 3.0 ([lensfun](https://github.com/lensfun/lensfun)), another reason to prefer the embedded data.

Highlight reconstruction and sharpening also have well-described algorithms behind GPL code. darktable's default "inpaint opposed" runs on the mosaic before demosaic and estimates a clipped channel from the cube-root mean of the opposing channels in a 3×3 superpixel, plus a global chroma correction ([darktable opposed.c](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/blob/master/src/iop/hlreconstruct/opposed.c); [manual](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/development/en/module-reference/processing-modules/highlight-reconstruction/)); no permissively licensed learned highlight model was found. RawTherapee's capture sharpening is a Richardson–Lucy-style Gaussian deconvolution on linear data straight after demosaic, with an automatic radius and a corner boost, for example σ 0.60 at the centre and 0.75 at the corners ([RawPedia](https://rawpedia.rawtherapee.com/Capture_Sharpening); [pixls.us](https://discuss.pixls.us/t/new-tool-capture-sharpening/14197)). Both can be reimplemented clean-room from their descriptions.

Benchmarking against Capture One works in three tiers. Synthetic sets (Kodak, McMaster, Gharbi's hard and moiré sets) catch demosaic regressions. Real raw data comes from **DND**, which includes the **Sony A7R** but is non-commercial and therefore evaluation-only ([DND](https://noise.visinf.tu-darmstadt.de/)), and from **PixelShift200**, four-shot Sony A7R III captures under CC BY 4.0 and the best-licensed, most on-target training set ([PixelShift200](https://huggingface.co/datasets/guochengqian/PixelShift200)). The last tier is blind A/B testing against Capture One, Lightroom and DxO exports, scored with no-reference measures of zipper, false colour and blur ([Gasparini et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1687-6180-2012-123)), slanted-edge MTF and pairwise viewer tests.

### One scene-linear order ties the stages together

The stages combine into a single scene-linear processing order. The table is the research's synthesis of the cited stage-level findings.

| # | Stage | Basis | Code provenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Decode ARW (ARW2 cRAW for A7R; uncompressed or tiled LJPEG-92 for A1); emit undithered values plus a quantization-step map | Format described by Dietz, rawler and LibRaw | In-house (or LGPL rawler) |
| 2 | Per-channel black subtraction, white normalisation, clip mask in pre-white-balance raw units | SR2 tags 0x7300/0x7310/0x787f | In-house |
| 3 | Illuminant estimate (chromaticity and class posterior) on a binned thumbnail; feeds stages 5, 10 and 11 | FFCC/C5-style | Clean-room from Apache-2.0 references |
| 4 | Vignetting or flat-field gain | Sony 0x7032 formula | Reimplemented formula |
| 5 | White-balance gains (as-shot 0x7313 or estimated) | Sony metadata | In-house |
| 6 | Highlight reconstruction on the mosaic (opposed channels, cube-root mean) | darktable description | Clean-room |
| 7 | Joint demosaic+denoise network with noise σ, quantization-step and clip-mask inputs | Gharbi/OIDN-family U-Net | Own weights |
| 8 | Capture sharpening (Gaussian-PSF Richardson–Lucy), or learned inside stage 7 | RawTherapee description | Clean-room |
| 9 | One geometric resample: distortion plus red/blue CA scaling | Sony 0x7037/0x7035 | Reimplemented formula |
| 10 | White-balanced Sony RGB → emulated ALEV III native, illuminant-conditioned and exposure-invariant, then re-apply ALEXA white for the scene illuminant | Nguyen, Tedla, Grönquist | Own |
| 11 | ALEXA white-balance gains and CCT matrix (mired-interpolated) at the user's CCT → AWG3; tint as an own green-gain mapping | aces-dev 2013 table | AMPAS notice |
| 12 | Exposure; optional AWG3→AWG4 | Matrix computed from ARRI primaries | From spec |
| 13 | LogC3 (EI 800) or LogC4 encode | ARRI specs | From spec |
| 14 | CDL, then look 3D LUT (log-to-log) with intensity blend | ALF4 chain | User-supplied LUT |
| 15 | DRT: ARRI LUT (user-supplied) or ACES 2 output transform | ACES 2 | Apache-2.0 port |
| 16 | Display encoding, ICC and export | moxcms | Permissive crate |

## A permissive Rust stack exists, with the decoder as the only LGPL seam

Decoding is where licensing and technical requirements meet. Only rawler covers the whole target matrix:

| Option | License | ILCE-7R cRAW | ILCE-1 lossless (LJPEG-92) | Status and notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rawler (dnglab) 0.8.0 | **LGPL-2.1** | Yes | Yes | Actively maintained, last release 2026-08-30; detects YCbCr M/S files |
| rawloader 0.37.2 | LGPL-2.1 | Yes | **No** | Maintenance-only commits in 2026 |
| rawkit (Graphite) 0.1.0 | MIT/Apache | **No camera entry** | **No**; hits `todo!()` and panics outside ARW1, ARW2 and uncompressed | ARW2 path asserts 12 bits per sample |

Sources: [crates.io rawler](https://crates.io/crates/rawler); [dnglab LICENSE](https://github.com/dnglab/dnglab/blob/main/LICENSE); [crates.io rawloader](https://crates.io/crates/rawloader); [rawkit lib.rs](https://github.com/GraphiteEditor/Graphite/blob/master/libraries/rawkit/src/lib.rs).

LGPL-2.1 §6 does allow statically linking rawler into an Apache/MIT binary, provided the binary ships with the license, the library's source and a way for users to relink; a fork of rawler that exposes quantization intervals or removes the dither would itself remain LGPL. Sony's lens-correction tags are named in rawler's tag table but used nowhere in it, and rawler's ARW decoder does not hand IFDs back to callers, although its TIFF module is public ([rawler decoders/mod.rs](https://github.com/dnglab/dnglab/blob/main/rawler/src/decoders/mod.rs)). The format scope is small (TIFF walk, SR2 decrypt, ARW2, uncompressed and LJPEG-92 tiles), and the quantization-step map needs decoder internals. Together these favour a narrow, in-house Apache-licensed ARW decoder with rawler and LibRaw kept as test oracles in CI; that is a recommendation, not an established fact.

GPU work belongs on **wgpu 30**, which is MIT/Apache and runs on Metal, Vulkan and DX12 ([wgpu](https://crates.io/crates/wgpu)). Its defaults of 8192-pixel textures and 256 MiB buffers ([wgpu-types Limits](https://docs.rs/wgpu-types/latest/wgpu_types/struct.Limits.html)) fit neither camera's full frame: an A1 frame is 8704 pixels wide ([rawler ILCE-1 analyze](https://github.com/dnglab/dnglab/tree/main/rawler/data/testdata/rawdb/Sony/ILCE-1/raw_modes)) and occupies about 816 MiB as float RGBA. The pipeline must therefore tile, with tiles of roughly 1–2k pixels, halos sized to the network's receptive field and f16 storage. wgpu 30 added HDR surface colour spaces and display-HDR queries, but two Apple wide-gamut fixes were still unreleased on 2026-10-10 ([wgpu CHANGELOG](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/trunk/CHANGELOG.md)), and RapidRAW pins wgpu 29 "to prevent P3 color shifts on Apple devices" ([RapidRAW Cargo.toml](https://github.com/CyberTimon/RapidRAW/blob/main/src-tauri/Cargo.toml)), so expect to pin a version. CubeCL 0.11 compiles Rust kernels to MSL, SPIR-V or WGSL and can share the app's wgpu device ([CubeCL](https://github.com/tracel-ai/cubecl)); rust-gpu calls itself "not yet production-ready" ([rust-gpu](https://github.com/Rust-GPU/rust-gpu)).

ML inference has two credible routes. burn 0.22 with burn-onnx is pure Rust and MIT/Apache, generates Rust code from ONNX at build time, and runs on wgpu/CubeCL on the same device as the image pipeline ([burn-onnx](https://github.com/tracel-ai/burn-onnx)), but 0.22 is a large breaking release ([burn 0.22 notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/tracel-ai/burn/release/v0.22.0)). ort 2.0-rc.13, bundling ONNX Runtime 1.28, is still a release candidate; its DirectML provider is "in sustained engineering" and its CoreML provider restricts ConvTranspose and Resize ([ort](https://crates.io/crates/ort); [ORT DirectML](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/DirectML-ExecutionProvider.html); [ORT CoreML](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/CoreML-ExecutionProvider.html)). candle lacks any wgpu or DX12 backend ([candle](https://github.com/huggingface/candle)), and wonnx has not been updated since 2023 ([wonnx](https://crates.io/crates/wonnx)). No published benchmark compares these runtimes on U-Net tiles, so the choice should follow a benchmark at the project's own tile size. Training belongs in PyTorch with colour-science (BSD-3) ([colour-science](https://pypi.org/project/colour-science/)), with models exported as both safetensors and static-shape ONNX and checked by golden-tile parity tests across PyTorch, ORT and burn.

The remaining layers have clear picks:

| Layer | Recommendation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Spectral colorimetry | `colorimetry` 0.1 (MIT/Apache) | CIE observers, illuminants, CCT/Duv and CAM16 ([colorimetry](https://github.com/harbik/colorimetry)) |
| Display ICC | moxcms (BSD-3/Apache) | [moxcms](https://crates.io/crates/moxcms) |

## Risks the research could not retire

**The largest scientific risk is that the ALEXA target is wrong**, in several ways at once. The HdM curve may still contain the linearization error. It includes an UltraPrime 40's transmission, so the emulation targets "ALEXA+UP40" as seen from "A7r+55/1.8" unless the lens is divided out, and no public transmission data exists for either lens. It also describes a single SXT unit, while Solomatov reports copy-to-copy variation of about 8% within a camera model ([arXiv 2304.11549](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.11549v2)). A strong numerical check exists, which is the research's inference: render a synthetic chart through the ALEXA curve at each of ARRI's 17 CCTs, fit a white-balanced native-to-AP0 matrix at each, and compare the results with ARRI's table. Residuals that vary strongly and non-linearly with CCT, or a grey axis that drifts with exposure, would expose a linearization fault. If the curve fails, it can be refined toward ARRI's matrices in the spirit of Solomatov's estimation of curves from colour matrices, or the developer can ask ARRI through the arriraw-dev@arri.de contact printed on the LogC4 spec ([LogC4 spec](https://www.arri.com/resource/blob/278790/bea879ac0d041a925bed27a096ab3ec2/2022-05-arri-logc4-specification-data.pdf)).

The second scientific risk is that illuminant-class estimation from RGB alone may not work, since no benchmark shows that it does. If it fails, the map falls back to chromaticity-only conditioning, which works well along the Planckian locus and worse under fluorescent light, exactly where Zhang and Funt found the largest mismatch volumes. The A1 also lacks a measured curve, leaving a Weta 7RM4 proxy or the developer's own estimate from chart shots using Jiang's low-dimensional curve prior ([Jiang et al.](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/182538?ln=en)).

Engineering risks are real but bounded: burn's API churn and the absence of U-Net runtime benchmarks, wgpu's unsettled wide-gamut behaviour on Apple, tens of seconds per network pass on CPU-only machines, and three unverified Sony details (whether the 2013 A7R writes the lens-correction tags, whether it has dual conversion gain, and the A1's exact gain-switch ISO). Two legal items fell outside the research. US patent 11948279 claims joint denoise and demosaic with a "lightweight U-Net" ([USPTO](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11948279)) and deserves a freedom-to-operate check. Using "ARRI" or "ALEXA" in the product name or UI was not researched either; the AMPAS license explicitly grants no trademark rights and notes "portions contributed by others as indicated" ([aces-dev LICENSE](https://github.com/ampas/aces-dev/blob/v1.3.1/LICENSE.md)), so the ARRI-contributed vendor files should be checked for notices of their own. Finally, two scope limits belong in the project's documentation: the emulation targets the ALEV III / AWG3 era, because ALEXA 35 colour cannot be derived from an SXT curve, and the public matrix route reproduces ARRI's 2013 processing, which differs from REVEAL's ACE4.

## Conclusion: implications for the development plan

The research changes what is hard. The neural parts the brief emphasises (sensor mapping, illuminant estimation, joint demosaic+denoise) are well-trodden, small and permissively reproducible. The project's defining assets sit at its two ends, and both are fragile: one unlicensed and possibly mislinearized ALEXA curve, and an ARRI rendering that exists only as all-rights-reserved LUTs. The real differentiator against Melara-style global matrices and commercial LUT packs is **illuminant conditioning**, and its accuracy will be limited by illuminant estimation and curve quality, so effort belongs in data validation, synthetic evaluation and an honest metamer-floor baseline before any network architecture work. A less obvious implication: the A7R is the right MVP for colour, because its curve comes from the same lab as the ALEXA's, but the hardest MVP for raw quality, because it records lossy cRAW only and has no AA filter. The A1 that follows reverses both.

The plan can treat the items below as settled, keep the second set open until the named evidence arrives, and schedule the third set before any production code.
