# Raw algorithms: prior-art record

Date: 2026-10-13. Scope: `docs/algorithms/01` to `04`. Background for `docs/14` and `docs/16`. The specs win where they differ. Nothing here is an instruction. This is a provenance record. It is not legal advice and not a patent clearance.

## 1. Purpose and method

### 1.1 Purpose

This record shows where the pieces of the four raw algorithms in `docs/algorithms/` already exist in papers, books, standards, patents, open-source projects and product documentation. The algorithms are the adaptive ratio demosaic (01), the log-guided fast demosaic (02), diffraction deconvolution (03) and output sharpening (04).

The decision of 2026-10-13 (`docs/16`, `docs/14`) is to implement the algorithms in Rust from the specs, with no C++ in the repository. The C++ references and the `tools/check.sh` oracle live outside it. The reference sources are not in this checkout: only `docs/algorithms/*.md` is. So this record rests on the specs. It compares nothing against the C++ source. The vectors stay as the fixed oracle (`docs/algorithms/INTEGRATION.md`).

Patents are not assessed here. They belong to [HUMAN M4-H4]. Section 2.2 lists the patent numbers that came up, as leads.

### 1.2 Method

- Each spec was split into pieces: a step, a formula, a constant or a numeric device. 74 pieces are assessed below. 9 could not be assessed (section 1.4).
- For each piece the check looked for the same technique, or a close variant, in open-source code (files and licences opened and read on GitHub), papers, books, standards, patents and vendor documentation.
- A first pass collected candidates. A second pass re-checked them. It opened what it could, removed what it could not confirm, and re-graded the relations and assessments. Entries that could not be identified were removed, and wrong links were replaced or cleared.
- A piece is not the same as a spec section. One section can hold several pieces, and a constant counts with the piece it belongs to.
- The second pass is called 'the checker' below.

Terms used in the tables:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Relation: same | The source states the same formula or procedure. |
| Relation: variant | The source has the same mechanism with a different test, constant, domain or form. |
| Relation: related | The source is background, a neighbouring technique or a primary source for an idea. |
| Assessment: Known | The piece is standard practice. |
| Assessment: Variant | The piece is a known mechanism with a different test, constant or form. |
| Assessment: Novel combination | Each ingredient is known. The combination, or the way it is used, was not found. |
| Assessment: No prior art found | Nothing was found in what could be searched. This is weak evidence (section 1.3). |
| Status: opened | The source file or page was opened and read. |
| Status: seen in part | Only search fragments of the file were seen. |
| Status: confirmed second-hand | A third-party file that cites the work was opened and agrees with the citation. The work itself was not opened. |
| Status: cited only | The details match the checker's knowledge. The work was not opened. Statements about its content come from recall. |

### 1.3 Limits

- **Reach.** In the sessions that made this record, only GitHub-hosted files (`raw.githubusercontent.com` and GitHub code search), PyPI, npm, the crates index and one Microsoft Research page could be opened. DOI resolvers, publishers, patent sites, arXiv, Wikipedia, university sites, standards bodies and vendor sites failed with DNS errors or proxy refusals. Each was tried once. No attempt was made to get round a block.
- **Search.** The web-search budget was already used up. Literature search was not possible. Only GitHub code search and file reads worked, and the number of code-search queries was small.
- **What was read.** Of the 204 references below, 62 were opened, 2 were seen in part, 10 were confirmed second-hand and 130 are cited only. Almost every paper, book, standard, patent and vendor page is cited only. For those, the bibliographic details match the checker's knowledge and the descriptions come from recall.
- **Negative results.** 'No prior art found' means none was found in what could be searched. GitHub code search is token-based, so it is weak negative evidence. It does not show that a piece is original.
- **Patents.** Patent numbers and titles are as reported. Dates are as recalled. None of the texts was opened. Status, term and claims were not checked.

### 1.4 Pieces left out

9 pieces came back from the check as unverified: no source could be opened and no literature search was possible. Their prior art, citations and assessments are dropped from this record. Each algorithm section lists them under 'Not assessed' with the form the spec gives, so the gap stays visible. They need a new search with working access.

### 1.5 How to read the tables

- A number in square brackets, such as [172], points to section 5. Each entry there has the full citation, the link, the licence and the status.
- A mark after a licence: ‡ copyleft (GPL, LGPL, CDDL, Artistic). Porters must not consult these (section 4.3). † proprietary, licence unclear or licence not verified. Do not copy (section 4.2). A licence marked * is permissive but the file carries extra notices (section 4.1).
- Piece IDs: A for spec 01, L for 02, D for 03, O for 04. The Spec column gives section numbers of the spec, without the section sign.
- The Status column gives the check's label, then how many of the piece's references were opened, seen in part, confirmed second-hand or cited only.
- Text in code font is taken from the specs.

### 2.3 Observations for the spec owner

These came out of the check. The specs are not changed here.

- **A5.** The pass-1 ratio uses unbalanced values while its direction tests are balanced, so the result is not gain-invariant. Spec 01 section 7 item 2 already says so.
- **L10.** The log2 and exp2 cubics are near-minimax but not minimax, and the exp coefficients sum to 0.99995, not 1. The objective used to fit them is unknown. The numbers are in L10.
- **D8.** For a Gaussian, the 80 % two-sided half-width is 1.2816 sigma, so the exact factor would be 0.780. The spec uses 0.75, about 4 % lower.
- **O10.** Method 0 is called 'HardThreshold' but computes a soft threshold (coring). ImageMagick, Pillow and pica use true hard gates.
- **D17.** J1 is the only piece the spec leaves to the implementer. Rust libm (MIT, with Sun's notice) is a ready option.

Notes on pieces:

- **A1.** The step order matches RCD: green at R/B sites, R/B at the opposite-colour sites along the diagonals, then R/B at G sites on the cardinal axes. RCD works in colour differences with a ratio-style green. The spec works in offset ratios. PPG runs the last two steps in the opposite order. Halide's version is permissive and a safe reference for the skeleton.
- **A2.** The spec's variance is the Poisson-Gaussian model. The darktable file shows the same (a, b) linear-variance parameterisation in a mainstream raw tool. EMVA 1288 is the primary source for the quantisation term q^2/12.
- **A3.** The algebra was checked: `x^2 = s^2 (g x + r^2)` has the positive root `x = (s^2/2)(g + sqrt(g^2 + 4r^2/s^2))`, which is T. Setting s = 10 gives K. Both formulas in the spec are correct. Both ingredients (the model and inverting it for a signal level at a target SNR) are known. Their use as a ratio offset was not found, but the search was limited.
- **A5.** Balanced direction tests are standard practice: dcraw and LibRaw scale the channels before demosaicing. The unbalanced ratio next to them is the spec's own choice, and section 7 item 2 of the spec already says the result is not gain-invariant. In the AMaZE file the gain variable appears only in clip thresholds, so it is no evidence for the split. The Epson patent estimates white-balance gains jointly with directional correlation, which is a different mechanism (known from a search summary only).
- **A6.** Hamilton and Adams classify with a green gradient plus a second derivative of the same colour. The spec's second-derivative term is of this family. The `1.6*rho` weight and the 3x3 colour-difference grid are not in the classifier as the checker knows it. No source was found for the +-4/+-5 extension taps.
- **A7.** The two-sided agreement test with a CA relaxation factor f was not found in any source. The three-way logic (one direction, the other, or all four on a tie) is the Hamilton-Adams pattern with a different test.
- **A8.** RCD weights each cardinal estimate by the gradient of the opposite direction, which is the spec's types 1 and 2 (`alpha_l = w_r`). DHT weights by the inverse square of the contrast between the centre and the same-channel sample two pixels away, the same kind of term as `rho*|P(0,-2) - c|`.
- **A9.** DHT starts every buffer cell at 0.5 so that zero is represented as 0.5, a small fixed offset with a purpose like K's but about five orders of magnitude smaller (as reported). RCD and AMaZE use a tiny fixed epsilon in ratio forms. AMaZE switches hard between a ratio estimate and a Hamilton-Adams estimate; the spec blends the two regimes continuously through K. The RCD README says the ratio-corrected approach replaces the additive Hamilton-Adams green with a multiplicative one. A large noise-scaled offset that spans both regimes was not found. Lukac and Plataniotis (2004) is a real unresolved risk: if it uses a constant beta in `(R + beta)/(G + beta)`, the offset-ratio form is known and only the noise-derived K and T are new. A person should read it.
- **A10.** VNG (Chang, Cheung and Pan) scores eight compass directions on a 5x5 window and lets a threshold decide which directions contribute. OpenCV's version averages only the directions below `T = minGrad + max(maxGrad/2, 1)`. It has no knight-move directions. Chang and Chen (2005) is likely the closest prior art for knight-move directions, but its details are unconfirmed. Menon's refinement averages colour differences along the chosen direction, where the spec averages greens. The exact eight-direction scoring and the 50/50 axis/oblique average were not found.
- **A11.** Halide chooses the diagonal with the smaller difference between its two same-colour samples, which is the spec's selector exactly, and adds a green-based correction (additive). RCD blends the two diagonals with a discrimination value. PPG scores both diagonals, picks the smaller and averages on a tie. The cost terms and the offset-ratio combination were not found as a whole.
- **A13.** AMaZE uses 0.75 as a threshold on how far a colour ratio is from unity, to choose an estimator. The spec uses 0.75 in a dominance test that scales a weight. The constant coincides; that is no evidence of lineage but is worth a glance when the spec author is asked about the constants. No source was found for the dominance test, the normalised-chroma cost scaling or the 0.03 floor.
- **A14.** The 5x5 coefficients match Framewave exactly, divisor 571. The kernel is also in several other projects. That it comes from Intel IPP's fixed Gaussian filter is a recollection and is not verified.
- **A15.** Correcting lateral CA on the raw mosaic before demosaicing is established (librtprocess shifts R and B against G by fitted block-wise shifts). The spec instead weights the direction decision by misregistration, which was not found. The constants 2.5, 18, 102, 121/123 and 0.1 px were not found anywhere. Korneliussen and Hirakawa (2014) may be close to a CA-aware demosaic; its method was not read.
- **A16.** The scale constants 2^-14 and 2^-21, the knot spacing `(i + 0.5)/(n - 1)` and the half-diagonal normalisation are confirmed in darktable's v2 branch and, independently, in the lensmeta decode and the silverbox CA scale. The tag IDs and array sizes are in ExifTool, Exiv2 and rawmakase. These are facts about a file format. Not confirmed: the 8 px resampling and the composition `r_src = r*F(r)`, which are the spec's own. Silverbox uses 1.1/16384 for distortion instead of 2^-14. Only `|shift_R - shift_B|` matters in the spec and distortion enters only through `r_src`, so the effect is small, but it deserves a test on real public sample files.

What is standard and what looks original:

**Standard.** The green-first skeleton in the same step order as RCD. Poisson-Gaussian variance (Foi 2008, EMVA 1288, darktable). White balance applied before demosaicing. H/V classification from a green gradient plus a same-colour second derivative (Hamilton-Adams). Choosing the smoother diagonal for R/B at B/R sites (Halide, PPG, DHT, RCD). R/B at G sites from the four axis neighbours. Cross-weighting of opposite directions (RCD). The 5x5 integer Gaussian over 571. Reflect-101 borders. The whole Sony lens-tag decode, scales and knot spacing included.

**Variants of known work.** Colour-difference-consistency direction scores (Menon, Chung and Chan, Hamilton-Adams). The three-way decision (Hamilton-Adams). Inverse-cost weighting (RCD, DHT contrast weights, Shepard). Ratio interpolation with a floor (the DHT 0.5 offset; RCD and AMaZE epsilons; Cok and Kimmel for the ratio principle). Eight-direction selection (VNG, Chang and Chen 2005, Wang and Jeon 2015).

**Looks original, within the limits above.** The noise-derived offsets K and T (the algebra is correct). A large offset that blends the colour-difference and colour-ratio regimes, with Lukac and Plataniotis unresolved. The `1.6*rho` second-derivative weight and the +-4/+-5 extension taps. The two-sided agreement test with a CA relaxation factor. The pass-2 scoring with its 50/50 axis/oblique average. The saturation penalty. The weighting of direction decisions by CA misregistration. Several of these are novel combinations of known mechanisms.

**Constants.** `1.6`, `0.75` and `0.03` (A6, A13); `2.5` and `0.1 px` (A7, A15); `18`, `102`, `121/123` and `kappa = min(ca, 1)` (A15). The `0.75` also appears as AMaZE's threshold, probably by coincidence. The 0.2 factor in the `s` mapping (A3) and the 50 in K are the spec's own.

Notes on pieces:

- **L1.** Malvar, He and Cutler report a PSNR gain of over 5.5 dB over bilinear on their scenes. The spec reports 1.5 to 4.6 dB on its own scenes. McGuire's year, volume and issue are confirmed through the libcamera shader header. A patent family is reported for the Malvar paper; its number is unconfirmed.
- **L2.** In residual interpolation the guide is the interpolated G or R channel, a guided filter makes a tentative estimate and the residual is interpolated. The spec has no guided-filter fit and no residual. Its guide comes from the raw mosaic and its transfer is multiplicative with b = 0 in closed form. No multiplicative or ratio-residual variant of RI is known to the checker.
- **L3.** RCD uses the identical 1 / 0.5 / 0.25 low-pass over the raw CFA in the linear domain, evaluated only at the sites that need it. Dubois describes the Bayer spectrum as baseband luminance `(R + 2G + B)/4` plus chrominance at three carriers, which (as recalled) the spec's linear-domain kernel computes; its log version is the geometric analogue. Alleysson et al. use a larger designed luminance filter.
- **L4.** No file that could be opened implements a log-domain geometric-mean guide for demosaicing. Four GitHub code-search queries found nothing relevant, which is weak negative evidence because the index is token-based. Related ideas exist in other fields: log-average luminance (Reinhard), the centred log-ratio (Aitchison), geometric-mean chromaticity (Finlayson et al.) and homomorphic filtering (Oppenheim et al.). Alleysson et al. 2002 is background only.
- **L5.** The algebra was checked: `K(w) = [(512 - 2k) + 512 cos w + 2k cos 2w]/1024` gives `K(pi) = 0` for every k, and the table values for K(pi/4) and K(pi/2) at P = 100 and -50 check out. The family equals [1,2,1]/4 plus `(k/1024)` times a stride-2 second difference, an unsharp-style term. That is a known construction in general form. No paper or file with this exact parametrisation was found.
- **L6.** The rule is block floating point: a block shares one power-of-two exponent chosen from its maximum. The spec's block is the frame, and the same exponent is used for all tiles. No source was opened for this piece.
- **L7.** Variance-stabilising transforms (glog, the generalised Anscombe) put a noise-scale term inside the nonlinearity. ACEScc and S-Log3 put a fixed offset inside a log. The spec ties the offset to the sensor's read-noise sigma and reuses it as the weight regulariser; nothing found does both. No source was opened for this piece.
- **L8.** CMSIS-DSP returns the natural log in Q4.11, not log2. Halide and Mesa split exponent and mantissa in float with a different range reduction and different coefficients. Mitchell's 1962 method uses the same split with a linear mantissa term.
- **L9.** Mesa's degree-3 minimax fit for `2^x` on [0, 1[ has the constants 0.999925, 0.695834, 0.226067, 0.078025. Its default is degree 5 with the constant term forced to 1.0, so that choice is not new. The spec's cubic has constant 1 and different coefficients, in Q16 integer arithmetic.
- **L10.** The checker reproduced the spec's stated error bounds and compared the fits (numpy/scipy; nothing was written to the repository). A free-constant relative-error minimax cubic for `2^f` gives 0.99992, 0.69584, 0.22604, 0.07804, which matches Mesa's to about five digits. A constant-1 relative minimax cubic gives 0.69512, 0.22765, 0.07707 (max relative error 8.6e-5) against the spec's 0.69591, 0.22493, 0.07912 (1.38e-4). For log2 with `p(0) = 0`, `p(1) = 1`, the minimax cubic is 1.42287, -0.58209, 0.15922 (max abs error 8.8e-4) against the spec's 1.42203, -0.58096, 0.15894 (-9.55e-4 to +7.04e-4). So the spec's fits are near-minimax but not minimax, and their objective is unknown. The spec's exp coefficients sum to 0.99995, not 1. A GitHub code search for 46597 19037 5208, 45607 14741 5185, 1.422027587890625 and 0.6959075927734375 returned only unrelated numeric files. That is weak negative evidence only.
- **L11.** Hamilton and Adams take a Laplacian term on the sparse CFA colour at stride 2. The spec's second difference is on the dense, log-derived guide at stride 1. Halide's second derivative of green is an additive correction for R/B interpolation, not a direction classifier. A small floor against division by zero is routine; in the spec p, tied to read noise, also serves as that floor.
- **L13.** Cok's patent interpolates hue at neighbouring samples and multiplies by the luminance at the target: a mean of ratios, where the spec uses a ratio of means. RCD scales each neighbouring green by a ratio of low-pass values (a 3x3 low-pass of the raw CFA, linear domain). AMaZE uses the same-colour sample two photosites away as the guide and falls back to Hamilton-Adams when the ratio is far from 1. Lukac and Plataniotis may add an offset to numerator and denominator (recalled, unverified), which would also bear on p.
- **L14.** The ratio-estimator reading (Cochran) is the checker's own identification and is mathematically straightforward. AMaZE's adaptive ratios have the form of a ratio of weighted sums, but per cardinal direction with raw same-colour CFA samples as the guide, and the H/V blend comes afterwards. The spec blends numerator and denominator first, then divides once, with a log-domain guide.
- **L15.** RCD's horizontal estimate is weighted by the vertical statistic: exactly the spec's cross weighting. Its statistics are sums of squared high-pass responses, so the effective exponent is 2 in amplitude, and the measure comes from CFA high-pass filters, not a guide second difference. The GBTF reimplementation uses `1/(S*S)` on window sums of colour-difference gradients. LMMSE fuses with inverse-variance weights (exponent 1, from memory). Menon, OpenCV and Halide make hard decisions.
- **L16.** Exponents 1 (LMMSE, from memory) and 2 (GBTF and RCD code, both opened) are documented. No source with exponent 3 on a window-mean second-difference measure was found. The technique is a known inverse-power family and the cube is a tuning constant within it.
- **L17.** OpenCV's edge-aware path uses a plain four-diagonal average with rounding, no ratio. The ratio version is the Cok-style constant-hue form. Halide chooses a diagonal and adds a green second-derivative correction instead of a ratio.
- **L18.** The definition is the standard average-with-rounding. The Intel manual mirror was blocked; the two open-source entries were opened.
- **L19.** OpenCV pads with BORDER_REFLECT_101 (edge pixel not repeated), which keeps parity for a pad of 2. colour-demosaicing uses scipy `mirror` and numpy `reflect`, both without repeating the edge sample.
- **L20.** RCD works on overlapping tiles with a 9 px border, and AMaZE on square tiles. Overlapping tiles are standard. Bit-exact tiling with an exact 5 px reach checked by perturbation is an engineering and test choice. Halide's bounds inference computes the exact stencil footprint.

What is standard and what looks original:

**Standard.** The Bayer luminance / chrominance frequency picture and the `(R + 2G + B)/4` luminance from a 3x3 binomial low-pass (Alleysson, Dubois, Lian; RCD uses the same low-pass in the linear domain). Constant-hue colour-ratio interpolation (Cok, Kimmel, Lukac and Plataniotis, Gunturk; ratio steps in RCD and AMaZE). Gradient or Laplacian direction selection and H/V blending, including RCD's cross weighting. A 5x5 window, the rounded halving average, reflect-101 padding, exponent-plus-mantissa polynomials for log2 and exp2, tile halos and block floating point.

**Variants of known work.** The ratio of weighted sums with one division (the ratio estimator; AMaZE's adaptive ratios). Residual interpolation and guided filtering as the nearest guide-driven family. The kernel family `[1,2,1]/4 + k*(stride-2 second difference)`. Inverse-power weights (exponent 2 in GBTF and RCD, exponent 1 in LMMSE). A second difference of the guide as the classifier (Hamilton-Adams use the sparse CFA). A noise-scale offset inside a log or square root (glog, generalised Anscombe, ACEScc, S-Log3).

**Looks original, within the limits above.** The combination: a geometric-mean luminance guide made by filtering fixed-point log2 of the raw mosaic and returning through exp2, used as a ratio guide with one division. No demosaic found uses it. Tying the log offset to the sensor read-noise sigma and reusing it as the weight regulariser. Exponent 3 on 5x5-smoothed guide second differences (a tuning constant within a known family). The fixed-point engineering: 4096 codes per stop, Q16 cubics with exhaustively bounded errors, a per-frame headroom, and bit-exact tile, phase and mirror equivariance.

**Constants.** The log2 cubic `(46597, 19037, 5208)/32768` and the exp2 cubic `(45607, 14741, 5185)/65536` appear in no source that was found (L10). The cubics are near-minimax but not minimax; the objective used is unknown.

Every piece of this spec was assessed.

Notes on pieces:

- **D1.** The feature exists in Capture One, Canon, Nikon and Fujifilm products. Their algorithms are undisclosed, so this is the same idea, not a known algorithm. Maple takes the same inputs (f-number, pitch) and the same luma-only idea but uses a Gaussian PSF and a damped Richardson-Lucy; it is proprietary. RawTherapee's capture sharpening has no optics input: a search of the file for f-number, aperture, Airy and diffraction finds nothing. Camera makers may hold patents on f-number and wavelength-dependent diffraction restoration filters; see section 2.2.
- **D2.** ExifTool defines the crop factor as 35 mm-equivalent focal length over focal length, and the 35 mm diagonal as sqrt(24^2 + 36^2) = 43.2666 mm. It does not derive a pitch. The registry-first order and the 0.5-20 um sanity band are the project's own choices.
- **D3.** RawTherapee builds a hard mask that is zeroed on a 21-pixel neighbourhood (5x5 without corners) around every raw sample at or above the white level. Maple fades the luma change linearly to zero near white. The spec uses a soft penalty from demosaiced RGB with a 0.8 threshold. Whyte et al. and Cho et al. handle clipping inside Richardson-Lucy. No source was found for the sorted-excess 25/5/1 penalty or the 0.8 threshold.
- **D4.** The open-cam repository is new (copyright 2026), so it corroborates the formula without being a primary source. poppy adds a central-obscuration term the spec does not need; with e = 0 it is the spec's pattern. Born and Wolf section 8.5.2 and Goodman are confirmed only as citations printed in the open-cam file. Airy's 1835 paper was added from memory.
- **D5.** poppy sums monochromatic PSFs with caller-supplied weights. Maple uses a single wavelength of 0.55 um. The checker verified the arithmetic: the ramp is continuous at 540 nm (`(620 - 540)/80 = 1`), w(461) = 1.9875, w(539) = 1.0125, and there are 159 wavelengths. The fixed ramp itself has no prior art found. The spec lists a photopic-like alternative weight for A/B testing; its constants were not matched to a source.
- **D6.** A closed form for the encircled energy exists, `1 - J0^2 - J1^2` (83.8 % inside the first dark ring; the checker confirmed 0.83778 numerically). The spec integrates the weighted polychromatic sum numerically instead. The 1/6 centre weight is not a tuned constant: with linear interpolation of P it is the exact quadrature weight, and the `j*P[j]` terms are exact too (checked). The taper is the right half of a Hann window of width 2 rho, since `cos^2(pi t/2) = 0.5 + 0.5 cos(pi t)`. scipy truncates a Gaussian at a fixed number of sigma; the spec truncates by energy fraction and ring-peak ratio. No source was found for 0.8, 4*r80, 6 px, 0.99 and 0.002.
- **D7.** Bilinear deposit of a point mass is cloud-in-cell assignment. The pixel-aperture model of strollswithmydog and Boreman integrates over a box aperture, which the spec deliberately does not; the bilinear splat acts like a tent kernel (the checker's reasoning, not found in a source). open-cam reaches a kernel by cropping the OTF in the Fourier domain. The ring-and-rotation splat into an 8x8 quadrant with symmetrisation was not found.
- **D8.** Maple uses `sigma_px = 0.42*(1.22*0.55 um*N)/pitch`, about `0.51*lambda*N`, from a closed form. The spec measures sigma_G from the sampled kernel. The slanted-edge method projects edge pixels along the edge into an oversampled profile (Burns; Reichenbach et al.); projecting kernel taps into 1/3-pixel bins with a two-bin split is the same device applied to a PSF, with a line fit instead of an FFT. The checker verified that a Gaussian's 80 % two-sided half-width is 1.2816 sigma, so the exact factor would be 0.780; the spec's 0.75 is about 4 % lower. No prior art was found for the 8-direction, 1/3-pixel, 0.85-range procedure.
- **D9.** Unsharp masking is standard. The 3.5*sigma + 1 radius rule is the spec's own; scipy uses a different truncation (4 sigma).
- **D10.** The core of the estimator is not novel. `top - c` equals twice the mean of the non-negative deltas (`top = c + 2*Sp/Np`; max difference 4e-16 over 2000 random windows), which is the textbook method-of-moments estimate of the upper endpoint of a uniform spread that starts at c. The 12.5 and 0.04 pull-in, the use of both sides and the clamp against the midpoint have no source found. The same identity appears in O16.
- **D11.** The first van Cittert step `y + (y - K conv y)` is the spec's residual with all coefficients fixed to 1 (a derivation). PETPVC is iterative with a global step alpha, where the spec fits spatially varying coefficients once. The one-step, per-pixel-fitted, multi-coefficient form has no source found.
- **D12.** The OpenCV guided filter was read line by line: eps added to the covariance diagonal, an explicit 3x3 inverse, box means and mean-filtered coefficient planes. That is the same architecture. The differences: the guided filter regresses a target on a guide, while the spec regresses a blur residual on three reblurred layers, weights by inverse noise variance, and gates the result. Its BORDER_REFLECT repeats the edge sample, unlike the spec's mirror. The three-coefficient fit over reblurred layers with an inverse-noise weight has no published match. RAISR fits per-class filters offline, not per pixel from the image itself.
- **D13.** scikit-learn's Ledoit-Wolf uses a shrinkage target `mu*I` with `mu = trace(S)/n`, which matches the spec's trace-proportional ridge; Ledoit and Wolf choose the weight from data, the spec fixes 0.0005. The branch logic (c_H clamp, tie, the -0.5 bound) has no source found.
- **D14.** Morozov's discrepancy principle chooses the regularisation so the residual is about the noise level; the spec applies a similar test per pixel as an on/off decision. White's damped RL does not update pixels whose residual is within a noise threshold. Maple damps where the blurred estimate is below a noise floor. RawTherapee gates on local contrast. The identity `Q ~ 1 - 2*K0 + sum(K^2)` for white noise is the squared norm of `delta - K` and is the spec's own derivation. The 0.5, 1/4 and 1/2 bands have no source found.
- **D15.** The spec's `box(u)/box(omega)` has the same form as normalised convolution. Here omega is a confidence from the fit residual and the numerator carries an extra gain factor, so gated pixels are pulled toward zero rather than ignored. astropy implements the binary-mask version. The form `(kE/E)^2` with kE = 2 and a floor of 0.01 was not found.
- **D16.** RawTherapee, Maple and OpenCV all carry a luma change to RGB by a multiplicative ratio, so the ratio step is established practice. None has a cap or an additive remainder. What has no source is the specific hybrid: a 1.5 cap on the ratio plus an equal additive remainder so that output luma equals L_s exactly. Fattal et al.'s colour step uses a ratio with an exponent.
- **D17.** Series plus Hankel asymptotics is textbook. Boost switches at 8 with rational approximations for small x. rust-lang libm (from FreeBSD) switches at 2 with rational forms and is MIT-licensed, with Sun's notice, which must be kept. Cephes (via scipy/xsf) uses Chebyshev terms on [0, 8]; its file carries only Moshier's notice, so its terms should be checked before copying anything. J1 is the only piece the spec leaves to the implementer.
- **D18.** scipy's `mirror` mode and OpenCV's BORDER_REFLECT_101 are the spec's rule. OpenCV's guided filter uses BORDER_REFLECT, which repeats the edge sample. Halo and tiling parity follows from the finite support of every step and is the project's own engineering, backed by its test plan.
- **D19.** The JDK source shows the same constants as splitmix64 and names the provenance of the mix function (Stafford variant 13). A permissive Rust implementation exists (Apache-2.0 OR MIT), so the generator need not be hand-written. Vigna's file is the public-domain original; its licence was not verified. The sum of 12 uniforms minus 6 as an approximate unit normal is a standard textbook device.

What is standard and what looks original:

**Standard.** The Airy pattern through J1 (open-cam, astropy and poppy implement the same formula; Born and Wolf, Goodman). J1 evaluation (DLMF, Abramowitz and Stegun; Boost, libm and Cephes read in source). Box filters and mirror borders. The Hann taper. The unsharp-mask high-pass. Luma-only sharpening with ratio transfer. Ridge regularisation. Pixel pitch from sensor size or crop factor. The feature of sharpening a raw photo by a diffraction PSF from f-number and pixel pitch.

**Variants of known work.** A weighted polychromatic PSF sum (poppy). A one-step van-Cittert-like correction with spatially adaptive coefficients (PETPVC is iterative with a global step). Guided-filter-style local least squares with coefficient averaging (OpenCV contrib, line by line). Normalised-convolution-style confidence averaging. Discrepancy-principle and damped-RL-style gating. Clip handling (RawTherapee mask, Whyte et al.). The envelope estimator, which is the uniform-endpoint method of moments.

**Looks original, within the limits above.** The combination into one per-pixel noise-weighted fit with a Q-based gate. The ring-and-rotation splat into an 8x8 quadrant. The line-spread cumulative-energy fit for sigma_G. The sorted-excess clip penalty. The capped ratio plus additive remainder (the ratio alone is not original).

**Constants.** No source was found for: the 461-619 nm ramp `(620 - lambda)/80` with its 540 nm knee; the radial step `0.42*N/(4*pi)`; 500 radial samples; 0.8 and 0.01; 4*r80 and 6 px; 6 angular samples per pixel; 0.99 and 0.002; pi/16, 3 bins per pixel, 0.85 and 0.8; the 0.75 factor for sigma_G and its 0.2-3 clamp; `3.5*sigma + 1`; 25/5/1; 12.5 and 0.04; the 0.0005 ridge; the 0.5, 1/4 and 1/2 gate bands; kE = 2 and 0.01; the 1.5 ratio cap. The 1/6 weight is exact quadrature, not tuned.

ImageMagick and Pillow add the delta only past a hard gate, per channel in encoded space. pica works on HSV value.
- **O2.** The pica wiki tabulates scale factors between tools (radius, amount, threshold). It gives no piecewise map and no constants like the spec's. The idea that a UI radius maps to a Gaussian sigma is universal, but no source gives this map, its constants or the 2.5 cap. The spec's table is self-consistent (checked by hand: `0.7*0.5 + 0.3 = 0.650`, `0.97*sqrt(0.41) = 0.621`).

- **O4.** OpenCV derives the size from sigma with `| 1` and a 3 or 4 sigma cutoff. libvips and ITK truncate by amplitude or error. The constants 5.0, 2.5 and the caps 13 and 33 match nothing found; they are design choices. The spec's tap table follows from the rule (checked by hand).
- **O5.** No source states or compares sharpening thresholds in linear light against encoded values. Other tools give thresholds in 8-bit levels of their own encoded values. The spec lists this as an open A/B in section 7.1 item 3.
- **O6.** The principle (sharpen at output scale, with a radius tied to output resolution) is established. libvips documents suggested sigmas by output resolution as a lookup, not a formula. No source was found for the 0.15 knee, the linear sigma rescale or the erf centre-tap threshold normalisation. The checker confirmed that `c(x)` is the centre tap of an untruncated pixel-integrated Gaussian and `1 - c^2` is the gain of the 2-D separable high-pass at its centre, as the spec says.
- **O7.** The geometry is correct (checked by hand): D = 580 mm at 300 ppi gives 1.99 px, matching R = 2 at `print_distance` 5000. Capture One has a viewing distance parameter; whether it changes the radius is not stated in what was seen. No source was found for the 2500 px-per-radius scale, the 15 px cap or the 5000 default.
- **O8.** The preset numbers (100 / 1.0 / 1 / 60 and 150 / 5000 / 1 / 0) have no source, and the spec marks them as unsigned (RA-H3). The Fraser and Schewe three-stage framing and the Lightroom and Capture One presets are known at feature level only. The reference sources are not in this checkout, so their provenance cannot be checked.
- **O9.** Finley's HSP page is the source named in the comments of Bokeh and c-ray, which both use these weights. Both use the formula for display brightness, not as a sharpener's luma. darktable's Euclidean norm is unweighted.
- **O10.** Pyctools has this function verbatim (coring: detail below the threshold is ignored, above it is reduced by the threshold). It is Donoho's soft threshold applied to an unsharp-mask high-pass. The spec's name 'HardThreshold' is misleading: ImageMagick, Pillow and pica use a true hard gate, and the spec's method 0 computes a soft threshold. No source was found for the `A/(1 - T)` compensation, which looks original.
- **O11.** The ratio-of-blurs form is normalised convolution with signal = the per-pixel soft gain `max(1 - T/|d|, 0)` and certainty = `d^2`, because `P = d^2 * gain`. That identity is the checker's derivation, not a claim from the papers. No source applies it to unsharp-mask detail.
- **O12.** Gating sharpening by edge or gradient strength is established (Adobe's Masking slider; RawTherapee's contrast mask; adaptive unsharp masking). No source has the g0 calibration from the step response, the 5x ramp or the G2-smoothed gate. libvips gates on the size of the high-pass, not on a blurred-luma gradient.
- **O13.** OpenCV's normalised Scharr kernel (smoothing {3, 10, 3}, derivative {-1, 0, 1}, scale 1/32) is the spec's gradient, giving slope per pixel. The coefficients were not read from Scharr's thesis.
- **O14.** Multi-band sharpening with summed band-pass layers is well established. LSFmod applies one limiter pass after sharpening but does not accumulate per stage. The accumulator-plus-single-limiter arrangement is straightforward and no specific source was found.
- **O15.** LSFmod (3x3 Minimum / Maximum plus overshoot allowances) is the closest permissive match. libplacebo and mpv's antiring clamp to local lo/hi from neighbouring samples, blended by a strength (mpv default 0.0, high-quality profile 0.6). RawTherapee's halo control is a soft, continuous limiter. AMD CAS scales the amount by headroom and does not clamp the result. The spec's specifics (a robust estimator, Gaussian-smoothed envelope maps, limiting only a share of the delta, a 5x5 window) differ from every instance found.
- **O16.** The estimator `b = 2*mean - a` for a uniform distribution with known lower bound a is a textbook identity (the checker re-derived it; the same identity as in D10). LSFmod uses plain min/max. No source was found for its use as a local-maximum estimator, the `n_hi / n_shi` weighting, the plateau pinning, the 1/25 floor or the `G_h` smoothing. These look original.
- **O17.** libplacebo mixes limited and unlimited output by a 0-1 strength; the spec splits the delta by amount. No source was found for the square-root mapping from percent to share, which is probably a perceptual tuning choice.
- **O18.** pica sharpens HSV value and multiplies R, G and B by the ratio v2/v1. Scaling RGB by a common luminance ratio is standard in tone mapping and sharpening. libvips and RawTherapee instead sharpen the Lab L channel and convert back; the spec's ratio avoids the round trip. The cap of 16 and the floor of 1e-20 are numeric safeguards with no source. Kasson et al. is known from its abstract only.
- **O19.** OpenCV's BORDER_REFLECT_101 and scipy's `mirror` are the spec's rule; BORDER_REFLECT repeats the edge sample and is the alternative the spec rejects. The rise and overshoot metrics rest on documentation summaries that could not be opened. The Lanczos-3 7 % overshoot figure and the tile-and-halo bit-exactness contract were not researched.

What is standard and what looks original:

**Standard.** The unsharp-mask core (ImageMagick, Pillow, pica, scikit-image, Petrou and Petrou). The pixel-integrated erf Gaussian with unit-sum renormalisation (Tides of Revival, crt-beans, Lindeberg). The Scharr 3-10-3/32 gradient (OpenCV). The quadratic-mean HSP luma with the 0.299 / 0.587 / 0.114 weights (Bokeh, c-ray). Hue-preserving ratio transfer of a luma change to RGB (pica). Reflect-101 borders.

**Variants of known work.** Method 0 is soft-threshold coring (Pyctools verbatim; Donoho). The tap rule follows OpenCV's size-from-sigma pattern with a different cutoff. The halo limiter that clamps to local min and max (LSFmod, libplacebo and mpv antiring, RawTherapee halo control). A print radius from viewing distance and the Screen / Print presets exist in Capture One, Lightroom export and one patent (all unopened).

**Looks original, within the limits above.** The specific method 1 (a smoothed, energy-weighted soft-threshold gain, which is normalised convolution with certainty d^2 by the checker's derivation). Method 2's step-response calibration of g0 and its 5x ramp. The rank-count weighting, plateau pinning and `G_h` smoothing of the robust envelope maps. The resolution rescale with the erf centre-tap threshold normalisation. The `A/(1 - T)` compensation. The square-root halo mapping.

**Constants.** No source was found for: the radius map `0.7R + 0.3` / `0.97*sqrt(R - 0.09)` and the 2.5 px cap; the 13 and 33 tap caps; the 5.0 and 2.5 of the tap rule; the 0.15 sigma knee; the 5x ramp; the 2500 and 15 of the print scale; the preset numbers; the 16 ratio cap.
