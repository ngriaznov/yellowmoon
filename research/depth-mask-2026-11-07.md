# A 25-million-parameter model unlocks depth masks

Yellowmoon can add a Depth mask with one new model and very little new image code. The model is **Depth Anything V2-Small**: Apache-2.0 code and weights, **24.8 M parameters, about 50 MB at f16**. It is a ViT-S and DPT network, smaller than the SAM 2.1 encoder the app already runs in its own pure-Rust CPU runtime ([Depth-Anything-V2 README](https://github.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2); [docs/07](../docs/07-ml-models.md)). It would run once per photo, in the background, on the same Input picture that Object, Subject and People read. It would leave a relative depth map in memory. Each Depth row would then cut a u16 plane from that map by near, far and softness, without running the network again. The existing raster pipeline does the rest, as it already does for the Background (blur) mask: it resamples the plane through the photo's geometry and pulls its edge to the photo with the guided filter. Apple and Sony supply no depth. macOS 14 to 27 has no public depth API. Spatial scenes are visionOS-only and expose no depth. ARW files carry no depth map. But Sony's maker note does record where the camera focused, and that can seed a one-click "Focus plane" pick that none of the editors surveyed offers. The market case is current. Camera Raw (April 2026), DxO PhotoLab 10 (September 2026) and ON1 ship depth masks that work on any photo; Lightroom, Capture One and darktable do not. The weak point is the same everywhere: depth edges at hair, foliage and glass. That is why every vendor pairs depth with segmentation or manual refinement. By our own count the network costs **about 101 GMAC on a 3:2 photo, about half of SAM's encoder**, so roughly 2 to 2.5 s of background work on the reference 4-core Xeon. Nobody has timed it on a Mac yet. Three owner decisions gate the work. First, whether to add a depth model at all: the 2026-10-24 "no depth model" ruling was about bokeh reshaping, not masks. Second, whether to bundle its 50 MB, as SAM's 77 MB were, or download it on first use. Third, whether Apache-2.0 weights whose unreleased teacher learned from non-commercial synthetic data meet `docs/14` rule 5, as the SAM precedent did for SA-1B.

## Depth masks split the raw-editor field in 2026

A depth mask became a real raw-editor feature only this year, and the field is split. Adobe's Camera Raw 18.3 (April 2026) lets the Range mask select by depth on any photo, raw or JPEG; reviewers say Adobe builds the map "similar to the way it creates one for the Lens Blur feature" ([Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/education/depth-range-masking-camera-raw-adobes-most-useful-new-photoshop-feature-902214); [Computer Darkroom](https://www.computer-darkroom.com/blog/2026/04/16/camera-raw-lightroom-classic-desktop-april-2026/)). **Lightroom and Lightroom Classic still enable Depth Range only for files with embedded depth**, which in practice means multi-lens phone captures. An Adobe Community answer on Lightroom Classic 15.3.1 confirms this ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-675/using-latest-version-of-lrc-15-3-1-depth-range-masking-is-greyed-out-and-not-available-using-shift-d-why-1628318)). DxO PhotoLab 10 (1 September 2026) is the only raw editor that documents its design. It estimates relative depth from one photo, then a refinement network aligns depth edges with the image, and that step runs in tiles so large files do not overwhelm the computer ([DxO grey paper](https://www.dxo.com/en/news/grey-paper-depth-masks/)). DxO frames the feature neatly: AI Masks find "what" is in the scene, Depth Masks find "where" it is ([DxO AI Masks](https://www.dxo.com/en/technology/ai-masks/)). ON1 Photo RAW had depth masks in 2025 and a "dramatically improved" one in 2026 that needs no depth metadata ([ON1 blog](https://www.on1.com/blog/5-photo-editing-techniques-to-try-in-on1-photo-raw-2026/)). DaVinci Resolve Studio has had an AI Depth Map effect since version 18. Resolve 21 (June 2026) adds AI CineFocus and a Photo page, so its depth tools now reach raw stills ([Newsshooter](https://www.newsshooter.com/2026/06/02/davinci-resolve-21-final-release/); [DaVinci Resolve Club](https://davinciresolveclub.com/davinci-resolve-21-photo-page/)).

The editors without it matter as much. Capture One has no depth mask as of 16.7.x, and an open idea asking for one had 33 votes. Its author works around the gap by exporting a greyscale map from Photoshop's Depth Blur filter and importing it as a mask ([Capture One Ideas FR-I-1652](https://captureone.ideas.aha.io/ideas/FR-I-1652)). darktable marked "Depth map masks" as won't-fix ([darktable #19517](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/issues/19517)). Pixelmator Pro and Affinity Photo only read the depth that iPhone Portrait captures embed ([DPReview](https://www.dpreview.com/news/7748026795/pixelmator-pro-1-3-1-released-with-portrait-masks-for-images-captured-in-iphone-portrait-mode); [Affinity forum](https://forum.affinity.serif.com/index.php?%2Ftopic%2F151192-depth-map%2F=)). For a Sony raw editor, embedded depth is irrelevant, so the depth must come from a model on the device.

|---|---|---|---|
| Camera Raw 18.3 | AI, any photo | Click to sample; range and feather; greyscale view, white near ([PHLEARN](https://phlearn.com/tutorial/how-to-use-ai-depth-masking-in-photoshop/)) | Mask stack: add, subtract, intersect with Subject, Sky and brushes |

The controls have converged on five ideas. You click the photo to sample a depth. You get a near/far band with a separate feather or softness. You can view the depth in greyscale, white for near. You can invert. And you combine the result with semantic masks by intersect or subtract. Resolve shows the two shapes a band can take: a levels-style ramp (Near and Far Limit as white and black points, plus gamma) for "everything behind this fades in", and an isolation slab (Target, Tolerance, Softness) for "only this plane" ([Elements.tv](https://elements.tv/blog/using-the-new-depth-map-feature-in-davinci-resolve-18/); [Sidney Baker-Green](https://sidneybakergreen.com/davinci-resolve-ai-depth-map/)). Lens Blur's Subject Focus, which sets the focus range from the detected subject, is a ready-made pattern for "select the subject's plane" in one click. **Every product exposes relative depth on normalised sliders. None shows metres, and none uses the camera's recorded focus distance** ([Adobe help](https://helpx.adobe.com/camera-raw/using/lens-blur.html); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/09/01/dxo-photolab-10-hands-on-finding-depth-all-over-the-place/)). One caveat applies to all the Adobe details: helpx.adobe.com and fstoppers.com could not be opened from the research session. Slider names and the Camera Raw version pairing therefore come from tutorials and search text, not from Adobe's own pages. Whether Lightroom gained the AI version in October 2026 is also unconfirmed.

The failures are the same in every product, and so are the fixes. Adobe's own engineers call depth for "ropes, fly-away hair, or foliage" challenging and admit "we can't make a depth estimation model that produces perfect results for every photo", which is why Lens Blur has refine brushes ([Adobe blog](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2024/06/26/inside-lens-blur)). Users report blur over large parts of a subject's hair and wrong results where hair and background pixels mix ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41751169)). Reviewers of DxO say the first mask can misread what is layered, that rounded objects with gradual depth are hard, and that the tool works best when the scene has real depth in it ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/09/01/dxo-photolab-10-hands-on-finding-depth-all-over-the-place/)). Resolve 21 Public Beta 4 "reduced edge shimmer, stair stepping, and halos" at depth edges ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/davinci-resolve-21-public-beta-4-released-canon-raw-highlight-fix-fusion-motion-path-keyframing-and-refined-ai-cinefocus/)). For stills, stair steps and halos after upsampling a coarse map are the matching risk. The fixes are manual brushes (Adobe), a post blur (Resolve), a refinement network (DxO), and intersection with segmentation masks (everyone). No reviewer reported on sky, glass, mirrors, macro or ultra-wide shots, so Yellowmoon must test those itself. No vendor publishes timings or the resolution at which it computes depth. The caching pattern is consistent, though. Depth is computed once, lazily, and every slider edit is a cheap remap of the cached map. Resolve users even render-cache a dedicated depth node and feed its alpha onward so scrubbing never recomputes it ([Toolfarm](https://www.toolfarm.com/tutorial/resolve-studio-21-new-features-tutorial-roundup/)).

## Depth Anything V2-Small is the one model that passes every gate

### Permissive weights exist at every size, but many licences conflict

Licence-clean depth weights exist at every size that fits in an app bundle. The upstream cards, checked on 2026-11-07, are the only licences that count, because third-party ports mislabel in both directions. One MLX port of the Apache-2.0 DA3MONO-LARGE is tagged CC BY-NC 4.0. Another port tags even the non-commercial Video Depth Anything Large as Apache-2.0 ([HF search](https://huggingface.co/api/models?search=mlx%20depth)). Where a project's GitHub README and its Hugging Face card disagree, the licence is unresolved, and a permissive tag on one side does not settle it.

DAv2-Small wins among them on four counts: the smallest file, the only first-party Apple-silicon timing, a working Rust reference in candle's `depth_anything_v2` example ([candle](https://github.com/huggingface/candle/blob/main/candle-examples/Cargo.toml)), and a licence chain that stays inside one licensor's grant (see below).

### Small models keep the depth ordering; nobody has measured their edges

A range mask needs two things from a depth model. It needs a coherent ordering of near against far, and it needs depth edges in the right place. The first survives shrinking well. On DAv2's DA-2K benchmark of point pairs across eight scene types, **ViT-S orders 95.3 % of pairs correctly against 97.1 % for ViT-L**, while diffusion models such as Marigold score 86.8 % ([DAv2 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09414)). On standard benchmarks the gap is just as small: KITTI AbsRel 0.078 for Small against 0.074 for Large, NYU 0.053 against 0.045 (same source).

Edges are another matter, and **no boundary score is published for any small permissive model**. Among large models, MoGe-2 ViT-L leads the permissive field on iBims boundary F1 with 17.9, ahead of Depth Pro (14.3) and DAv2 (13.9) ([MoGe-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02546)). In MoGe-3's averaged table, InfiniDepth reaches 19.3, MoGe-3 16.0 to 17.3, Depth Pro 16.3 and MoGe-2 15.6, while "Depth Anything 3" scores only 6.61 ([MoGe-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17967)). That last row is for an unnamed DA3 variant run by a rival team, and it conflicts with DA3's own claim of "substantially richer detail and finer structures" ([DA3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10647)). So DA3's edges need our own check before anyone relies on them. MoGe-2's authors also show that edges sharpen with input resolution and token count ([MoGe-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02546)), which suggests small models at 518 px will be soft.

Hair is out of reach for every model. On Depth Pro's matting-set test of hair and fur, the best boundary recall is Depth Pro's 0.173; DAv2 manages 0.107 ([Depth Pro paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02073)). A depth-only mask will not resolve strands at any model size. This is the strongest argument for the small model. Its job is to say which plane. The photo, through the guided filter, and the segmentation masks Yellowmoon already has say where the edge is.

### No model sees more than 2.4 MP of a 24 to 61 MP frame

Native working sizes run from 0.15 MP (DPT at 384²) to 2.36 MP (Depth Pro at a fixed 1536²); DAv2 works at 518² ([Depth Pro paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02073)). A 24 MP Sony frame is about 88 times the area of DAv2-Small's 3:2 input, and a 61 MP frame about 2.5 times more again. Raising DAv2's input is allowed ("You can increase the size for even more fine-grained results" ([README](https://github.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2))), but fixed-resolution ViTs degrade off their training size ([MoGe-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02546)), and attention cost grows with the square of the token count. Tiling is the other route. PatchFusion (MIT code) tiles to 4K but takes 84 s per VGA image on a V100 in its original form ([Depth Pro paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02073); [PatchFusion](https://github.com/zhyever/PatchFusion)). BoostingMonocularDepth is "for academic use only" and may be covered by patents ([LICENSE](https://github.com/compphoto/BoostingMonocularDepth)). Every shipping design therefore runs one coarse pass over the whole frame and then upsamples against the photo. That is what DxO describes, and it is what Yellowmoon's mask pipeline already does.

### Apple's 25 ms is a speed bar, not a path

The only first-party Apple-silicon figure is Apple's own: its F16 Core ML package of DAv2-Small at 518×396 runs in 24.6 ms on an M3 Max and about 33 ms on an M1 Max ([Apple card](https://huggingface.co/apple/coreml-depth-anything-v2-small); [Apple gallery](https://developer.apple.com/machine-learning/models/)). The two sources disagree on which compute unit produced the M1 Max number: the card says Neural Engine, the gallery says "All" and GPU. Ports report trouble off the reference path. DA3 Core ML packages warn that moving to another compute unit "can silently change the numerics (FP16 attention overflow) or crash on the GPU" ([port card](https://huggingface.co/mlboydaisuke/Depth-Anything-3-Small-CoreML)). A MoGe-2 ViT-B port took 41 s to load and ran three times slower with the Neural Engine allowed ([port card](https://huggingface.co/outmake/moge-2-vitb-coreml)). Apple measured its F16 package at an abs-rel error of 0.0089 against PyTorch FP32 ([Apple card](https://huggingface.co/apple/coreml-depth-anything-v2-small)), so its output is not the reference output. These numbers show what the hardware can do. Yellowmoon cannot ship that route anyway, because every model must run in Rust and exports must be identical bytes (section 4).

### Every strong permissive model inherits non-commercial data through a teacher

The weights' licences are clean. Their ancestry is not. DAv2 trains a DINOv2-G teacher on five synthetic sets: BlendedMVS, Hypersim, IRS, TartanAir and Virtual KITTI 2. It then labels 62 M real images with that teacher (BDD100K, Google Landmarks, ImageNet-21K, LSUN, Objects365, Open Images V7, Places365, SA-1B). "Student models are trained purely on these pseudo-labeled real images" ([DAv2 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09414)). **Hypersim is CC BY-SA 3.0, and Virtual KITTI 2 is "for non-commercial purposes only"** ([Hypersim](https://github.com/apple/ml-hypersim); [VKITTI2](https://europe.naverlabs.com/research/computer-vision/proxy-virtual-worlds-vkitti-2/)). DA3's teacher and MoGe-2 also train on Hypersim, and DA3's teacher on VKITTI2 as well ([DA3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10647); [MoGe-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02546)). No strong permissive model escapes this.

DAv2-Small's chain is the cleanest of them. Its teacher was never released, so the whole chain sits inside one licensor's Apache-2.0 grant. DepthART and Distill-Any-Depth differ in kind: they learned from the released DAv2-Large, whose own weights are CC BY-NC 4.0. There the restriction is on the teacher's weights, not on its data.

`docs/14` rule 5 says "Shipped weights only from CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, Apache or our own data" and keeps non-commercial data evaluation-only ([docs/14](../docs/14-licensing.md)). The SAM 2.1 row is the precedent. Yellowmoon ships Meta's checkpoint because its licence is Apache-2.0, and its model card states Meta's training data, SA-1B included, "as Meta states it; we use none of it" ([SAM model card](../data/models/sam2/MODEL_CARD.md)). DAv2-Small fits that pattern exactly. The owner should still confirm it in so many words, because the earlier bokeh research already flagged DAv2's teacher chain as needing "the same owner decision" ([bokeh-map research](bokeh-map-2026-10-24.md)).

## Apple and Sony supply focus, not depth

### macOS has no depth API, and Core ML would break two hard rules

Apple offers nothing that turns a 2D photo into depth on a Mac. Vision's 2026 documentation lists segmentation, pose, saliency, optical flow and custom Core ML requests, and **no request that outputs depth or disparity** ([Apple Vision](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision)). Its one 3D request, `VNDetectHumanBodyPose3DRequest`, returns body joints, not a depth image ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vndetecthumanbodypose3drequest)). RealityKit's `Spatial3DImage` makes "a 3D spatial scene created from a 2D image" with `generate()`. But its platform list is visionOS 26 only, and it has no accessor for depth, mesh or points ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/imagepresentationcomponent/spatial3dimage); [JSON](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/realitykit/imagepresentationcomponent/spatial3dimage.json)). An Apple engineer confirmed that "the spatial scene APIs mentioned above are for visionOS only" ([forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/787237)). Nothing from WWDC26 brought it to macOS 27 ([WWDC26 guide](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc26/guides/visionos/)). `AVDepthData` and Core Image's `auxiliaryDisparity` only read depth that a camera embedded, and return nil when a file has none ([AVDepthData](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avdepthdata); [auxiliaryDisparity](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coreimage/ciimageoption/auxiliarydisparity)). Foundation Models takes images in but returns text and structured values, never pixels ([Foundation Models](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels)). Apple's Core ML gallery lists exactly one depth model, DAv2-Small ([Apple gallery](https://developer.apple.com/machine-learning/models/)). Press coverage says WWDC26 introduced "Core AI" as Core ML's successor, but that rests on one secondary source ([InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/06/apple-core-ai-wwdc)).

Running DAv2-Small through Core ML would therefore be the only Apple route, and it fails Yellowmoon's rules twice. Hard rule 6 says "every model runs in Rust" ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)). The Vision bridge is accepted because it calls an OS framework rather than a bundled model ([docs/14](../docs/14-licensing.md)). And Core ML's precision "varies based on the hardware and software versions" ([coremltools](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/apple/coremltools/main/docs-guides/source/typed-execution.md)), which breaks bit-identical exports between the app and `ym` on Linux. Apple's useful contribution is indirect: the Subject and People masks already in the app are the natural partners that give a depth band its edge.

### Sony records where it focused; how far is a coarse guess

Sony ARWs write no EXIF `SubjectDistance` and no plain focus distance. What they do write is more useful for a mask than it first looks.

| Maker-note tag | What it gives | Seen in 128 real ARWs (local ExifTool 12.76 run on `testdata/`) | Use for a depth mask |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FocusLocation` 0x2027 / `FocusLocation2` 0x204A | "Location in the image where the camera focused"; the image centre when unknown ([ExifTool Sony.pm](https://github.com/exiftool/exiftool/blob/master/lib/Image/ExifTool/Sony.pm#L1531-L1543)) | 78 files, ILCE-6300 and newer; absent on A7, A7 II, A7S, A7R, A6000, NEX; 20 of 78 exactly at the centre | Where to sample the focus plane; a point exactly at the centre is ambiguous |
| `FocusFrameSize` 0x2037 | Width and height of the AF box around that point ([Sony.pm](https://github.com/exiftool/exiftool/blob/master/lib/Image/ExifTool/Sony.pm#L1717-L1727)) | Valid only on ILCE-1, 7M4, 7CM2, 6700 | How large a box to sample |
| `FocusPosition2` (enciphered 0x9402 table) | A byte from 80 to 255, 255 = infinity; ExifTool's `FocusDistance2` formula "may be wrong", and the data "seems to be proportional to magnification not a focus distance" ([Sony.pm](https://github.com/exiftool/exiftool/blob/master/lib/Image/ExifTool/Sony.pm#L10904-L10930)) | 118 of 128 files; 0 (no value) on 25, all manual or adapted lenses plus two others; impossible distances on some (0.032 m, 0.166 m, and 113.5 m in APS-C crop mode) | A soft prior for how deep the in-focus band is, never a metric truth |
| Focal length, f-number, lens format (`Tag9416` and others) | Real optics ([Sony tag table](https://github.com/exiftool/exiftool/blob/master/html/TagNames/Sony.html)) | Present on current bodies | Depth-of-field width |

The local results come from the research session's own ExifTool run over public files and the owner's photos in `testdata/`, not from a web source. They cover the A7 III, A7 IV, A7R IV, A1 and A7C II. The A7R V and A9 III were present only as synthetic files, so their real behaviour is unconfirmed. Yellowmoon already parses the location and frame tags into `ym_raw::makernote::SonyFocus`, and `docs/20` notes the point "is still read from the maker note but no longer used" ([makernote.rs](../crates/ym-raw/src/makernote.rs); [docs/20](../docs/20-auto-crop.md)).

Reading ExifTool's formula backwards suggests that `FocusPosition2` is a log-magnification code in steps of 1/16 stop. If so, each step changes distance by about 4.4 %, and the largest finite value is about 1,879 times the focal length (roughly 45 m at 24 mm). The formula multiplies by the 35 mm-equivalent focal length, which would inflate distances about 1.5 times on APS-C and in crop mode, matching the 113.5 m outlier. This is an inference from ExifTool's source, not a measurement, and V1's data rule forbids test shoots to check it.

With that caution, standard depth-of-field maths still yields a useful band ([ExifTool Exif.pm](https://github.com/exiftool/exiftool/blob/master/lib/Image/ExifTool/Exif.pm#L4870-L4911)). An A7 IV with a 90 mm macro at f/11 focused at 0.31 m is sharp over about 5.7 mm. An A1 at 35 mm, f/8, focused at 34 m is beyond its 5.1 m hyperfocal distance, so it is sharp from about 4.45 m to infinity. One is a tight slab; the other says "the whole background is in focus".

The design that follows is a **Focus plane** pick. Sample the relative depth map (its median) inside `FocusLocation` and `FocusFrameSize`, or inside a small box where the frame is absent, and centre the band there. Use the depth-of-field ratio from a valid `FocusPosition2` only to suggest the band's width. Fall back to a default width when the byte is 0 or 255, and to a manual pick when the location is absent or sits exactly at the centre. No editor in the survey does this. Every one of them asks the user to click.

## The Background mask already solved the hard parts

### The network is a smaller cousin of SAM in ym-ml

Yellowmoon's models already run in its own pure-Rust CPU code, in f32, under one determinism rule. That rule is: "f32 in a fixed order, no fused multiply-add, `libm` for `exp`, `erf`, `tanh` and `log`, every output row computed by one task whatever the pool". SAM 2.1 small proved it: **1, 2 and 8 threads give identical bits, and its mask IoU against Meta's own code is 0.99978** ([docs/07](../docs/07-ml-models.md); [docs/algorithms/06](../docs/algorithms/06-masks.md)).

DAv2-Small's encoder is simpler than SAM's Hiera-S: 12 global-attention blocks at 384 channels, 6 heads, patch size 14, with no windows and no pooling ([dinov2.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2/main/depth_anything_v2/dinov2.py)). `ym_ml::sam2` already has most of the maths: the packed matrix product, layer norm with f64 sums, exact GELU, softmax with f64 sums, multi-head attention for any head size, PyTorch-exact bicubic resizing of positional embeddings, and a transposed convolution written as a matrix product ([kernels.rs](../crates/ym-ml/src/sam2/kernels.rs); [decoder.rs](../crates/ym-ml/src/sam2/decoder.rs)).

The new parts are small. They are a 14×14 patch embedding, the class token, LayerScale, and DINOv2's positional resize. That resize uses a `+0.1` offset and PyTorch's `scale_factor` coordinate rule, an exact-parity hazard the oracle fixtures will catch. The DPT head adds a 4×4 stride-4 transposed convolution, dense 3×3 convolutions on 64 and 32 channels, bilinear upsampling with `align_corners=True`, an aspect-keeping bicubic resize to multiples of 14, and a final ReLU ([dpt.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2/main/depth_anything_v2/dpt.py); [blocks.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2/main/depth_anything_v2/util/blocks.py)). The dense convolution must copy `bokeh_net`'s stem and not the `jdd` runtime, which picks fused multiply-add on AVX2 machines and so breaks the rule ([cpu_runtime.rs](../crates/ym-ml/src/cpu_runtime.rs)).

The weights pipeline copies SAM's. A CI script converts the checkpoint to f16 safetensors with a self-describing header and publishes it as a prerelease asset that is never "latest". `tools/fetch` downloads it with a SHA-256 check. Oracle fixtures come from the upstream PyTorch code on public photos, as CI-only test tooling. The model card states the upstream licence and training data "as ByteDance states them" ([docs/07](../docs/07-ml-models.md); [docs/12](../docs/12-distribution.md)). `sam2` is about 3,500 lines; a ViT-S plus DPT runtime that reuses its kernels should be smaller (a judgement, not a measurement).

### One map per photo, one cheap cut per row

The Background (blur) mask is the template. A per-photo learned map is made once, keyed and held in memory. "Level and Softness cut a new plane from the cached cells (no network run), the other three re-run steps 2 to 5 only" ([docs/algorithms/06](../docs/algorithms/06-masks.md)). Object contributes the rest of the pattern. A background worker waits 150 ms and can be cancelled. A viewer never waits for the encoder. An export makes its own features unless the keys match. And there is a single Input picture that all AI masks read: the photo as the Input renders it, with the Neutral look, at 1024 px ([object.rs](../crates/ym-core/src/pipeline/graph/object.rs); [pipeline/object.rs](../crates/ym-core/src/pipeline/object.rs)).

A Depth source then needs these pieces, all mapped onto existing code. Add `MaskKind::Depth` (JSON `"depth"`, in the Find group) with near, far and softness on a normalised 0 to 1 scale, plus the shared feather, grow and refine ([masks.rs](../crates/ym-core/src/settings/masks.rs)). Add a `DepthMap` beside `BlurMap`, made from the same `object_source` picture and keyed like `object_key`. Add `RasterSource::Depth`, which cuts S₀ in f64 and quantises it to u16, with a cache key of map key, near, far and softness ([mask_raster.rs](../crates/ym-color/src/mask_raster.rs)). Because S₀ lives in the photo frame, before crop, straightening, quarter turn and lens bend, those geometry changes cost nothing new ([docs/23](../docs/23-nodes.md)).

The build itself enforces the rest. `StepKind::declared` and `OpKind::declared` are "`match`es with no wildcard arm", so a new kind stops the build until it is mapped, here to `OpKind::Raster` ([schedule.rs](../crates/ym-core/src/pipeline/schedule.rs)). The depth plane's key joins the grade-plan key that the level cache reads ([docs/17](../docs/17-gpu.md)). A `DepthModel::probe`, like `ObjectModel::probe`, feeds `mask_kinds`, `ym doctor`'s Masks row and the `mask` error. A missing or damaged weights file therefore stops that photo with exit code 1, "never taken as one that selects nothing" ([docs/10](../docs/10-cli.md)).

The CLI needs no new flag: `ym --settings` already reads mask rows as JSON. A row in the proposed format would look like `{ "kind": "depth", "near": 0.0, "far": 0.35, "softness": 0.3, "feather": 0, "grow": 0, "refine": 0.7 }`. The field names are a proposal, not a spec. Hard rule 4 is kept: depth maps join SAM features and blur maps in "small in-memory caches, never on disk" ([docs/23](../docs/23-nodes.md)). The row's eye and Show mask work for any raster row with no kind-specific code. A "Pick depth" click can copy the Colour range's `sample_chroma`, reading the cached map at the photo-frame point with no network run.

### Normalise per photo, select in disparity, ramp like Brightness

The network's output has no metric scale and no fixed zero. The model research lists DAv2's output as affine-invariant disparity, larger for nearer ([DAv2 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09414)). The integration research could not reopen the paper, though, so the sign and the treatment of sky must be confirmed in the oracle fixtures before the spec relies on them. Selection should stay in disparity-like units. Inverting to depth needs the unknown shift and sends the sky to infinity. Disparity spreads the near and middle distances and compresses the far field. Picking a subject or a foreground band is therefore easy; separating two distant ridges is not.

The row's numbers should live in a space normalised per photo, so that a pasted "far 30 %" means something on another photo and the copy rule can be "worked out again", like Background's. There are two deterministic choices. Percentile scaling is simple, but a big sky shifts everything. Rank normalisation makes a slider read as "share of the photo", but a large flat area squeezes it. Either one must be computed exactly, in f64 with fixed tie rules, and it becomes part of the pipeline version.

The cut itself should mirror the Brightness range trapezoid, `S((d̂ − (near − q))/q)·(1 − S((d̂ − far)/q))`, with a minimum ramp like `RANGE_RAMP_MIN = 0.02` so that a tiny numerical difference cannot flip a pixel ([docs/algorithms/06](../docs/algorithms/06-masks.md)). Expect sky and haze to collapse to the far end, so a "far" band will take the sky with distant hills. Mirrors, water and windows take the depth of what they show or of their surface, unpredictably. Depth Pro's authors call translucent surfaces and volumetric scattering ill-posed ([Depth Pro paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02073)). The model card's known limits should say so, as SAM's does.

### Edges come from the photo and from segmentation

At DAv2's default input, a 3:2 photo becomes 518×784 depth cells. On a 6000×4000 export each cell spans about 7.7 px. Refine edges at 100 uses the He–Sun–Tang guided filter with a 48 px radius, about six cells. That is enough to pull a band's edge to a nearby luma edge, "hair, a ridge, a window frame" ([docs/algorithms/06](../docs/algorithms/06-masks.md); [guided filter](https://people.csail.mit.edu/kaiming/publications/pami12guidedfilter.pdf)). This is the "guided feathering" use the guided-filter paper describes. It is O(N), already tile-exact and already on the GPU, so the cut-then-refine order needs no new spatial kernel.

Its limit is that the guide is luma only. It cannot separate a dark coat from dark foliage. A three-channel guide would help, but that is a spec change and a new kernel. The other order, upsampling the depth first with joint bilateral upsampling or the fast bilateral solver (MIT reference code) and then cutting, keeps edges crisp at every band setting ([Kopf et al.](https://www.johanneskopf.de/publications/jbu); [bilateral solver](https://github.com/poolio/bilateral_solver)). It needs a full-resolution f32 plane per view, though, and an iterative solver fits badly with "box-based filters with absolute block origins". Neither is needed for a first version.

The real edge fix is the one every vendor uses: segmentation. The existing fold already intersects. Add a Subject row, then subtract a Depth row with Invert on, and the result is Subject times Depth ([docs/algorithms/06](../docs/algorithms/06-masks.md)). The matte from Vision or SAM then carries the hair edge and the depth carries the layering. One more pairing matters. Sony portraits at f/1.4 to f/2.8 often have smooth, defocused backgrounds, and a "defocused input lacks the edges monocular depth relies on" ([FlashReFocus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23297)). There Background-from-blur is strongest and depth weakest. In deep-focus landscapes and architecture the reverse holds. The two sources complement each other, and the product should present them that way.

### Determinism: the CPU reference makes every export

GPU, Core ML and PyTorch inference are not bit-stable across machines. PyTorch states that "Completely reproducible results are not guaranteed across PyTorch releases, individual commits, or different platforms" ([PyTorch notes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pytorch/pytorch/main/docs/source/notes/randomness.md)). Yellowmoon's own device contract promises identical bytes only on the "same machine, adapter and driver" ([docs/17](../docs/17-gpu.md)). The consistent design keeps f16 weights, widens them to f32 and computes on the CPU under SAM's rules. An export makes its map "from the CPU reference" picture, as Object and Background already do, so the app and `ym` give the same bytes on every machine "as far as IEEE 754 goes" ([docs/algorithms/06](../docs/algorithms/06-masks.md)). The normalisation and the cut are f64 and are then quantised, like `background_s0`. A burn or ort device path could serve the viewer later if the CPU is too slow on a Mac, "still deterministic within a stated bound", but exports would still recompute on the CPU. The Vision precedent, where answers may differ across macOS versions, is the exception and should stay one.

### Cost: about half a SAM encode

No published GFLOPs figure for DAv2-Small at 518 could be opened, so the integration research counted multiply-adds itself. The count cross-checks against ViT-S at 224² and against SAM's documented 200 million softmax `exp` calls.

| Input | Tokens | Total | Attention share | Against SAM's 180 GMAC encoder |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 518×518 | 1,370 | 57.6 GMAC | 30 % | 0.32× |
| **518×784 (3:2, aspect kept)** | 2,073 | **100.6 GMAC** | 39 % | **0.56×** |
| 1036×1036 | 5,477 | 438 GMAC | 63 % | 2.4× |
| 1036×1554 (3:2) | 8,215 | 864 GMAC | 72 % | 4.8× |

SAM's encoder takes "3.1 s best and 3.6 s median" on the reference 4-core Xeon, and about a third of its work is `erf` and `exp` ([docs/algorithms/06](../docs/algorithms/06-masks.md)). Scaling from that gives an estimate for DAv2-Small at 518×784 of about 7.7 CPU-seconds, or **2 to 2.5 s wall time on four threads**. Doubling the input to 1036×1554 would take over 20 s, which is not interactive. Memory is small next to SAM's 360 MB: about 99 MB of f32 weights, tens of MB of activations, and a 1.6 MB cached map per photo. These are estimates on the documented Linux machine. **No Apple-silicon CPU timing exists for any Yellowmoon model yet** (M0-H4), and that is the number that decides whether the CPU path suffices. A vectorised deterministic `exp`, already floated as an owner decision in `docs/algorithms/06` §6.5, matters more for this model than for SAM, because attention is a larger share of its work.

## Recommended plan: decide, port, wire, then measure

The plan below keeps every hard rule. The model runs in Rust. The app and the CLI share one core and one CPU export path. Nothing is saved per photo. There is no network access unless the owner chooses a first-run download. And it needs no measurements of Yellowmoon's own: oracle fixtures and evaluation use public photos and the MIT-licensed DIODE depth set, the one standard depth benchmark found with a clearly permissive licence ([DIODE](https://diode-dataset.org/)). ETH3D and KITTI are non-commercial ([ETH3D](https://www.eth3d.net/); [KITTI](https://www.cvlibs.net/datasets/kitti/)).

### Open questions

Several facts are still unconfirmed, and each has a cheap way to settle it. DAv2's output sign (larger for nearer) and its sky handling come from memory of the papers. The first oracle fixture will settle both. Whether the released DAv2-Small checkpoint uses the code defaults for batch norm and the class token was not checked against its tensors. The conversion script will show it. PyTorch's `interpolate(scale_factor=…)` coordinate rule could not be read, so the positional resize is a parity risk that only the fixtures will expose. Whether `object_picture` is shared between SAM, Vision and a third consumer, or remade for each, decides whether the Input picture or only its code path is shared. That one needs a code read.

Others need real work. The key unknown is the Mac CPU time. If DAv2-Small takes much longer than SAM's encoder on Apple silicon, the device preview path moves forward. No small permissive model has a published boundary score, and DA3's 6.61 in a rival's table contradicts DA3's own claims. Only our own check on DIODE and on public portraits can rank them. The licences of the matting sets used for hair evaluation (AM-2k, P3M-10k) are unknown. `FocusPosition2` accuracy per body and lens, and its behaviour in crop mode, cannot be measured under the V1 data rule. And no real files from the A7R V, A9 III, A7S III, A1 II or A7 V were available, so their focus tags are confirmed only by ExifTool's tables.

Outside the repository, several things should be watched rather than assumed. DA3-LARGE-1.1's Apache-2.0 tag might be a deliberate relicence. Marigold's README now contradicts its v1-0 card. InfiniDepth's registration terms clash with its card. Lightroom might yet gain AI depth. And Apple's reported Core AI framework is untested here. None of these blocks the plan above.

## Conclusion

The research turns a feature that sounds like a machine-learning project into a plumbing job. The difficult parts of a depth mask are tile-exact edge alignment, geometry that follows crops and turns, a background worker, in-memory caching and identical bytes between the app and the CLI. Yellowmoon solved all of them when it built Background-from-blur and Object. The network itself is a smaller relative of the SAM encoder already running in Rust. The remaining risk is not technical. It sits in two owner judgements, about a teacher chain everyone in the field shares and about 50 MB of bundle size. And it sits in one unmeasured number: the Mac CPU time.

The more interesting finding is where Yellowmoon can do better than the products it would follow. Every editor surveyed treats depth as a standalone slider that the user aims by clicking, and every one of them struggles at hair and at already-blurred backgrounds. Yellowmoon has three signals none of them combines: depth for layering, a blur map that is strongest exactly where depth is weakest, and the Sony focus box that says which plane the photographer meant. Presenting the Depth row as one partner in that set, with Subject or Object for the edge, Background for shallow-focus shots and Focus plane as the default pick, turns the field's shared weakness into a design that is plainly better than "click and widen".
