# Physics, not diffusion, builds cinema lens character

Yellowmoon can give still photos convincing cinema lens character (the streak flares, oval bokeh, detuned edges, swirl and glow of large-format anamorphics and vintage cinema glass), and the strongest route is a physically based optics engine working on its scene-linear raw data, guided by small licence-clean depth and matting models, not a generative image editor. The owner is right that Gaussian blurs and radial vignettes look fake, but the professional tools (Unreal's convolution bloom, Maxon's ray-traced flares, Nuke's depth-driven Bokeh node, Adobe's Lens Blur) fix this with better physics, not generative models: energy-conserving, long-tailed, field-varying kernels applied to highlights restored above the clip, with ghosts placed on the line from the source through the frame centre. Every licence-clean generative editor breaks several hard constraints at once; the smallest, FLUX.2 [klein] 4B, needs 3–9 GB of weights, takes four steps of 5–7 s each per 1 MP edit on an M1 Max, works at 1–2 MP against 24–60 MP photos, has no Rust implementation, does not repeat bit for bit across Apple chips, and returns 8-bit display-referred pixels that hold nothing in the clipped highlights where flares start. ML does pay in two places: monocular depth (Depth Anything V2-Small: Apache-2.0, 24.8 M parameters, a working candle port) plus hair matting, which guide the reshaping of the bokeh already in the photo into anamorphic ovals and swirl, and offline lens simulation that turns expired-patent prescriptions into small fitted formulas.

## The reference lenses fall into four kinds, each with its own signature

The productions known for strong lens character used four kinds of glass, and each leaves a different, recognisable mark on the image.

| Kind of lens | Documented on | Visible signature |
|---|---|---|
| Large-format front anamorphic | Panavision Ultra Vista 1.65× on Dune (2021) ([ASC](https://theasc.com/articles/dune-fear-is-the-mind-killer), search excerpt only); ARRI ALFA anamorphics, rebuilt from Master Anamorphics and detuned, on The Batman ([No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/why-does-batman-look-so-gorgeous-and-how-you-can-copy)); Panavision anamorphic, C-series per a crowd-sourced listing, on Blade Runner (1982) ([ShotOnWhat](https://shotonwhat.com/blade-runner-1982)) | Horizontal streak flares, oval bokeh, highlights that are "not quite round" ([No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2018/07/watch-two-master-cinematographers-ridley-scott-blade-runner)), falloff towards the edges |
| Detuned or vintage spherical | Rehoused 1980s Moviecam lenses, Soviet Helios and Jupiter lenses at 37, 58 and 85 mm, and a 57 mm Petzval lens "where you can dial in your effect" on Dune: Part Two ([ARRI Rental](https://www.arrirental.com/en/about/overview/news/interview-on-dune-part-two-with-greig-fraser-acs-asc); [Definition](https://definitionmagazine.com/features/dune-2)); Helios 44-2 and Jupiter lenses on The Batman's car chase ([4K Shooters](https://4kshooters.net/2022/05/06/the-batman-film-uses-vintage-helios-44-lenses)) | "Center sharpness that reduces as it moves towards the edge and reduced contrast" ([4K Shooters](https://4kshooters.net/2022/05/06/the-batman-film-uses-vintage-helios-44-lenses)), swirl, glow, characteristic bokeh |
| Front-of-lens effect plate | Silicone sealant on a glass plate in front of the lens on The Batman ([VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/?p=13135598)) | "Abstract lens flares" and a "spiderweb of light" around point sources |
| Clean modern primes | Zeiss Master Primes on Blade Runner 2049 ([American Cinematographer, Dec 2017](https://cdn.theasc.com/AC1217-Blade-Runner-2047.pdf)) | Little lens character: even sharpness, low flare, round bokeh |

The detuned lenses show what "character" means in practice. The Batman used two sets of large-format anamorphics, "one being optically pristine and the other detuned so it could not focus on anything outside the center of the frame" ([VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/?p=13135598)), which gave "resolution in the center but… a harsh falloff in the edges" ([No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/why-does-batman-look-so-gorgeous-and-how-you-can-copy)); Weta had to build custom tools to match "how much smearing and lensing artifacts you get with the detuned lensing" ([VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/?p=13135598)). On Dune: Part Two, Greig Fraser used vintage glass because "the larger ALEXA sensors are so extraordinary that I felt we needed to dirty the image up a bit", and cropped from one corner of the large image circle so that "the left half of the image [falls] off to a blur while the right half is clean" ([ARRI Rental](https://www.arrirental.com/en/about/overview/news/interview-on-dune-part-two-with-greig-fraser-acs-asc)), an off-centre sharp area that a field-varying renderer can reproduce directly. J.J. Abrams' Star Trek (2009) is the usual reference for blue horizontal anamorphic streaks, some of which were added digitally to CG shots ([Westword](https://www.westword.com/?p=40669027)).

Two limits follow. No published measurements (MTF, flare spread, bokeh shape, distortion) were found for any of these lens sets, and no public verified prescription exists for the Ultra Vista, the ALFA, the Master Primes or the C-series, so Yellowmoon can reproduce the kind of character each family has, not a replica of a named lens. And the presets must carry descriptive names only, never production titles or lens and brand names ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md), rule 3).

## Highlight traits need no depth; bokeh traits cannot exist without it

The visible traits come from five physical mechanisms, each with a distinct signature. Every air–glass surface reflects about 4 % of light, and pairs of reflections form ghosts that "typically lie on the straight line joining the light source and the principal point", sometimes clipped into arcs by the aperture and tinted "blue, purple, or pink" by the coatings ([Wu et al., ICCV 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12485)). Diffraction at straight iris edges makes starbursts, n spikes for an even blade count and 2n for an odd one ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffraction_spike)). Scattering from dust, scratches and haze in the glass makes long-tailed glow and a contrast-lowering veiling glare ([Talvala et al.](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/glare_removal/)). ARRI sells Flare Sets for its Master Anamorphics whose special coating "encourages flaring, ghosting and veiling glare", industrial proof that coatings, not the anamorphic design alone, set flare character ([Film and Digital Times](https://www.fdtimes.com/2015/03/18/master-anamorphic-flare-sets/)). In front-anamorphic lenses the cylindrical elements ahead of the stop squeeze the entrance pupil into an oval, so out-of-focus points become ovals, which rear anamorphics do not produce ([US 10,126,533](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10126533); [RedShark News](https://www.redsharknews.com/production/item/3478-the-look-that-hollywood-loves-and-the-lenses-that-create-it?hs_amp=true)). Off-axis, the barrel truncates the pupil and turns edge highlights into cat's-eyes while centre highlights stay round ([Rosenman](https://richardrosenman.com/elevating-photorealism-why-cats-eye-bokeh-optical-vignetting-are-essential-for-after-effects-compositors/)).

| Trait | Physical cause | Needs per-pixel depth? | What drives it in a single still |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ghost chains | Paired reflections, coating tints | No | Linear highlight map and its position relative to the optical centre |
| Anamorphic horizontal streak | Reflections smeared along the cylinder's power axis | No | Same highlight map |
| Starburst | Diffraction at iris blades, per wavelength | No | Highlights plus f-number and blade count |
| Glow, bloom, veiling glare | Scatter in glass, coatings and housing | No | Whole linear image, energy-conserving convolution |
| Smeared front-plate flare | Scatter from a dirty or coated plate in front of the lens | No | Highlight map, with a random-phase "dirty" pupil |
| Detuned edges, field softness | Field curvature, astigmatism | Approximately no | Field position (a field-varying PSF) |
| Lateral CA, barrel distortion, natural vignetting | Lens geometry | No | Field position |
| Oval anamorphic bokeh | Squeezed entrance pupil | **Yes** | Circle of confusion from depth, focus distance and f-number |
| Cat's-eye, swirl | Optical vignetting (and, disputed, astigmatism) | **Yes** | Circle of confusion plus field position |
| Soap-bubble or onion-ring bokeh, axial CA | Spherical aberration, aspheric surface errors | **Yes** | Signed circle of confusion (in front of or behind focus) |

The first family scales with highlight energy and dominates night and backlit photos; the second lives in the out-of-focus regions and dominates shallow-focus portraits. Commercial defocus tools confirm that the second family needs depth: Nuke's Bokeh node works "according to a Z depth map, Deep data, or Camera information", and without depth "the result is uniformly defocused" ([Foundry](https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/content/reference_guide/filter_nodes/bokeh.html)). The cause of swirl is still disputed between optical vignetting and astigmatism ([Kasson](https://blog.kasson.com/the-last-word/lomography-58-mm-petzval-psfs); [Pentax Forums](https://www.pentaxforums.com/forums/10-pentax-slr-lens-discussion/334715-what-lenses-swirl-2.html)), so for a renderer swirl is a tuned artistic shape rather than a derived one. Breathing is a focus-pull effect and does not exist in a still.

Two consequences follow for Yellowmoon, one an advantage and one a limit. Flare is additive light in linear space, and "the flare-causing light source is likely saturated in the input image", whose "saturated pixels… contain little information" ([Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12485)). Yellowmoon already knows exactly which photosites clipped (the R5 clip mask in [docs/05](../docs/05-raw-processing.md)) and works in scene-linear AWG4, so it can restore a lamp's or the sun's energy above the clip before drawing its flare; a JPEG plug-in must guess, and an 8-bit generative model has nothing to read. Added glare also makes sources look brighter than white, as a perceptual test of glare on digital images showed ([Spencer et al., SIGGRAPH 1995](https://en-cg-web.coecis.cornell.edu/pubs/1995/SSZG95.html)), which helps an SDR app that already compresses its highlights. The limit concerns photos shot wide open, which already contain round discs: turning them into ovals means first recovering an all-in-focus image, a step current methods hand to a generative deblurrer because restoration is ill-posed ([FlashReFocus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23297)), and none of those is licence-clean. Depth models are also weakest there, because a "defocused input lacks the edges monocular depth relies on" ([same](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23297)). Narrowing the discs the Sony lens recorded, or giving them an exact new shape, therefore stays out of reach; making them taller or stretching them tangentially needs no deblurring, because it only extends the blur that is there (see the addendum on reshaping).

## Professional tools look real because they model optics in linear light

The owner's complaint about "standard math" is one engine vendors make about their own defaults. Epic's documentation calls its standard bloom, a sum of Gaussian blurs at 1/2 to 1/32 resolution, "not conservative (it can result in an overall brightening of the image)" and says it "lacks the visual complexity of the Bloom Convolution". The convolution version models "the scattering and diffraction of light within the camera" as an FFT convolution with a kernel image that is the lens's response to a single point, conserves energy, and gives "a continuum of responses ranging from star-like bursts to diffuse glowing regions"; Epic credits the realism to "a visually interesting, non-symmetric kernel image" whose hot centre is far brighter than its tail ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/bloom-in-unreal-engine)). Defocus vendors make the same point about bokeh: generic tools give highlights "the exact same circular shape from the center of the screen all the way to the outer corners", while real lenses change across the frame ([Rosenman](https://richardrosenman.com/elevating-photorealism-why-cats-eye-bokeh-optical-vignetting-are-essential-for-after-effects-compositors/)). The failure is not math as such; it is math that is short-tailed, symmetric, display-referred, identical everywhere in the frame and not energy-conserving.

Commercial tools fall into four families, and none uses generative models for these effects. Element libraries such as Video Copilot Optical Flares and Sapphire LensFlare place artist-built sprites along a hotspot–pivot line ([Video Copilot](https://www.videocopilot.net/products/opticalflares/); [Boris FX](https://borisfx.com/documentation/sapphire/ofx/lensflare/)). Plate vendors such as Lens Distortions sell real flares shot on black, including a "Master Prime" collection, under "ALL RIGHTS RESERVED" terms ([Lens Distortions](https://lensdistortions.com/)). Maxon's Real Lens Flares traces "the geometry of the lens' physical designs", exposes "the optical coating on each virtual lens element", and produces flares that "move, morph, and merge" consistently with f-stop ([Maxon](https://help.maxon.net/rg/en-us/Content/html/01-Real-Lens-Flares.html)). Kernel and depth tools such as Nuke's Bokeh node offer aperture-blade kernels, a "Kernel Aspect Ratio" for anamorphic material, and spherical- and chromatic-aberration controls ([Foundry](https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/content/reference_guide/filter_nodes/bokeh.html)). The high end works the same way: to match The Batman's silicone-plate flares, Weta shot its own silicone-plate elements and built a tool from them ([VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/?p=13135598)). The closest commercial reference for stills, Adobe's Lens Blur, builds an AI depth map and then offers shaped bokeh including Cat Eye and an oval anamorphic option ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/in_hi/camera-raw/desktop/edit-and-enhance-images/sharpening-and-noise/lens-blur.html)), which is exactly the split recommended here: ML for perception, classical kernels for rendering.

The tools imply a concrete recipe. Composite in scene-linear light before any tone or display transform, and restore clipped sources above the clip before convolving, as Unreal's "Convolution Boost" does. Build kernels physically as PSF = |F{pupil}|² per wavelength, which Wu et al. integrate over 73 wavelengths from 380 to 740 nm through a spectral response matrix, a role Yellowmoon's shipped Sony spectral curves can fill. Add a long exponential or power-law tail for veiling glare and normalise to unit energy. Place ghosts as chains on the source–centre line, each clipped by the aperture and tinted by its coating, and draw the anamorphic streak as a long-tailed one-dimensional kernel with a sharp core, accompanied by elliptical ghosts, rather than a stretched Gaussian. Vary every defocus kernel with field position. Hullin et al. traced ghosts through full prescriptions with coatings ([MPI](https://resources.mpi-inf.mpg.de/lensflareRendering)); Lee and Eisemann's paraxial version runs at about 297 fps against 4.1 fps but loses the aberration-warped ghost shapes ([Eurographics](https://diglib.eg.org/items/4a62c7cf-d1f0-40af-b211-7a85c3e8cafd)). A still has no frame-rate budget, so the full trace is affordable.

The physics needs one input the vendors keep private: lens prescriptions. Public ones exist for vintage and some anamorphic designs. The lentil database (MIT per its README) holds 24 lenses, among them a 1954 Cooke 2× anamorphic (US2832262), a Panavision rear anamorphic at 1.34× squeeze, a Schneider anamorphic (US6310731), a Speed Panchro, a Biotar and a Kodak Petzval ([lentil lenses.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zpelgrims/lentil/dev/polynomial-optics/database/lenses.json)), and expired patents such as US3217597 and US4017160 carry numerical anamorphic tables ([US3217597](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3217597); [US4017160](https://patents.justia.com/patent/4017160)). Coating data are missing almost everywhere. Wu et al. chose lab capture over rendering for that reason, while noting that "lenses of similar design share similar internal reflection paths" ([Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12485)). Yellowmoon can therefore credibly offer the character of a vintage double-Gauss, a Petzval or a 1950s front anamorphic, never a replica of a named cinema lens.

## Generative editors fail Yellowmoon's constraints; depth and matting models pass

### Diffusion editors are strong at 1 MP and wrong for this pipeline

The open instruction editors are now good: on vendor-reported GEdit-Bench figures, Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (7.877) and FireRed-Image-Edit (7.943) score above Nano Banana Pro (7.738) ([FireRed card](https://huggingface.co/FireRedTeam/FireRed-Image-Edit-1.0)), and several ship Apache-2.0 weights with Apache-2.0 text encoders. No benchmark, however, measures the task Yellowmoon needs: add a lens effect and change nothing else.

| Model | Weights licence | Size | Measured speed near 1 MP | Native resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FLUX.2 [klein] 4B | Apache-2.0, Apache-2.0 Qwen3-4B encoder ([HF](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-4B)) | 3.88 B; 23.7 GB repo; 3.3–9.3 GB loaded in mflux ([mflux](https://github.com/filipstrand/mflux/blob/main/src/mflux/models/flux2/README.md)) | 5.0–6.8 s per step × 4 steps on M1 Max, 4-bit ([mflux](https://github.com/filipstrand/mflux/blob/main/src/mflux/models/common/README.md)) | 1024² |
| Qwen-Image-Edit, -2509, -2511 | Apache-2.0 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-Edit-2511)) | 20.4 B; 57.7 GB | Original model: about 25 min for 10 steps on a 64 GB M2, PyTorch MPS ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Aug/19/qwen-image-edit/)) | ~1 MP |
| LongCat-Image-Edit | Apache-2.0 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/meituan-longcat/LongCat-Image-Edit)) | ~6 B; 29.3 GB | No Mac figure | ~1 MP |
| Mage-Flow-Edit | MIT on a mirror; official repo unread ([HF mirror](https://huggingface.co/natalie5/Mage-Flow-Edit)) | 4 B plus a 4 B encoder; 17.5 GB | 1.02 s per edit on an A100 (Turbo); no Mac figure | 512–2048 |

The licence traps sit just outside this list. Qwen-Image-2.1 moved to a non-commercial research licence ([LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1/blob/main/LICENSE)); FLUX.1 Kontext [dev] and FLUX.2 [klein] 9B are non-commercial ([HF](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-9B)); FLUX.3 Klein uses a gated, revenue-capped community licence; OmniGen2 bundles a research-licensed Qwen2.5-VL-3B ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct)); and HiDream-E1.1 needs Llama 3.1 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/HiDream-ai/HiDream-E1-1)). Every lens-flare or bokeh LoRA found sits on a FLUX.1-dev, Krea or SDXL base and inherits its restrictions ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Muapi/lens-flares-and-backlighting-effects-for-flux-by-ethanar)).

Against Yellowmoon's rules the shortlist fails in several independent ways. Even the smallest clean editor is gigabytes, two orders of magnitude above the ~30 MB at which [docs/12](../docs/12-distribution.md) already moves weights to a first-run download. Twenty to thirty seconds per 1 MP edit makes it an explicit "Generate" action, never a slider. Resolution is a wall, since "existing diffusion-based models remain constrained to resolutions below 1K" and upscaling an edit yields "hallucinated details [that] contradict the original high-resolution… source" ([EditBridge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18063)), while a frame-wide anamorphic streak cannot be tiled independently. Content drifts: in one measured Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 test the stock pipeline shifted the image by up to 14 px and changed **19 % of pixels outside the edited region**, falling to 0.7–0.8 % only when the reference was fed at native size ([lilting.ch](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/qwen-image-edit-pixel-perfect-referencelatent); a single-author test on an illustration, not a photo). Determinism holds per machine at best: Diffusers states that "full reproducibility across different hardware is not guaranteed" ([Diffusers](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/en/using-diffusers/reusing_seeds)), and a careful MLX port decodes bit-identically on M4 chips but reaches only 43.6 dB on an M5 Pro ([mflux](https://github.com/filipstrand/mflux/blob/main/src/mflux/models/flux3/README.md)). Yellowmoon's own GPU contract also promises identical bytes only on the same machine, adapter and driver and agreement "within the stated tolerances" elsewhere ([docs/17](../docs/17-gpu.md), rule D8). A feed-forward depth network can meet such a tolerance; a multi-step sampler carries rounding differences through every step, so its tolerance cannot be bounded in advance, and the CPU reference every kernel needs would take minutes per photo. The quietest failure is the decisive one: editors return display-referred, roughly 8-bit images, so the clipped source that decides whether a flare looks real arrives empty.

The Rust route is thin as well. candle's FLUX example is text-to-image only and no Qwen-Image or FLUX.2 port exists ([candle](https://github.com/huggingface/candle/blob/main/candle-examples/examples/flux/main.rs)); burn has no diffusion model ([burn](https://github.com/tracel-ai/burn)). stable-diffusion.cpp (MIT, C++ on ggml with Metal) does run FLUX.2 klein and the Qwen edit models and has Rust bindings in `diffusion-rs` ([sd.cpp](https://github.com/leejet/stable-diffusion.cpp); [diffusion-rs](https://github.com/newfla/diffusion-rs)), but it would put a large C++ engine into a core whose rule is that "every model runs in Rust" ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)).

The owner's view has a real basis: a generative editor can paint a plausible flare or a shaped bokeh from a text prompt without any lens model. What it paints, though, is invented at preview resolution: highlight energy it cannot see, ghost positions not tied to the optical centre, and changes to pixels the lens would never touch, which also collides with the settled principle "No masks, layers or retouching" ([docs/00](../docs/00-overview.md)). The blind bake-off below is the fair way to settle how much that matters to the eye.

### Learned bokeh and flare models offer no shape control under a clean licence

No learned bokeh model combines a permissive licence with custom aperture shapes. BokehMe and MPIB have Apache-2.0 code, but in BokehMe the shape comes from its classical scattering renderer and the neural part only repairs depth edges flagged by an error map; its authors say neural renderers "lack controllability and have difficulty in generating stunning bokeh balls" ([BokehMe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.12614)). The rest fail on licence or weight: Bokehlicious has no licence and trains on RealBokeh, which is CC BY-NC-SA ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/timseizinger/RealBokeh_3MP)); AnyBokeh is under the non-commercial S-Lab licence on a FLUX.1-Fill-dev base ([repo](https://github.com/itsmag11/AnyBokeh)); BokehDiff needs a 13 GB SDXL base ([repo](https://github.com/FreeButUselessSoul/bokehdiff)); and FlashReFocus has no licence and warns that its outputs change "by a few 1/255" between runs ([repo](https://github.com/ming053l/FlashReFocus)). An arXiv search found no learned anamorphic-bokeh method at all ([arXiv query](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:anamorphic+AND+(abs:bokeh+OR+abs:lens+OR+abs:defocus))), and in NTIRE 2026 expert raters marked down the entries with the best pixel metrics for visible artefacts ([NTIRE 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05510)), a reminder that PSNR does not capture what makes bokeh read as real.

Flare research is almost entirely about removal. The one permissive asset is Google's release with Wu et al.: Apache-2.0 simulation code and **5,001 flare-only images under CC BY 4.0**, 2,001 of them lab captures through a smartphone lens ([google-research](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/flare_removal)). Flare7K and BracketFlare are non-commercial ([Flare7K](https://github.com/ykdai/Flare7K)) and FlareX is GPL-3.0 ([FlareX](https://github.com/qulishen/FlareX)). None contains anamorphic flares, so the anamorphic streak has to come from procedural or traced optics in any case.

### Depth and matting are mature, permissive and small

Monocular depth is the part of ML that fits Yellowmoon without strain.

| MoGe-2 ViT-S/B/L | MIT ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Ruicheng/moge-2-vitl-normal)) | 35 M / 104 M / 331 M | Metric; aspect ratios from 2:1 to 1:2 |

The best-edged model is out: Apple's Depth Pro scores a boundary F1 of 0.409 against 0.228 for DAv2-Large in its own table, but its weights are "exclusively for Research Purposes" ([Depth Pro paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02073); [HF](https://huggingface.co/apple/DepthPro)), and DAv2-Base/Large/Giant and DA3-Large/Giant are CC-BY-NC. Every model in the table runs natively at 0.15–2.4 MP, so depth has to be upsampled 10–400× by area to reach a 24–60 MP frame, and hair needs a matte, as phones already do: Apple's iOS 13 semantic mattes for hair, skin and teeth are what "get great hair details against the background" ([WWDC 2019](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/260)). Determinism needs care, because Apple measured its F16 Core ML package at an abs-rel error of 0.0089 against FP32 ([Apple](https://huggingface.co/apple/coreml-depth-anything-v2-small)); a fast ANE preview is acceptable, but exports should take depth from one fixed path in a Rust runtime, as Yellowmoon already does for its 1.1 M-parameter crop network on the CPU ([docs/07](../docs/07-ml-models.md)).

### Lens simulation belongs offline, as a data factory

Offline lens simulation turns a prescription into the field- and depth-dependent PSFs, vignetting and distortion that the lens stage needs. It fits the rule that Python produces data and never runs in the app: trace a patent prescription offline, fit compact formulas, commit only the formulas. Anamorphic designs need cylindrical surfaces in the tracer; lentil's database already records "cyl-x" and "cyl-y" geometry ([lenses.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zpelgrims/lentil/dev/polynomial-optics/database/lenses.json)).

## Recommended: a physical lens engine, learned depth, generative models only offline

### Three stages, two of them without ML

The strongest achievable design is one lens stage in scene-linear AWG4, after the sensor map and before the highlight range (C7), the look and the display transform, so that added light stays additive and the clip mask is still at hand.

| Stage | Traits (lens families that motivate it) | Method | ML | Depth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Highlight optics | Glow, veiling glare, starburst, ghost chains, anamorphic streak, smeared front-plate flare (front anamorphics, detuned and vintage glass, effect plates) | Energy-conserving FFT convolution with spectral pupil kernels; ghosts traced from a patent prescription; one-dimensional long-tailed streak; random-phase "dirty" pupil for the smear | None | No |
| 2. Field optics | Detuned edges with a movable sharp area, tangential smear, lateral CA, optical vignetting, mild barrel (detuned anamorphics, vintage spherical) | Field-varying kernels from traced tables | None | No |
| 3. Bokeh reshaping | Taller, anamorphic ovals and a little swirl, made from the bokeh already in the photo (front anamorphics, Petzval and vintage glass) | Blur map from edges, the camera's focus point, EXIF and depth; stretch along one direction in proportion to the existing blur; bright bokeh balls redrawn as ellipses | Depth plus matting | Yes |

Stages 1 and 2 need no ML at all; they are deterministic GPU kernels with a CPU f64 reference, matching the project's kernel contract, and they carry the traits these lens families are known for: streaks and ghosts for the anamorphics, edge falloff and smear for the detuned sets. One architectural point needs design work: the long tails of glow and veiling glare span the whole frame, while Yellowmoon renders in tiles that must match the whole frame bit for bit (rule D5 in [docs/17](../docs/17-gpu.md)). The natural fit, an engineering inference rather than a tested design, is to compute the low-frequency tail once on a reduced whole-frame plane, as the existing reduced path does, and add the sharp cores and streaks per tile.

Stage 3 is where ML earns its place, as perception only: Depth Anything V2-Small or a DA3 Small/Base model, plus ViTMatte-S or BiRefNet for hair, makes the blur map consistent and keeps the stretch off the sharp subject, while the shapes come from the stretch and the ellipses (addendum on reshaping). Photos with little background blur change little, which is intended; the highlight and field stages still give them character. A generative editor stays out of the pipeline; if the bake-off below ever favours it, it belongs behind an explicit, labelled "Generate" action on FLUX.2 [klein] 4B, or Mage-Flow-Edit once its licence is confirmed at the source, composited as an additive linear layer with determinism promised per machine only.

### The data it needs is all available without own measurements

Every input is public or computed, which keeps the plan inside the V1 rule against own measurements, captures and test shoots ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)). Lens characteristics are synthetic: computed offline from public lens designs and stored as fitted formulas, and described as synthetic, never as measurements. Lens designs come from prescriptions Yellowmoon transcribes from expired patents (US2832262, US2701982, GB157040A, US2500046A, US3217597, US6310731), cited per lens, with lentil's MIT database as a cross-check rather than the source. Coatings are assumed single-layer MgF₂ quarter-wave films, tuned by eye and documented as an artistic choice. The camera side uses the Sony spectral curves Yellowmoon already ships plus f-number and focal length from EXIF. Flare validation uses the CC BY 4.0 Wu et al. set to check ghost spacing, tint and haze statistics, with attribution in NOTICE; bokeh validation uses synthetic renders with known depth from Blender; and tuning uses CC0 night, backlit and shallow-focus photos from [docs/test-photo-sources.md](../docs/test-photo-sources.md). Copyrighted footage cannot serve as validation data, so the character is judged in the owner's blind tests.

### What is not achievable from a single still

Replicas of named cinema lenses are impossible without their prescriptions and coating data; the presets reproduce a family's character. Narrowing the round bokeh the Sony lens recorded, or giving it an exact new shape, needs a generative deblurrer, none of which is licence-clean; stretching it into taller ovals does not. Breathing is a focus-pull effect and has no meaning in a still. Bit-identical generative output across Macs and native generative editing at 24–60 MP are not available from any model studied.

### Settle the disagreement with a blind bake-off, then decide

The owner's hypothesis can be tested cheaply before any engineering commitment. If the generative results win clearly, the case for a generative layer rests on evidence rather than taste; if they do not, the physical plan proceeds without doubt. ## Addendum 2026-10-24: reshape the bokeh that is there, do not add new bokeh

**The owner's direction (2026-10-24):** "we might need not to add bokeh at all, just modify existing one to give it character, maybe a bit of swirly, but definitely more anamorphic oval. I think modification is a better route than adding fake bokeh." Stage 3 above is this reshaping stage; nothing adds new bokeh. The owner accepts that a photo without bokeh is left almost unchanged by it, since the highlight and field stages still give it character.

Why it is feasible without a generative deblurrer: anamorphic ovals are taller than they are wide, and swirl is defocus stretched along the tangential direction. Both only need the existing blur to be *extended* along one direction, never narrowed. A round disc of radius r convolved with a short line of length L along an axis becomes an oval of r by r + L/2, so blurring each out-of-focus pixel along one direction by an amount proportional to its own existing blur radius turns round bokeh into ovals and leaves in-focus pixels (radius about 0) untouched. This is anisotropic defocus magnification, a classical technique (Bae and Durand, "Defocus Magnification", Eurographics 2007, which magnifies existing blur in proportion to an estimated blur map; not re-read for this note). Narrowing a disc, or changing its shape exactly, would still need deconvolution and stays out of scope.

What it needs:
- **A map of the blur already in the photo** (radius per pixel). Sources, combined: a classical single-image blur estimate from edges (for example Zhuo and Sim's re-blur gradient ratio, 2011; to be implemented from the paper), the camera's focus point that Yellowmoon already reads from the Sony maker note, f-number and focal length from EXIF, and the depth model (first-use download) to make the map consistent across smooth areas where edge-based estimates have nothing to measure.
- **A subject matte** so the stretch gathers only from the background and never smears the sharp subject's edge into it (the matting model, same download).
- **Bright bokeh balls redrawn as true ellipses**: detected as flat-topped discs of the estimated radius in out-of-focus areas, their energy and colour measured, and each replaced by an ellipse of the same energy (vertical for anamorphic, tangential for swirl). These are the shapes a viewer reads first; the directional stretch handles the rest of the background.
- **Direction field**: vertical everywhere for anamorphic; tangential, growing with distance from the centre, for swirl.

Risks: a wrong blur map stretches in-focus texture (conservative thresholds, strength falls to zero below a minimum radius); overlapping bokeh balls are hard to separate; photos with little background blur get little effect, which is the intended behaviour of a modification.

## Conclusion

The core finding inverts the question. The convincing part of cinema lens character is a physical response to light energy and pupil geometry, and Yellowmoon is unusually well placed to compute it because it keeps scene-linear raw data, knows which photosites clipped and holds spectral camera curves, none of which a JPEG plug-in or a generative editor has. Generative models throw away exactly the information that matters, the energy above the clip, and replace it with plausible pixels at a fraction of the needed resolution. The owner's frustration with Gaussian math is justified, but the cure is the physics that Gaussian math leaves out, not a learned prior.

ML's right role is perception: telling the renderer where the depth edges and the hair are, so that oval and cat's-eye bokeh can be drawn by the same physical kernels. With prescriptions transcribed from expired patents and coatings tuned by eye, Yellowmoon can offer the character of the big lens families (front anamorphic streaks and ovals, detuned edges, vintage glow and swirl) at full resolution and deterministically, while saying plainly that it reproduces a family's character, not a named lens.
