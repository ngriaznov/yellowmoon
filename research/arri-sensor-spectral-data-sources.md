# ARRI's own matrices beat the D-21 curve

Only one ARRI spectral sensitivity dataset is both numeric and permissively licensed: the **ARRI D21** curve in the Academy's rawtoaces-data, tagged **Apache-2.0** since July 2025. It describes a 2008 sensor from before the ALEXA, and ARRI itself keeps that sensor outside its ALEXA colour pipeline. Every public ALEXA-generation curve falls into one of three groups. HdM Stuttgart's ALEXA SXT curve has no license and its own authors flag it as wrongly linearised. ARRI's five-camera mean from CIC 2015 exists only as a plot in a paper. ARRI's internal ALEXA and Mini LF curves were never released. **Nothing exists for the ALEXA 35's ALEV 4 sensor.** Two unreviewed simulations by the research team found that the D21 fits ARRI's published ALEXA matrices worse than most stills cameras: **mean ΔE00 ≈2.8, rank 42 of 52**, against about 1.4 for a Sony A7 III. On that evidence the D21 should not be the ARRI baseline. The best usable target for an Apache-2.0/MIT project is a **matrix-fitted ALEV III estimate**. It combines a spectral prior built from rawtoaces-data's Apache-2.0 curves with ARRI's AMPAS-licensed native-to-ACES matrices at **17 colour temperatures (CCTs)** as constraints. The HdM curve and the CIC 2015 figure serve only as unshipped validation references. Simulations put such an estimate at roughly **5–9% spectral error** and about **0.5–0.7 ΔE00 median on a ColorChecker**. The honest uncertainty band is about **±10%**, and the largest risks sit in narrow-band light and the far-red tail. The estimate represents the classic ALEXA (ALEV III, 2010–2022) and pairs with the Weta ILCE-7RM4 curves already chosen for the A1. The most valuable next step is to download HdM's zip in an ordinary browser and test its ALEXA curve against ARRI's 17 matrices.

## One open ARRI curve exists, and it predates the ALEXA

The only ARRI spectral data that is both numeric and permissively licensed is `data/camera/ARRI_D21_380_780_5.json` in the Academy Software Foundation's rawtoaces-data. It holds 81 samples from 380 to 780 nm at 5 nm, with each channel normalised to a peak of 1.0 (R at 590 nm, G at 540 nm, B at 470 nm). Response is near zero past 720 nm, which indicates an IR-cut filter ([rawtoaces-data D21](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/rawtoaces-data/blob/main/data/camera/ARRI_D21_380_780_5.json)). Its provenance is thin. The table entered AMPAS rawtoaces on 7 November 2016 in a commit titled "more data added", alongside Canon, Nikon and Dalsa curves. There is no record of who measured it, how, or through which lens ([rawtoaces commit 13a523e](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/rawtoaces/commit/13a523ec14787401540a948456dc7be6375dd205)). The header names only "scitech" as the laboratory. That is most likely the AMPAS Science and Technology Council, but no source says so.

The license field stayed `null` until a Weta FX maintainer set it, along with the other legacy files, to **Apache-2.0 on 11 July 2025** ([commit 75ed806](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/rawtoaces-data/commit/75ed806039dffb3f2933a82fdd6d0d0bbd143ad5)). The 2019 Zenodo packaging of the same rawtoaces data carries an older "Other (Attribution)" label ([Zenodo 3372171](https://zenodo.org/records/3372171)). For a permissive project this is defensible footing, provided the project records the provenance chain: AMPAS 2016, then Zenodo 2019, then ASWF Apache-2.0 in 2025.

The curve is very likely a genuine measurement. Christian Mauer's 2009 diploma thesis at FH Köln measured an Arriflex D-21 with 39 interference filters and published a 10 nm table. That table peaks at the same R and G wavelengths (590 and 540 nm) and shows the same violet lobe in the red channel near 410 nm ([Mauer 2009](https://epb.bibl.th-koeln.de/files/166/Mauer_Christian.pdf)). In the research team's own unreviewed comparison the two agree within a few hundredths across most of 400–700 nm and differ mainly in the blue peak (470 vs 460 nm). The thesis is open access but cleared only for use "for scientific purposes", with no Creative Commons grant ([TH Köln record](https://epb.bibl.th-koeln.de/frontdoor/index/index/docId/166)). It can therefore cross-check the D21 but cannot replace it.

Every source closer to the ALEXA fails on license, form or correctness. HdM Stuttgart's Open Film Tools database lists an "Arri Alexa SXT (Zeiss UP40mm/1.9, Nuke/Arri-SDK demosaicing)". It was measured with a diffraction-grating spectroscope plus a chart-based chromaticity correction ([HdM camera responsivities](https://www.hdm-stuttgart.de/open-film-tools/en/camera_responsivities); [Karge et al., CIC 26](https://library.imaging.org/cic/articles/26/1/art00012)). In October 2026 the page still reads "The ARRI ALEXA responsivity is incorrent. We did a wrong linearization with ARRI RAW Converter", with a fix promised in "next days". It states no license, and the only rights text is "© Hochschule der Medien 2023" ([HdM legacy page](https://hdm-stuttgart.de/open-film-tools/english/camera_responsivities/)). The Wayback Machine holds no copy of the zip ([Wayback API](https://archive.org/wayback/available?url=hdm-stuttgart.de/open-film-tools/english/camera_responsivities/camera_responses.zip)). Glenn Butcher's ssf-data repository ships only an ICC profile derived from the curve, because the "license could not be determined" ([ssf-data README](https://github.com/butcherg/ssf-data/blob/master/Arri/Alexa_nuke-arriraw-sdk/openfilmtools/README.md)).

The most authoritative ALEXA curve is Figure 1 of Leonhardt and Brendel's CIC 2015 paper, "Modelled spectral sensitivity functions of the ARRI ALEXA". It averages five cameras measured with a double monochromator from 380 to 780 nm at 5 nm, but it exists only as a plot ([Leonhardt & Brendel](https://community.acescentral.com/uploads/short-url/zQoSiPuY57okAWV5vYUlHWHKs7N.pdf)). HdM's project summary plots "the manufacturer's curve for an ARRI Alexa using a ZEISS UltraPrime" as a reference ([OFT Project Summary](https://www.hdm-stuttgart.de/open-film-tools/en/publications/ProjectSummary.pdf)). An ARRI-co-authored 2022 paper ran its simulations with an "ARRI ALEXA Mini LF mean spectral sensitivity" ([Gudemann et al.](https://www.hdm-stuttgart.de/~froehlichj/CIC2022/Color%20Reproduction%20in%20LED%20Wall%20Virtual%20Production%20Stages_20221010_02.pdf)). So ARRI holds rigorous ALEXA data and shares it with academic partners, but has never released numbers. Colour-science, colour-datasets, the Solomatov–Akkaynak repository and Netflix OpenVPCal all lack ARRI camera curves ([colour-datasets](https://github.com/colour-science/colour-datasets); [Spectral-sensitivity-estimation](https://github.com/COLOR-Lab-Eilat/Spectral-sensitivity-estimation)).

## ARRI treats every ALEV III camera as one spectral target

For most of the camera's history, it is clear which ARRI sensor counts as "the ALEXA". Every ALEXA from the 2010 Classic through the Mini LF and ALEXA 65 uses the ALEV III photosite. The Super 35 bodies use one sensor, the LF and Mini LF use two stitched together (A2X), and the 65 uses three (A3X) ([ARRI ALEV sensors](https://arri.com/en/learn-help/technology/alev-sensors); [Mini LF FAQ](https://arri.com/cn/learn-help/learn-help-camera-system/frequently-asked-questions/alexa-mini-lf-faq)). All of them share LogC3 and AWG3 ([ARRI LogC in VFX](https://www.Arri.com/resource/blob/31918/66f56e6abb6e5b6553929edf9aa7483e/2017-03-alexa-logc-curve-in-vfx-data.pdf)). ARRI published one set of native-to-ACES matrices for "ALEXA", dated November 2013 ([aces-dev ARRI IDTs](https://github.com/ampas/aces-dev/tree/v1.3/transforms/ctl/idt/vendorSupplied/arri/alexa)). At the ALEXA 65 launch ARRI said its colorimetry would "match well with any member of the ALEXA family" without extra correction ([NoFilmSchool](https://nofilmschool.com/2014/09/arri-reveals-6k-65mm-cinema-camera-alexa-65)). Leonhardt and Brendel measured unit-to-unit scatter at about 0.004 in g and 0.007 in r chromaticity ([Leonhardt & Brendel](https://community.acescentral.com/uploads/short-url/zQoSiPuY57okAWV5vYUlHWHKs7N.pdf)). One good ALEV III curve can therefore stand for the whole classic ALEXA look. The one documented exception is telling. The ALEXA Studio's internal ND 1.3 filter got its own matrix set because of "different colorimetry produced as a side effect" of the filter ([aces-dev ARRI IDTs](https://github.com/ampas/aces-dev/tree/v1.3/transforms/ctl/idt/vendorSupplied/arri/alexa)). Changes to the filter stack clearly matter.

The filter stack is part of the look. ARRI designs its IR and UV filters to keep enough near-red for skin tones ([ARRI image processing](https://arri.com/cn/learn-help/technology/image-processing)). In 2010 an ARRI representative explained that the ALEXA avoided a 680 nm cut because "realistic and pleasing flesh tones require some far red", and Art Adams described the hot mirror as cutting "at around 700nm" ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/arri_alexa_and_far_red_a_problem_thats_already_been_solved/)). The D21 curve's red channel has already fallen to about 6% of peak at 680 nm ([rawtoaces-data D21](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/rawtoaces-data/blob/main/data/camera/ARRI_D21_380_780_5.json)). That suggests an earlier cut than the ALEXA's, though the inference rests on one journalist's figure.

The D-21 sits outside this family, even though its sensor came from the same supplier lineage. Cypress designed the D-20/D-21 imager and drew on that experience for the ALEXA sensor ([FierceSensors](https://www.fiercesensors.com/embedded/cypress-s-cmos-image-sensor-designed-into-movie-camera)). ARRI says all its digital cameras use CMOS sensors "jointly developed with onsemi" ([onsemi release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220914005298/en/onsemi-Imaging-Technology-Enables-Next-Era-of-Digital-Cinematography)). No source says the D-21's filter dyes carried over to ALEV III. ARRI's REVEAL processing supports ARRIRAW from all its other digital cameras "except ARRIFLEX D-20/21" ([ARRI REVEAL](https://www.arri.com/reveal)). And aces-dev has never shipped a D-21 input transform ([aces-dev v1.3 ARRI IDTs](https://github.com/ampas/aces-dev/tree/v1.3/transforms/ctl/idt/vendorSupplied/arri/alexa)).

At the other end of the timeline, ALEV 4 in the ALEXA 35 is a new onsemi-developed sensor with a 6.075 µm pixel and a Bayer filter array whose dyes are unpublished ([onsemi release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220914005298/en/onsemi-Imaging-Technology-Enables-Next-Era-of-Digital-Cinematography); [FDTimes](https://www.fdtimes.com/pdfs/free/115FDTimes-June2022-2.04-150.pdf)). ARRI never claims its spectral response matches ALEV III. It claims instead that REVEAL processing makes the two generations "match extraordinarily closely" ([ARRI REVEAL learn page](https://www.arri.com/en/learn-help/learn-help-camera-system/image-science/reveal-color-science)). The ALEXA 265's revised 65 mm sensor is "super color-matched" to the ALEXA 35 in the same way ([ARRI Rental](https://www.arrirental.com/en/about/press/arri-announces-the-small-and-lightweight-alexa-265-camera-revolutionizing-65-mm-cinematography)). ARRI has published no native ALEV 4 matrices, only a fixed AWG4-to-AP0 transform applied after LogC4 decoding ([aces-dev v4 IDT](https://github.com/ampas/aces-dev/tree/v1.3.1/transforms/ctl/idt/vendorSupplied/arri/alexa/v4)). Since REVEAL, the ALEXA look is a target that processing calibrates to, and public data reaches only ALEV III.

## The D-21 misses ALEXA's matrices by twice a Sony's error

The research notes disagree on the D21's role. The dataset inventory recommends it as the ARRI baseline, optionally refined toward ALEXA behaviour by fitting it to ARRI's matrices. That case rests on three facts: the D21 is the only Apache-2.0 ARRI curve, it shares a design lineage with ALEV III, and it is a real measurement. The other two notes tested the D21 numerically against ARRI's ALEXA matrices and recommend against it. The test results below are **the research team's own unreviewed simulations**. They were run in colour-science 0.4.7 on the published D21 curve and ARRI's 2013 matrix file. They are not published results, and the scripts were not preserved.

The first simulation computed a rawtoaces-style least-squares input transform from the D21 curve ([colour.matrix_idt](https://colour.readthedocs.io/en/latest/generated/colour.matrix_idt.html)) and compared it with ARRI's ALEXA matrix ([Alexa_aces_matrix.txt](https://github.com/ampas/aces-dev/blob/v1.3.1/transforms/ctl/idt/vendorSupplied/arri/alexa/AlexaParameters-2013-Nov-13/Alexa_aces_matrix.txt)). The matrix distance (Frobenius norm of the difference) was **0.43 at 5600 K** and 0.38–0.50 across CCTs, with green-into-red mixing of the opposite sign. Sony α curves from the same repository came in at 0.16–0.21. Simulated D21 responses pushed through ARRI's matrix gave a ColorChecker mean of **2.80 ΔE00 (max 5.61) at 5600 K** and **3.73 (max 9.64) at 3200 K**. Ranked against all 52 cameras in rawtoaces-data under the same ALEXA matrix, the D21 came **42nd** (2.77 with a different reflectance set). The Sony A7 III scored 1.38 and the A7R III 1.49. The second simulation fitted curves to all 17 ARRI matrices. The D21 reproduced them with an RMS error of 0.315 per coefficient. The plain mean of 48 measured cameras scored 0.065, and a fitted three-component estimate about 0.01. Refining the D21 until it matched moved it about **10.5% in relative full-scale error (RFSE)** away from its original shape.

Several caveats apply, but none of them rescues the D21. ARRI's matrices include deliberate rendering, such as nudging saturated reds toward blue to favour skin ([Frame.io](https://blog.frame.io/2024/06/10/arri-reveal-color-science-explained/)), so even a true ALEV III curve would leave residuals. That rendering intent hits every camera equally, though, so it cannot explain why the D21 scores twice as badly as a Sony. The thin provenance does not explain the result either. The curve agrees with Mauer's independent measurement, and with its own least-squares matrix the D21 renders the chart at 0.94 ΔE00. The file therefore describes a well-behaved camera that simply differs from the ALEXA. For scale, the D21's distance from the ALEXA matrix is roughly double the shift ARRI's own ND 1.3 filter caused, which was 0.21–0.24 by the same matrix-distance measure, computed from ARRI's two published tables. The tests used only ColorChecker reflectances, so saturated and narrow-band behaviour is untested.

The evidence favours demoting the D21. The lineage argument holds up but says nothing about colour. The numeric tests are the only direct evidence, and both point the same way using different methods. A "refined D21" is just a matrix-fitted estimate that starts from a prior further from the ALEXA than the average camera. The refinement has to remove the D21's character before it can fit ARRI's matrices, so the D21 adds bias without adding ALEXA information. If the simulations hold when reproduced, the D21 belongs in the project only as an optional "ARRI D-21" look and as a test fixture. Pairing the Sony curve with ARRI's matrix and no ARRI curve at all would do better than treating the D21 as "the ARRI sensor".

## ARRI's 17 matrices pin an ALEXA estimate to about ±10%

Solomatov and Akkaynak showed that a camera's spectral sensitivities can be estimated from its published colour matrices alone. Their objective penalises the angle between predicted and published matrices, and a learned prior keeps the curve physically plausible. Leave-one-out tests over 51 measured cameras gave a **median RFSE of about 8% (range 4–17%)**. That is comparable to the 6.96% difference they found between two units of the same camera model ([arXiv 2304.11549](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.11549v2)). They used only Adobe's two-illuminant DNG matrices, but their equations generalise to any number of illuminants. ARRI's 17 native-to-AP0 matrices at 2000–11000 K therefore fit the method directly ([aces-dev ARRI IDTs](https://github.com/ampas/aces-dev/tree/v1.3.1/transforms/ctl/idt/vendorSupplied/arri/alexa)).

Their repository has no LICENSE file ([Spectral-sensitivity-estimation](https://github.com/COLOR-Lab-Eilat/Spectral-sensitivity-estimation)). A permissive project must therefore reimplement the method from the paper and build its prior from rawtoaces-data's 52 Apache-2.0 camera curves ([rawtoaces-data](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/rawtoaces-data)). The AMPAS license on the matrices allows copying and derivative works, provided the notice is kept and no endorsement is implied ([aces-dev LICENSE](https://github.com/ampas/aces-dev/blob/v1.3.1/LICENSE.md)). The research team's reading, which is not legal advice, is that a curve computed from them falls within that grant.

The research team's unreviewed simulations sized what to expect, and the 17 matrices carry far less information than their 153 coefficients suggest. Every row sums to 1, so they preserve white, and they vary with CCT along only about two strong and two weak directions. In a leave-one-camera-out test with a two-component PCA prior, 17 matrices recovered curves at **4.9% median RFSE**. That was no better than two matrices (2900 K and 6500 K), and only modestly better than a 6.5% mean-camera baseline. A three-component prior fitted the matrices more closely but wandered spectrally, reaching 8.5% RFSE: the problem is ill-posed. The most valuable missing constraint is white balance. ARRI's matrices expect pre-balanced input and leave out the gains, and adding per-CCT camera white points cut the three-component error **from 8.5% to 5.1%**. Wrong guesses about ARRI's derivation recipe, such as Planckian versus daylight illuminants or 190 versus 24 training patches, moved results by only 0–2.5%. Accuracy is limited by non-uniqueness more than by the unknown recipe.

Applied to the real ARRI file, a three-component fit matched all 17 matrices to about 0.01 RMS per coefficient. It peaked near R 600, G 510 and B 460 nm. A secondary red lobe near 520–540 nm looks like a fitting artefact. Eight variants of prior and assumptions spread across about 10% RFSE, which is the honest uncertainty band to publish with the curve. Downstream, the simulated emulation error on a ColorChecker was **0.67 ΔE00 median at D65 (p90 1.46)** and 0.51 at 3200 K, against 1.71 for a generic mean camera. Errors will be larger under LED walls, neon and fluorescent light. Depending on the light, ARRI's own authors found 19–29% of test spectra "critical" for the ALEXA: the camera renders them alike while a human sees them as different. They state that no non-linear characterisation can resolve this ([Leonhardt & Brendel](https://community.acescentral.com/uploads/short-url/zQoSiPuY57okAWV5vYUlHWHKs7N.pdf)).

The chosen Sony side has two consequences, both this report's inference. First, the error budget is dominated by the ALEXA side. The Weta ILCE-7RM4 curves are measured, sampled at 5 nm from 380 to 780 nm and normalised to the global peak ([rawtoaces-data](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/rawtoaces-data)). The research team's own comparison of the Weta curves found that recent full-frame Sony bodies differ from each other by about 0.005–0.015 of peak. The ALEXA estimate's 5–10% uncertainty is several times larger. The two metrics differ, so treat the comparison as order-of-magnitude.

Second, the prior fills every direction the matrices cannot see, and that includes the far-red tail behind ALEXA skin tones. The published method samples only 400–700 nm ([Spectral-sensitivity-estimation](https://github.com/COLOR-Lab-Eilat/Spectral-sensitivity-estimation)), and because human colour matching is nearly blind past about 680 nm, the matrices give little leverage there. A prior drawn mostly from Sony, Canon and Nikon stills cameras will pull those directions toward a generic stills camera, and partly toward the 7RM4 itself. That would understate real Sony-to-ALEXA differences. Fitting on the full 380–780 nm grid with priors that include and exclude the Sony bodies turns this risk into a measured spread.

## Measured ALEXA curves serve as unshipped referees

The licensed estimate needs an independent check before anyone calls the result spectral emulation. The unlicensed ALEXA curves suit that job well, provided they stay out of the shipped package.

The HdM SXT file is the only numeric ALEV III measurement, and one simple test decides its value. Compute least-squares matrices from it at ARRI's 17 CCTs, compare them with ARRI's table, and measure the curve's distance from the matrix-fitted estimate. A curve that reproduces ARRI's matrices is probably the corrected version. Residuals that drift strongly with CCT point to the linearisation fault. The measurement also includes a Zeiss Ultra Prime 40 mm in the optical path ([HdM camera responsivities](https://www.hdm-stuttgart.de/open-film-tools/en/camera_responsivities)). If HdM grants a license, the research team's unreviewed simulation suggests that a smooth multiplicative correction fitted to ARRI's matrices can largely undo a linearisation-type error. It cut a simulated 4.1–4.6% RFSE to 1.3–1.4%, and ColorChecker error from about 1.4 to about 0.1 ΔE00. It cannot remove lens or IR differences. Without a license, refining the HdM curve does not cure the missing permission, and German database rights may also apply.

The CIC 2015 figure is the most authoritative ALEXA curve, and digitising it with a tool such as WebPlotDigitizer gives a second check. Under US law the extracted values are probably uncopyrightable facts, even though the figure itself is protected ([University of Idaho copyright guide](https://lib.uidaho.edu/services/data/data-management/guide/copyright)). EU database rights were not researched, which is one more reason to keep the digitised curve as an unshipped validation target. The figure's legibility, axes and normalisation are unknown, because the PDF could not be downloaded during research. The ARRI-supplied curve in the OFT project summary and Jan Fröhlich's 2018 dissertation ([PDF](https://www.hdm-stuttgart.de/~froehlichj/diss/Jan_Froehlich_Thesis_2018_02_15_Acrobat_300dpi.pdf)) may provide more figures.

Two routes could add genuinely new constraints. The first uses ARRI's free Reference Tool, which renders legacy ARRIRAW to AWG3 or AWG4 with CCT and tint overrides ([ARRI Reference Tool](https://arri.com/en/learn-help/learn-help-camera-system/tools/arri-reference-tool)). Re-rendering one sample frame at many CCTs, then regressing the output against raw values on flat regions, could expose the white-balance gains the matrices leave out. It is also the only identified route to any native ALEV 4 constraint. It is untested, though. ART's license agreement was not read, ARRIRAW metadata does not carry the gains ([SMPTE RDD 55](https://pub.smpte.org/latest/rdd55/rdd55-2022.pdf)), and ARRI's sample footage carries no license ([sample footage notes](https://www.Arri.com/resource/blob/31926/29d3a7ccd3a1de9dca8d5283be0c6f28/2023-06-arri-sample-footage-technical-information-data.pdf)). At best it is an offline developer experiment.

The second route is simply to ask. HdM (Karge, Fröhlich, Eberhardt) could release the corrected SXT file under CC BY or CC0. ARRI could be contacted through the arriraw-dev@arri.de address printed on the LogC4 specification ([LogC4 spec](https://www.arri.com/resource/blob/278790/bea879ac0d041a925bed27a096ab3ec2/2022-05-arri-logc4-specification-data.pdf)), asking for numeric ALEV III data or permission to publish a curve validated against it.

## Conclusion

The project's open-source "ALEXA sensor" will be an estimate, which is a better position than it sounds. ARRI's licensed matrices encode exactly the colour step the camera applies. In simulation they constrain a curve to within roughly the unit-to-unit spread found between copies of one camera model. The only licensed ARRI measurement turns out to describe a different colour generation. The project should name its target precisely: an ALEV III spectral model estimated from ARRI's published matrices, with a stated ±10% band. It should neither claim measured ALEXA data nor present the D21 as the ARRI sensor.

Accuracy here is limited by the kind of information available more than by its volume. More matrices add almost nothing. White-balance gains and one independently measured ALEV III curve would add most of the remaining value. Engineering effort is therefore best spent on a validation harness and an ensemble of estimates. The ALEXA 35 stays out of spectral reach until ARRI publishes native ALEV 4 data or the Reference Tool route proves viable. Until then the "REVEAL look" can only be approximated by placing ALEV III colour in a LogC4 container.
