# Give autoset eyes in three layers

*Research of 2026-10-19. The owner then chose a simpler route the same day: `autoset` is parked, Auto on `main` stays the rule, and an optional Apple Auto runs where Apple Intelligence's on-device model is available (`docs/16`, 2026-10-19).*

Yes, Yellowmoon can make `autoset` understand what is in the scene, but no single downloadable model does it, and the clearest failure in the owner's blind A/B needs no model at all. Six of autoset's pictures marked as needing a fix were close-ups made 1.3–2.2 EV brighter than the camera, four of them long-telephoto shots, most likely of a small subject against a distant background. The ARW carries the camera's focus point in five of the six (Sony's FocusLocation tag, present in 73 % of local close-ups), and with a focus map computed from the raw it locates the subject, so metering that subject and capping the lift before its highlights reach the shoulder is deterministic arithmetic that also runs in the Linux CLI. For meaning (faces, sunsets, rooms, backlight) the only models usable today are Apple's, at runtime. Vision's classifier, saliency, subject masks and face detector are on every Mac that runs macOS 14. On macOS 27 with Apple Intelligence, the Foundation Models framework takes the photo and answers in a typed, range-checked structure: in effect the "heavily quantized small VLM" the owner asked for (Apple's 2025 on-device model stored its decoder at 2 bits per weight), already installed, with nothing to ship. These become an optional layer on top of `autoset`: a Preferences switch, on by default on Apple-silicon Macs that have the models, that turns quantised scene facts into bounded offsets through a table the owner sets by hand. Plain `autoset` runs everywhere else. The other three families fail Yellowmoon's rules before scene skill even matters. Capable open encoders are web-trained, so under the owner's 2026-10-18 decision they can only teach. The Qwen-Image Natural-Exposure LoRA paints pixels under a non-commercial licence. A bundled quantized Qwen would add about 0.74 GB, answer lighting questions correctly less than half the time and break app–CLI parity. Across platforms, SceneNet, the owner's own encoder, remains the route, and it has become cheaper: Google's CC BY machine labels on Open Images already tag 66k sunsets, 16k backlit scenes, 113k close-ups and 218k rooms. One caution shapes the order of work: the indoor and sunset-or-backlit losses (14 and 15 photos) sit inside 95 % intervals of roughly 21–70 %, so only the close-up failure is established, and indoor, which lost on look and white rather than exposure, needs diagnosing before any model is aimed at it.

## The blind A/B proves one failure and only hints at two

The final blind A/B (207 photos, 414 trials, answered on 2026-10-19) passed against the old rule, 63.3 % with a Wilson lower bound of 58.0 %. It failed against the camera JPEG on all three counts: **58.0 % pooled with a Wilson lower bound of 51.4 %, indoor at 42.9 %, and 6.8 % of autoset's pictures marked as needing a fix against the camera's 3.4 %** ([STATUS](../docs/STATUS.md)). Two patterns in those answers frame any scene-aware change. On the 100 held-out Play Raw photos autoset beat the camera 68.5 %. And brightness is mostly what the owner rewarded: **where the camera floor held autoset at the camera's exposure the comparison was even (50.5 %); where autoset was at least 0.5 EV brighter it won 72 %** (owner constraints (working notes, not kept)). A logistic fit of the first blind test's answers pointed the same way: against the camera, autoset was chosen more often the brighter and the warmer it was (P > 0 of 0.97 and 0.94), and the people it lost were darker or warmer than the camera's ([STATUS](../docs/STATUS.md)). A scene-aware correction that pulls exposure down must therefore fire narrowly, or it gives back the wins it exists to protect.

**The close-up failure is large, consistent and mechanical.** The six close-ups marked as needing a fix sat **+1.27 to +2.19 EV** above the camera's exposure. Four were shot at 210–500 mm and f/6.3–f/8, most likely birds or animals against a distant background, and FocusLocation was present in five of them, near the frame centre (close-up focus tags (working notes, not kept)). On four of them Auto contrast also rose by +2.3 to +2.7 ([STATUS](../docs/STATUS.md)). The mechanism is the textbook one. A small, correctly lit subject on a large dark or defocused field pulls the global brightness quantiles down, so a model reading only those quantiles lifts the whole frame and burns the subject. The camera metered these frames darker and the owner preferred that. Sony offers a metering mode for exactly this case: Highlight metering, "suitable for shooting the subject while avoiding overexposure", alongside spot metering linked to the focus point and face priority in multi-metering ([Sony Help Guide](https://helpguide.sony.net/ilc/2450/v1/en/contents/0406_metering_mode.html)). The only recent controlled study of subject-weighted against global metering agrees. Tedla, Yang and Brown found **a crude saliency-weighted histogram preferred at 0.71 against 0.23 for a global mean-to-key**, no significant difference "under relatively standard lighting", and a failure where the viewer wanted the background ([Tedla 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.04542)). A 1.3–2.2 EV error is also far outside the 0.2–0.3 EV by which expert retouchers disagree with each other ([Yang 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02269)), so this is a real error, not a matter of taste.

**The indoor and sunset losses are hypotheses, not findings.** Thirteen of the 14 indoor photos are CC0 files that autoset rendered at the camera's own exposure, so indoor lost on look and white, not exposure ([STATUS](../docs/STATUS.md)). The camera JPEGs of 12 of the 14 indoor files and 9 of the 15 sunset files also carry Sony's DRO local tone mapping (a local exiftool count, scene-cue notes (working notes, not kept)), which no global setting reproduces. Sunset or backlit, at 46.7 %, probably averages two opposite intents: raise a backlit subject, or hold a sunset and its silhouettes dark and warm. By my arithmetic from the published shares, **the 95 % Wilson intervals are about 21–67 % for indoor and 25–70 % for sunset or backlit**, so neither stratum is distinguishable from an even split. They justify a diagnosis, not a model aimed at them.

The test photos are now spent. Findings drawn from them need new photos to confirm ([STATUS](../docs/STATUS.md)). About 210 of the 219 Play Raw topics with an ARW have already gone into the two blind tests (110 in the first, 100 in the final; [STATUS](../docs/STATUS.md)). The owner's own photos are allowed as test data, and the blind tests accept "any photos, yours or public ones, since nothing of the test is committed" ([STATUS](../docs/STATUS.md), [test-photo sources](../docs/test-photo-sources.md)).

| Failure in the A/B | What `autoset` lacked | Cue that addresses it | Where the cue can come from | Expected effect |

| Close-ups 1.3–2.2 EV too bright (6 fixes; 4 at 210–500 mm) | where the subject is, and how bright its highlights already are | subject region plus a subject-highlight cap | Sony FocusLocation and FocusFrameSize, a raw focus map and classical saliency, on every platform; Vision foreground mask and attention saliency on Macs | camera comparison 58.0 % → **59.4 % if the six (taken as losses) become ties, 60.9 % if wins** (arithmetic on photos already seen) |

## Four of five model families fail before scene skill even matters

Every candidate faces four tests drawn from the project's rules. The first is **licence**: shipped weights only from CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, Apache or our own data ([docs/14](../docs/14-licensing.md)), plus the owner's 2026-10-18 decision that scene understanding comes from "our own encoder trained on CC BY images with labels from Apache-2.0 vision-language models run offline" ([docs/16](../docs/16-risks-and-decisions.md)). The second is **determinism and app–CLI parity**: same input, settings and version give the same bytes, and the app and `ym` agree (rules 6 and 8 in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)). The third is **size and speed**: model weights are "a few MB in total", anything beyond about 30 MB moves to a first-run download ([docs/12](../docs/12-distribution.md)), and autoset's features have a 40 ms budget (docs/18 (`docs/18` on the parked branch `claude/autoset`)). Only then comes **scene skill** on the questions that matter.

### Licence-clean models are too narrow, but licence-clean data is abundant

Three families of genuinely clean models exist, and none fits the task. **SekoKuva Mobile 423K** has Apache-2.0 code and weights trained from random initialisation on Open Images V7 boxes: 423,020 parameters, 1.6 MB fp32, ONNX included. It reaches 67.9 % top-1 on 65 *object* classes and has no scene classes ([card](https://huggingface.co/bcbertenex/sekokuva-mobile-423k)). Its 9.9M-parameter sibling was trained by SimCLR on 7.2M CommonCatalog CC BY images and reports only 40.7 % k-NN accuracy on a house benchmark, and its card warns it "is not a zero-shot image classifier" ([card](https://huggingface.co/bcbertenex/sekokuva-mobile-10m)). **Mitsua's CLIP** is trained from scratch on open and public-domain images, but its weights are CC BY-SA, part of its data is a Mitsua-owned CC BY-NC set, and the checkpoint is 885 MB ([card](https://huggingface.co/Mitsua/mitsua-japanese-clip-vit-b-16)). The cleanest face detector found is a 2018 TensorFlow 1 SSD trained on Open Images faces, with mAP 73 at 20 ms, whose initialisation could not be verified ([TF1 zoo](https://github.com/tensorflow/models/blob/master/research/object_detection/g3doc/tf1_detection_zoo.md)). One earlier choice falls here. The MIT-licensed YuNet face detector proposed for `autoset`'s face input is trained on WIDER FACE, which its Hugging Face card lists as **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0** ([WIDER FACE card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/CUHK-CSE/wider_face), [YuNet](https://github.com/opencv/opencv_zoo/tree/main/models/face_detection_yunet)). BlazeFace has Apache-2.0 weights trained on Google's own consented smartphone images, which are not released and are not one of rule 5's listed sources ([model card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/MediaPipe%20BlazeFace%20Model%20Card%20(Short%20Range).pdf)).

The data side is much better. Open Images V7's annotations are CC BY 4.0, and its images are "listed as having a CC BY 2.0 license" ([Open Images V7](https://storage.googleapis.com/openimages/web/factsfigures_v7.html)). Every one of the 41,620 validation rows and 1,743,042 train "boxable" rows checked carries the CC BY 2.0 URL, with author and source fields enough for an attribution manifest ([train metadata](https://storage.googleapis.com/openimages/2018_04/train/train-images-boxable-with-rotation.csv)). More useful still, **Google's 164.8M machine-generated image-level labels are part of those CC BY 4.0 annotations**, and at confidence ≥ 0.7 they already cover autoset's scene questions at scale: **Sunset 65,787 images, Backlighting 16,312, Silhouette 11,633, Night 169,255, Close-up 113,072, Room 217,673, Restaurant 33,219, Stage 46,743, Snow 104,371, Beach 75,382, Food 275,693 and Human face 469,492** ([machine labels CSV](https://storage.googleapis.com/openimages/v7/oidv7-train-annotations-machine-imagelabels.csv), counted in the licence notes (working notes, not kept)). The human-verified labels are object-centric: Sunset has 144 positives, while Night, Backlighting and Close-up have none ([human labels CSV](https://storage.googleapis.com/openimages/v7/oidv7-train-annotations-human-imagelabels.csv)). So the machine labels supply the scale, and their precision per class is unpublished and has to be measured. COCO helps little: only 26.1 % of train2017 (30,836 images) carries a clean licence ([COCO annotations](http://images.cocodataset.org/annotations/annotations_trainval2017.zip)). Creative Commons' own guidance says BY and SA obligations trigger only on sharing, while NC applies to every use ([CC guidance](https://creativecommons.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Using-CC-licensed-Works-for-AI-Training.pdf)), and under rule 5 a pool of CC BY images keeps SceneNet's weights Apache-2.0, while any CC BY-SA image makes them CC BY-SA 4.0 ([docs/14](../docs/14-licensing.md)).

### Web-trained encoders make good teachers, which is all the owner's decision allows

The one important new fact here is Meta's **Perception Encoder**. It releases Apache-2.0 checkpoints down to a T/16 of about 6M image parameters (62.1 % zero-shot ImageNet) and an S/16 of about 22M (72.7 %). That is the first sub-25M CLIP with a clean weight licence and real zero-shot skill ([PE README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/perception_models/blob/main/apps/pe/README.md)). SigLIP 2 remains the strongest Apache encoder at B size (78.2 % at B/16), trained on Google's internal WebLI of "10 billion images" ([SigLIP 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14786)). The MIT CSAIL **Places365 CNNs** are an odd case. Their weights "can be used under the Creative Common License (Attribution CC BY)" and their unified script predicts 365 scene types, indoor or outdoor and SUN attributes such as indoor lighting, exactly the labels autoset lacks. Yet "the copyright of all the images belongs to the image owners" ([Places365](https://github.com/CSAILVision/places365)). Rule 5 governs training data, so all of these fail it as shipped weights. The owner has already chosen an encoder of our own over a frozen backbone ([docs/16](../docs/16-risks-and-decisions.md)), and nothing found here argues for reopening that: a frozen PE-Core-S would add about 44 MB in f16 and could never be bit-identical between x86 CI and Apple silicon. Across 75 platforms one model gave "up to 26 different softmax vectors in the last layer" for one input ([Schlögl et al.](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/af076c3bdbf935b81d808e37c5ede463-Abstract-Conference.html)). Apple's own open models, MobileCLIP2 and FastVLM, are excluded even as teachers by their research-only licence, which bars "product development" ([AMLR licence](https://huggingface.co/apple/DFN2B-CLIP-ViT-B-16/blob/main/LICENSE)). As offline teachers, PE-Core-L, SigLIP 2 So400m and Places365-R50 complement the VLMs where the VLMs are weakest, on lighting attributes (see the quantized-VLM subsection).

### The Natural-Exposure LoRA paints pixels under a research licence

The model the owner asked about, prithivMLmods/Qwen-Image-2.1-Natural-Exposure-LoRA, is a rank-16, 84 MB image-to-image LoRA for the diffusion model Qwen-Image-2.1. Its trigger prompt is "Transform the image with balanced neutral exposure". It was trained on **38 pairs** of manually adjusted images, carries the status "Preview / Experimental", and lists high-contrast scenes, complex lighting and strong colour casts among its limitations ([model API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/prithivMLmods/Qwen-Image-2.1-Natural-Exposure-LoRA)). It outputs a regenerated picture, not settings, and its base model is **33.1 GB** under the Qwen Research Licence: "FOR NON-COMMERCIAL PURPOSES ONLY", with "Non-Commercial" defined as "research or evaluation purposes only" ([licence](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1-PE-I2I/raw/main/LICENSE)). Settings could be pulled out offline by inverse fitting: render at defaults, let the editor correct, then solve for the EV, white and contrast whose Yellowmoon render matches the edit's global statistics. The taste, though, would be one person's 38 pairs aimed at "balanced neutral exposure", the flat, generic look the owner has steered away from, and the owner's own blind answers are a better target. The earlier Qwen-Image and Qwen-Image-Edit releases are Apache-2.0 but equally web-trained pixel editors (generative notes (working notes, not kept)). The verdict is evaluation only, at most.

### A quantized Qwen answers the wrong questions, slowly, and differently on every backend

"Ask Qwen-Image for the parameters" cannot work literally. Qwen-Image's condition encoder is a frozen Qwen2.5-VL-7B (8.29B parameters, 16.6 GB), and its "understanding" tasks come out as images ([model_index](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image/blob/main/model_index.json), [technical report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02324)). Even at 4-bit that encoder needs about 5.5 GB ([GGUF](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct-GGUF)). The realistic version is a small Qwen. **Qwen3.5-0.8B** (Apache-2.0, February 2026) has a ViT-B-sized vision tower and costs 533 MB at Q4_K_M plus a 205 MB projector ([card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B), [GGUF](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.5-0.8B-GGUF)). SmolVLM2-256M fits in about 0.24 GB ([GGUF](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/SmolVLM2-256M-Video-Instruct-GGUF)). Measured speeds exist only for Max-class chips. On an M1 Max a 0.5B VLM with 729 visual tokens takes **535 ms** to its first token, and Apple's FastVLM-0.5B 166 ms ([FastVLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13303)). On an M4 Max, Qwen3-VL-4B at 4-bit takes **1.2 s** per cold request at 448 px ([vllm-mlx](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19139)). Extrapolated to 8 GB base chips, that is about 0.3–1 s per photo, which turns a 500-photo import into minutes, and nothing may be cached between sessions.

The answers would also be poor. VLMs read numbers worst: Q-Align found accuracy "significantly lower" when models are asked for numeric scores than when scores are derived from level tokens ([Q-Align](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17090)). The one 2026 study that tuned a VLM for white balance needed LoRA training, discrete red/green/blue cast answers and about 11 passes per image to tie a small CNN. **Asking the same 7B model once for a number more than doubled its error, 3.59° against 1.52°** ([VLM-CC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19613)). On the attribute that matters for backlit scenes, every open VLM from 2B to 8B scores **44–47 % on ShotBench's "lighting condition"** ([ShotBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21356)), and on telling single exposure settings apart "most models perform close to random guessing" ([CameraBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10090)). Parity fails too. llama.cpp's CUDA flash attention shifts Qwen3.5 image embeddings by about 3.5 % and "can change greedy output" ([llama.cpp #29629](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/29629)). MLX gives a float32 matmul error of 5.2e-2 on M5 against 3.8e-5 on M4, which its maintainers call expected ([MLX #3534](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx/issues/3534)). The Linux CLI would have to run the same model on the CPU at seconds per photo and still disagree with Metal. Shipping it would also put web-trained weights in the app, against the 2026-10-18 decision, and push the bundle far past the 30 MB first-run threshold. Small Qwens belong offline, as **teachers** for SceneNet, read through the log-probabilities of a fixed answer list rather than free text. On Macs with Apple Intelligence, the owner's wish (a heavily quantized on-device VLM that looks at the photo and answers in a fixed format) is met by Apple's own model, without any of these costs.

### Apple's built-in models are the only ones usable at runtime today

Apple ships three relevant pieces with macOS, and the owner's decision settles how they may be used: at runtime, on the user's Mac, never to train, label for or improve a Yellowmoon model (owner constraints (working notes, not kept)). The developer agreements' clause against using "output generated from an Apple model to train, fine-tune, or improve another artificial intelligence model" ([Xcode and Apple SDKs Agreement](https://www.apple.com/legal/sla/docs/xcode.pdf)) is therefore no obstacle to runtime use. Calling documented APIs to choose processing settings is ordinary use. The clause still rules out the teacher route, and it means no fitted model may consume Apple outputs at any stage.

**Vision** offers the broadest, cheapest signals. Its classifier returns per-class confidences over a fixed **1,303-identifier taxonomy**, and Revision 2 (macOS 14) improved accuracy and latency on the same labels ([objc2-vision header](https://docs.rs/objc2-vision/0.3.2/objc2_vision/static.VNClassifyImageRequestRevision2.html)). The list holds `sunset_sunrise`, `sun`, `sky`, `snow`, `beach`, `night_sky`, `interior_room`, `kitchen`, `bedroom`, `living_room`, `restaurant`, `bar`, `concert`, `candle`, `lamp`, `people`, `bird` and `animal`. It has no `indoor`, `portrait`, `close-up`, `macro`, `backlit`, `silhouette` or `dusk` ([label list](https://gist.github.com/ktustanowski/56c0d7541813868fed4aceb60ab5d149)). The scores are per-class operating points, not a softmax, and "are meant to be compared within the same class, not across different classes" ([WWDC19](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/222/)). Attention saliency, "generated by where people looked", returns a 64×64 heat map with a bounding box ([saliency observation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnsaliencyimageobservation)). The foreground instance mask (macOS 14) separates "noticable objects" from the background ([docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vngenerateforegroundinstancemaskrequest)). Face rectangles come with a capture quality that rewards "better lit, sharper" faces ([docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vndetectfacecapturequalityrequest)). The network behind Apple's scene analysis has 16M parameters and runs all its heads "in under 9.7 milliseconds" on an iPhone 13 Neural Engine ([Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/on-device-scene-analysis)). No Mac figure is published, so tens of milliseconds per request is an estimate to measure.

**Foundation Models** is the on-device language model of Apple Intelligence. Image prompts (`Attachment` with a `CGImage`) arrived only in **macOS 27** ([Attachment](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/attachment)). Guided generation forces the answer into a `@Generable` Swift type with enumerated choices and numeric ranges through constrained sampling ([guided generation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generating-swift-data-structures-with-guided-generation)), and `.greedy` sampling "always produces the same output for a given input" ([greedy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generationoptions/samplingmode-swift.struct/greedy)). The 2025 on-device model has about 3B parameters with **decoder weights at 2 bits per weight** and a 300M-parameter vision encoder ([Apple 2025 update](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/apple-foundation-models-2025-updates)), so it is, literally, a heavily quantized small VLM. It runs only on Macs with **M1 or later**, with Apple Intelligence on, the device and Siri language set to one of 16 supported languages (Ukrainian and Russian are not among them), and up to 8–14 GB of model assets downloaded ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/121115)). Apple's only speed figures are for phones: about 0.6 ms per prompt token to the first token and 30 tokens per second on an iPhone 15 Pro ([Apple 2024](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-apple-foundation-models)). That puts a structured answer about a photo at roughly **1–4 s** on a Mac, an estimate. Apple publishes no evidence that the model judges exposure well; its image evaluations are side-by-side preferences on general understanding ([AFM 3](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-third-generation-of-apple-foundation-models)).

**Core Image's** `autoAdjustmentFilters` runs on every Mac, Intel included, analysing "histogram, face region contents, and metadata properties" ([guide](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/GraphicsImaging/Conceptual/CoreImaging/ci_autoadjustment/ci_autoadjustmentSAVE.html)). It returns a tone curve, shadow and highlight adjustment, vibrance, face balance and red-eye correction, and **no exposure or white-balance filter** ([API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coreimage/ciimage/autoadjustmentfilters(options:))). It encodes a generic display-referred taste and says nothing about what is in the scene, so it stays out of the first Apple layer.

## The camera already recorded the subject autoset ignored

The fix for the established failure is the cheapest item in this report, and it belongs in `autoset` itself, on every platform. Sony bodies since about 2015 write **FocusLocation** (maker-note tag 0x2027) into the ARW, as `W H X Y` image coordinates of "the location in the image where the camera focused", or the frame centre when that cannot be obtained. Newer bodies add **FocusFrameSize** (0x2037), the AF box around it. The AF-area mode records **Animal Eye Tracking** and **Human Eye Tracking** on recent bodies ([exiftool Sony.pm](https://github.com/exiftool/exiftool/blob/master/lib/Image/ExifTool/Sony.pm)). Across the 434 local real ARWs, FocusLocation is present in **69 %** of files and **73 % of close-ups (36 of 49)**, off-centre in 26 of those 36, with FocusFrameSize valid in 137 files from 2021-on bodies. It is missing on the A7, A7 II, A6000 generation, NEX and SLT bodies (scene-cue notes (working notes, not kept)). This is the camera's own scene understanding, the same information Sony's spot-to-focus-point link ([Sony Help Guide](https://helpguide.sony.net/ilc/2450/v1/en/contents/0406_metering_mode.html)) and Canon's "Detect priority AE" ([Canon community](https://community.usa.canon.com/t5/EOS-DSLR-Mirrorless-Cameras/R5-Mk2-detect-Priority-AE-with-AF/m-p/581336)) use to meter the subject. `autoset` reads none of it today; its 39 inputs are brightness quantiles and shares, clip statistics, `illum`'s light, Bv, ISO and flash (docs/18 (`docs/18` on the parked branch `claude/autoset`)).

A subject region needs a second, physical cue, because FocusLocation sat near the centre in all five flagged files and exactly at the centre in two, one of them manually focused (close-up focus tags (working notes, not kept)). The right cue for a sharp bird on a smooth background at 500 mm is **focus**: "salient regions are often photographed in focus" ([Jiang et al. 2013](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2013/html/Jiang_Salient_Region_Detection_2013_ICCV_paper.html)). A raw processor can measure it better than an sRGB tool. Yellowmoon already estimates the noise sigma of each raw, so band-pass energy on the green channel against that noise floor separates "sharp" from "smooth but in focus". EXIF focal length and aperture tell when depth of field is shallow enough to trust the map. Where neither tag nor focus map helps, the Minimum Barrier saliency detector runs at **77 frames per second on one thread** ([Zhang et al. 2015](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2015/papers/Zhang_Minimum_Barrier_Salient_ICCV_2015_paper.pdf)), and it is the detector Tedla's preferred method used. On benchmarks it scores a much weaker Fβ of 0.511 against 0.714 for 2017 networks ([DUTS paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Wang_Learning_to_Detect_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf)), but global exposure needs a subject *weight*, not a precise boundary.

The proposed rule is a guard like the existing highlight guard. Seed the subject at FocusLocation, sized by FocusFrameSize or a fixed share of the frame. Refine it with the focus map, and treat a centred location with manual focus, or a missing tag, as unknown. Compute the subject's p50 and p98 against the frame's. Then **cap the exposure so that the subject's p98 stays below the display shoulder, and never go below the camera floor**: `EV = guard(max(L, min(EV_model, EV_subject_cap)))`. Because brighter pictures won 72 % of the time, the cap must bind only when subject and frame disagree strongly, a bright subject on a dark field. That is also the only case where Tedla saw subject and global metering diverge. The arithmetic on the seen photos is modest but real: the six fixes are 2.9 % of 207 photos, so, taking them as losses, turning them into ties moves the camera comparison from 58.0 % to **59.4 %**, and into wins to **60.9 %**. That is an upper bound from photos already seen, which only fresh photos can confirm. It is also exactly option (c) of the open question on shipping `autoset` v1 ("tune, for example the over-bright close-ups, and test again on new photos", [STATUS](../docs/STATUS.md) question 31). Everything in this layer is f64 arithmetic on data already in the raw file, so the Linux `ym`, the golden tests and app–CLI parity are untouched apart from a re-baseline.

Two more cheap cues go into the same layer, first as diagnostics. An **indoor signal** from Bv (local indoor median 2.4 against 5.8–6.9 for other strata), ISO, noise and `illum`'s CCT matches ISP practice, where an indoor/outdoor decision driven by scene brightness switches the white-balance logic ([HiAscend ISP guide](https://www.hiascend.com/document/detail/en/canncommercial/800/apiref/appdevgapi/ispdevug_0229.html)). Brightness alone misreads deep shade as indoor ([USPTO 7912279](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7912279)), and a daylight CCT is the veto. Context of this kind is worth having for white balance. A 5k-parameter model, Time-Aware AWB, cut mean angular error by **7 %** with capture metadata and noise statistics and by **14 %** with time of day added ([Time-Aware AWB](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05623v2)), and a **539-parameter** mapping learns a preferred white from a neutral one ([Zhao, Afifi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01342v1)). A **backlight-or-sunset decision** combines subject brightness at FocusLocation against its surround, a bright top region with sky-like or warm chromaticity, a bimodal luminance histogram (the patent literature's backlight signature, [US8805063](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8805063)) and `illum`'s low CCT. None of these should move a setting until the indoor diagnosis and fresh sunset photos show which way the owner wants each case to go.

## Apple's models join autoset as an optional, on-by-default layer

The owner's decisions fix the frame. The Apple layer is runtime-only, an add-on on top of `autoset` and never a replacement, offered as a Preferences setting only where the models are available, **on by default** there, with plain `autoset` on Linux, in CI golden tests, on older macOS and wherever the models are missing. Results that vary with macOS version or chip are accepted (owner constraints (working notes, not kept)). What remains to design is which models, what they may change, how the CLI follows, and how the default is earned.

### Two tiers, detected at run time on every launch and batch

| Tier | Models | Needs | Detection (all cheap property reads) | Per-photo cost | Falls back to plain `autoset` when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A: Vision** | classifier (revision 2), attention saliency, foreground instance mask, face rectangles and capture quality | macOS 14 or later on Apple silicon (Yellowmoon's floor is macOS 14, [docs/00](../docs/00-overview.md)) | `supportedRevisions` contains revision 2; the Neural Engine is among `supportedComputeStageDevices` | tens of ms (estimate) | the revision is unsupported, a request throws, or results are empty |
| **B: Foundation Models** | Apple's on-device model with an image attachment and a `@Generable` answer | macOS 27, M1 or later, Apple Intelligence on, supported language, model downloaded | `SystemLanguageModel.default.availability == .available`; `capabilities.contains(.vision)`; `supportsLocale` | about 1–4 s (estimate) | any check fails, or a call throws `rateLimited`, `guardrailViolation`, `refusal`, `timeout` or `assetsUnavailable` |

Availability can change while the app runs: a user switches Apple Intelligence on, or the model finishes downloading. So detection repeats at launch, when Preferences opens and before each batch ([SystemLanguageModel](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/systemlanguagemodel), [UnavailableReason](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/systemlanguagemodel/availability-swift.enum/unavailablereason)). Tier A supplies the faces, room and sunset labels and subject masks behind every row of the failure table. It reaches every Apple-silicon Mac Yellowmoon supports and costs milliseconds, so it should come first. Tier B adds what Vision's taxonomy lacks: a categorical reading of backlit portrait against silhouette, a small subject on a dark ground, or tungsten interior against shade. It should follow only after it beats tier A alone in its own blind test, because its cost raises a harder problem (below).

The integration is a macOS-only crate, compiled out on Linux. Tier A goes through `objc2-vision` 0.3.2 (Zlib OR Apache-2.0 OR MIT), which binds every request above, explicit revisions and compute devices ([crates.io](https://crates.io/crates/objc2-vision)). Foundation Models is Swift-only. The third-party `foundation-models` crate (MIT OR Apache-2.0) lists no image attachments ([crates.io](https://crates.io/crates/foundation-models)), so tier B needs a small Swift package with a C ABI built by `cargo`. It must construct only `SystemLanguageModel` sessions: `PrivateCloudComputeLanguageModel` runs on Apple's servers ([docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/privatecloudcomputelanguagemodel)) and would break rule 7. Runtime use falls under Apple's Acceptable Use Requirements, which list nothing close to exposure analysis of the user's own photo ([AUR](https://developer.apple.com/apple-intelligence/acceptable-use-requirements-for-the-foundation-models-framework/)). Whether an ad-hoc-signed app (Yellowmoon has no Developer ID, [docs/12](../docs/12-distribution.md)) can use Foundation Models without an entitlement is undocumented, and it is the first thing to check on a Mac.

### A hand-set table turns quantised scene facts into bounded offsets

Two rules govern how Apple outputs reach the picture. First, **no fitted model ever sees them**. `autoset` and SceneNet are trained without them, and the mapping is a small table whose constants the owner chooses by eye, never a regression on Apple outputs. Second, **the model never sets a number**. Tier B is asked for scene facts from fixed lists (scene type, whether the subject is darker than ideal, whether the background should stay dark, the light source, a confidence), read greedily, because VLMs answer categories far better than numbers ([Q-Align](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17090), [VLM-CC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19613)). Apple's multimodal guide recommends exactly this pattern, "Use the Generable protocol to constrain responses … or classify images", with greedy sampling to "always pick the most likely option" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/analyzing-images-with-multimodal-prompting)). The table below shows the shape. Its thresholds are illustrative starting points for the owner, not measured values.

| Scene fact (quantised) | Read from | Action on top of `autoset` | Bound |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small subject on a dark ground (mask or saliency box under about 15 % of the frame, subject key well above the frame's) | Vision foreground mask or attention saliency; tier B "small subject" | the same subject-highlight cap as the core, with Vision's mask where FocusLocation is missing or centred | never below the camera floor |
| Backlit face (face area over about 2 %, face key at least 1 EV below the frame's) | Vision face rectangles; tier B "backlit portrait" | raise exposure toward the face's key | at most +0.5 EV; the highlight guard still decides the sky |
| People present | Vision faces or person segmentation | less preferred-white warmth on that photo (the owner's deferred skin wish) | one step of `white_preference` |
| Sunset or sun, no face | `sunset_sunrise` or `sun` at a fixed precision operating point; tier B "silhouette" | hold exposure within +0.3 EV of the camera floor and keep the warmth | — |
| Interior with low Bv | Vision room labels, `restaurant`, `bar`, `candle`, `lamp`; tier B "tungsten interior" | one white or contrast step, chosen after the indoor diagnosis | — |

The combined offset stays within ±0.7 EV on a 0.1 EV grid. It is applied to the model's exposure before the camera floor and the highlight guard, both of which the owner set and which stay last. Burst frames take their shared median as today. Each `autoset` version gets its own table version, so that SceneNet and the Apple layer never correct the same cue twice once SceneNet ships.

### Determinism holds per Mac, and `ym` follows the same switch

Vision promises no bit-identical results. A pinned revision holds only among the revisions the installed system supports, and the default "uses the latest" revision of whatever SDK the app was built against ([revision semantics](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnclassifyimagerequestrevision1)). Apple says it "will remove CPU and GPU support for some requests on devices with a Neural Engine" ([WWDC24](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10163/)), and Core ML's precision "varies based on the hardware and software versions" ([coremltools](https://apple.github.io/coremltools/docs-guides/source/typed-execution.html)). Foundation Models has already shipped three model versions (26.0–26.3, 26.4 and 27.0), and "the older model is only included as part of the beta program" ([updating prompts](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/updating-prompts-for-new-model-versions)). The owner accepted all of this, so the design treats **the macOS build, the Vision revision, the model variant and the prompt version as part of "version"** under rule 8, and makes the variation small and explainable. Classifier scores become booleans at fixed precision operating points ([VNClassificationObservation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnclassificationobservation)). Mask areas are rounded to 1/64 and face keys to 0.1 EV, and every `GenerationOptions` field is set explicitly, because unset ones may change "across OS or model updates" ([GenerationOptions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generationoptions)). Coarse steps reduce flips at thresholds but cannot remove them. Every export records in XMP whether the layer ran and with which revision, variant, prompt and macOS build, so two different exports of one file can always be explained.

App–CLI parity then holds per platform, as the owner framed it. `ym` on a Mac reads the same preference as the app, so by default it makes the app's picture, and on Linux it makes plain `autoset`. Golden tests and the parity tests pass the switch off explicitly and keep their single baseline. The Apple layer gets its own Mac-only tests of invariants rather than bytes: outputs inside the schema, offsets inside their bounds, fallback on every error, and identical results on repeated runs on one machine. Tier A's tests should be able to run on the Apple-silicon CI runners that already build the app ([docs/16](../docs/16-risks-and-decisions.md)), which is not yet tried. Tier B's need a Mac with Apple Intelligence on, which puts them on the owner's list.

Tier B adds a problem tier A does not have: **its fallback depends on timing**. A background app can be rate-limited ([rateLimited](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/languagemodelsession/generationerror/ratelimited(_:))), and an export that silently falls back on a timeout would differ from the same photo viewed a minute earlier. At 1–4 s per photo, a 500-photo export that runs tier B everywhere would also add about 8–33 minutes. Showing tier B's result only while viewing would break the rule that the app and `ym` export the same bytes. The workable policy is to **ask tier B only about photos that the deterministic cues and tier A leave ambiguous**, such as a possible backlit subject with no face found, using the same gate in the app and in `ym`. Exports should wait for tier B rather than time out quietly, and record the outcome in XMP. In the viewer, the photo shows at once with `autoset` and tier A, and tier B refines it when its answer arrives, using `prewarm()` to hide the first load ([prewarm](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/languagemodelsession/prewarm(promptprefix:))).

### The Preferences setting

Apple's interface guidelines ask apps to "clearly identify when and where you use AI" and, "when possible, consider offering a non-AI fallback" ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)). The owner's switch does both, and nothing in Apple's terms requires a separate consent dialog for an on-device feature that is on by default. The proposal follows the UI rules: short, plain British English, saying what happens ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md), [docs/11](../docs/11-ui.md)).

| Item | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Place | Preferences, one row with a switch, after Display rendering |
| Shown | only on Macs where tier A answers (Apple silicon, macOS 14 or later); hidden on Intel Macs, older systems and in the browser build |
| Label | **Use Apple's scene analysis in Auto** |
| Note under it | "Auto uses models built into macOS to see what the photo shows, such as faces, a sunset or a room, and adjusts exposure, white and contrast to suit. It runs on this Mac. Nothing is sent anywhere." |
| Extra line, only when tier B is available | "With Apple Intelligence on, it also asks Apple's on-device model about some photos. This takes a second or two each." |
| Default | On |
| Off | Auto is Yellowmoon's own `autoset`, the same picture `ym` makes on any computer |
| Stored as | `apple_scene_analysis: true` in `preferences.json` (an eighth field, read and ignored on Linux); never per photo, and the layer's readings live in memory only for the session (rule 4) |
| Command line | `ym render --scene-analysis on\|off`, default the preference on a Mac; on Linux, or where unavailable, `on` prints "Scene analysis is not available on this computer. Auto uses Yellowmoon's own settings." and continues |
| Reports | `ym info --json` and the Automatic card show what was read and what moved, as one row such as "Backlit face · +0.4 EV" (no new card); `ym doctor` lists each tier and why it is or is not available |
| Exports | XMP `ym:SceneAnalysis`: `none`, or the Vision revision, model variant, prompt version and macOS build |

### The layer keeps its default only if a Mac blind test says so

On by default means most Mac users will see the Apple layer's picture, so it must earn that default the way `autoset` had to. The acceptance test is a blind A/B on a Mac, run on **fresh photos**: `autoset` with the layer against `autoset` without it, stratified as before. It should show wins where the layer claims to help (close-ups without FocusLocation, backlit faces, sunsets, interiors and skin) and no stratum below 45 %, with no rise in pictures that need a fix. Those thresholds mirror gate 5 (docs/18 (`docs/18` on the parked branch `claude/autoset`)) and need the owner's agreement. Tier B gets the same test against tier A alone. Bychkovsky's finding that "scene descriptors … did not improve the results" for global adjustment ([Bychkovsky 2011](https://people.csail.mit.edu/vladb/photoadjust/db_imageadjust.pdf)) is the standing warning that semantic input can fail to help, and the per-stratum test is how to find out. The measurements only a Mac can make (Vision and Foundation Models latency on M1–M5, the ad-hoc-signing question, which compute devices each request still accepts, and repeatability on one machine) belong under "Waiting on Mykyta".

## SceneNet stays the cross-platform answer, and got cheaper

The Apple layer makes the Mac build smarter than the Linux build by design. Closing that gap is still SceneNet's job, as the owner decided: our own 1–4M-parameter encoder, trained from scratch on CC BY images, labelled offline, giving `autoset` a short vector of scene probabilities on every platform (docs/18 (`docs/18` on the parked branch `claude/autoset`)). Four findings change its plan.

The labels are partly free. Open Images' CC BY 4.0 machine labels already exceed 50k images at confidence ≥ 0.7 for most of SceneNet's classes, and the human-verified subset lets their precision be measured per class before use. They enter as CC BY data, a cleaner footing than VLM outputs, although the same "does the rule look through to the labeller?" question applies, and the owner answered it for VLM teachers on 2026-10-18 ([docs/16](../docs/16-risks-and-decisions.md)). **The VLM teachers should be steered away from lighting.** Small open VLMs sit at 44–47 % on ShotBench's lighting conditions ([ShotBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21356)), and generic VLMs trail CLIP at plain classification, 60.6 % for the best proprietary VLM against 79.2 % for the best CLIP on ImageNet, a gap that narrows sharply as the label set shrinks ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18415)). Use Qwen3.5 or Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5 and Gemma 4 for content questions with a small fixed answer list, scored by log-probability. Take backlight and light-type labels from a CLIP-style teacher (PE-Core-L or SigLIP 2 So400m) with a small head trained on a few hundred audited examples. CLIP-LIT found hand-written prompts unable to describe "the precise luminance conditions" and learned backlit prompts "using only hundreds of images" ([CLIP-LIT](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Liang_Iterative_Prompt_Learning_for_Unsupervised_Backlit_Image_Enhancement_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)). Add Places365-R50's CC BY-licensed indoor/outdoor and lighting attributes as a further teacher ([Places365](https://github.com/CSAILVision/places365)). Apple's models are not on that list, under the owner's decision.

Faces need a clean source off the Mac, now that YuNet is out. The best is a face head that shares SceneNet's trunk, trained on Open Images' Human face boxes (16M boxes over 600 classes in the set, [Open Images V7](https://storage.googleapis.com/openimages/web/factsfigures_v7.html)). It brings the owner's skin wish to Linux and to Macs without the Apple layer. A fully clean initialisation exists as well: formula-driven pretraining on synthetic images uses no real photographs and matched ImageNet-21k pretraining for a ViT-B (82.7 % against 81.8 % after fine-tuning) ([formula-driven pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09132v1)), and Mitsua's fractal-only Swin-B under CC BY 4.0 is a working example ([card](https://huggingface.co/Mitsua/swin-base-multi-fractal-1k)).

Compute is small. SekoKuva 423K trained in about 7 hours on a laptop RTX 4050 ([card](https://huggingface.co/bcbertenex/sekokuva-mobile-423k)). An A100 trains ResNet-18 on ImageNet's 1.28M images for 88 epochs in 187 minutes ([FFCV](https://github.com/libffcv/ffcv-imagenet)), which puts a 3.8M-parameter student on 1–2M images at roughly 2–10 A100-hours by extrapolation, with labelling the larger cost. No published accuracy exists for a small clean model on scene classes. The nearest anchor, ResNet-50 from scratch on 365 Places classes, reaches 55 % top-1 ([Places365](https://github.com/CSAILVision/places365)), and 16 coarse classes should do better, so the 300-image owner audit measures it. On determinism, a student small enough to run in hand-written, fixed-order Rust without FMA contraction and with the project's own activation polynomials could be bit-identical between x86 and ARM. That is a design option, not a sourced result. Otherwise the CPU provider with fixed threads and tolerance-based golden tests applies ([Cruz Romero and Maldonado Guerra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23227)).

## Conclusion

"Understanding what is in the scene" breaks into three facts autoset lacks: where the subject is, what the light is, and what the photographer meant. For global exposure the first matters most, and the camera wrote it into the file. The established failure therefore costs no model, no weights and no licence question, only a maker-note tag Yellowmoon does not yet read and a focus measure it can compute against its own noise model. The second and third facts are where models earn their place, and the evidence that they help global settings is thin enough that each must win its own blind test, stratum by stratum, before it moves a default.

The owner's two wishes, a quantized on-device VLM and Apple's models at runtime, turn out to be the same thing on a Mac: Apple's heavily quantized, roughly 3B-parameter model behind a typed API, already installed and costing nothing to ship. Accepting it makes Yellowmoon's Auto deliberately platform-dependent. That is a sound trade only if every export says which Auto made it, if the layer stays bounded on top of a deterministic core, and if its on-by-default status is earned on fresh photos. The 207 already answered can diagnose; they can no longer prove.
