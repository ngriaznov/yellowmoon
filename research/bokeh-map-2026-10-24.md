# Bokeh map from a single photo: models, data, licences, recommendation

Research date 2026-10-24. Scope: what Yellowmoon's lens-character stage L1 (`docs/21-lens-character.md` §4 L1, §7) needs, what exists, what is licence-clean, and what to do.

Method: licence files were read from `raw.githubusercontent.com` (LICENSE text, first lines), dataset pages were fetched as HTML, papers were read as PDFs (DMENet, BTBNet, D-DFFNet, Galetto and Deng, Alvarez-Gila et al., Seeing Through the Blur), and the Hugging Face API was queried for model and dataset licences. Anything I could not open is marked **(unverified)** and listed again in section 7.

---

## 0. Short answer

1. **No pretrained model qualifies.** Every single-image defocus-map or defocus-blur-detection model found has at least one of: a copyleft or missing code licence (DMENet, SYNDOF generator, IFAN are AGPL-3.0; most DBD repos have no LICENSE file), weights trained on data with no grant or a non-commercial grant (SYNDOF is built partly from SYNTHIA, CC BY-NC-SA 3.0; CUHK is "collected from internet" with no terms), or an ImageNet-pretrained backbone. Hugging Face has nothing usable.
2. **The training data for our own model can be fully clean** (Open Images CC BY 2.0 photos plus CC BY 4.0 instance masks, DIODE under MIT, TartanAir under CC BY 4.0; Hypersim is CC BY-SA 3.0 and would make the weights CC BY-SA). The recipe is a published one (SYNDOF's layered thin-lens synthesis, Alvarez-Gila's inpainting against halos, Galetto and Deng's sharpness filter), adapted to disc kernels, linear light and bright highlights.
3. **Recommendation: (b), train our own small model (about 2 M parameters), staged behind the classical detector the spec already has.** One reason beyond accuracy: the re-blur edge-gradient method of §7 measures how sharp an *edge* is, and a bokeh ball is a disc with a sharp rim, so it will report the ball as sharp. Isolated night-light bokeh, the case the anamorphic effect is for, is exactly where that method is weakest (my reasoning, not yet measured on the repo's code; section 1).
4. Cost is small: a first useful model is roughly 1 to 2 L4-hours (about $2 to $3 at the repo's Modal rates), a full run with ablations about $10 to $15 (estimates, +/- 2x). The remaining Modal balance recorded in `docs/STATUS.md` on 2026-10-23 was about $13.5 of $30, so the owner's yes is needed.

## 1. What the map must deliver, and a problem with the spec's classical route

From `docs/21` §4 L1 and §7:

- `b` per pixel, a disc blur radius in half-diagonal units, on the lens plane (long edge at most 1024 px), plus a confidence `c`. `b_min` = 0.004, `b_cap` = 0.03, so on a 1024 x 683 plane (half-diagonal about 615 px) the range of interest is about 2.5 px to 18 px of disc radius.
- Used for (1) the stretch gather lengths `ℓ(p) = (q−1)·b(p)`, (2) finding bright balls at `b ≥ 2·b_min`, (3) the "little out-of-focus background" test (less than 2 % of the frame at `b ≥ 2·b_min`).
- A false positive (large `b` on sharp detail) smears real detail; a false negative leaves bokeh untouched, which is harmless. So the detector should be **conservative: precision over recall**, with a usable confidence.
- Depth must not leak in: a far wall at f/11 is sharp, so the map has to be supervised on blur, not distance.

**The weakness of the classical detector (my analysis, from the method's own edge model).** Zhuo and Sim's re-blur ratio assumes a step edge blurred by a Gaussian and reads the blur from the gradient ratio at that edge. A defocused point light is a disc whose rim can be nearly as sharp as the lens allows, with a radius of 10 px or more. The rim gives a steep gradient, so the method reads "sharp edge, small `b`" while the true blur radius is the disc radius. In a dark background with only lights (night city, Christmas lights, backlit foliage highlights) there are no other edges to propagate from, so step 3 of §7 spreads little. The same holds for any detail smaller than the blur kernel. This is a prediction from the method's assumptions: the spec's synthetic tests (§14) should include "isolated bright discs of known radius on dark noisy ground" to confirm it before committing either way. Learned estimators can read disc size directly from the blob, and they use global context in textureless regions, where edge methods have nothing.

Evidence that a learned map is meaningfully better than Zhuo and Sim when both are well trained (numbers from the DMENet paper, CVPR 2019, Tables and Fig. 4; those are DMENet's own numbers on 22 RTF images and 200 CUHK images, not a like-for-like test of anything we could ship):

| Method | RTF MAE (lower better) | RTF MSE | CUHK binarised accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zhuo and Sim 2011 (the spec's method) | 0.143 | 0.037 | about 73 % (my reading of the bar labels in Fig. 4) |
| DMENet (learned, domain-adapted) | 0.088 | 0.012 | about 87 % |

Why binary detectors do not fit even if they were clean: their labels are human judgements of "the blurry part" (semantic, subject versus background), not blur radius; they are trained almost only on photos that contain some blur, so wide-DOF photos are out of distribution; and the backbones are 20 to 50 M parameters.

- **Depth Anything V2 Small**: Apache-2.0 code and weights (HF card), 24.8 M parameters. Depth, not blur; the owner ruled depth out for the product. - **Apple frameworks** at run time: would not be bit-identical between the app and `ym` on other platforms or across macOS versions (hard rules 6 and 8).

### 5.2 Recipe for a synthetic defocus set with a known blur map

Precedents: SYNDOF (DMENet §3: thin-lens CoC, layers, blurred alpha, back-to-front blend, label = blur at the layer's *sharp* position so that depth edges are not smeared), Alvarez-Gila et al. (blur masks from object masks, **inpaint the foreground before blurring the background to avoid halo artifacts**, noisy labels accepted), BokehMe ([Apache-2.0 code](https://github.com/JuewenPeng/BokehMe): linearising gamma and a `--highlight` option "to enhance RGB values of highlights before rendering for stunning bokeh balls").

1. **Linear light.** Decode sRGB to linear. JPEGs clip at 255, but real bokeh balls come from highlights far above clip, so boost pixels with a max channel above about 0.9 by a random 2^U(0..5) with a soft knee, and paste 5 to 40 random small "lamps" (1 to 2 px, radiance 8x to 300x, warm and cool tints) into the layers that will be blurred.
2. **Depth layout, three kinds** (mix per sample):
   a. true depth (DIODE, TartanAir);
   b. Open Images instance masks as layers: pick one or two instances as in focus or at random depths; everything else is background with a smooth random depth field (tilted plane, horizon split, low-frequency noise);
   c. all-in-focus samples (about 25 to 35 % of the set) so that "sharp at any distance" is learned.
3. **Camera.** Random focal length, f-number, focus distance, thin-lens CoC per pixel (`c(x) = α·|x − S1|/x`, DMENet Eq. 1), CoC radius in px capped around 0.05 half-diagonal (slightly above `b_cap`), a large share of samples below `b_min`, resolution random in 512 to 1024 px long edge. **Label = disc radius in half-diagonal units** (so the net's output is in the spec's units at any scale).
4. **Layers.** 16 to 32 depth bins, each premultiplied colour and alpha blurred with that bin's kernel, composited back to front with the blurred alpha (SYNDOF used up to 350 Gaussian layers; GPU-side FFT convolution makes 16 to 32 enough). Inpaint foreground holes before blurring the background layer.
5. **Kernel library, not only a disc:** uniform disc; disc with soft rim (rim sigma 5 to 15 % of r); bright-rim and dark-rim (spherical aberration); onion-ring ripple; 5 to 9 blade polygons; cat's-eye (disc intersected with a shifted disc) towards the edges; slight ellipticity; Gaussian for the smallest radii. Real lenses differ, and Yellowmoon's input is a Sony lens's bokeh, so the net must not learn one kernel.
6. **Sensor and encoding.** Poisson-Gaussian noise at random ISO levels, mild blur and JPEG-like artifacts on some samples to cover the source's processing, then the fixed encoding the Rust side will feed (log or sRGB-like of the binned lens plane).
7. **Loss.** Predict log-radius and a log-scale (Laplace negative log-likelihood), so a textureless region gets a wide scale instead of a wrong confident value. Ignore invalid-depth and disoccluded pixels.

### 5.3 The source photos' own blur: how papers handle it, and what to do

- **DMENet/SYNDOF**: avoids it by using sources that have none (rendered Sintel and SYNTHIA, 46 lab-captured Middlebury scenes) and never uses Flickr photos as sources.
- **Galetto and Deng**: keep only 32 x 32 patches whose variance of the Laplacian (after a 3 x 3 Gaussian) is above 1000; those patches are "regarded as in sharp focus" and form class 0, and every other class is made by blurring such a patch.
- **Alvarez-Gila et al.** (Pascal VOC): explicitly accept it: "the employed SegVOC12 dataset does indeed contain some pictures exhibiting preexisting partial blur ... the artificially generated ground truth will contain a certain amount of noisy pixel-level labels", and still reach state of the art on CUHK and DUT without seeing a real blurred image.
- **BTBNet**: uses "clear" images from the Berkeley set with Gaussian blur applied to half of each.

For Open Images specifically (assessment):
- *Works well for*: licence consistency with the crop model's pipeline, the 1024 px cap (an image shot at 24 MP and shown at 1024 px has its native blur shrunk by about 6x, so moderate native blur falls under `b_min`), the instance masks, diversity.
- *Risks*: Flickr is rich in deliberately shallow-DOF photos (share **not measured**); no EXIF (cannot filter by f-number); JPEG and sharpening; sources with strong native bokeh would label real blur as 0 and teach the net that blur is sometimes invisible.
- *Mitigations (all cheap)*: (1) a sharpness filter on the source (Laplacian-variance fraction of patches at 512 px; Galetto and Deng's threshold as a starting point); (2) mix with DIODE and TartanAir where the label is exact; (3) one self-cleaning round: run the first model on the *unblurred* sources and drop or down-weight pixels where it already predicts blur above `b_min`, or set that label to `sqrt(b_added² + b_src²)`; (4) the Laplace loss; (5) before building anything, run the classical estimator over about 5,000 sampled Open Images photos and report the share with large native blur, to size the problem.

## 6. Recommendation

### Choice

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| (a) pretrained model as is | **Not possible.** Nothing has clean code, weights and data (sections 2 to 4). |
| (b) train our own small model | **Recommended**, in the staged form below. |
| (c) classical only | Acceptable as the first shipped version (it is the current spec), but with the known blind spot for bokeh balls and a weak result in textureless regions. Keep it as the fallback and as the baseline the model must beat. |

### Stage 0, free, do first (regardless of ML)
- Implement the classical detector as specified, and add to the spec's tests the cases that matter: isolated bright discs of known radius on dark noisy ground, balls in front of textured background, a defocused thin line, an f/11 sharp scene with textureless walls.
- Add a **direct disc-size measurement for balls** (scale-space blob or disc-template matching on the highlight mask) so balls do not depend on the edge-ratio output. This also makes the later model's job easier to check.
- EXIF physical gate: with focal length `f`, f-number `N`, sensor size and a focus distance `S1`, the largest possible disc diameter on the sensor is `f² / (N·(S1 − f))` (background at infinity); if that is below `b_min` of the half-diagonal there can be no bokeh whatever the content says. Sony ARW focus distance was **not found in ExifTool's Sony tag list (unverified)**, so this may be usable only as a weak prior (lens minimum focus distance as `S1`), or with manual input; the spec uses the AF point, which is a different quantity.

### Stage 1, the model (what I would train)

- **Architecture** (fits ≤ 5 M with room): MobileNetV2-style encoder like the crop net (`crates/ym-ml/src/crop_net.rs` already runs inverted residual blocks, depthwise convolutions, folded batch norm and SiLU in f32 on one thread), 5 stages to stride 32, a small U-Net/FPN decoder using nearest or bilinear upsampling plus 3 x 3 depthwise-separable convolutions (no ConvTranspose or Resize ops, per `docs/07`), skip connections, output at half resolution: 2 channels (log-radius, log-scale). **About 1.5 to 3 M parameters, 6 to 12 MB as f32 safetensors.** Dilated or pooled global-context block at stride 32 for textureless regions.
- **Input:** the lens plane at its own scale (long edge 512 to 1024 px), encoded with a fixed log/sRGB-like curve (luminance plus chroma). Train on random crops of 384 x 384 with scale augmentation, so the net sees 2.5 to 18 px radii at lens-plane scale. At run time use the plane at 1024 (not 512: at 512 `b_min` would be 1.2 px).
- **Budget:** at most about 3 k MACs per input pixel (about 2 GMAC for a 1024 x 683 plane). My estimate for the Rust CPU path is about 0.3 to 1 s on an M1 (4 threads with a fixed partition keeps the bits identical), **not measured**; the spec's 0.5 s target for the planes is tight, so either keep the net on the half-resolution path or allow it a share of that budget. Determinism follows the crop net's recipe: f32, fixed order, parity test against PyTorch, SHA-256 in the model folder, model card, `NOTICE` entries.
- **Outputs to the spec:** `b = exp(mean)` and `c = f(exp(−scale))` replace step 1 to step 3 of §7; step 4 (caution) and the 2 % test stay. The existing guided upsampling in L1 still applies.
- **Optional later:** a real-photo stage using a blur-addition consistency loss on unlabelled Open Images photos (`f(blur(x, s)) ≈ sqrt(f(x)² + s²)`), the idea behind the re-blur method turned into a loss. Untested; mention only.

### Training cost (estimates, not benchmarked)

Rates from `training/crop/budget.py`: L4 $0.000222 per second ($0.80 per hour) with a 1.25 margin.

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Data prep (about 60 k Open Images photos plus masks, DIODE, TartanAir subset) | Modal CPU, about $1 |
| Rendering | on the GPU in PyTorch (FFT convolution per layer), about 100 to 200 samples per second at 384 x 384 on an L4 |
| First useful model, about 0.6 M samples | 1 to 2 L4-hours, **about $2 to $3** |
| Full run, about 2.4 M samples (150 k steps of batch 16) | 4 to 8 L4-hours, **about $5 to $10** |
| Pilot ablations (kernel library, source filter, share of true depth), 4 runs of one fifth the length | about $3 to $5 |
| **Total programme** | **about $10 to $15** (range 2x either way) |
| 4-core CPU (no GPU) | Smoke tests only. A 2 M-parameter net costs about 1 to 2 GFLOP per 256 x 256 sample for forward and backward; at a realistic 15 to 30 GFLOP/s PyTorch CPU throughput that is 5 to 10 samples per second including rendering, so 0.6 M samples take 1 to 3 days and 2.4 M samples 4 to 8 days. A 128 to 192 px, 100 k sample mini run (a few hours) is useful for debugging and for checking the loss, not for shipping |

The `docs/STATUS.md` entry of 2026-10-23 records about $13.5 of the owner's $30 Modal balance left, so a full programme would need the owner's yes (as question 23 did for the crop model).

### Evaluation and go/no-go

1. Spec §14 analytic scenes (b within 25 % where confident, below `b_min` on sharp regions and strands, none on the sharp scene), plus the new cases in stage 0.
2. False-positive rate on real sharp photos: the CC0 RawDB Sony files and the ARW files already under `testdata/lens-photos/` (share of pixels with `b ≥ b_min` and confidence).
3. Held-out synthetic Open Images (MAE of log-radius).
4. Qualitative: photos from `testdata/lens-photos/` with obvious bokeh and the night/backlit photos of `docs/test-photo-sources.md`.
5. Optional, evaluation only and the owner's decision: CUHK, DUT-DBD and RTF numbers, to compare with the literature. Never trained on, never committed, no redistribution.
6. **Go** if the model beats the classical-plus-ball detector on 1 and 2 without losing on 3; otherwise ship the classical one and keep the data generator.

### Owner decisions this needs
- Modal spend of about $10 to $15 (balance recorded as $13.5).
- Whether CUHK/DUT/RTF may be used for local evaluation.
- Whether DMENet may be used as an offline teacher on Open Images photos (same kind of decision as FrameLite). I do **not** recommend it: AGPL code that needs TensorFlow 1.15, weights from NC-derived data, trained on Gaussian blur not discs, and it is weak on isolated bokeh balls for the same reason as every edge-leaning method; it would add legal exposure for little gain.
- Whether to accept CC BY-SA training data (Hypersim). Not needed; avoid.

### Honest uncertainty
- Whether a 2 M-parameter net trained only on synthetic defocus of Flickr photos generalises to Sony raw lens planes is the main risk. Precedent is encouraging but indirect: Alvarez-Gila et al. reached state of the art on CUHK/DUT with synthetic-only supervision (but with a ResNet-101 DeepLab), Galetto and Deng reached 0.97 patch accuracy on synthetic Gaussian-blurred patches (not a real-photo number), and DMENet needed domain adaptation because its *rendered* training images were too unlike real photos (their ablation, Fig. 3). Starting from real photographic sources should help; the kernel library and highlight boosting are the parts I trust least.
- The claim that the re-blur method misreads bokeh balls is a prediction from its edge model, not a measurement on the repo.
- Cost and speed numbers are back-of-envelope.
