# Blur node: what other tools offer, and how a mask is handled (2026-11-18)

Background for `docs/33-blur.md` (task BL-01). The owner's request, 2026-11-18: "Can we in next release also add blur node? It should tolerate mask", then "We want different type of blur too." **Public sources only.** This text describes the world; it contains no instructions (`AGENTS.md`). We copy no code and no proprietary formula (`docs/14`). **Where a source was unclear or could not be opened, the text says so.** The spec wins where they differ.

Method and limits: web search and page fetches on 2026-11-18. Adobe's help pages and the Blackmagic manual mirror answered some fetches with HTTP 403 or 404, so for those the facts below come from the search results' own summaries of the pages and are marked *(summary)*. Nothing here was tried in the products (we have no licences to hand), so every statement about behaviour on a mask is what the documentation or a user report says, not a measurement.

## 1. DaVinci Resolve

**The Blur palette (Color page, Blur / Sharpen / Mist).** A single Radius control: raising it blurs, lowering it sharpens, and the default 0.50 leaves the picture alone. H/V Ratio makes the effect directional (0.50 is equal; above favours the horizontal axis, below the vertical). Many controls are three ganged sliders for red, green and blue that can be unganged, so the radius can differ by channel. Scaling multiplies the radius effect (it matters for Mist) and Mix blends with the original. *(summary of the Resolve 18 manual's mirror: https://www.steakunderwater.com/VFXPedia/__man/Resolve18-6/DaVinciResolve18_Manual_files/part3148.htm and neighbours; the pages themselves returned 404 to a direct fetch, so quoted behaviour is from the search summary. I found no source that explains Scaling and Mix per channel in detail: unclear.)*
- **Mask.** The palette lives in a node. A node's key (qualifier, window, matte) limits where the node's result is shown. Resolve's manual describes keys as limiting the node's effect; I found **no statement of a matte-aware option** in the Blur palette. The behaviour users describe, and the one the architecture implies, is **blur the node's whole input, then blend by the key** (our behaviour (a)). I could not verify this in the documentation text itself: *unclear, treat as likely*.

**ResolveFX Blur category (Studio and free versions differ).** Box Blur (variable quality; a Border Type setting for the picture's edge: Black, Replicate, Reflect, Wrap Around), Directional Blur ("constrained to a single angle"), Gaussian Blur ("smooth blur... like looking through semi-opaque glass"; separate horizontal and vertical strength), Lens Blur (Studio only; "a high-quality simulation of optical lens blurring"; **the bokeh shape can be customised with a separate node input**), Mosaic Blur (pixelate), Radial Blur ("motion blur that would occur were the image spinning around its center point") and Zoom Blur ("motion blur that would occur were a camera moving toward the image"). Source: the DaVinci Resolve manuals, ResolveFX chapter, e.g. https://ltbits.github.io/davinci-resolve-manuals/DR18/DR18-RM12-ResolveFXOverview.pdf and https://ltbits.github.io/davinci-resolve-manuals/DR16/DR16-RM-11–ResolveFX.pdf *(summary from the search; the PDFs were not read in full)*.
- **Mask.** The same as the palette: an effect on a node, limited by the node's key. A Lens Blur in Studio takes a depth or matte input for where to blur (the manual's "node input" for the shape; a depth map input is how Resolve's Lens Blur is driven, *not verified here*). No matte-aware convolution is documented in what I read.

**Take-away for us.** Resolve offers the same family we are planning: Gaussian (Soft), Lens, Directional (Motion), Zoom, Radial (Spin), Box and Mosaic. A type selector inside one tool is not Resolve's way (they are separate plug-ins); it is Photoshop Blur Gallery's and Lightroom's (§2, §3).

## 2. Photoshop

- **Gaussian Blur and Lens Blur filters.** Lens Blur has a **depth map** (source layer or channel, focal point, "blur focal distance", invert) that sets where pixels sit in depth; where the map is black there is no blur. Iris controls: shape (triangle to octagon), radius, **blade curvature**, rotation. **Specular highlights:** a brightness and a threshold that treat pixels above the threshold as highlights and make them brighter before the blur, so lights become clear discs. Noise to match the grain. Source: the CS3 manual text and third-party guides found by search, e.g. https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/753900/adobe-photoshop-cs3.html?page=219, https://computerhope.com/jargon/p/photoshop-lens-blur.htm *(old; the current menus may differ; I found no current official page)*.
- **Blur Gallery.** Field Blur (pins with blur amounts, a gradient between them), Iris Blur (a focus ellipse, falloff outside), **Tilt-Shift** (a sharp band, a gradient of blur on both sides), Path Blur (motion along a path) and **Spin Blur** (radial motion). Field, Iris and Tilt-Shift also have Bokeh (brighten highlights into orbs) and Noise. Source: https://jkost.com/blog/2017/06/tips-for-working-with-blur-gallery-in-photoshop-cc.html, https://www.pcworld.com/article/3569135/how-to-use-photoshop-blur-gallery-for-bokeh-effects-and-more.html (tutorials; Adobe's own help pages did not appear in the results).
- **Mask.** A layer mask or a selection limits where the filter's result shows. A Lens Blur's own depth map is an input of the filter, not a mask. The **halo** is the well-known failure of "blur the whole layer, then mask": photographers describe blurring a background with the subject still in the layer, so the subject's colours "bleed outward before the mask is applied", and the cures are cutting the background onto its own layer (so the subject is not in it) or filling the hole with content before the blur. Source: https://www.uniquephoto.com/community/qa/how-can-i-avoid-haloing-when-blurring-the-background-in-photoshop and https://community.adobe.com/questions-712/problem-with-halo-in-gaussian-blur-1185310. Photoshop's Lens Blur with a depth map is described as designed to avoid this, *without a statement of how*: unclear.

## 3. Lightroom and Camera Raw

- **Lens Blur (2023 on).** A depth map made by AI (or the device's depth, for HEIC), shown by Visualize Depth (warm near, cool far, white in focus); **Subject Focus**, or Point or Area Focus, sets the focal range; **Blur Amount** (default 50); **Bokeh shape** (Lightroom desktop lists Circle and Oval/Anamorphic; Camera Raw lists Bubble, 5-Blade, Ring, Anamorphic, Cat Eye); a **Refine** brush for Focus or Blur with Amount, Size, Feather, Flow, Auto Mask. Source: https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom/web/edit-photos/apply-effects/apply-lens-blur.html and https://helpx.adobe.com/camera-raw/using/lens-blur.html *(search summaries; the pages returned 403 to a direct fetch)*.
- **Mask.** The depth map **is** the blur's selection, and Refine is a brush on it. Lens Blur is a global adjustment, so it does not take a local mask of its own *(unclear: not stated in what I could read)*. The treatment of the subject's edge (halo) is not documented.

## 4. Capture One

I found **no native lens or depth-based blur tool**. Users describe workarounds (a layer with Clarity and Structure turned all the way down, brushed into the background, with the subject masked out) and two open feature requests, one for a true Gaussian blur (Feb 2026, "Awaiting review") and one for a blur brush; third-party plug-ins (ON1's Lens Blur with a second mask for falloff) are the usual answer. Sources: https://support.captureone.com/hc/es/community/posts/15145090245789-Background-Blur-Lens-Blur-like-Lightroom, https://support.captureone.com/hc/de/community/posts/14569887883037-Bokeh-tool-addition, https://captureone.ideas.aha.io/ideas/FR-I-2633 *(forum posts; older ones may be out of date, release notes not checked)*. So there is nothing to say about its mask handling beyond "a layer mask, so blur then blend".

## 5. Summary: what each tool does with a mask

| Tool | Blur | Mask |
|---|---|---|
| Resolve (palette, ResolveFX) | Gaussian-like radius with H/V ratio; Box, Gaussian, Directional, Lens, Radial, Zoom, Mosaic plug-ins | A node's key limits the result: **blur all, then blend** (likely; not stated). No matte-aware option found |
| Photoshop | Gaussian, Lens (depth map, iris, highlights), Blur Gallery (Field, Iris, Tilt-Shift, Path, Spin) | A layer mask: **blur all, then blend**; the halo is a known fault; cures are manual (cut the background out, fill the hole). Lens Blur's depth map is its own input |
| Lightroom, Camera Raw | Lens Blur: AI depth map, focal range, amount, bokeh shapes | The depth map is the selection; Refine brush on it. Edge treatment not documented |
| Capture One | none native | a layer mask over a Clarity workaround: blur all, then blend |

**Reading.** Every tool that has a plain mask is "blur all, then blend" (our (a)); the cure for the halo is by hand. A matte-aware blur (our (b)) is what a compositing tool's "premultiplied" blur or a "masked" blur does, and it is the cure the community names (§6).

## 6. The halo problem and its cures

**The fault.** Blur the whole picture, then show the result through an inverted subject mask: near the subject the blurred background has taken colour from the subject, which now shows as a glow around it (red jacket, pink rim on a dark street: `design/blur/09-respect-mask-edges.jpg`). The mask sits exactly on the edge, so the leak is in what is visible.

**Normalized convolution (mask-weighted blur).** Blur the picture times the mask and the mask alone, then divide: `blur(X m) / blur(m)`. Where the mask is 1 over the whole kernel the two blurs cancel to the plain blur; near the mask's edge the blurred mask is below 1 and dividing by it restores the brightness the "black hole" took away. A forum thread states exactly this recipe for a masked blur that "avoids darkening pixels near a protected subject": blur image times mask, blur the mask, divide, apply through the mask, and cites **Knutsson and Westin, "Normalized and differential convolution" (CVPR 1993)** as the origin (https://discuss.pixls.us/t/difficulty-with-blurs-and-masks/50274/21; the page gives the paper's title and a link only, I could not read the paper). The same idea is used by tools that **exclude masked-out pixels from the blur** (DL-C's Precise Gaussian Blur: "excludes masked-out pixels from the blurring operation... the mask must be white in the areas to be blurred": https://www.dl-c.com/Documents/WebHelp/topics/idh-precise-gaussian-blur.htm), by Face Refinement in this repository (`docs/27` §7.2, "mask-normalised blur") and, in its general weighted form, by the "normalized convolution" of the image-processing literature.

**Other cures.** (1) Cut the subject out and fill the hole with plausible background before the blur (needs inpainting; fragile). (2) Matte-aware or "edge-aware" blur in compositors (premultiplied colour: multiply by the matte, blur, divide by the blurred matte: the same formula). (3) Layered or occlusion-aware lens models that blur each depth layer separately and composite near over far: a research paper on lens blur fields reports that a "nonlinear normalized image formation model" gives a plausible result without halo artefacts (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11535; an overview of the idea, not an implementation we use). (4) Bilateral and guided filters stop at edges of the *picture*, which is a different question (smoothing, not blurring a region).

**What it costs, and its limit.** Normalized convolution needs two blurs (the weighted picture and the weight), so about 4 channels instead of 3. Its limit: it **never spreads light out of the mask**. Blur a subject with the mask on the subject and its outline stays sharp, because the blur is made from subject pixels only and is shown only where the mask is. For "a glow that spreads out" the plain blend is the right one. This is why `docs/33` keeps the plain behaviour as a switch.

## 7. Blur types in the field, and the kernels

| Type | Kernel | Where seen | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian | the Gaussian; separable | all | the default everywhere; the cheapest large blur |
| Box | a square | Resolve (Box Blur) | fast; looks flat; we do not need it (a Gaussian is as cheap and nicer) |
| Lens, disc or polygon | a disc or a regular polygon of n blades, uniform | Photoshop Lens Blur (iris shape, curvature, rotation), Lightroom (bokeh shapes), Resolve Lens Blur | the look of out-of-focus lights; not separable; needs a bright-highlight treatment to look like bokeh |
| Directional, motion | a line | Resolve (Directional), Photoshop (Motion, Path) | one angle and a length |
| Zoom | a radial line, length proportional to the distance from a centre | Resolve (Zoom Blur), After Effects | a centre |
| Radial, spin | an arc about a centre | Resolve (Radial Blur), Photoshop (Spin Blur) | a centre and an angle |
| Tilt-shift | any blur through a band (a Linear mask) | Photoshop (Tilt-Shift), phone editors | **a mask plus a blur**, not a type |
| Field, Iris, depth-driven | blur radius varies with a map | Photoshop (Field, Iris), Lightroom (Lens Blur) | a varying radius; the map is a mask in our model |
| Mosaic | blocks | Resolve | not a blur; skip |
| Surface, Smart, bilateral | edge-preserving | Photoshop | smoothing, `docs/27`'s area; later |

**Why linear light.** In scene-linear light a bright lamp is many times the sky's value, and a blur adds light, so a lamp blurred over a disc stays bright: this is what a lens does and it is what makes bokeh balls. In a log or gamma-encoded picture the lamp's value is compressed, so the blurred lamp is a dim smear with a dark fringe where bright meets dark. Photoshop's Lens Blur adds "specular highlights" controls for exactly the case where the file's highlights are clipped (not bright enough): they brighten pixels above a threshold before the blur. A raw file reconstructed to scene light keeps lights bright (up to the sensor's clip), but clipped lights are weaker than the real ones, so a Highlights control earns its place in the Lens type. (Sources for the Photoshop control: §2.)

**Fast large kernels (ideas only; we write our own).** A Gaussian is separable; a large one is cheap on a coarse grid (Film's `docs/24` §3.9a does this). A disc is not separable: fast methods are a direct gather on a reduced grid, a sum of complex Gaussians (Garcia, "Circular Separable Convolution Depth of Field", GDC 2017, from memory, not read for this note) or sampled patterns; a pyramid of box means serves a gather along a line or an arc. We use the gather on a reduced grid and a pyramid, which keep tiles bit-exact (`docs/33` §3 and §7).

## 8. What the plan takes from this

1. One node with a **Type**; photographers know Photoshop's Blur Gallery and Lightroom's Lens Blur as one tool with a choice of looks, and Resolve's palette as one radius.
2. **Soft, Lens, Motion, Zoom** in; Spin later (same code as Zoom); Tilt-shift is a Linear mask; depth-driven blur is a Depth mask (the node's mask is the "map").
3. Linear light; Gaussian cut at 4 sigma; the disc as a coverage-weighted gather; a Highlights weight for Lens.
4. **Mask-aware by default** (normalized convolution), with a switch for the plain blend. Neither Resolve, Photoshop, Lightroom nor Capture One has it as a built-in option in what I found.
