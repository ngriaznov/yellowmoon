# 21 · Lens character

Lens character gives a photo the look of another lens: how its highlights flare, how its edges soften and darken, how the out-of-focus background is shaped, and, with an anamorphic attachment, taller oval bokeh, horizontal streaks and the anamorphic bend. It is a **Lens** tab, the second tab of the tool panel: a Lens card with the seven lenses as rows and an Amounts card with three sliders, **Flares**, **Edges** and **Anamorphic**. This file covers what the person sees, the setting, where the lens acts in the pipeline, the four stages, the core's API, the lens data, the bokeh detector, the command line, determinism and speed, the app, and the tests.

The reasons, sources and rejected routes (generative editors among them) are in `research/lens-character-2026-10-23.md`. The owner's decisions of 2026-10-24: lenses only, no film looks; existing bokeh is reshaped and none is ever added ("modification is a better route than adding fake bokeh"; "if photo has no bokeh - it will almost unmodified, but we also do other things for lens simulation"); lens data are synthetic, computed from public lens designs; "Flares and field effects need its controls. Bokeh reshaping will increase with raising anamorphic slider"; Anamorphic also shapes the flares; a lens choice above the sliders with seven lenses including Default; Anamorphic works with every lens and brings the anamorphic distortion; the picture zooms in so no corner is empty. Later the same day the owner dropped the depth model the first version of this spec used: "Why do we need depth model? We need only bokeh detection model so we can modify it"; "Depth on its own does not mean we need to modify bokeh. And we are adding no bokeh, remember? We just modify it." The bokeh detector (section 7) finds the blur already in the photo from the photo itself.

Everything here runs in Rust, the same in the app and `ym`. The optics are classical kernels (a CPU f64 reference and a WGSL kernel each, `docs/17` §6). Nothing is downloaded: the bokeh map is a small learned model that ships in `data/models/bokeh/`, runs in Rust once per photo, and has the classical detector as its fallback (section 7). Nothing is fitted or traced at run time: the lens data are formulas and small tables computed offline (section 6).

**Status: built (branch `lens-character`, pre-release). All four stages have CPU references and device kernels; the Mac timings and the Metal parity numbers are open (`docs/STATUS.md`).** Lens character does not change `PIPELINE_VERSION`: with the three sliders at 0 the lens stages do not run, and every pixel is what it is today.

## 1. What the person sees

The **Lens** tab (the owner's request of 2026-10-25: "lens should be its own tab like Look, Light and others"; it had been the fourth card of the Look tab, under Tone, below the visible part of the panel) is the second tab, ⌘2, in a soft violet (#BD8CFF), and has two cards (`docs/11` *Lens card* has the details of the controls). Its blurb is "How a lens draws light: flares, softer edges, the shape of blur. Off until you raise a slider."

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Lens** card, header | "Lens" (no Reset: the Amounts card's does that) |
| **Lens** list | the seven lenses below as rows of a glasscn `RadioGroup` (`ChoiceList`, `Item` rows), one row per lens: the name in bold and the line under it, in the order of the table; the chosen row is ringed in the tab colour |
| **Amounts** card, header | "Amounts" and the card's Reset link |
| **Flares** | 0 to 100, step 1, detent and Reset at 0, shown with `formats.integer` |
| **Edges** | the same |
| **Anamorphic** | the same |
| Note | a `RowNote` under the sliders, for the bokeh line when there is one (below); the chosen lens's line is in its row and is not repeated |

| Lens | Line |
|---|---|
| Default | "Clean and modern: even sharpness, faint glow." |
| Classic | "Warm and gentle: soft glow, amber flares, smooth falloff." |
| Vintage | "Glows wide open, lower contrast, soft edges, a little swirl." |
| Petzval | "Very sharp centre, strong swirl, fast falloff." |
| Triplet | "Soft corners, busy flares, a crisp centre." |
| Sonnar | "Smooth and glowing, with gentle edges." |
| Detuned | "Sharp only in the centre, with smeared flares." |

The names are the approved list; two of them need a check before they ship (section 12, decision 1).

**What each slider does.** The lens decides what the effects look like (the colour of its ghosts, how its edges soften, how much it swirls); the sliders set how much. 100 is the lens's full character, as its data describe it, with one exception: the flare elements a lens's data hold too faintly to see (a modern coated lens's ghosts) are raised at the top of Flares to the floors of section 4, L3, so that 100 reads as the lens at its worst, never as a lens that does nothing.

- **Flares**: glow around bright light, veiling glare (lower contrast near light), ghosts on the line from a light through the frame's centre, and starbursts on small lights. All of it grows with the light the photo really holds, including what the sensor clipped (section 4, L3). A small clipped light (a lamp, the sun, a window) was far brighter in the scene than the clip level shows, and its flare is drawn from that: at Flares 100 every lens gives a sun or a street light a clearly visible glow and clearly visible ghosts, and with Anamorphic 100 a thin horizontal streak through its brightest lights, the light's own colour at the light and turning blue as it fades. A large clipped area (a sky, a wall) is a surface and flares only as much as its own light does. The lens's start amounts (about 30 to 50) give the same at a third to a half.
- **Edges**: the softening of the edges, the darkening towards them, colour fringes, the lens's own distortion, and swirl and cat's-eye shapes in the out-of-focus background.
- **Anamorphic**: an anamorphic attachment in front of the chosen lens, as real adapters are used on spherical lenses. It makes out-of-focus highlights taller ovals, turns flares into horizontal streaks and oval ghosts (Flares sets how much), bends the picture the anamorphic way (a horizontal barrel: horizontal lines near the top and bottom bow, verticals stay nearly straight, and the left and right sides are drawn in a little), makes the edge darkening oval, softens the left and right edges more than the top and bottom, and adds horizontal colour fringes. The picture zooms in a little as it rises (a few per cent at 100; the attachment's data set it), so no corner is ever empty.

| Action | What happens |
|---|---|
| Choose a lens | `kind` changes, and Flares and Edges move to that lens's starting amounts (its data's `start`; Default's are 0 and 0). Anamorphic stays. One undo step |
| Move a slider | the picture follows, as with any slider; one undo step per drag |
| Reset (the Amounts card's link) | Default, and the three sliders at 0 |
| Before (`\`) | the camera's default picture, with no lens effect at all (`docs/02` *The Before view*) |

**The bokeh line** shows while a slider asks for bokeh reshaping:

| State | Line |
|---|---|
| little to reshape | "This photo has little out-of-focus background, so its bokeh changes little." |

Otherwise there is no bokeh line. A photo without bokeh still gets flares and edges: the owner's rule.

**Elsewhere.** Copy settings and Paste carry the lens like any setting; "Apply to" copies the look only (`docs/01` *Applying to several photos*); Re-run leaves it alone. The viewport, the filmstrip thumbnails and the look tiles show the lens; a thumbnail or look tile reshapes the bokeh only once the photo's blur map exists (it is made when the photo is shown in the viewport or exported). The automatic estimates (exposure, white lift, white balance, Apple Auto, Auto crop) are made without lens effects, so a lens slider never moves them.

## 3. Where the lens acts

A new stage group, **Lens (L1 to L3)**, sits between the raw group (after R9) and the colour group (before C1), on scene-linear camera RGB balanced with `g0`: the light the emulated lens delivers to the sensor. **L4**, the anamorphic bend and the lens's distortion, is geometry, applied while sampling.

| # | Stage | Slider | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Bokeh reshaping | Anamorphic (taller ovals); Edges times the lens's swirl (tangential stretch, cat's-eye) | per tile, with lists made once per photo |
| L2 | Field optics: edge softness, colour fringes, falloff | Edges; Anamorphic's own terms | per tile |
| L3 | Highlight optics: glow, veiling glare, ghosts, streaks, starbursts | Flares, shaped by Anamorphic | a reduced plane and lists made once per photo, then per tile |
| L4 | Anamorphic bend, the lens's distortion, the zoom | Anamorphic; Edges | geometry (`docs/01` *Geometry*) |

Why there:

- Added light stays additive, and white balance, exposure, the sensor map, the highlight range (C7), the look and the display transform then treat flare light like the rest of the scene, as they would a real lens's.
- The clipped light is still recognisable (R5 leaves a pixel at its clip level in every channel only where every channel clipped), so it can be restored for the flares (L3).
- A colour, grade or look edit does not re-run the lens: the group's key is the raw group's key, the reduction, the lens setting and, when Anamorphic is above 0, the axis (below).
- The photo's own lens corrections (R3, R5b, R9) still run first. The emulated lens adds its character on top; the Sony lens's corrections are never switched off to get it (the research note's decision).

**Coordinates.** The optical centre is the centre of the photo's area after R9's auto crop (the sensor's centre), not of the person's crop. Every length in the lens data is in units of the half-diagonal of that area, and the field radius `ρ` is the distance from the centre over the half-diagonal, so a view reduced `k` times uses the same shapes at `1/k` of the pixels. The **anamorphic axis** is the horizontal of the canvas: the photo as the person sees it after the camera's and their own quarter turns, before straightening (section 12, decision 4). In the buffer it is the x or the y axis.

**Planes and lists, once per photo.** Some parts of the lens see the whole frame (a ghost lies across the centre from its light; glow reaches far), while tiles must equal the whole frame bit for bit (`docs/17` D5). Those parts are therefore made once per photo, on the CPU, from small whole-frame images, and shared by every tile and every scale:

- the **lens plane**: the raw group's image at `k_L`, the largest power of two (at least 2) that keeps the frame's long edge at or above 1024 px, so the plane is 1024 to 2047 px long (a 6000 px frame gives `k_L` = 4, an 8640 px frame 8; frames under 2048 px use 2, and test frames under 1024 px may use 1);
- the **tail plane**: the lens plane halved again until its long edge is at most 512 px;
- the **source list** (L3) and the **ball list** and **blur map** (L1).

Their key is the raw group's key, the lens `kind` and its data version, the camera profile (for the spectral projection, section 6) and the camera's focus point; the slider amounts are not in it, so moving a slider re-runs only the per-tile passes. Each tile then reads them at its own pixels' absolute positions.

**Regions.** A reduced view (the fit view, thumbnails, look tiles) runs the lens over its whole frame. At full resolution the lens output of a rectangle is computed from the raw group's region grown by the lens halo (`LensPlan::halo_px`, which depends on the amounts and the photo's blur map), assembled from the raw group's cached tiles as `Job::region` already does; the raw tiles keep their own cache and key, and the lens output has its own. An export tile grows its raw source rectangle by the halo the same way.

**Render graph.** `docs/01` *Render graph* gains the row "Lens (L1 to L3) | `lens`, and everything that invalidates the raw group; the orientation when Anamorphic is above 0", and its Geometry row gains L4.

**As built (L1 to L3 in the graph, CPU; `crates/ym-core/src/pipeline/graph/lens.rs`).** The decisions the build made:

- **Where.** The lens sits behind the five places the raw group leaves the graph (`docs/01` *The renderer and its keys*): `Job::lens_group_backing(k)` (fit view, thumbnails, look tiles, histogram and readout: `apply` over the whole group at 1/k, cached per lens setting), `Job::lens_region_pixels(rect)` (the 100 % view: lens tiles on the 512 px grid, cached), `Job::lens_region(rect, false)` and `Job::lens_region_resident(rect)` (exports, the export tail and both DNG targets: the raw region grown by `halo_px`, one call, `apply`, cropped to the rectangle; nothing cached), and `Job::lens_tiles_ready` / `Job::lens_warm_tiles` (so a 100 % view with a lens is not stuck coarse). Without a lens each is the raw method it mirrors. On the CPU route the lens's caches are host images; on the device route they are device images (the block *the lens on the device*, below).
- **Planes.** One set per photo in `GraphState`, outside the `ByteLru`s (and counted in `PhotoGraph::bytes`), made from the raw group at `k_L`; for a frame under 1024 px (`k_L` = 1, only tiny test frames) the whole frame is assembled from the raw tiles. An **export makes its planes itself** from the CPU reference raw group and keeps them for the request, so the bytes of an export do not depend on what the viewer looked at (and a device-made group never feeds them): the app and `ym` write the same file.
- **The axis is in the key whatever the amounts**, not only when Anamorphic is above 0: L3's starbursts and ghosts follow the canvas's orientation too. A quarter turn therefore makes the lens passes again.
- **The focus point** is the maker note's point as a fraction of the sensor frame, shifted and scaled by R9's crop and clamped to the frame (the distortion correction's few pixels are ignored: the detector needs the area). Sony's "centre when unknown" is not told apart from a real centre point.
- **The camera's colour** comes from the profile's spectral curves read from the data folder (`RenderOptions::data_root`), projected once per camera. When the curves cannot be read the lens uses a neutral camera and the render's notes say "The camera's colour curves could not be read, so the lens colours are neutral."
- **Thumbnails and look tiles** make the photo's planes like any other request (including the blur map when a slider asks for bokeh), so they show the lens exactly as the viewport does and do not depend on what was opened first; section 1's "only once the blur map exists" is not needed. The price is one set of planes per photo whose graph a thumbnail touches (the renderer keeps three graphs).
- **Budgets.** The reduced lens groups take up to 1/32 and the 100 % lens tiles up to 1/12 of the CPU budget, per photo; a slider drag leaves one entry per value until the oldest are evicted. An export adds the lens's halo to the 160 px its working set is reserved for.
- **Counters.** `StageRuns::lens_planes`, `lens_groups`, `lens_tiles`, and, for the stage caches below, `lens_fields` (the field stage ran over a group or a tile) and `lens_flares` (the flare terms did); `StageTimings::lens_ms`; all zero without a lens (a test pins it for every lens).
- **Measured (CPU reference, `cargo test -p ym-core --test render_lens -- --ignored --nocapture lens_timing`; 24 MP synthetic photo, Vintage 60/50/50, 4 shared cores at load 4 to 6, workspace crates at the dev profile's `opt-level = 1`, so a release build is several times faster).** Fit view (1500 x 1000, lens group at 1/4): 2.0 s without a lens (cold), 10.7 s with the lens the first time (planes and group), 10.1 s for a Flares move and 10.4 s for an Edges move (each move runs L1 to L3 over the 1500 x 1000 group again). 100 % view of 1500 x 1000: 12.6 s without a lens (12 raw tiles), 37.8 s with it the first time (6 more raw tiles for the halos, 12 lens tiles) and 21.3 s for a Flares move (12 lens tiles, about 1.8 s each). Export: 79.8 s without a lens (70.5 s in the raw stages), 289 s with it (88.5 s in the raw stages, the planes' group included; 190.9 s in the lens, 24 tiles). These are the CPU reference's numbers, the guard against regressions; the targets of section 9 (a slider move in at most 100 ms) are for the kernels on the device, which the blocks below describe; those numbers are from before the stage caches and the L3 changes, whose effect is measured there.

**As built (stage caches; `graph/lens.rs`, `LensPlan::{field_key, flares_key, field_is_identity}`).** `Z = L2(L1(X))` reads Edges and Anamorphic but not Flares; the flare terms of L3, `F (core + tail + items)` (`ym_lens::flare_terms`, `f64`), read Flares and Anamorphic but not Edges and not `Z`. The two halves are cached on their own, and the picture is `(1 − a) Z + terms` in the one expression and order `ym_lens::apply` uses (`apply` is `apply_field`, `flare_terms` and `add_flares`; a test pins that a cached `Z` or cached terms give the bits a fresh run gives):

- `lens_field_groups` and `lens_field_tiles` hold `Z`, keyed by the raw group's key, `LensPlan::field_key` (lens, data versions, Edges, Anamorphic, camera, frame, axis, the planes' key) and `k` or the tile; `lens_flare_groups` and `lens_flare_tiles` hold the terms, keyed by `LensPlan::flares_key` (lens, Flares, Anamorphic, camera, frame, axis, `g0`, f-number) and `k` or the tile. `lens_groups` and `lens_tiles` still hold the finished picture. With Edges and Anamorphic at 0 the field stage is the identity (`field_is_identity`) and `Z` is the raw group itself, not cached.
- A Flares move runs L3's terms over the cached `Z` (`lens_flares` counts one run, `lens_fields` none); an Edges move runs L1 and L2 and reuses the terms; an Anamorphic move runs both. On the device the terms are not cached (a Flares move uploads nothing and the kernel is cheap, below); `Z` is cached as device images.
- **Which stage costs what** (the CPU reference, `cargo run -p ym-lens --example bench_stages`, a 1500 × 1000 group of a 6000 × 4000 frame, 60/50/50, the dev profile, CPU seconds; before the L3 changes below / after): Vintage L1 1.8 / 1.9, L2 5.6 / 5.0, L3 6.1 / 0.4, the whole lens 14.1 / 8.6; Default L1 1.9 / 1.9, L2 2.0 / 1.5, L3 6.2 / 0.2, the whole lens 9.5 / 3.9. L3 dominated, because of the ghosts of many sources (the block under *L3*); after the change L2 does.

On the 24 MP photo of the *Measured* bullet above (`lens_timing_of_the_views_on_24_megapixels`, same machine under load, so the wall times carry the colour pass and the raw work too; the lens's own time in brackets): the fit view's Flares move 10.1 s becomes 3.2 s (lens 0.5 s), its Edges move 10.4 s becomes 5.7 s (lens 3.4 s), the first view with the lens (planes and group) 10.7 s becomes 8.1 s (lens 5.9 s), and the 100 % view's Flares move 21.3 s becomes 5.4 s (lens 2.7 s).

**As built (the lens on the device; `graph/lens_device.rs`, `ym-gpu` `kernels/lens_{reshape,field,flare}.rs`).** The decisions the build made:

- **When.** `GpuStages::LENS` (bit 7, in `ALL` and `USED_BY_EXPORTS`, not in `SIGNED_OFF` until the Mac run; `YELLOWMOON_GPU` names it `lens`) and a view whose results stay on the device (`Job::keeps_on_device`), or an export on the device. Otherwise the CPU route above, which is also the fallback.
- **A tile is one job.** A reduced group is made in output tiles of 1024 px, the 100 % view on the raw group's 512 px grid, an export in its rectangle. A job gathers the raw window (the tile grown by the halo of L1 and L2, or of L3) from the raw group's pieces where they are (`encode_gather`; a raw tile that lives on the host is uploaded inside the job), runs L1 (where it acts) and L2 into `Z`, runs L3 over `Z`, and the result is a `ResidentImage` of pieces that the sampler and the colour pass read directly. No pixel is read back for the lens: a Flares move on the fit view reads back the finished frame and nothing else (a test counts the bytes). Every pixel is a function of its absolute position and of the input within the halo, so tiles of any size give the whole frame's bits (D5).
- **Per photo on the device** (`LensDevice`): the spread functions of the lens on the camera, the tail plane and spike tables of the planes, the lens's rim, aperture and normal-tail tables, and the scale data (the glow core's taps, the drawn items and their bins) of the last six plan shapes. None depends on Flares, so a Flares move uploads nothing (`F` and `1 − a` are uniforms); an Edges move changes a uniform.
- **L1 is a kernel too.** Where L1 acts, the tile gathers the raw window L1 reads (L2's input rectangle grown by L1's halo, `BokehPass::halo`, united with the window L3's glow core reads), runs the twelve reshaping dispatches over L2's input rectangle (*As built (L1 on the device)*, section 4) and L2 reads their output; L3 still reads the raw picture. The blur map is uploaded once per photo with the planes and the balls' shapes once per plan shape and scale (`LensDevice`), so an Anamorphic or Edges move uploads a few tens of kilobytes and reads back the finished frame and nothing else. The CPU lens is the fallback and the reference.
- **The light's colour is a kernel too (`lens_light`).** Where Edges or Anamorphic move light (`!field_is_identity`), the tile's raw window goes through `encode_light` (`ym-gpu` `kernels/lens_light.rs`, `shaders/lens_light.wgsl`, the rule in `lens_light_colour.wgsl`, shared with `lfl_restore`) before L1 reshapes it and L2 gathers it; L3 still reads the picture as it is. The rule is a function of one pixel and the clip levels, so tiles of 512 and 1024 equal the whole frame bit for bit and a pixel the rule leaves alone keeps the input's bits (`tests/gpu_lens_light.rs`: worst difference to the CPU reference 9.5e-7 of values up to 3.42, 2.8e-7 relative, bound 4e-7; 41 867 samples changed, 133 093 untouched and exact; poison equals plain).
- **Failures.** A device error in a tile falls back to the CPU lens for the request with a note (`The graphics card could not apply the lens, so the CPU did: …`) and ends the photo's use of the device lens, except an error about size; a shader that does not compile ends it for the process; a strict policy (`Require`) fails the request; an export starts again wholly on the CPU (`Job::take_gpu_restart`), so its file is the CPU export. `tests/gpu_faults.rs` pins each fault (`oom`, `shader`, `lost`, `tile@N`, `cancel@N`) on an export with a lens.
- **Exports** follow the other stages: the planes of an export are still made from the CPU reference group, so the app and `ym` write the same file (`crates/ym-cli/tests/parity_gpu.rs`). The device export equals the CPU export within the kernels' tolerance (the largest difference in a 16-bit PNG was 106 of 65535, none of 1.44 million samples over 256), not bit for bit: the two routes are different arithmetic, as with every other device stage. A photo with reshaped bokeh (L1's kernels in every lens tile job) differs a little more at a few pixels: worst 1025 of 65535, 29 of 1.44 million samples over 256, all at places where a threshold of L1 (the sharp-edge test, the strand test, a ball's box) decides on raw groups that differ in the sixth digit (the CPU's and the device's raw chains); the same L1 on one input differs from its reference by 9.4e-5 at the worst pixel.
- **Tests** (`tests/render_lens.rs`, device cases skip without an adapter): the CPU lens against the device lens at the fit view and the 100 % view (1 code of 255), with the raw group on the host and on the device; the reshaped case, at the fit view and the 100 % view, and Anamorphic and Edges moves that read back the frame and not the group (1 code); the counters of a slider move on tiles and on a group; no group readback; identity at all-zero amounts with no lens pass; the export tail; viewport jobs between the tiles of an export change nothing (D7).

## 4. The stages

Starting values below (thresholds, caps, the headroom) are set by eye on the CC0 night, backlit and shallow-focus photos of `docs/test-photo-sources.md` and kept in `ym_lens::consts` or the lens data, each with a doc comment.

### L1 · Bokeh reshaping

It reshapes the blur already in the photo and never adds blur where there is none. Blurring each out-of-focus pixel along one direction, by an amount proportional to its own blur, turns a round disc of radius `r` into an oval `r` wide and `q·r` tall, and leaves pixels in focus untouched (anisotropic defocus magnification; Bae and Durand, 2007). Narrowing a disc would need deconvolution and is not done.

1. **The blur map** (radius `b`, in half-diagonal units), on the lens plane.
   - The bokeh detector (section 7) gives the map and a confidence per pixel (the learned network's map when its model is installed, else the classical detector's); nothing else goes into it.
   - With fewer than 50 measured edges, or less than 2 % of the frame out of focus (5 % with the network) and fewer than 5 clear balls, nothing is reshaped and the render says why (the note's "little out-of-focus background"). Out of focus is `b ≥ 1.5·b_min` with confidence for the classical detector (2 %) and `P ≥ 0.9` for the network (5 %, section 7, *The gate's share*).
   - Per tile the map is upsampled with a joint bilateral filter guided by the tile's own pixels (radius 8, `docs/17` D4), so hair and fine edges follow the picture, and it is set to 0 where the tile's local gradient energy shows a sharp edge. No matting model is used (section 11).
2. **Bright balls** (the shapes a viewer reads first) are found on the lens plane by their size, not through the blur map, whose edge measure reads a sharp rim as sharp: a scale-space search for bright blobs on the square root of the brightest channel (the colour of a light counts, not only its luminance), each measured as the region above 40 % of the way from its ground to its plateau. A ball is compact (at least 0.12 brighter than its ground in square-root units, 2.5 times in light, axes within 1.6, filling 80 % of its ellipse, a ring of which at most 15 % is above half way from the ground to the plateau: a light beside a bright thing, the curl of a hook or a neighbour in a cluster, is still a light), may have a soft rim (the contrast it needs grows with the rim's width above what a ball of its size always has; a rim wider than 0.9 radii needs the blur around it to say the ground is out of focus and, with the learned map, the map to call at least half of the ball's own disc blurred (a bud in a blurred garden has the blur all around it and is a smooth bright thing in focus, not a light), and 1.3 radii is the most) and may be clipped, coloured or dim; and the edges around it must not say its ground is sharper than it is (a lamp in focus), unless the scene has at least three such lights. At most 512, brightest first; of two closer than 1.2 radii the dimmer is dropped. Each records its centre, radius, rim softness, energy per channel above its ring and the ring's plane fit, and whether it is *clear* (it also passes the strict rules of the first build: at most a twentieth of its ring bright, a rim of at most 0.9 radii, no sharp ground around it); only clear balls count towards "the scene is one of lights" (section 7).
3. **Take the balls out.** In each ball's disc the picture is replaced by the plane fit of its ring (the ring's pixels that read sharp, a hat or a hand beside the light, are left out of the fit), by the ball's weight: 1 over the ball, falling smoothly to 0 half a pixel beyond the disc's edge over the glow's width (`BALL_GLOW_FADE`: the zone between the ball's radius and the disc's; at least a pixel), so that no edge of the disc shows where a neighbour's light overlaps it. The disc reaches 1.15 r plus 1.5 px, or farther: to where the ball's own light has died away into its ground (at most 2 r plus 1.5 px, and never across a pixel that reads sharp). What stayed in the picture would be stretched by the gather and stand beside the stretched ball as a pillar; a ball whose light does not die away (one on a bright region, one in a cluster) keeps the 1.15 r disc.
4. **Stretch.** Two one-dimensional gathers, first across the anamorphic axis (vertical on the canvas), then tangential. Each pixel `p` averages samples on a line through it of half-length `ℓ(p)`:
   - the line is a biweight, the weight `(1 − u²)²` of a tap at `u` times the half-length from the pixel (zero in value and slope at both ends), not a box: a box's two ends cut the picture at an edge, and a small change of a pixel's line (its length, a sample dropping out) then changed its value by a visible step, which read as stripes ('curtains') on a textured blurred ground;
   - vertical: `ℓ = √(7/3)·(√(3 (q² − 1)) / 2)·b(p)` (2.29 b at `q = 2`), where `q = 1 + (Q_max − 1)·anamorphic/100` and `Q_max = 2` (section 12, decision 3). A disc of radius `b` has a one-dimensional variance of `b²/4` along a line, an oval `q` times taller `q²b²/4`, and a biweight line of half-length `ℓ` adds `ℓ²/7` (a box line of half-length `L` adds `L²/3`, the first build's `ℓ = 1.5 b`), so this `ℓ` gives a texture blurred by a disc the spread of a texture blurred by the oval. The shorter `ℓ = (q − 1)·b` matches only the oval's extent and gave about half its extra spread, which read as a faint stretch. (No one-dimensional line turns a disc into an ellipse: the ellipse's slice at each distance from its axis needs its own line, two points `(q − 1) h` either side for a slice of half-height `h`, so the best line of any shape, found by least squares, is two touching discs, and the spread is what the line is matched on.);
   - tangential: `ℓ = e·(√(7/3)·σ(ρ)·b(p) + a(ρ)·H)`, `e` = edges/100, `H` the half-diagonal in pixels, held to `0.10 H`, with the lens's swirl profile `σ(ρ)` (blur radii, 0 at the centre) and its astigmatism `a(ρ)` (half-diagonals, 0 at the centre): the first term grows with the blur of the pixel, the second does not (the tangential and the sagittal focal surfaces part with the field radius, however far the point is out of focus), so even a mildly blurred background swirls towards the edges (the shipped lenses' tables and the Petzval's rule: *As built (field swirl)*);
   - a sample counts with the weight of a smoothstep of its blur over the pixel's own, nothing at one half, everything from 0.85, times the profile, and the weights are renormalised (the pixel's own value has the weight 1), so a sharp subject never smears into the background and the background never spills onto it, and no sample is let in or out at one place;
   - `ℓ` is 0 below half of `b_min` (0.004), grows to its full value at `b_min` and the blur is capped at `b_cap` (0.03), so the halo is at most `2.29·b_cap` (vertical) plus `min(e (1.53 σ_max b_cap + a_max), 0.10)` (tangential), in half-diagonals, plus the upsampling's 8 px and the feather below; samples are spaced evenly, at most 65 a line, read with linear interpolation;
   - the blur a gather uses is the map's, held at the cap, times a ramp that fades the stretch out towards the border of the map (a subject's outline, a hole where the detector found sharp detail) over 80 % of the longest line (2 to 96 px; *As built (the ramp, round 3)*): a pixel's own line and what it takes from others both die away at the border, so the glow around a sharp subject is not dragged out along the lines (a blurred bright halo, 30 px wide, of an ice tip was a hard-edged bright column). A pixel takes a blur from the map only when its colour is near the footprint mean of some blurred cell around it: a bright bud against its leaves is not near any, so the picture is not torn along the bud's outline;
   - where the picture shows detail sharper than its blur says (the sharp-edge test of *As built*), the stretch is held back by the *density* of those places in a 15 x 15 window around the pixel, from nothing below 0.2 to altogether above 0.7, not pixel by pixel: a speck of a few pixels holds nothing back, a strand's length or a region of detail holds everything, with edges as wide as the window.
5. **Stretch the balls' own light.** A ball is not drawn again from a model of it (an ellipse with the energy it had: its edge was hard, its middle flat and too bright, and a dark ring showed where the take-out's fill lay beside it). Its own pixels are stretched. The light of a ball is the picture above the plane fit of its ring, per channel, so its soft rim, its glow, their colour and a clipped core are all in it. Each pixel of the stretched picture inside the ball's patch reads that light where the pixel was before the stretch (`d/f` along the long axis, `d` across, `d` the offset from the ball's centre, `f` the stretch of the patch; bilinear in the picture) and adds it with the ball's weight of step 3, so that the take-out and the return of the light are one smooth function and no ring is left. The surface brightness is kept, not the energy: a ball stretched twice is as bright as it was over twice the area (a clipped ball, whose energy is not known, is not dimmed), its edge across the stretch is as wide as it was and along it `f` times as wide. The patch is the disc of step 3 stretched by `f = 1 + (q − 1) + (q_t − 1)`: `(q − 1) r` across the anamorphic axis plus `(q_t − 1) r` along the tangent (the sign chosen to add), with `q_t = √(1 + 4 s²/3)`, at most 4, for `s = e (σ(ρ) + a(ρ) H / (k r))` (the aspect of a disc of radius `r` blurred along a tangential box line of half-length `s r`, which is what the tangential gather matches: 2 at `s` = 1.5; `k` = √(7/3), and the line held to `0.10 H` as the gather's is), the long axis turned to the sum. The lens's cat's-eye cuts the patch with a soft mask: the patch's ellipse intersected with a copy of it shifted radially (each by half the offset; Edges scales the offset; the mask's edge is at least 1.5 px and at most half the glow's width). The lens's rim profile (`bokeh.rim`) modulates the light as a gain relative to its mean over the disc (a flat profile changes nothing), over the ball and fading to 1 over 0.3 radii beyond its edge, and by the share of it the ball's rim takes (all of it below a rim 0.3 radii wide, none from 0.8: a soft glow has no rim for the profile to shape, and on the pink highlight of an ornament it drew rings). The light is read only where the map says the ball was taken out (`smoothstep` of the blur between half of `b_min` and `b_min`); where two discs overlap, the earlier (brighter) ball keeps its weight of the light of the shared place and the later one the rest (no edge where the discs meet, no light drawn twice); and the patch is left off the pixels of a sharp subject (known in focus, see *As built*) and strands in front of it (thin dark lines), and not where the blur map is merely unsure. Everything is a function of the ball and the pixel's absolute position, so edges are soft at every scale and tiles agree.

Where the detector is not confident, `b` is taken as 0 and nothing is stretched.

**As built** (`ym_lens::l1`; decisions the stage text left open):

- **Planes.** `LensPlanes::bokeh()` holds the map, the balls and the cells known to be in focus (the subject: below) only when `LensPlan::needs_blur_map()`; `LensPlanes::serves(plan)` says whether planes still fit a plan (the planes' key leaves the amounts out, so a plan that gains Anamorphic needs planes built again; `l1::apply` refuses planes without the map with `LensError::Frame`). `ym_lens::reshape` and `reshape_halo` run L1 alone (tests and diagnostics).
- **Upsampling.** The map is read at the tile's scale from the coarsest of its reductions whose cells are at least a pixel apart; each pixel takes the cells within `min(8, 1.6 × the spacing)` pixels, weighted by distance and by how close the tile's own colours (3 x 3 smoothed square roots) are to the cell's footprint mean, and a cell's weight is its confidence (a confident 0 counts); with less than a quarter of the weight confident the pixel is 0, and so is a value below `b_min`. A pixel also takes a blur only when its colour (three smoothed square roots) is within 0.25 of the footprint mean of some blurred cell of confidence 0.3 or more near it (a bright bud against its leaves is not), and the stretch fades out over a feather of 80 % of its longest line (2 to 96 px) towards the border of the blurred region, so the line lengths of neighbouring pixels differ gradually. The sharp-edge test compares the picture's gradient energy with that of its 3 x 3 mean over 5 x 5 windows (a sharp edge loses more than 1.8 times over, above 4 noise units: at 1.4 the crisp rims of defocused discs, edges of 2 to 3 px in a field blurred by ten or more, were flagged as detail and stayed as small unstretched bites), only where the blur is at least 4 px at the tile's scale, dilated by 2 px, and holds the stretch back by the density of its flags in a 15 x 15 window, not pixel by pixel (*As built (soft gathers)*); it is not applied along a ball's rim (7 px either side of its radius, which is itself a sharp edge) nor to the ball's own light inside it (a pixel as bright as half its plateau: a glass ornament's highlight), but it is to a thin dark line there (a pixel much darker than the pixels three either side of it along a row or a column: a strand in front of the light) and to the edge of a subject beside it. The halo is the two gathers' reach (or the reach of a stretched ball's patch, `(f − 1)` of its disc's radius, where that is more), the feather, the density window's 7 px and `ceil(support) + ceil(cell/2) + 3` (at least 7) px of the tile's own picture.
- **Gather.** Every pixel's line is `2⌈ℓ⌉ + 1` taps (at most 65, evenly spaced): the pixel's own value with the weight 1, then each tap with the biweight profile of its offset times the sample rule's smoothstep of its blur over the pixel's own, the blur at a tap read bilinearly like the picture, and the weights renormalised; the tangential gather the same along the tangent. The swirl profile is read at the plain field radius `ρ` (not the oval one of L2). (The first build summed a box line exactly from running integrals in 64-bit fixed point, then over its unit segments; the biweight has no end to cut and the taps are the same function of the pixel's position on the CPU and the device. The feather's and the density's window sums are running sums of counts, exact in `f64`, so any tiling gives the same bits.)
- **Balls.** Take-out radius `1.15 r` plus 1.5 pixels of the work grid, or where the ball's light has died away (the first ring beyond `r`, half a pixel wide, whose median is within the smaller of 3 % of the ball's height over its ground and 15 % of the ground, and at least 3 noise units, of the ground; at most `2 r` plus 1.5 px; never beyond the nearest pixel that reads sharp past the rim zone). Inside the take-out disc and a pixel beyond it the map is the ball's radius (except where the sharp-edge test says sharp or a pixel is known in focus; the extra pixel keeps the disc's own edge, a circle of whole pixels, from being where the take-out or the stretched light stops), and the stretch of those pixels fades out with the ball's weight towards the disc's edge (the vertical gather inside the disc smears what lies in it, and a seam showed where that ended); elsewhere the map has no reading of its own. The patch is the take-out disc stretched: semi-axes `a = f·r_out` along the long axis and `b = r_out` across, with `f = (r + |s|)/r` and `s = (q − 1) r` along the cross direction plus `(q_t − 1) r` along the tangent (sign chosen to add); the cat's-eye is two copies of the patch, each half the offset `c(ρ) · edges · r_out` away radially, intersected, with a soft edge; the rim table is read at `i / 15`; every ball is a record of eleven four-lane vectors on the device. A ball changes nothing (is neither taken out nor drawn) when `q`, the swirl and the cat's-eye leave its shape round.
- **Subject and the oval.** The planes hold the cells known to be in focus: the subject anchor and the places next to a strong sharp edge (the classical detector), the cells whose probability of being out of focus is at most 0.1 (the network's map); a region of fewer than 60 cells of the work grid (a dilated flag of a speck of noise or dust) is not a subject; they are pooled with the map's levels. A pixel is a subject when half of the weight of the cells around it, by the colour-guided weights of the upsampling, is known in focus; the oval is not drawn on a subject pixel that has no blur of its own, nor on a strand (a thin dark line that the picture's sharp-edge test flags; a flag on a speck does not count), and is drawn on every other pixel inside its shape, whatever the map says there (a map that is merely unsure, or a classical map's hole over a hanger, used to cut the oval: a flat top, or the ball missing altogether). Nothing the map holds is stamped on a subject pixel.

**As built (the ball's own light; `l1/draw.rs`, `l1/tile.rs` `take_out` and `redraw`, `consts.rs`)** (the owner's look at the sheets: "bokeh squeezed and it adds additional harder dark circle"). The build before this one drew every ball again from a model: an ellipse with the energy the ball had, a hard edge of a pixel (or a rim of the ball's measured width), a flat middle (a rim profile of the lens over a disc) that was too bright where the ball was clipped, its soft glow taken out of the picture and not put back, so that the fill of the take-out (the plane fit of the ring) lay around the ellipse as a darker circle, and specks and notches where the mask of the cat's-eye or a neighbour's light met the take-out disc. The ball's own pixels are stretched now (step 5 of *L1*), and what changed with it:

- **One weight for taking out and giving back.** `Shape::weight(d)`: 1 over the ball, falling by a smoothstep to 0 half a pixel beyond the take-out disc over `BALL_GLOW_FADE` (1.0: the whole glow zone) of the glow's width, at least a pixel. The take-out replaces `v` by `v + w (fit − v)`, the patch adds `w · (picture − fit)⁺` read at the position the pixel had before the stretch; where the two read the same pixel (the row through the ball's centre across the long axis) the sum is the original picture, so nothing is left to show a ring.
- **The disc's own edge never shows.** The map of a ball's disc (the ball's radius) reaches a pixel beyond the take-out disc and its stretch fades with the weight (the ramp inside a ball is at least the ball's weight), so the circle of whole pixels the take-out ends on is not where the take-out or the vertical gather inside it stops; with a hard stop the arc of the disc showed beside a neighbouring light (28322, ball 1, the pale disc to its right).
- **Shared light.** Where two balls' discs overlap (a cluster), the earlier, brighter ball keeps its weight of the light of the shared place and the later one the rest; a hard exclusion drew the later ball's patch with a cut along the earlier one's disc.
- **The lens's rim profile on soft balls.** The profile is measured on crisp discs; applied to the soft highlight of an ornament (rim 0.8 radii wide, 28322, the pink one on the red ornament) it drew faint concentric rings. A ball takes `1 − smoothstep(0.3, 0.8, softness)` of it (`RIM_SOFT_FROM`, `RIM_SOFT_TO`).
- **The tangential stretch is the stretch of the gather.** `q_t = √(1 + 4 (σ e)²/3)`: the tangential gather of half-length `σ e b` (box-equivalent) turns a disc into the spread of an ellipse of that aspect (2 at `σ e = 1.5`), and the patch is stretched by the same; the cat's-eye mask and Anamorphic combine with it as before (the patch's long axis is the sum of the two stretches).
- **Measured** (28322, the lens plane of 1737 x 1158, Anamorphic 100, Default, the four balls of its 27 that stand alone, the half-level region of the green channel): the stretch of the ball's own height over width **2.00** (1.94 to 2.15; the ellipse it replaced 2.29, 1.96 to 2.43: a cut-out taller than the ball), the light of the region over the ball's **1.85** (1.80 to 1.92; the ellipse 1.16, 0.97 to 1.26, which kept the energy): `q` times, less what the half-level region leaves out of the glow. The five brightest balls at 3 times and the pink highlight of the big ornament at 2 times, `off | before | after`, are in the review sheets (`compare/`): the ovals read as the original balls stretched, with their soft rim, their warm glow and colour, no darker circle and no flat white; the pink highlight stretches with its glow blending into the ornament and no crescent.
- **What stays.** Where a ball overlaps a light that is not itself a ball (a pale disc beside it), the neighbour's light inside the take-out disc is stretched with the ball and outside it is not: the seam is a soft arc now, not an edge, but it is there. Where a ball touches the border of the blur map or a sharp edge, the take-out and the patch stop at the map's pixel-level border (a stair of single pixels along a diagonal border, 28322 balls 5 and 6 at 6 times; the ellipse had the same stairs and a dark crescent besides).
- **Tests** (`tests/bokeh.rs`, `l1/draw.rs`): a soft ball with a glow on a flat ground has no rise of light beyond its edge and its edge along the stretch is `q` times the one across; discs and soft, clipped balls come out `q` times taller with `q` times the light (3 %); the whole frame's light grows by `(q − 1)` times the balls' light and by nothing else (1 %); a pixel reads the ball where it was before the stretch; two overlapping discs share the light without a step; a rim of 1 changes nothing, a soft ball takes none of the profile; the tiles equal the whole frame bit for bit (CPU and device: the halo includes the reach of a patch, `(f − 1) r_out`, since a pixel reads the picture and the map that far from itself).
- **Swirl (Petzval and Vintage; the tables below were raised and given an absolute part by *As built (field swirl)*, further down).** The Petzval's traced swirl was too small to see (`σ` 0.09 at half the field and 0.94 at the corner: tangential ovals of aspect 1.005 and 1.47). Now its `σ(ρ)` is `1.5 ((ρ − 0.3)/0.7)^1.28` outside `ρ = 0.3` and 0 inside (continuous slope, rising), at Edges 100 a tangential line of 1.5 blur radii at the corner (an oval twice as long as wide, `q_t = 2`), 0.30 at half the field (aspect 1.06), 0.85 at three quarters (1.40); the Vintage's is a third of it (0.5 at the corner, aspect 1.2); the other lenses keep their traced profiles (data README, *What is traced and what is tuned*, which has the rule). A ball becomes a tangential oval of aspect `q_t` with the cat's-eye cut, and with Anamorphic the two stretches add; the tangential gather uses the same biweight line (`√(7/3)·σ e b`), so the background's smear along the tangent matches the balls'. Measured: the elongation of the test balls at their field radii equals `q_t` to 3 % (`tests/bokeh.rs`); the shipped tables are pinned by a test.

**As built (L1 on the device; `ym-gpu` `kernels/lens_reshape.rs`, `shaders/lens_reshape.wgsl`, `ym_lens::device::{BokehPass, BokehPlaneData, BokehScaleData}`, `crates/ym-lens/src/l1/gpu.rs`).** The decisions the build made:

- **Passes.** Twelve dispatches over the regions of one tile (`BokehPass::tile(want)`, the one source of the rectangles for the CPU reference and the kernel: the output, the tangential gather's input `r1`, the vertical gather's input `r0` (`r1` grown by the vertical reach, or by the reach of a stretched ball's patch where that is more), the region the map is made over `rb` = `r0` and the feather, the region the sharp places are known over `rbs` = `rb` and the density window, and the picture read `need` = `rbs` and the upsampling's reach): the cells' colour guide (`lens_reshape_guide`: the footprint means of the root colours of the cells `rbs` reads); the blur map over `rb` (`_blur`: the colour-guided upsampling, the acceptance tests); the sharp places in four passes (`_lumroot` over `rbs` grown by 6, `_grad` the gradient energies of the field and of its 3 × 3 mean over `rbs` grown by 4, `_flags` over `rbs` grown by 2, `_dilate` over `rbs`, which dilates and lifts them around a ball's rim and light unless a strand or a subject is there); `_protect` over `rb` (the density of the sharp places in the 15 × 15 window holds the blur back, smoothly; the ball's own blur inside its take-out disc and a pixel beyond; and the bit that says a drawn patch may not cover the pixel); `_cover` and `_ramp` over `r0` (the feather's counts, then the ramp, which stores the gathers' blur: the map's, held at the cap, times the ramp, and inside a ball's disc at least the ball's weight); `_takeout` over `r0` (each ball's disc replaced by its ring's plane fit by the ball's weight); `_vgather` over `r1`; `_final`: the tangential gather over the output and the balls' own light drawn back (it reads the picture, which is why it binds the input, and the blur map). Six of the passes read the tile's own picture, so the input window is `need`; the intermediate planes live in the job's scratch buffers (the sharp-edge chain's fields and the taken-out picture share one, since the chain is dead before the picture is made).
- **Data, uploaded once.** Per photo (`BokehPlaneBuffer`, cached beside the planes in `LensDevice`): the blur map's levels as `(a, c, s)` per cell and the summed-area table of the blurred cells (`LensPlanes::bokeh_plane_data`). Per plan shape and scale (`BokehScaleBuffers`, the last six kept; `LensPlan::bokeh_shape_key` and the frame, the scale and the planes in the key): the lens's field radii, swirl, rim (and the rim's mean over a disc) and astigmatism (fourteen four-lane vectors of header), the geometry of the map level's cells (centre as an integer and a fraction, footprint), the shape of every ball the plan changes (made by the CPU reference's own `shape_of` in `f64`: centre, the patch's semi-axes and long axis, the cat's-eye's offset and the mask's edge, the stretch, the share of the rim profile the ball takes, the take-out radius, the ball's radius, the reach, the blur a pixel in the ball gets, the ring's plane fit, and the earlier balls that share its disc with their weights' widths; eleven four-lane vectors a ball) and the bins that cull them (32 px on the absolute pixel grid, every ball whose influence can reach one of its pixels, in list order). The detector, the balls and the map stay on the CPU, once per photo. An Edges or Anamorphic move changes the shapes and uploads them (a few tens of kilobytes); a Flares move uploads nothing and does not run L1 at all.
- **One function of the position.** The pixels of a tile equal those of the whole frame bit for bit because every pixel is a function of its absolute column and row, the planes and the input within the halo, read in the CPU reference's order: the intermediate fields are read with the coordinates clamped to the frame (the reference's clamp to a region that touches the frame's edge, since a region never ends inside the frame within reach of a pixel it serves); a ball's centre and a cell's centre are an integer and a fraction, so the offset that enters a distance is small (D4); a tap on a line is the pixel's integer position plus an offset of at most the line's length; the balls a pixel meets are those of its 32 px bin in list order (the take-out blends compose in that order, the oval's light is added in that order). Three places where the reference was not already a function of the position alone were changed with the kernel: the sharp-edge test no longer waits for a pixel of the region with a blur of 4 px (it ran only when the region had one, so a region with a ball and no such pixel had no flags where its neighbour had them; it now also runs when a ball is near, which is where the flags are read); the vertical gather's exact mean is the direct sum above; and the ball's oval is drawn only in the box of its reach, `[floor(cx − reach), ceil(cx + reach)]` by the same for y, in the kernel as in the reference (the first-order distance of the coverage leaves a faint tail beyond it).
- **The gathers' loops.** Both gathers are the same loop on both sides: the pixel's own value with the weight 1, then up to `LR_HALF_TAPS` (32) taps either side at offsets `k ℓ / n` (`n = ⌈ℓ⌉`, at least 1), each the biweight profile times the smoothstep of its blur (read bilinearly) over the pixel's own, read bilinearly from the field; the loop stops at the first tap whose profile is 0. The kernel needs no sum of unit segments and no table, and a tile cannot change a pixel: a tap is the pixel's integer position plus an offset of at most the line's length. The CPU reference is the same function in `f64`. The cost per pixel is the taps' reads (`2⌈ℓ⌉ + 1`, at most 65, each of two pixels of the field and two of the blur), the density of the sharp places in `_protect` (225 reads of a plane of 0 and 1) and, in `_final`, a loop over the balls of the pixel's bin, each a handful of operations where it does not reach the pixel and about eighty where it does.
- **Fast-math (K8).** The sums that decide something (the weights of the upsampling and the tests on them, the luminance, the energies and their window sums, the 3 × 3 mean, the strand test, the product that sets a line's length and so its tap count) sit behind the OR barrier; the kernel's only integers are the position arithmetic and the counts of the feather. The kernels have not been through the clang model of Metal's compiler.
- **Parity** (`crates/ym-gpu/tests/gpu_lens_reshape.rs`, lavapipe): 45 cases (every lens at four Edges and Anamorphic pairs on a frame of out-of-focus balls over a blurred texture, two lenses on the y axis, a sharp subject with strands, a night scene of soft and clipped balls), 90 million samples: all but 9 samples within 2e-5; the largest difference 1.09e-4 (white = 1; balls reach 14; a Petzval case at Edges 30, Anamorphic 50, where a tap count or a sample weight went the other way in one pixel). The bound is 2e-5 plus 1e-5 of the value (a ball carries more than white), a flipped decision allowed in 5e-6 of the samples and none above 5e-4. The frame at 1/2 and 1/4 (the map's levels 1 and 2): 6.7e-6 and 1.9e-6. The far corner of an 8658 × 5772 frame: 8.9e-7, 3.3e-7 and 4.5e-7 at the near corner, the centre and the far corner. Tiles of 512 and 1024 equal the whole frame bit for bit (1400 × 900, balls across the boundaries, both axes; against the reference, Vintage 5.0e-5, Petzval 8.6e-5); a source cut out with another origin gives the same bits; poison equals plain; a run in between changes nothing (D7); the binding is 81 MiB at a 1024 px tile of a 6000 × 4000 frame (Petzval 100/100, a halo of 527 px: the Petzval's tangential line of 1.5 blur radii at the corner reaches 2.3 `b_cap`, so its halo grew with it); no pass exists with Anamorphic and Edges at 0, or on planes with little blur. On hardware the bound is 1e-4 until the Metal run.
- **Timing** (lavapipe, a software rasteriser on 4 shared cores at a load of about 3, so relative numbers only; the reference at the dev profile): an Anamorphic move on the real photo's plane (28322, 31 balls, 9 % blurred, the learned map): the fit-view group 0.93 to 1.02 s in the reference and 1.30 to 1.34 s on the device with the uploads of the shapes and the picture and the readback (2.1 s the first time, which compiles the twelve pipelines), where the build before the soft gathers took 0.76 to 0.88 s and 0.73 to 0.79 s; a 1024 px tile at full resolution 0.95 to 1.03 s against 1.8 s (0.86 to 0.98 s and 0.68 to 0.80 s before). The 45 parity cases take 18.7 s in the reference and 25.3 s on the device (14 s and 16 to 18 s). The device is slower than the reference now: lavapipe has no per-pass timestamps, so where the time goes was not measured, but each tap of the two gathers reads the blur bilinearly besides the picture (the sample rule's smoothstep needs the blur at the tap), `_final` loops over the balls of its bin with a patch of some eighty operations where one reaches the pixel, and the 15 x 15 density window of `_protect` costs 225 reads a pixel (a version of it without the clamps changed nothing, so it is not the cost). Whether a move reaches the target of section 9 (100 ms) is a Mac number (LC-H3); a separable density (a thirteenth, horizontal pass) and a blur read once per two taps are the first things to try if the Mac number is short.
- **Wiring** (`graph/lens_device.rs`): the tile job gathers the one raw window, runs `encode_reshape` over L2's input rectangle and `encode_field` on its output; the cached `Z` of a Flares move skips both. Failures follow the block *the lens on the device*; `tests/gpu_faults.rs` pins each fault on an export of a photo of defocused lights (`reshaped`, so L1's twelve dispatches are in the lens tile job) beside the noise photo, and `crates/ym-cli/tests/parity_gpu.rs` holds the app's export and `ym render` to the same bytes on that photo, with the dispatch count showing L1 ran (the learned map's verdict on a synthetic scene is its own business, so these tests use a data folder without it).

**As built (field swirl; `l1/draw.rs` `tangent_half_length`, `l1/tile.rs`, `consts.rs`, `field.astigmatism` in `lens.json`)** (the owner: "swirly, vortex-like"). The swirl of the build before drew a tangential line proportional to the blur, `√(7/3)·σ·e·b`, and at the fit view the blur of a background is a few pixels, so the lights became 2:1 ovals at the corner and the texture hardly moved: a before and after of Petzval 100/100 showed almost nothing, and a stronger table (3.5:1 at the corner) only stretched the lights further without making the picture swirl. The owner chose the *field swirl*: the outer field gets extra blur along the circles, growing towards the edges (field curvature), so even a mildly blurred background swirls; in-focus subjects near the edges soften too, as with the real lens.

- **The line.** A Petzval has strong astigmatism off axis: the tangential and the sagittal focal surfaces part with the field radius, so a point's blur is longer along the tangent than across it by an amount that does not depend on how far out of focus the point already is. The half-length of the tangential gather's biweight line is now

  `ℓ_t(p) = knee(b) · min( e · (k · σ(ρ) · b(p) + a(ρ) · H),  0.10 H )`

  with `k = √(7/3)` (`PROFILE_SCALE`), `e` = Edges/100, `b(p)` the pixel's blur (the gathers' blur: the map's, held at `b_cap`, times the ramp), `H` the half-diagonal in pixels, `ρ` the plain field radius, `σ(ρ)` the lens's swirl (blur radii: the half-length of the box line with the spread of the gather) and the new `a(ρ)` its astigmatism (half-diagonals; `field.astigmatism`, nine values like `swirl`, 0 at the centre, not falling). The first term is the old one and grows with the blur; the second does not. The `knee` (nothing at half of `b_min`, all from `b_min`) multiplies both, so the whole line fades in where the pixel starts to count as blurred, and `TANGENT_REACH_MAX` = 0.10 holds the line to a tenth of the half-diagonal (at `b_cap` the old swirl, 1.5, reached 0.069; the new `σ` of 2 reaches 0.092 and with the astigmatism 0.122, which the bound cuts: a smaller halo and binding, paid for by the few pixels of the largest blur at the corner, where `b` is above 0.0229). One function, `tangent_half_length(e, σ, a, b_px, H)`, serves the reference, the halo, the balls and (written again in WGSL, behind the same barriers) the kernel. Everything else of the gather is as it was: the biweight profile with no ends that cut (so no curtains), the sample rule (a tap counts by a smoothstep of its blur over the pixel's own: an in-focus pixel has no blur, is never touched and never pulled in, and the background never spills onto it), the ramp towards the border of the map, the density of the sharp places holding the line back.
- **The tables** (`data/lenses/README.md`, *What is traced and what is tuned*, has the rule). Petzval: `σ(ρ) = 2 t^1.3` and `a(ρ) = 0.03 t^1.64`, `t = (ρ − 0.2)/0.8` from `ρ` 0.2 outwards and 0 inside it (continuous slope, rising), stored at the nine radii: `σ` 0 · 0 · 0.054 · 0.277 · 0.559 · 0.879 · 1.229 · 1.604 · 2.0 (a disc becomes an oval 2.5 times as long as wide at the corner: 1.5 and 2.0 before) and `a` 0 · 0 · 0.0003 · 0.0025 · 0.0060 · 0.0106 · 0.0162 · 0.0227 · 0.030, which is 27 px at the corner of a 1500 px fit frame (`H` 901 px) and 108 px on 24 MP (`H` 3606), 5 px and 22 px at half the field. Vintage: a third of each (`σ` 0.67 and `a` 0.01 at the corner). The `σ` of the stronger table tried first (2.9 at the corner, 3.5:1) was too much once the astigmatism acts on top of it: the pink ornament of 28322 at half the field became a vertical smear with its hook a bright sliver; 1.5 (the old table) with the astigmatism was already plain, 2.0 keeps the lights' form. **Astigmatism of the traced lenses: 0.** The spread functions of the tables are the traced best-focus spreads, and L2 draws them over the whole frame, defocused or not; the tangential minus the radial rms spread of the traced tables (mean over the eight wavelengths, half-diagonals) at `ρ` 0.5, 0.75 and 1 is Default −0.0001, +0.0003, +0.0009, Classic −0.0012, −0.0026, −0.0000, Vintage −0.0024, −0.0015, +0.0009, Triplet −0.0008, −0.0043, −0.0015, Sonnar −0.0004, −0.0000, +0.0010, Detuned −0.0008, −0.0005, +0.0006 (and the Petzval's own +0.0004, +0.0006, +0.0001): below a pixel where positive, mostly negative (coma), so putting it in the bokeh gather would count it twice. Data: `field.astigmatism` is optional (a file without it reads as zeros, so older data still load) and checked (nine non-negative values, 0 at the centre, not falling); the seven lens files carry it and their `version` is 2.
- **Where it acts.** Wherever L1 acts: on the pixels the blur map calls blurred (the learned map's, with the gates of *As built*), under the same sample rules, so the pixels of a sharp subject keep their values. In-focus content near the edges is softened by L2, not by this: with Petzval at Edges 100 and Flares 0 the gradient of the sharp dog of 28322 (the left of the frame, fit view) is kept as local contrast (gradient over mean light, the sharpest 15 % of 24 px blocks of the fit frame, L2 alone, Petzval, Edges 100, Flares 0): 0.86, 0.76 and 0.61 of its value without the lens at `ρ` 0 to 0.3, 0.3 to 0.5 and 0.5 to 0.7 on 28322, and 0.91, 0.64, 0.49 and (0.7 to 0.85) 0.53 on 26276, whose dog's fur reaches the outer field; so L2 already softens the outer field visibly and needed no change. (It is the traced spread functions, widened by Edges: `psf_half_width` 0.012 at the corner, 11 px at the fit view.)
- **Balls.** The ball's own light is stretched by the same line: a ball of `r` pixels is a tangential box line of half-length `ℓ_t(r)/k` blurred, `s = e (σ + a H/(k r))` radii (`tangent_half_length` at the ball's radius, the held reach included), so `q_t = √(1 + 4 s²/3)`, and a small ball of the outer field, whose astigmatism is many of its radii, becomes a lemon along its circle: on the 1500 px fit frame at `ρ` 0.9 (`σ` 1.68, `a` 0.024) a ball of 12 px has `s` 2.9 and `q_t` 3.5, one of 24 px 2.3 and 2.8, and the balls of 8 px and less are held at 4; at `ρ` 0.6 (`σ` 0.82, `a` 0.0097) a ball of 8 px is 2.0 and one of 24 px 1.6. Kept smooth (the stretched patch of *the ball's own light*, no ring, no hard edge, the cat's-eye mask as before). `q_t` is held to `TANGENT_ASPECT_MAX` = 4 (a ball of 2 px at the corner would be a line: it is a lemon 4 to 1, a line of 8 radii). With Anamorphic the two stretches add as before (the long axis is their vector sum).
- **Streaks and curtains.** The line is long (27 px at the corner of the fit frame, plus the swirl) and the owner disliked the vertical line texture the first anamorphic gather made on textured blur, so the 100 % windows of the corners of 59499 (foliage) and 26276 (a deck, fur) and the outer field of 28322, 50585 and 39211 were looked at, `off | now | new`, at Petzval 100/100 and 30/70 and Vintage 100/100/100 (the review's compare pages): the textured blur is smeared along the circles with no parallel streak texture and no column that steps, because the profile is the biweight (a line of `ℓ` whose length varies slowly with the position, zero in value and slope at both ends), so no short radial smoothing proportional to `a` was needed and none is drawn. Fur and hair in focus keep their strands (L2 softens them as before).
- **Halo, memory.** `BokehPass::halo` covers the longest line, `t_halo` = `ℓ_t` at `b_cap`, `σ_max` and `a_max` plus the pad: at most `0.10 H` + 1. On an 8658 × 5772 frame (50 MP, `H` = 5203) with the largest blur (`b_cap`), Anamorphic 100 and the Petzval at Edges 100: vertical 359 px, tangential 522 px, feather 96 px, halo 999 px (before this change, with `σ` 1.5 and no astigmatism: 836). The largest binding of a 1024 px tile is 104.5 MiB and of a 512 px tile 72.1 MiB; the 1024 px tile needed 171.5 MiB with the scratch laid out as it was (five planes: the square roots, two energies, the flags and the sharp places), and about 139 MiB before this change at that blur (halo 836). The scratch is three planes now (the flags are written over the square roots, which are dead once the energies are made, and the sharp places over the first energy, which is dead once the flags are made): `ym-gpu` `lens_reshape.rs` `layout`, with the kernel unchanged (the offsets are in the uniform). On a 6000 × 4000 frame with the test scene (`b` up to 0.0194) the largest binding of a 1024 px tile is 65.4 MiB (halo 683 px; 81 MiB, halo 527 px, before).
- **Device.** `lens_reshape.wgsl` `lr_final_main` computes the same line (`lr_prof_at` reads the swirl and the astigmatism from the header of the scale's table, now 14 vectors: radii, swirl, rim, the rim's mean, astigmatism; the constant `LR_TAN_MAX`), the balls' shapes come from the reference's own `shape_of`, so the kernel's balls changed with no new code. Parity on lavapipe (`gpu_lens_reshape.rs`, shipped data): 45 cases, 90 045 000 samples, the largest difference 3.94e-4 (Vintage, Edges 60, Anamorphic 70: a tap count that flipped), 5 samples above 2e-5 plus 1e-5 of the value, none above 5e-4; at 1/2 and 1/4 of the frame 4.5e-6 and 1.8e-6; the corners of an 8658 × 5772 frame 8.9e-7 (near), 3.3e-7 (centre) and 6.9e-7 (far). Tiles of 512 and 1024 equal the whole frame bit for bit (1400 × 900, Vintage and Petzval; against the reference 5.0e-5 with 3 samples over 2e-5, and 1.8e-5). The scratch's new layout changed no bit of the 45 cases (the same worst difference). Timing: the 45 cases take 107 to 124 s in the reference and 125 to 157 s on the device (uploads included; two runs) with the machine at a load of 10 to 20 on 4 cores, so only relatively; the tangential gather now runs 55 to 65 taps in the outer field where a mildly blurred pixel ran under 10, and the lens of 28322 at Petzval 100/100 on the fit view took 21.5 s on the CPU reference where it took 13.0 s (Flares 100 and the veil are part of it; a similar load).

### L2 · Field optics

With `e = edges/100`, and `e' = e + (1 − e)·κ·(q − 1)` the **edge amount**: Edges plus the attachment's own share, so that at Edges 0 the anamorphic terms below still act, alone (`e' = e` at Anamorphic 0; at `e' = 0` L2 is the identity). Every `e` below is `e'`.

- **Point spread.** Each lens has spread functions at nine field radii (`ρ` = 0, 1/8, …, 1) and eight wavelengths, 33 × 33 cells over ±`w(ρ)` (cell-centred, section 6) in a frame turned to the radial (x, outwards from the frame's centre) and tangential (y, anticlockwise on the screen) directions. The plan projects them onto the camera's three channels (section 6). A pixel gathers with the blend of its two neighbouring radii (linear in `ρ'`, held at the last table beyond 1), turned to its own direction and scaled in width to a half-width of `ω = cap(e'·(1 + S)·w(ρ'))·H` pixels, `H` being the half-diagonal in pixels and `S` the side softness below (`w(ρ')` and the cap: *As built (the field law)*). The channels' kernels have their own centroids, which is the lateral colour fringe, kept at 0.3 of the traced offset from green (`LATERAL_COLOR_SCALE`, same block); the anamorphic attachment multiplies the axis part of that offset by `1 + h·(q − 1)` (horizontal fringing). The CPU reference does so by reading each channel of the gathered picture that much further along the axis (linear interpolation between neighbouring pixels, the gain applied after): to first order the same as translating the kernel, for one gather instead of three. The width is bent towards `w_max` = 0.012 (about 62 px on a 50 MP frame) and never exceeds it.
- **Taps.** A kernel of half-width `ω` is `n × n` point masses at its cell centres, `n = min(17, 2·⌈ω⌉ + 1)`: the 33 × 33 table resampled area-weighted and renormalised to sum 1, each mass read from the input by bilinear interpolation. A kernel below `ω` = 0.5 px is a copy of the input (the gain still applies). A kernel whose cells would be more than one texel apart at 17 taps (`ω` above about 8.5 px) reads a 2×, 4×, … box-reduced copy of the input instead, whose blocks are aligned to absolute frame coordinates so that tiles agree; the input is replicated beyond the frame edge. A kernel costs at most 289 taps; the 17 is a speed budget (`ym_lens::consts::MAX_TAPS_SIDE`) and can be raised to 33 where the cost is acceptable.
- **Falloff.** A gain `1 − e·(1 − T(ρ'/n))` on all three channels, with the lens's relative illumination `T` (a concave curve through the nine values, carried on beyond its last radius at its end slope, read at the oval radius over a scale `n` that is 1 without the attachment: *As built (the field law)*).
- **Anamorphic.** The field radius becomes oval, `ρ' = √((α·x)² + y²)` in half-diagonal units along the axis (x) and across it (y), with `α = 1 + κ·(q − 1)`, so falloff and softness grow faster towards the left and right; the attachment adds its own side softness `S = a_s·(q − 1)·x²`, which widens the kernel (`1 + S` times). The kernel's direction is the geometric one, from the frame's centre to the pixel. At Edges 0 these terms act alone through `e'`.

Reduced views use the same kernels at `1/k` scale (a kernel below one pixel is a copy).

### L3 · Highlight optics

With `F = flares/100`:

- **Restored light** `R`: the image times `1 + f·(2^h − 1)`, `h = 3` EV (`LENS_HEADROOM_EV`), where `f` is the clip test: a ramp from 0 to 1 as `min_c(X_c / g0_c)` goes from 0.95 to 0.98 (`g0` is the clip level of each channel in the group's scale; R3's gain only raises clipped values, so they still pass; on the lens plane the ramp runs from 0.90 to 0.97, because binning mixes clipped and unclipped photosites). Only the flares read `R`; the picture's own clipped pixels are not raised. A small clipped patch is assumed to hold more than 3 EV, as the block *As built (flare strength)* says.
- **Highlights** `H`: `R` weighted by a soft knee (a smoothstep) from 0 at half the clip level to 1 at the clip level of the pixel's brightest channel, so a coloured light that clipped in one channel keeps its colour in its ghosts.
- **The light budget.** The lens data give the fractions of light at Flares 100: glow `a_g`, veiling `a_v`, each ghost `a_i`, starburst `a_*`, and the attachment's streak `a_s` (the plan reads them after the floors of *As built (flare strength)*: `LensPlan::flare_strengths`). With `Z` the output of L2 and `a` the sum of the fractions in use (the streak's with its Anamorphic factor) times `F`:

  `out = (1 − a)·Z + F·(a_g·K_g⊛R + a_v·K_v⊛R + Σ a_i·G_i[H] + a_*·B[H] + S)`

  where `S`, the streaks, is not a share `a_s` of the light: it has its own law (*Streaks*, below), and the streak's fraction `a_s` (times the Anamorphic factor) is only the strength of its peak and the share of the picture's light the budget `a` takes (at most 0.4 %).

  Every kernel and shape has unit energy, so the light the image loses comes back spread out, and the light the sensor clipped comes back too. The starburst's fraction is in use in proportion to its strength (below): `a` equals `LensPlan::light_fraction()` at full strength and is smaller under it, and the light a weak starburst does not take stays in the picture. A tint (a ghost's coating, the streak's) is the reflectance spectrum projected on the camera's channels and scaled to a mean of 1 over the channels, so it colours the light and does not change its energy. The light a ghost's clipping circle blocks is lost.
- **Glow** `K_g`: a core within 8 px (and at most 0.0015 half-diagonals), gathered per tile at full resolution, plus the rest, which is smooth and is convolved with the tail plane by an FFT. The tail falls as a power law and widens with shorter wavelengths, per the lens data; the camera's channel weights mix the eight wavelength kernels (each of unit energy) into one kernel per channel. A cosine window (1 up to 70 % of the core radius, 0 at it) splits the kernel into the core, `K_c s`, and the tail, `K_c (1 − s)`; the core radius is the smallest of `flare.glow.core_radius`, 0.0015 and 8 px of the full-resolution frame. Both parts are normalised on the grid they are used on: the core's taps at the tile's scale sum to `a_g·m_c` (`m_c` is the core's share of the kernel's energy), the tail kernel on the tail plane sums to `a_g·(1 − m_c)`, so they add up to exactly `a_g` at every scale. The tail plane is read at each pixel's position with a Catmull-Rom curve, held at 0 or above. Detuned's glow and starburst come from a pupil with a seeded random phase (computed offline, so it is data), which gives its smeared flares.
- **Veiling** `K_v`: a very wide, nearly flat kernel on the tail plane (a flat disc of `flare.veil.radius` with a cosine edge over its outer 40 %, the same in every channel); the contrast the lens loses near light. It is folded into the tail plane with the glow's tail, both already weighted by their fractions.
- **The source list**: the connected components of the pixels of the lens plane whose `H` reaches 0.02 clip levels in some channel (8-connected, in raster order), each larger than 0.02 half-diagonals split into cells of that size on a grid fixed to the plane, each with its energy per channel (`H` summed, times the area of a plane pixel in half-diagonals squared), centroid and second moments (with the footprint of a plane pixel) and its extent, the larger side of its bounding box; at most 256, by energy, ties by position.
- **Ghosts** `G_i`: per source and ghost, the lens's aperture shape (blade count, roundness, rim profile) of apothem `s_i` (the radius of its inscribed circle; `roundness` blends the polygon with that circle), centred at `c + m_i·(p_s − c)` on the line from the source `p_s` through the centre `c`, cut by a circle of radius `R_i` centred at `c + n_i·(p_s − c)` (the arcs of ghosts from lights near the edge), tinted by the ghost's coating (section 6; its tint is pale, and a ghost is bounded in brightness: *As built (flares as light)*). The ghost's density is the rim profile times a Gaussian step at the edge (the steps of the shape and of the clipping circle have a standard deviation of `√((max(soft, 0.08)·s)² + the source's variance + (1 px)²)`: the ghost is widened by the source's extent, and an edge is never sharper than a pixel, which keeps the drawn energy exact on any pixel grid), divided by its exact integral. With Anamorphic, ghosts widen along the axis by `1 + κ_g·(q − 1)` and the attachment adds its own oval ghosts (round ellipses, widened the same way, at the Anamorphic factor of their fractions).
- **Starbursts** `B`: for compact sources (extent at most 0.004), the lens's spike table (its blade count, `|F{pupil}|²` over wavelengths, radius at most 0.08, unrotated in the canvas's orientation), stronger as the photo's f-number grows: the strength is `clamp((N − 2)/(f_full − 2), 0, 1)` for the f-number `N` (EXIF; f/4 when it has none). A pixel receives the exact mass of the table's piecewise-linear reconstruction over its area.
- **Streaks** `S` (Anamorphic above 0 only; `Items::streaks`, the constants are `STREAK_*` in `consts.rs`), from the lights (the connected components of the source list, not their cells), in these steps. (1) A light of less than `STREAK_E_MIN` (0.004) of energy `e` (`mean_c(E_c / g0_c)`, clip levels times half-diagonals squared) makes none, and a light inside the bounding box of one four times its size or more belongs to it (a glint on a lit window). (2) Lights whose rows (positions across the axis) are within 0.012 half-diagonals plus 1.5 times the sum of their widths, and within 0.35 of each other along the axis, are one streak, at the brightest one's place along the axis and the energy-weighted row across, as wide as the rows they spread over: lamps in a row, the gaps of one patch of sky. (3) Its **width** across is the standard deviation of the light, at least 2.5 px of the fit frame (`STREAK_SIGMA_MIN_HD`), and 0.1 of the light's size, added in quadrature; across the axis the streak is that Gaussian (80 % of its light) and a halo four times as wide (20 %), so a line has no sharp feet. (4) Its **peak** on the axis at the light is `P = 100 a_s·(q − 1)/(Q_max − 1)·e/(e + 0.02)` clip levels in the brightest channel (at most 0.4), divided by `1 + (σ/0.04)²` for a wide light and taken to 0 between a width of 0.03 and 0.055 half-diagonals (a lit wall, a window or blurred pavement is a surface and draws none), multiplied by the light's energy over the strongest light's (both as divided) and by 0.75 for every light stronger than it; only the five strongest lights are drawn and none whose peak would be under 0.03 clip levels, and the sum of peak times reach is brought down to 0.4. (5) Its **reach** along the axis is `reach·(q − 1)/(Q_max − 1)` (0.4 in the data) times `clamp(0.5 + 0.5 log10(e / 0.045), 0.3, 1)`, and its profile the attachment's `(1 + |a|/core)^−power` (core 0.04, power 1.5) taken to 0 by a cosine from 0.35 of the reach on, so it ends with a zero slope and no visible end. (6) Its **colour** is the light's own at the light and, over 0.4 of the reach (a smooth step), turns to the tail's: the streak tint at half its chroma, `1 + 0.5 (tint − 1)` per channel, so a streak is never more saturated than half the coating's blue. A pixel receives the exact mass of the band over its area.
- Ghosts, starbursts and streaks are drawn analytically per pixel from the list; each tile gets, from the plan, the items whose bounds overlap it, in list order.
- **Energy on a pixel grid.** The glow's core taps and tail kernel are normalised on the grid they are used on, and the streak and the starburst give each pixel the exact mass over its area, so these parts return exactly their light at every scale (to the rounding of `f32`). A ghost is a smooth density sampled at the pixel centres: its raster sums to its light within 1e-7 for a round aperture with the lens's rim and edges up to a tenth of the radius (1e-11 with a flat rim), and within about 1e-5 for a polygon aperture, whose corners are creases of the gauge. Light that leaves the frame, and the part of a ghost its clipping circle blocks, is lost. The tail plane is made once per photo whatever Flares says (the planes' key leaves the amounts out), about 0.3 s for a 6000 × 4000 frame (lens plane 1500 × 1000) on a loaded 4-core machine.

**As built (L3, CPU and device; `ym_lens::l3`, `ym-gpu` `kernels/lens_flare.rs`, `shaders/lens_flare.wgsl`).** A photo with many lights took minutes (a night or neon photo: 11 to 30 lights 216 s on the CPU, a full list of 256 sources 8 to 10 minutes) because every ghost of every source was evaluated at every pixel it reaches, and the shipped lenses' ghosts reach most of the frame (an apothem of up to a whole half-diagonal, 12 of them, and the attachment's). Two changes, both in the item list (`l3/items.rs`), so the CPU reference and the kernel share them:

- **Ghosts too faint to see are not drawn.** Each ghost has a peak density (its brightest channel's amplitude times the rim's peak). The faintest are left out while their peaks add up to no more than `GHOST_PRUNE_BUDGET` (2^-18 clip levels, a quarter of the step of a 16-bit code at the clip level), a function of the whole list and never of the tile, so any tiling draws the same items. At most that much light is missing at any pixel. Default's ghosts have fractions of 1e-5, so for most photos nothing of them was drawn; Petzval's and Triplet's (up to 4e-3) were (the *flare strength* block below raises Default's, and they are drawn).
- **Ghosts that vary slowly are drawn on grids.** A ghost whose edge (a Gaussian step of at least a pixel, usually tens or hundreds) and rim curve change slowly, and whose pixel rectangle is at least 16 384 px, is evaluated at the nodes of a grid of spacing `L` = 2, 4, 8, 16 or 32 px (the largest that keeps 2.5 nodes across its narrowest feature) and read back by bilinear interpolation. The node `(j, k)` of a grid sits at the pixel corner `(L j + L/2, L k + L/2)` and holds the sum, in list order, of the densities of the grid's ghosts whose rectangle grown by `L` holds it; a pixel is a function of four nodes, a node of its place and the list, so any tiling gives the whole frame's bits (D5; items within 64 px of a tile count for its nodes). The device does it in one more dispatch per grid (`lens_flare_nodes`) before the main pass. The error of the interpolation is below 0.03 % of a ghost's peak and 5e-5 of its light on the test lights (`ym_lens::flare_terms_exact` is the exact sum). The streaks, starbursts, glow core and tail plane are as before.

The item lists come from the CPU reference's own `Items` (`ym_lens::device::FlareScaleData`), so the two read one source; 8 storage bindings, the last being the restored light or the nodes. The tail plane, the spike tables and the lens's tables are uploaded once per photo and lens.
- **The light budget of a frame with nothing in it** (a question from the contact sheets). The picture's light is `(1 − a) Z + F (…)` and the glow's tail and the veil spread the light they take over the plane, so what they send beyond the frame's edge is lost (section 4, *Energy on a pixel grid*). That is by design and it is what a lens does: the veiling glare at a pixel is the light of the scene within the frame, so the corners get less than the centre. On a uniform frame at Flares 100 with Edges and Anamorphic at 0 the mean kept 99.8 % of its light with Default, 97.2 % with Vintage (centre 98.4 %, corner 94 %), pinned by `a_uniform_frame_loses_only_the_light_that_leaves_it`; with the strengths of the *flare strength* block it keeps 98.9 % and 96.4 %. The larger loss seen on a flat frame at Default 60/40 is L2's falloff, the lens's relative illumination (Edges 40 alone leaves 93.8 % of the mean), not L3.

**As built (flare strength; `l3/lights.rs`, `strength.rs`, `consts.rs`, `anamorphic.json` version 2).** The contact sheets of 42 photos showed the flares as the weakest element: on the setting-sun photo Default at Flares 60 gave a soft glow and one ghost that showed only with the difference amplified four times, Anamorphic 100 a faint haze, and the moon at f/16 nothing. `cargo run -p ym-lens --example flare_measure` measures the light each element adds (clip levels, mean of the channels, the lens plane of a real photo or a synthetic frame with lights; the lens folders with one element left on are made by a script in the scratch folder, not in the repository), Flares 100, Edges 0:

- **What was weak** (the sun of `27998_DSC00319`, 1 025 clipped pixels on a 1512 px plane, 0.04 half-diagonals across; Default; the sky beside the sun is at about 0.5 of the clip level): no ghost at all (the budget below left all twelve out, and the brightest would have been 1e-8 of the clip level); the glow 0.036 at its peak, 1.1e-2 at 24 px from the sun's centre and 2.6e-3 at 48 px; the starburst 4e-5; the streak at Anamorphic 100 2.2e-3 at 50 px and 6.5e-4 at 100 px from the sun. On the night photo of a road (13 lamps and the moon) the streak was under 1e-3 beyond 50 px and the lamps' glow under 2e-3 at 12 px.
- **Why**, three causes, each worth a factor of 100 or more. (1) *The light the flares draw from.* Every element spreads a light's energy over an area thousands of times the light's own (a ghost of 0.17 to 1.3 half-diagonals in radius, a streak a frame wide), so its brightness is the element's fraction times the light's energy over that area. The restored light of a clipped pixel was 3 EV (8 times) above the clip level, for the sun and for a grey wall alike. A real lamp or sun is far brighter: the reflected-light meter equation `L = K N^2 / (t S)` with `K = 12.5` (ISO 2720) puts mid grey at 3 200 cd/m^2 for the sunny-16 exposure and at 1.8 cd/m^2 for f/2.8, 1/60 s, ISO 3200, and the clip level of a raw file is taken to lie about 2.5 EV above mid grey, so about 1.8e4 and about 10 cd/m^2. The sun's disc is 1.6e9 cd/m^2 (sources: the luminance table of redcrab-software.com; the same table lists a high-pressure sodium street lamp at 5 000 to 10 000 cd/m^2 and a white LED bulb's surface at 1e5 to 3e5): 16 EV above the clip level at the first exposure, 9 to 10 EV for the sodium lamp and 13 to 15 for the LED at the second. A ghost of the sun that looks faint on the photo is `fraction x 1.6e9 / 1.8e4 x (the sun's area over the ghost's)`, which is how real ghosts get to be visible: 1e-5 x 9e4 x 1e-3 = 1e-3 of the clip level. (2) *The fractions.* The data are physical for the glass they describe: bare glass reflects 4 % of the light at every air to glass surface (MIT, *Computational Photography*, ch. 9: `0.96^30 = 0.29` for 30 surfaces), so a ghost of two bare surfaces holds `0.04^2 = 1.6e-3` of the light; a single-layer MgF2 coating leaves about 1 to 1.5 % per surface (1e-4 to 2e-4 a ghost), a multi-layer one 0.1 to 0.5 % ([US 9,915,761](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9915761) reports about 0.1 % at one wavelength; 4e-6 to 2.5e-5 a ghost). Default is a multi-layer lens whose twelve ghosts hold 1e-5 each, which is right, and invisible; Zeiss shows the same lens uncoated, where "bright ghost images of the candle appeared" (*technical article about the reduction of reflections for camera lenses*, Distagon 2.8/21, 26 air to glass surfaces). The veil and glow are in range: Kondo, Chiba and Yoshida (SPIE 274, 1981, *Veiling glare in photographic systems*) found lenses from under 1 % to over 10 % and rated 1.5 % or less very good, 1.5 to 3 % usually good, 3 to 6 % usually poor and more than 6 % poor (the glare index of ISO 9358, the black spot in a bright field); the data give Default 0.9 % (glow and veil), Classic 4.3 %, Vintage 8 %, Petzval 6.5 %. (3) *The streak's profile.* Its core was 0.001 half-diagonals and its tail fell as `(1 + x / core)^-1.5`, so only 7 % of its light lay beyond a tenth of a half-diagonal from the light: the streak that cylinder lenses draw across the frame (a line that fades slowly and is still there at half the frame's width, as in the anamorphic references of the research note) could not be seen.
- **Not the cause: the ghost budget.** `GHOST_PRUNE_BUDGET` leaves out only ghosts whose peaks add up to under 2^-18 of the clip level, and it is a function of the whole list and of the plan's shape, which does not depend on Flares. Its peaks are those at Flares 100, so a ghost that is visible at any amount up to the chosen one is drawn once it is visible at 100. Default's ghosts were pruned because they were invisible; with the strengths below none is pruned for being faint (a test builds the list for every lens and light; another pins that faint ghosts are still left out).

What changed (CPU reference and kernels together; the kernels read the same lists and planes, so none of `lens_flare.wgsl` changed):

- **Extra headroom for small clipped lights** (`ym_lens::light_boost_ev`). A clipped patch (8-connected pixels of the lens plane whose clip test is above 0) with an equivalent diameter `d` (that of the disc of the same area, in half-diagonals) holds `2^(3 + b(d))` times its clipped light, `b(d) = 9 EV` up to `d = 0.025`, falling linearly with `ln d` (2.25 EV a doubling) to 0 at `d = 0.4`: a clipped sky or wall is a surface, a lamp, a window and the sun are lights. Between the two the energy a patch sends into its flares, its area times its brightness, falls only as `d^-0.25`, so a lamp, a window and the sun each give a flare of the same order, as they do in a photo. The ghosts, the starbursts and the streaks read the sources' energy with the whole `b`; the glow and the veil read it with half of it (`GLOW_BOOST_SHARE`), as an extra layer of the tail plane: the light the headroom adds beyond the plain 3 EV is spread by the tail kernel scaled to the glow's whole fraction and by the veil, a second convolution made only when some light is boosted, so the glow and the veil take exactly their fractions of `R` plus the extra light (the energy test sums it). The core, gathered per tile from the plainly restored picture, is as it was. A map of `b` per lens plane pixel is made once per photo with the source list, so every tile and scale reads one number.
- **A cap on the lights' extra light** (`LIGHT_BOOST_ENERGY_CAP`, `LensPlanes::light_boost_scale`). The extra light the headroom adds to the highlights of the plane (clip levels times half-diagonals squared, the mean over the channels) is 1.0 for the road of lamps, 1.5 for a window with the sun in it and 2.9 for the sun of `27998`, and 8 for a sun behind clouds, 21 to 27 for a beer table and the fairy lights of a Christmas tree, 51 for a floodlit palace and 77 for a bright window with a dog in front of it. Without a limit the clouds and the tree turned the whole picture to a purple haze at Default 60 (the ghosts of dozens of lights add up to a veil; the others were not looked at), so above 3 the extra is reduced by the same factor for every light (`3 / sum`; 0.38 for the clouds, 0.11 for the tree, 0.04 for the dog and the window) where it adds up: in the ghosts (`Source::ghost_energy`) and in the glow's extra layer. The streak and the starburst are local, a line through its own light and a star on it, and keep the whole extra (`Source::energy`), so a floodlit palace of 219 lights still gets a streak from each; a photo of many lights flares in haze like one of a few. The scale is made with the map, once per photo.
- **Floors on the strengths** (`FlareStrengths`, `LensPlan::flare_strengths`; `light_fraction`, the budget `a` and the energy tests read it). At Flares 100 a lens's ghosts together take at least 0.004 of a light's energy, shared out in proportion to `fraction / max(s, 0.15)^2`: a ghost's density falls as `1 / s^2`, so the small ghosts, which read as shapes, get most of it and the frame-filling ones, which at an equal share turned a photo of a sun behind clouds to purple haze, almost none (Default's smallest ghost, 0.17 in radius, rises 226 times, its frame-filling ones 4 times). The floor is twice what twelve ghosts of two single-layer surfaces take and a fifth of twelve bare pairs. The attachment's own ghosts take at least 7e-4, the glow at least 0.02 and the starburst at least 0.02 where the lens has them (never when it has none). Nothing is lowered. The ghosts' sums rise 36 times for Default and Detuned, 12 for Vintage, 4.6 for Sonnar, 2.8 for Classic, and not at all for Triplet (5e-3) and Petzval (bare glass, 0.027); the attachment's 3 times; Default's glow 3.3 times (the other lenses' are above it), the starbursts 2.5 to 6.7 times. The budget `a` (the share of the picture's light that the flare elements take and spread) at Flares 100, Anamorphic 100 and the full starburst strength is now Default 0.052 (was 0.017), Classic 0.072 (0.054), Vintage 0.109 (0.091), Sonnar 0.079 (0.060), Triplet 0.095 (0.083), Detuned 0.071 (0.053), Petzval 0.097 (0.098); a uniform frame without any light keeps 98.9 % of its light at Default and 96.4 % at Vintage (0.2 % and 2.8 % before, the picture gives up the ghosts' and the starburst's shares even where there is no light to draw them from, which the accounting `out = (1 - a) Z + ...` has always done and which the larger fractions now show).
- **The streak** (`anamorphic.json` version 2, tuned by hand like the rest of it): fraction 0.004 (was 0.005), core 0.008 (was 0.001), power 1 (was 1.5): a tail that falls as `1 / x` and is cut over the last fifth of the reach carries 45 % of the light beyond a tenth of a half-diagonal and 23 % beyond 0.3.
- **The device**: nothing in the kernels. The GPU suites' bounds changed in two places because the streak is now bright: `PARITY_BOUND_50MP` of `gpu_lens_flare.rs` (the far-corner test reads the streak's Gaussian across the axis 2 to 3 standard deviations out, where a light's centre stored in `f32` moves the value by up to 1e-4 of itself) and the picture-mixed case, which is relative like the others. The 19 parity cases are at 7e-6 of the peak against the bound 2e-5.

**As built (flares as light, not stickers; `l3/items.rs`, `l3/sources.rs`, `l3/restore.rs`, `consts.rs`, `anamorphic.json` version 3).** The review of 85 photos at ten lens settings found the flares drawn as stickers: ghosts as flat saturated discs 100 to 300 px across with an almost hard edge (violet for Default and Vintage, orange for Classic, hard pink dots for Sonnar), streaks that were pure saturated blue lines brighter than their lights, one from every small light and a flood from every large one, and a violet cast in the shadows near lights. What the build found and changed, CPU reference and device together (the kernel got one number more for each streak and the clip pattern's neutral; everything else is in the item list; the ghosts' softness, brightness bound and strength below were changed again by the third review, see *As built (the light's colour, ghosts as light, the veil as haze)* further down):

- **The colour of the restored light was not the cause.** The suspicion was that a clipped light is restored at its clip levels, `(g0_R, g0_G, g0_B)`, a magenta that the coatings then turn violet. R5 already raises a fully clipped quad to a neutral (`docs/05`, step 5), so the clipped pixels of the lens plane are neutral (35231: `(3.10, 3.02, 3.01)` against `g0 = (2.89, 1, 1.51)`), and the chromaticity of the light the sources send into their flares (`H` summed over the sources, over its mean) is 1.05 / 0.98 / 0.97 for 35231, 1.05 / 0.98 / 0.96 for 28322, 1.00 / 1.00 / 1.00 for 51584 and 1.01 / 1.01 / 0.99 for 27308; the sun of 15985, a real sunset, is 1.20 / 0.92 / 0.88 and stays warm. The violet came from the ghosts' tints (next bullet), added to by their brightness. One real case is left: with highlight recovery switched off a clipped pixel stays at `(g0_R, g0_G, g0_B)`, and was restored as that colour. A pixel whose channels, in clip levels, are within 10 % of each other is the clip pattern and is now restored as a neutral of the same total (`Restore::without_clip_cast`, `CLIP_PATTERN_SPREAD`; `lfl_restore` does the same), so a recovered core, a coloured light and an unclipped pixel are as they were. (The third review found the star of 60855 violet all the same: green clips first with highlight recovery on too, see *As built (the light's colour, ghosts as light, the veil as haze)* below.)
- **Ghost tints are pale.** A coating's tint on the camera's channels differs by 1.8 (Default, Detuned), 3.3 to 4.5 (Classic), 2.0 to 2.8 (Vintage), 1.2 to 2.1 (Sonnar), 1.9 to 4.5 (the attachment's ghosts) between the largest and the smallest channel, and the sum of a dozen such ghosts over the dark parts of a picture is a violet veil. The tint is brought to at most 1.2 (`GHOST_TINT_MAX_RATIO`, `l3::items::limited_tint`): raised to the power `ln 1.2 / ln(largest / smallest)` and scaled to a mean of 1, which keeps the hue and the order of the channels. - **A ghost's edge is a ramp.** `soft` is at least 0.08 radii (`GHOST_SOFT_MIN`) when drawn (the data hold 0.03 for half of the ghosts): the Gaussian step's 10 to 90 % width, 2.56 standard deviations, is 20 % of the radius or more (`a_ghosts_edge_is_a_ramp_of_a_fifth_of_its_radius_whatever_the_data_say`: 21 % for a data `soft` of 0).
- **A ghost has a brightest value.** A small clipped light is assumed thousands of times brighter than the clip level, and spread over a ghost's area that was still many times the clip level for the smallest ghosts (Classic's 0.006 and Sonnar's 0.016 radii: up to 3.9 and 5.6 clip levels, the "confetti") and 0.04 to 0.1 for the others, a disc brighter than mid grey. A ghost under its bound is as it was, so the energy of faint ghosts is still their fraction of the light (the energy tests use ghosts of 0.1 half-diagonals and a light the bound does not reach). The old streak was a power-law profile of unit mass times the light's energy, with the energy 4096 times the clip level for a small light: a line one to four pixels thick (the light's own standard deviation), 100 times the light's value at its core, pure blue (the tint of the attachment's coating, 0.45 : 0.74 : 1.81 on the camera, 4 times between the channels) and, because it was made for every cell of a large clipped area, a stack of such lines. Now (the *Streaks* bullet above has the law and the constants): there is one streak per light (a connected component of the source list: the cells of a large clipped area are one light, so 26276's hard-edged band and 53511's and 29348's blue flood are gone); its peak is `0.4 e / (e + 0.02)` clip levels at most and divided down for a wide light, so a light of 1 600 pixels makes a fainter, wider streak than one of 16 and a source of 10 000 pixels not a thousand times a source of 10; its reach follows the logarithm of `e`; the red and green die out sooner than the blue, so the streak is the light's colour at the light and blue at the tail (blue over red 1.4 at 12 px and 2.1 at 150 px on a white light); no light makes one under `e` of 0.004 and only the ten brightest of a frame do (28322 has fifteen tree lights and gets faint lines through the brightest ones, and the dog's face none); the sum is capped. A light whose width is 0.03 to 0.055 half-diagonals fades to nothing, a wider one is a surface and draws none (`STREAK_SURFACE_*`, on the energy-weighted width, so a sun in a hazy sky counts by its core: 15985's sun, width 21 px, draws; 26276's pavement, 48 px, and 29348's windows, 80 and 158 px, none). The streaks share a frame by strength: each is its energy (over the width's penalty `1 + (σ/0.04)²`) over the strongest light's, and 0.75 times the one before it (`STREAK_REL_POWER`, `STREAK_RANK_DECAY`), five at most (`STREAK_MAX_LIGHTS`), none whose peak would be under 0.03 clip levels (`STREAK_PEAK_FLOOR`: a star of `e` 0.008 draws 0.05 at Flares 100, a lamp 0.2 to 0.3), and their sum of peak times reach is at most 0.4 (`STREAK_TOTAL_CAP`). Streak counts, before to after: 61118 10 to 2, 28322 10 to 5 (the weakest at a sixth of the strongest, before the sum is capped), 29348 4 to 0, 26276 2 to 0, 55204 10 to 2 (the moon, the lamp row), 28105 1 to 1, 60855 2 to 2, 52786 3 to 2, 15985 1 to 1.
- **Soft across, soft at the ends.** The width is at least 2.5 px of the fit frame (`STREAK_SIGMA_MIN_HD` 0.0028, was 1.5 px) and grows with the light's size (0.1 of it, in quadrature); across the axis the streak is that Gaussian (80 % of the light) and a halo four times as wide (20 %). Along the axis the profile is one for all channels, `(1 + |a|/core)^−power` with core 0.04 and power 1.5 (was 0.05 and 1.2), taken to 0 by a cosine from 0.35 of the reach on (`STREAK_FLAT`, was 0.8): no step and a zero slope at the end (28105's sun streak adds 10 codes at 0.8 of its visible length, 2.4 at 0.9 and 0 at its end on the left, 7 to 2.6 to 0 on the right, which is 325 and 329 px; the test `the_streak_ends_smoothly_to_nothing` pins steps under a tenth of a code).
- **Colour.** The streak is the light's colour at the light and turns to the tail's over 0.4 of its reach (a smooth step, `STREAK_TURN`); the tail's colour is the streak tint at half its chroma (`STREAK_TAIL_CHROMA`, `1 + 0.5 (tint − 1)` per channel), so a streak is never more saturated than the coating's blue at 50 %, and lower near the core. The tint in `anamorphic.json` is the traced reflectance times the transmission of the glass, `0.02 + 0.98 smoothstep((nm − 400) / 60)` (optical glass absorbs the violet): the camera reads it as 0.53 : 0.87 : 1.60 and the tail as 0.76 : 0.94 : 1.30, and the added light on the 55204 sky reads blue (CIELAB hue 250 to 270 degrees on the sRGB frame at 100 to 200 px, 227 to 235 at 60 px where it is nearly white; the violet tail was 292 to 298 degrees, and a tail with more green, 0.80 : 1.02 : 1.20, added 0.13 : 0.4 : 1 in linear sRGB, a cyan-leaning blue).
- **Reach.** The data's reach is 0.4 (was 1). The visible length (both sides together, where the streak adds at least 5 codes on a plain display) of the brightest streak, before to after, at Anamorphic 100 and at Vintage 100 / 100 / 100: the moon of 55204 71 to 51 % and 62 to 51 % of the frame's width (at 2 codes 79 to 59 % and 66 to 58 %), the sun of 28105 100 to 39 % and 100 to 42 % (the old streak was still 10 to 25 codes at the frame's edge on both sides), the sun of 61118 73 to 27 % and 74 to 14 %, a tree light of 28322 92 to 35 % and 93 to 41 %, the star of 60855 23 to 10 % and 21 to 10 %.
- **The device.** One profile for all channels: `lfl_streak` evaluates the mass along the axis once, not three times, and across the axis the core and the halo (four table lookups). The record of a streak is the light's place in pixels of the frame (along, across), the width, the colour at the light, the reach, and the colour of the tail; the pixel's edges are measured from the light (`(pixel − place) / half-diagonal`) and not from the frame's centre, which in `f32` put each edge of a pixel 3e-4 px off at 50 MP, 5e-4 of its width: the 50 MP near-corner case went from 3.6e-5 of 0.152 (2.4e-4 of the peak) to 2.9e-6 (1.9e-5), the bound is 5e-5 now (was 2.5e-4). `lfl_mass` integrates the profile's height (8 Gauss-Legendre points) for a pixel on one side of the light and not the difference of two cumulative values, which lost digits (each good to 6e-8 of itself, the pixel's mass 1e-4). The 19 parity cases: 1.9e-6 of values up to 1.58 (1.5e-6 relative; was 4.2e-6).
- **Not changed.** The peak law (`e / (e + 0.02)`, at most 0.4 at Flares 100), the streak's budget share and the light's colour (the source's own, from the same restored light the ghosts use).

### L4 · Anamorphic bend, distortion and zoom

The map from the photo to the canvas, in half-diagonal units with x along the anamorphic axis:

- the attachment: `x' = x·(1 + μ·x²)`, `y' = y·(1 − β·x²)`, with `μ = μ₀·(q − 1)` (the horizontal barrel; the traced attachment gives `μ₀ < 0`, the sides drawn in) and `β = β₀·(q − 1)` (horizontal lines bowing);
- the lens's own radial distortion `r' = r·(1 + e·(δ₁r² + δ₂r⁴))`.

The lens's distortion applies first and the attachment's bend after it. In half-diagonal units of the canvas, centred on its centre, the canvas position of a photo position `p` is `z·F(p)` (`F` the two steps and `z` the zoom below), and a canvas position `c` reads the photo at `F⁻¹(c / z)` (`LensWarp::photo_to_canvas` and `canvas_to_photo`). A reduced view takes the warp of the full-resolution canvas (`LensWarp::rescaled`) so every scale shares one zoom.

Sampling needs the inverse, found by three Newton steps from the output position (f64 in the CPU reference; on the device with the per-tile rebase of D4). The **zoom** `z` is the smallest factor of at least 1 for which every point of the canvas edge (200 samples a side), divided by `z`, maps inside the photo with a 1 px margin, so the canvas has no empty corner. The map sits in the geometry sampler between the canvas and the photo (`pipeline/geometry.rs`): straightening and the crop act on the bent, zoomed canvas, so the crop frame and the panel work on the bent picture.

- At full resolution the sampling is then Catmull-Rom everywhere, as for a straightened photo.
- A tile's source rectangle is the bounding box of its mapped edge (64 samples a side), plus the filter's support and 2 px.
- `pick_point_at` and `white_pick` go through the map.
- Auto crop analyses the picture without lens effects (`docs/20` §4) and its frame is applied to the canvas as it is. The bend is mild and the canvas is full, so the frame holds nearly the same content and "No border in photos" still holds.
- The Before view has no bend.
- A DNG carries the bend in its pixels: `Geometry::upright` gets it in the buffer's own orientation, with the axis the canvas gives.

**As built (L4, CPU and device; `ym-gpu` `cpu/sampler.rs`, `kernels/sampler.rs`, `shaders/sampler.wgsl`; `ym-core` `pipeline/geometry.rs`).** The decisions the build made:

- **Where it lives.** `ym_gpu::cpu::sampler::SamplerWarp` is the map as plain `Copy` data (`mu`, `beta`, `d1`, `d2`, the zoom, the Newton steps, and whether the axis is the canvas's y): the same operations in the same order as `ym_lens::LensWarp` (a test holds them equal bit for bit, also on reduced canvases), because `ym-gpu` does not depend on `ym-lens`; `ym_core::pipeline::geometry::sampler_warp` builds it from a `LensWarp`, which gained four accessors (`mu`, `beta`, `distortion_k1`, `distortion_k2`) and nothing else. `Geometry::warp`, `Grid` (through its geometry) and `SamplerGrid::warp` carry it, so it is in every `{:?}` the frame and display keys print, and a slider move is a new key. The identity is `None`: a photo without a bend has exactly the geometry, the kernels and the bytes it had.
- **Built once per photo.** `Job::geometry()` (now `&mut self` and fallible, as it asks for the lens plan; `Job::base_geometry()` is the old one, enough for sides and sizes) takes the warp from the photo's `LensCtx`, which fits the zoom on the full-resolution canvas (`RawPlan::out_w x out_h`, sides swapped by the turns) when the plan is made and caches it with the plan. Anamorphic 0 on a lens without distortion, or Edges 0 with it, is the identity. A grid takes its own canvas from the buffer it samples (`rescaled`: the half-diagonal of the reduced canvas is the unit, as for L1 to L3), so every scale shares the zoom. `Renderer::geometry(src, cancel)` hands the geometry out for checks.
- **The order.** The inside test is on the canvas position after the crop and the straighten, before the bend, so the pixels that are inside, their intervals and `inside_bounds` are what they were. Then `canvas_to_photo`, the division by the canvas size, the turns, the EXIF orientation and the clamp to the buffer. Straightening and the crop act on the bent canvas; Auto crop is untouched.
- **The kernel.** A grid with a bend is never a pixel copy. Its shrink is the largest over the canvas of `|J^-1 v| / z`, `v` each of the output's two step vectors on the canvas and `J` the Jacobian of `F` at the photo position that canvas point reads, taken on a 9 x 9 lattice of the canvas that holds its corners and edges (`SamplerWarp::max_shrink`; a fine scan finds nothing above it); Catmull-Rom up to 1.25, `Box(n)` beyond, `n = ceil(shrink - 0.25)` from 2 to 4. It is one number for the grid, taken from the grid alone, so a tile, the whole frame, the CPU and the device choose alike. At full resolution it is 1.12 to 1.16 at Anamorphic 100 (the sides, where the attachment draws the picture in), so exports and 100 % views are Catmull-Rom for every shipped lens (a test).
- **Source rectangles.** `SamplerGrid::source_bounds` (and `Grid::source_bounds` for a grid with a bend) follows the boundary of what the rectangle samples through the bend: each edge of the rectangle where it lies on the canvas and each edge of the canvas where it lies in the rectangle, both clipped exactly to the other, 65 samples each, the result clamped to the buffer as the sampler clamps, plus the caller's margin and 2 px. The map is a diffeomorphism of the canvas, so the box of the boundary is the box of the region. Without a bend the four corners are used as before. An export's working-set reservation grows by a quarter of the tile for a bend.
- **Pick.** `Geometry::final_to_oriented` applies the inverse map to the canvas position before the turns; `white_pick` stays in the photo's own coordinates, before the bend.
- **The DNG.** `Geometry::upright` carries the warp with the axis swapped when the buffer's sides are (`SamplerWarp::transposed`); the DNG's preview is the baked picture bit for bit, turned back (a test, for the camera orientations that swap the sides, a user's turn and a straighten angle).
- **On the device.** The tables stay what they were: the exact integer-and-fraction position of the chain *without* the bend. The kernel (`warp_pos`) takes the canvas centre's offset to that position (the integer part exact, so no `f32` of order 1e4), in half-diagonal units, runs the same three Newton steps in `f32` written for the offset `e` from the start (the residual is `e + (F(x) - x)`, so nothing of order 1 is subtracted from something of order 1), and moves the exact position by the displacement `e - (z - 1) t`, in canvas pixels through the dihedral map's linear part (a signed permutation of unit scale: exact). Everything depends on the absolute column and row through the tables, so tiles equal the whole frame bit for bit (D5), and the poison test holds (D7). No decision compares sums, so no fast-math barrier is needed (K8); the clamp is on integers. CPU against device: the position differs by a few ulps of the displacement, about 1e-5 px on a 200 px frame and 1e-4 px at 50 MP, which `tests/gpu_sampler.rs` holds to the sampler's own bound of 1.2e-6 in value on a 1152-case matrix (measured 7.2e-7, against 6.0e-7 without a bend; 5e-6 on hardware until the Metal run) and to 1e-5 at the far corner of the 50 MP frame (measured 4.9e-6).

## 6. The lens data

`data/lenses/`, committed, about 6 MB in all:

- `<id>/lens.json`: `id`, `version`, `name`, `line`, `start` `{ flares, edges }`, `provenance`, `field` (the radii, `T(ρ)`, the distortion `δ₁, δ₂`, the swirl `σ(ρ)`, the astigmatism `a(ρ)`, the cat's-eye `c(ρ)`), `flare` (glow and veiling fractions and tails, the ghosts `{ m, s, n, R, a, coating }`, the starburst's blade count, rotation and strength), `bokeh.rim`.
- `<id>/tables.safetensors`: the spread functions (9 radii × 8 wavelengths × 33 × 33) and the spike table, f32 (the glow's tail per wavelength is a formula, `flare.glow` with its `chroma`).
- `anamorphic.json`: the attachment (`Q_max`, `μ₀`, `β₀`, `κ`, `κ_g`, `a_s`, `h`, the streak's profile, fraction and tint, its oval ghosts).
- `SHA256SUMS` and `README.md` (what each file holds, the statement below, the designs).

### File format

All lengths are in half-diagonal units of the emulated frame (a 36 × 24 mm frame, half-diagonal 21.633 mm, at the design's focal length); spectra on the 31-sample grid 400, 410, …, 700 nm; the eight PSF wavelengths are 420, 460, 500, 540, 580, 620, 660 and 700 nm.

`index.json`: `{ "version": 1, "lenses": ["default", "classic", "vintage", "petzval", "triplet", "sonnar", "detuned"], "attachment": "anamorphic.json" }` (the menu order).

`<id>/lens.json`:

`<id>/tables.safetensors` (f32, little-endian, each slice summing to 1):

|---|---|---|

**Grid of the tables.** A table is a set of cell masses: sample `k` of `n` (33 for `psf`, 129 for `spikes`) is the mass of the cell `[−1 + 2k/n, −1 + 2(k + 1)/n]` in units of the half-width, so the table spans exactly `±w` (or `±radius`), the middle sample is centred on the origin and a slice sums to 1 (1e-4). A table written with samples *at* `−w … +w` would be 3 % too wide.

**Checks.** The loader refuses a lens folder (`LensError`, never a panic) whose `SHA256SUMS` (`sha256sum` format, `<hex>  <relative path>`, one line for every file it reads and for `README.md`) does not match, whose `index.json` does not list all seven lenses once each, whose statement is not the exact sentence, whose radii do not run strictly increasing from 0 to 1, whose `T` is not 1 at the centre, in (0, 1] and falling, whose `σ(0)` is not 0, whose numbers are not finite, whose lists or tensors have another length or shape or hold a negative sample, or whose fractions of light at Flares 100 and Anamorphic 100 add up to 1 or more. `bokeh.rim` is the key of the file format; the stage text's `bokeh.edge` is read as the same table.

`anamorphic.json`: `{ "id": "anamorphic", "version", "provenance", "q_max": 2.0, "bend": { "mu0", "beta0" }, "field": { "kappa", "side_softness", "fringe" }, "ghost_stretch": κ_g, "streak": { "fraction", "reach", "core", "power", "tint": [31] }, "ghosts": [ … as above ] }`.

**What the fractions mean to the plan.** The fractions of light in `lens.json` and `anamorphic.json` are the lens as built: physical for the ghosts (the product of two surfaces' reflectances and the share of the beam that survives the apertures), tuned by hand for the glow, the veil, the starburst and the streak. The plan reads them through `FlareStrengths` (`LensPlan::flare_strengths`), which raises the ghosts, the glow and the starburst to floors at Flares 100 where a coating is too good to show, and never lowers one (section 4, L3, *As built (flare strength)*). The streak's profile is tuned: core 0.05 half-diagonals, power 1.2, fraction 0.004 since data version 3 (the fraction is the strength of the streak's peak, 100 times it in clip levels).

**Colour.** Coatings and tints are reflectance spectra (31 samples, 400 to 700 nm), and the spread functions and tails are given per wavelength. The plan projects them onto the photo's camera channels with the profile's spectral curves (`data/cameras/sony/`, `docs/03`), so a blue streak is the blue that body's sensor would record. Stand-in bodies use their profile's curves.

**Provenance.** Every file says, in `provenance.statement`: **"Synthetic, computed from public lens designs, not measured."** The designs are prescriptions Yellowmoon transcribes itself from expired patents or nineteenth-century publications; each lens names its source (patent number or publication, year, focal length, f-number) and its design type in generic words (double Gauss, triplet, Petzval portrait lens). Lens designs never come from third-party lens libraries. Coatings are assumed (single-layer MgF₂ quarter-wave films, multi-layer for Default), tuned by eye and documented as an artistic choice. Default is a well-corrected double Gauss with multi-layer coatings; Detuned is Default with its elements decentred and tilted.

**How they are made.** The data are computed offline by a lens simulator that is not part of the repository or the app, and are fitted into the formulas and tables above; only those are committed. They cannot be regenerated from the repository alone, and `README.md` says so. Tests check what can be checked from the data alone (section 14).

**Licences.** `docs/14` gains a row: lens prescriptions transcribed from expired patents and old publications (facts of a design; provenance per lens; [HUMAN] check, section 13).

## 7. The bokeh detector

Reshaping needs to know where the photo is already out of focus and by how much, not how far away things are: a far wall in a photo taken at f/11 is sharp and must stay as it is. The detector therefore measures the blur the Sony lens left in the photo, from the photo alone, once per photo on the lens plane (`LensPlanes::build`), deterministically in f64 on the CPU:

1. **Blur at edges.** The re-blur gradient ratio of Zhuo and Sim (2011, implemented from the paper): the plane's luminance is blurred again with a Gaussian of 1 px, and at each strong edge (local maxima of the gradient magnitude above a threshold set from the plane's noise) the ratio `ρ_g` of the gradient magnitudes gives the blur `σ = 1 / √(ρ_g² − 1)` px, a sparse map with a confidence from the edge's contrast.
2. **The subject anchor.** The camera's focus point (`SonyFocus`, `docs/04` §Container, as a fraction of the frame in the buffer's orientation) marks its area as in focus with high confidence; without one, nothing is anchored.
3. **Spread to every pixel.** The sparse values and confidences are spread over the plane by edge-aware normalised convolution (a joint bilateral filter guided by the plane's colours, run over a fixed pyramid), so a value travels along its own surface and stops at colour edges. The result is `b` (in half-diagonal units) and a confidence `c` per pixel.
4. **Bright balls are measured by their size, not their edges.** A defocused light is a disc with a rim, which the edge measure of step 1 reads as sharp when the rim is sharp (`research/bokeh-map-2026-10-24.md` §1). Bright, compact blobs are therefore found directly (a scale-space blob search on the brightest channel, section 4, L1 step 2: sharp-rimmed or soft, clipped or not, any colour) and their measured radius is written into the map over the disc and its surroundings (2.2 radii), with high confidence, wherever the map has no reading of its own; on a dark night background they are often the only out-of-focus evidence there is.
5. **Caution.** `b` is set to 0 where `c` is low, below `b_min`, and where the plane shows sharp texture. The balls are not looked for only where the map says blurred: a light has its own depth and the map around a dark night is empty; they are judged by the edges around them (section 4, L1 step 2).

**As built** (`ym_lens::l1::{detector, disc, balls, spread}`; decisions the steps above left open):

- **Work plane.** The detector runs on the lens plane, or on its 2 x 2 reduction when the plane's long edge is 1536 px or more; the smallest radius it reports is `max(b_min, 2.2 px)` of that plane, because a sharp edge on a binned plane already reads as a disc of about a pixel.
- **Step 1.** One re-blur of 1 px cannot read the blurs of interest (a disc of 8 px gives a ratio of 1.01); the plane's luminance, in linear light (a defocus blur is a convolution there), therefore goes up a ladder of Gaussians of 0, 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 px, and the gradient ratios of the five consecutive pairs (each with its own non-maximum suppression, 4 sectors, and the other level's peak over 3 x 3) give the radius through tables of the ratio of a step edge blurred by a *disc* (its half-circle line spread, a native blur of 0.6 px in quadrature) instead of `1 / √(ρ² − 1)`. Each reading is weighted by how well its ratio is conditioned (`|d ln r / d ln ρ|`) and its contrast over the noise (square-root luminance, judged by Immerkaer's Laplacian estimate); a reading is dropped when a weighty reading of a finer pair, within 1.5 of its radius, is below 0.55 of its radius (a region blurred by a disc holds no sharper edge: the coarse levels of a fine texture would otherwise read as blur).
- **Balls** (`l1/balls.rs`; the rules of section 4, L1 step 2, in numbers). Candidates are the scale-space maxima of the differences of Gaussians of the square root of the brightest channel (half-octave steps from 1 to 22.6 px of the work plane, response at least 0.012 and 3 noise units). Each is measured on the linear plane: the plateau `T` (75th percentile within half the seed radius), the ground `B` (median of a ring), and the region above `B + 0.4 (T − B)` (flood fill from the centre) whose area gives the radius and whose second moments give the elongation (at most 1.6) and fill (at least 0.8 of the ellipse); the ring around it is calm when at most 15 % of it is above `B + 0.5 (T − B)` (a twentieth for a clear ball; a ball of more than 2.5 % of the half-diagonal in radius needs the twentieth, since what shares the ring of a large light is a neighbour it overlaps). The rim softness is `(d20 − d80) / r` from the radial profile. A ball needs `√T − √B ≥ 0.12 + 0.3·max(0, softness − max(0.2, 1.5 px / r))` (the rim of a ball is 1.5 px wide whatever it is, and the optics' own blur is not held against a small one), a softness of at most 1.3 (over 0.9 only with blur evidence around it), `T / B ≥ 2.5` and 6 noise units over the ground, and a radius of at least `max(3.2 px, 0.0025 hd)`. Context: in the ring of 1.5 to 3 radii the sharp-edge pixels may fill 5 % (35 % for a dense map, where it is the lower median of the share over the ring's eight sectors, those with fewer than six pixels left out, so that a subject on one side of a light does not veto it), the edges' mean radius must not read under 1 px or sharper than a third of the ball (a light in focus), and a ball with no edges around it needs a radius of 5 px and at least three such balls in the scene; a plane noisier than 0.008 (square-root units) holds no supported ball. A verification pass drops what the finished list contradicts. The edge readings inside a ball's take-out disc are dropped and the ball's radius is written over 2.2 radii where the map has no reading and the pixel is not known in focus, so the rim is not an edge of the blur. The planes are `active` with at least 5 *clear* balls (those that also pass the first build's strict ring, rim and surround rules) whatever the blur's area: balls that needed the relaxed rules are found and drawn in a photo that is reshaped, but do not make one.
- **Step 4.** Pull-push over a pyramid of 2 x 2 means with a 3 x 3 bilateral window per level (colour tolerance 0.25 in square-root units: a textured background has many colours, so the guide stops only large differences); a level is trusted where the readings in its window weigh 0.25, finer levels first, the rest filled from the coarser level by a guided upsampling. The result per pixel is the mean and variance of the log radius and the cover; `c = cover / (1 + variance / 0.12)`.
- **Step 5.** `b = 0` where `c < 0.3`, in the anchor (radius 0.05 half-diagonals, in focus with `c = 1`), within 2 px of a strong sharp edge (a reading of a radius under half the smallest, 5 noise units), and where the blurred region (4-connected) is smaller than two discs of its own mean radius and holds no ball. The map holds `b` in half-diagonals; the largest `b` (held at `b_cap`) sets the stretch's halo. The planes are `active` (reshaping on) when at least 50 edges were measured (readings of weight 0.1 plus the balls' perimeters) and either 2 % of the frame has `b ≥ 1.5·b_min` with `c ≥ 0.5` or at least 5 clear balls were found. (`b ≥ 2·b_min` was the gate first, but a blur of 5 px on a 1500 px plane, a clear bokeh to the eye, reads at 1.5 to 2 `b_min`; on 32 real photos, nine of the eleven with bokeh pass and three of the other 21 do, cloud patches among them; the two that do not are a bridge blur too wide for the ladder and a mild one.)

The report says "little out-of-focus background" when the planes are not `active` (above). Its tests use synthetic scenes with a known blur per region (section 14). The owner allows a learned model here ("Bokeh map extraction can use ML if needed, of course"). No pretrained model qualifies: every defocus-map and blur-detection model found has a copyleft or missing code licence, or weights trained on data with no grant or a non-commercial one (`research/bokeh-map-2026-10-24.md`). The learned route is therefore a small model of our own (about 2 M parameters, a crop-net-style encoder with a small decoder, input the lens plane, output log blur radius and a confidence), trained on synthetic defocus with a known blur made from licence-clean sharp photos (Open Images, CC BY 2.0, with its CC BY 4.0 masks; DIODE, MIT), bundled and run in Rust like the crop model (section 11). The owner, 2026-10-24: "Our blur map model can be bundled with Yellowmoon": its weights (about 1.5 M parameters, 6 MB in f32) go in `data/models/bokeh/` with a model card, as the crop model's do, with no download. The classical detector above is the first version, the fallback, and the baseline the model must beat. Research-only blur sets (CUHK, DUT-DBD, RTF) may measure both locally (the owner, 2026-10-24); they live only in the git-ignored `testdata/`, are never committed and never trained on.

**As built, the learned map's runtime** (`ym_ml::bokeh_net`, `crates/ym-ml/src/bokeh_net.rs`; `LensPlanes` uses it when it loads, see the paragraph after this list):

- **The network.** `training/bokeh/export.py`'s file, `data/models/bokeh/bokehnet.safetensors` with a `SHA256SUMS` (`BokehNet::load_bundled(data_root)`; `from_file` and `from_bytes` skip or take a checksum), batch norms folded, 1,522,122 parameters, 3,364 multiply-adds per input pixel. The architecture is read from the header (stem, blocks, taps, decoder), and a file whose encoding constants, activation, strides or tensor shapes differ from what the runtime implements is refused at load (`MlError::Unsupported`, `Weights`, `TensorShape`). All maths is `f32` on the CPU in a fixed order (bias first, then input channels or taps in index order; the global mean in `f64`), with no fused multiply-add and `libm` for `exp` and `log2`, so the bits are the same on every run, on any number of threads (one task per output row) and, as far as IEEE 754 goes, on every platform.
- **From the plane to the map** (`BokehNet::map(&PlanarRgbRef) -> BokehMap`, one call, no half-diagonal to pass: `b` is a ratio). The lens plane (planar camera RGB, scene linear, any scale; 1024 to 2047 px long) is box-reduced by exact area weights to a long edge of 1024 px (a plane that is not longer is used as it is, never enlarged), encoded as `training/bokeh/encode.py` does (median-normalised, `log2(1 + v / K) / 16`), padded with replicated edge pixels to a multiple of 32, run, and the answer cropped to `⌈w/2⌉ × ⌈h/2⌉` cells (a 1024 x 683 plane gives 512 x 342). `b = max(r, 1 px) / hd`, `hd` the half-diagonal of the reduced plane in its pixels before padding; `c = exp(−β / 0.25)` with `β = exp(clamp(s, −6, 3))`. The map is raw: the rules of step 5 (`b = 0` where `c` is low, below `b_min`, near a sharp edge, in the anchor) stay with the caller, and the network is not told the focus point. `BokehMap::sample(u, v)` (fractions of the plane), `sample_px(x, y, w, h)` and `resample(w, h)` read it bilinearly at any plane size, held at the edge beyond the outer cell centres.
- **Measured** (parity with the committed `bokeh-3` weights, 2026-10-25; the speed figures are those of the 1,000-step smoke export, the same architecture, and were not measured again). Parity with PyTorch's folded network on the fixture's four cases (three integer patterns of 96 x 64, 64 x 96 and 32 x 32 and one linear plane of 64 x 64): largest difference of `(μ, s)` 2.1e-5 (limit 1e-4; 3.6e-6 to 2.1e-5 over the cases); the encoder's probes 4.7e-10 (limit 1e-6); a whole plane through `map` agrees with the fixture's `b` (in pixels), `c` and `P` to 2.3e-5; the probability against `encode.py`'s 1.3e-7; the export's folding changed the network by 3.5e-5 at most (its limit 2e-4). Two runs and 1, 2, 3 and 5 threads give identical bits, on planes that need padding and on one that is reduced. Speed on a 4-core machine shared with other builds (load 6 to 9), workspace crates at the dev profile's `opt-level = 1`, so a release build is several times faster (not measured): 1024 x 683 plane 1.2 s (reduction 26 ms, encoding 51 ms, network 1.1 s, conversion 11 ms), on one thread 2.2 s; 1512 x 1006 plane 1.5 s; loading the weights 60 to 70 ms. Where the network's time goes: the stem 8 %, the encoder's twelve blocks 63 % (the last three, at stride 32 with 224 and 416 channels on 22 x 32 cells, alone take 25 % of the network's time for a thousandth of its pixels: a kernel that handles several rows per task would help there), the decoder 28 % (its last step, at half resolution, 14 %), the head under 1 %. A GPU path is not needed now.
- **Tests.** `crates/ym-ml/tests/bokeh_net.rs` takes the weights from `data/models/bokeh/` (committed since 2026-10-25) with the fixture `crates/ym-ml/tests/data/bokehnet_parity.json` (written by `export.py --fixture`, SHA-256 of the weights inside), else from `testdata/bokeh-smoke/` (`bokehnet.safetensors`, `parity.json`), else prints a note and passes. The dev example `crates/ym-ml/examples/bokeh_maps.rs` runs the network on the planes `lens_sheet.rs --dump-planes` writes and draws `model_b.png` and `model_c.png` in the detector's colour scales, for a side-by-side look.
- **Plugging it in**: see the next paragraph.

**As built, the learned map in the lens** (`LensPlanes::build_with_map`, `ym_lens::BlurMapInput`; `ym-core` `session.rs` and `pipeline/graph/lens.rs`; `ym_ml::bokeh_net::{detection_probability, BokehMap::p, MIRRORED_PASS}`):

- **Loading.** `SessionEnv::bokeh_model` is loaded once beside the crop model, from `<data_root>/models/bokeh` (checked against its `SHA256SUMS`); `Core::open` hands it to the renderer (`RenderOptions::bokeh_model`), the app and `ym` alike. A missing or damaged folder logs a warning and the classical detector runs (`LensPlanes::build`). `Job::lens_planes` runs the network on the lens plane it already holds, only when the plan reshapes bokeh (`LensPlan::needs_blur_map`), and falls back to the classical detector if the network fails.
- **The decision is `P`, not `c`.** The network's `c = exp(−β / 0.25)` says how sure the radius is, so it is high on sharp areas and low (0.1 to 0.4) inside blur; a gate on it throws the blur away. `P = P(b ≥ 2·b_min)` is the tail of the Laplace(`μ`, `β`) the network gives for `ln r` (`1 − ½·exp(−(μ − t) / β)` for `μ ≥ t`, `½·exp(−(t − μ) / β)` below, `t = ln(2 b_min hd)`; a fixture from `training/bokeh/encode.py` pins it to 2e-6). `BlurMapInput` carries `b = exp(μ) / hd` (at least a pixel) and `P` per cell (2 x 2 pixels of the plane the network saw), resampled bilinearly to the work grid. A cell is blurred when `P ≥ 0.7` (it carries its `b`, every other cell carries 0) and weighs `max(P, 1 − P)`, how sure the decision is; "the photo has bokeh" counts the cells with `P ≥ 0.9` (5 % of the frame, `MAP_MIN_STRONG_AREA` in `l1/detector.rs`, or 5 clear balls; the classical detector keeps its 2 %: see *The gate's share* below), which is the lower-quantile rule `b·exp(−1.61 β) ≥ 2 b_min` and turns the low-confidence blobs of a featureless sky into nothing. The sharp-edge test of L1 is not widened. The anchor, the balls (found on the plane; the cells with `P ≥ 0.7` give the evidence around them, the cells whose `b` is under `b_min` count as sharp pixels, up to 35 % of a ring as the lower median over its eight sectors; the cells with `P ≤ 0.1` are known in focus: nothing is stamped on them and the oval is not drawn over them) and the cleaning of specks are the classical detector's.
- **Mirrored pass.** `MIRRORED_PASS` (on) runs the network a second time on the padded input flipped left to right, flips the answer back and combines the two: the mean `μ` and the scale `mean(β) + |μ₁ − μ₂| / 2`. It cuts the thin sharp structures read as blur (31 % to 10 % of their pixels in the synthetic scenes of `training/bokeh/README.md`) and the blobs on a featureless sky: the share the gate counts on four photos with patches of sky (16238, 55204, 60942, 61159) goes from 3.6, 1.3, 22.6 and 1.2 % to 1.2, 1.1, 5.7 and 0.0 %, and the number of them that are reshaped from two to one. It costs a second network run.
- **Cost** (the 32 planes of `lens-sheets`, 1737 x 1158 px, the dev profile with `opt-level = 1` on 4 shared cores, so a release build is several times faster; milliseconds per photo, median and largest): the network alone 793 and 1,303 (plain), 1,544 and 2,400 (mirrored); the planes given the map (balls, stamping, cleaning) 1,094 and 2,007; the classical detector's own planes 1,492 and 2,861. With the network and the mirrored pass a photo's planes cost about 2.6 s against 1.5 s, once per photo and only when the plan reshapes bokeh; the plain pass adds 0.3 s.
- **Measured against the classical detector** (32 real photos, 11 known to have bokeh; the lens's rule `P ≥ 0.7`, `P ≥ 0.9`, mirrored): the network reshapes 8 of the 11 (the classical detector 9) and 1 of the other 21 (the classical 3, patches of cloud among them). Of 26 further photos it reshapes 1, a field of bokeh the classical detector measures at 30.6 %, and the classical detector 4 (that one, a reflection on water, a plate against dark bands, branches). The three it misses: 29348 (neither detector sees it), and 14077 and 49668, which the classical detector reshapes at 2.8 and 12.4 %: the network reads the blur of 49668 at about `2 b_min` (`P` near 0.6, a mild blur of the gravel), and the classical blobs on the face of 14077 are noise, not blur. Counting the cells at `P ≥ 0.7` for the gate would reshape both (10 of 11) and four photos with a patch of sky (16238, 55204, 60942, 61159) as well; it is the constant `MAP_STRONG_P` in `l1/detector.rs`. Where the network is active its map is far more complete than the classical one (a dandelion's whole background, a swallow's water, a bee's backdrop, ice against sky, a kitten's flowers, against the classical detector's patches).
- **The gate's share, `bokeh-3` (2026-10-25).** The share of the frame at `P ≥ 0.9` that makes a photo "have bokeh" is `MAP_MIN_STRONG_AREA` in `l1/detector.rs`, **5 %** (the classical detector keeps 2 %, `MIN_BLUR_AREA`: its own measure, `b ≥ 1.5·b_min` with confidence, on its own 32 photos).
