# 06 · Nodes and masks (the maths)

This file is the maths of `docs/23-nodes.md`: what a corrector node computes in what order, how the graph of nodes becomes a plan that is evaluated, the two trims, how masks combine (the key input among them), the formula of every mask kind, the raster pipeline that Object, Subject, People, Background and Brush masks go through, the SAM 2.1 small runtime, and the tests that pin them. The numbered sections are cited by the code (`ym-color`, `ym-gpu`, `ym-core`, `ym-ml`). The data, the interface and the tasks are in `docs/23`; the colour stages it builds on are in `docs/02`.

*Built in N-10* (the shapes, the ranges, the combination, the key links and the blend; `docs/23` section 15): `ym_color::mask` holds the maths (`MaskOp::of` derives each row's constants once in f64, `PreparedMasks::mask` is the combination), the CPU chain calls it per pixel, and the colour kernel reads the same `MaskOp`s narrowed to f32.

*Built in N-13* (the raster pass and Brush; `docs/23` section 15): `ym_color::mask_raster` holds the maths (`RasterSteps::of` turns Grow, Feather and Refine edges into whole pixels at a render's scale, `RasterRow::plane` runs the steps over a window of the output grid and quantises, `draw_brush` makes a Brush row's `S0`), `ColorChain::rasters` and `Frame::with_rasters` give the CPU chain the planes, and `ym-gpu`'s `kernels/mask_raster.rs` records the same steps as dispatches of `mask_raster.wgsl` and the colour kernel reads the planes at binding 7. The details this file had left open are written into section 5 below (the rounding of the sizes, the three boxes of Feather, the Fast Guided Filter's blocks and upsampling, the guide, the quantising, the halo, the strokes of Brush).

Notation. `v` is a LogC4 triple, `lin = decode(v)` linear AWG4 (`docs/02` Constants). `Y(lin)` is the AWG4 luminance row of C9, `L = encode(Y(lin))`. `S(t) = t²(3 − 2t)` for `t` in [0, 1] (smoothstep; 0 below, 1 above). `e₁ = (1, −½, −½)/√1.5` and `e₂ = (0, 1, −1)/√2` are the chroma axes of C9 and C9b, and `c(v) = (e₁·v, e₂·v)`. All CPU arithmetic is f64; the GPU runs the same expressions in f32 (`docs/17` K7: constants are written from the Rust constants).

## 1. One corrector node

### 1.1 Order

A node takes the LogC4 value `v_in` that its picture source made (C8, for Main, whose source is the Input) and gives `v_out`:

```
1. trims        lin  = decode(v_in) ⊙ g          g = 2^ev · d(temperature, tint)        (section 2)
                v_t  = encode(lin)
2. C9           v_9  = grade(v_t, L_t)           L_t = encode(Y(lin))                   (docs/02 C9)
3. C9b          v_w  = warp(v_9)                                                         (docs/02 C9b)
4. C11          v_c  = curve(v_w)                                                        (docs/02 C11)
5. mask         m    = mask(v_in, L_in)                                                  (section 3)
                v_out = v_in + m · (v_c − v_in)
```

- **Identity skipping.** A step at its identity is skipped, so a node at defaults changes no bit. Step 1 is skipped when `ev = 0`, `temperature = 0` and `tint = 0` (each exactly 0); step 2 as in `docs/02` C9 (each row at 0 is skipped); step 3 when the node has no pin that moves or acts (`docs/02` C9b); step 4 when all four curves are the identity (two points at (0,0) and (1,1)). A node whose steps 1 to 4 are all skipped, a bypassed node and a node whose `m` is 0 everywhere are skipped as a whole (`v_out = v_in`, and its masks are not evaluated).
- **The luma `L`.** C9 and C11 need the luma of the pixel they read. For Main, whose input is C8's, `L_t = encode(Y(lin))` is computed from the linear values that C7 made, with the trim's gain in them when there is one: no `decode(encode(x))` round trip, so Main with no trim has the bytes C9 had before nodes existed. For every later node `lin = decode(v_in)` is what is known, and `L_t = encode(Y(decode(v_t)))` (with `lin` taken after the trim when the trim runs). `L_in`, the luma the Brightness range reads, is `encode(Y(decode(v_in)))` (for Main the same linear values as above, before the trim).
- **The mask reads the node's input.** `m` is a function of `v_in` (and of the pixel's place, for shapes and raster masks), never of `v_c`: grading a node does not move its own mask or the next node's (`docs/23` section 4.7).
- **The blend is in LogC4.** The look's intensity blends the same way (`docs/02` C10). `m = 1` gives `v_c` exactly and `m = 0` gives `v_in` exactly (the kernel does not compute `v_in + 1·(v_c − v_in)`: it branches on `m` being exactly 1 or exactly 0, so a node without masks has no rounding of the blend).

### 1.2 What changes in the chain

Before nodes the per-pixel chain after C8 was C9 → C9b → C10 (look) → C11 (curve) → C12 → C13. It is now **the nodes in plan order (each: trims, C9, C9b, C11, mask blend) → C10 → C12 → C13**: the curve moved in front of the look (owner, 2026-11-01). A photo with both a curve that is not the identity and a look other than Neutral renders a little differently; every other photo renders as before (section 1.4). LogC3-to-Rec.709 looks already ran the curve first (`docs/02` C10), so every look now follows one rule.

### 1.3 From the graph to the plan

The settings hold a graph (`docs/23` section 3): corrector nodes, the Input and the Output, and links between typed ports (`picture` and `key`). The core **compiles** the graph into a plan; the CPU reference evaluates the plan's steps in order and the kernels read the same plan.

1. **Live nodes.** The Output's picture input has one link. A node is **live** when it is needed: it is on the chain of picture links that leads back from the Output to the Input, or its key output feeds (through any number of key links) the key input of a live node, or it is a picture ancestor of such a node. Other nodes are **dead**: they are kept in the settings and do nothing (their cards are dimmed).
2. **Order.** The plan is a topological order of the live nodes over all links, picture and key (a validated graph has no cycle). Ties are broken by Main first, then by the card's `pos` (x, then y), then by the id, so the order is the same on every machine. A step is one node: `(node, picture source, key source)`.
3. **Registers.** Each step reads the picture value of its picture source and writes its own output picture value; a value is kept until its last reader. Values are LogC4 triples and the plan gives each a *register*; a step that reads a key (section 3, step 2) reads the key value of its key source, a scalar `m`, kept in a *key slot* until its last reader. Both are per pixel: a pixel's whole plan is computed from the pixel's own input and nothing else, so tiles stay exact.
4. **Passes.** A run of per-pixel steps on one pixel is one **pass**. A graph without joins (every graph of this version: a node has one picture input, and the Output has one) compiles into **one pass**, as long as its steps need at most `PLAN_REGISTERS = 8` pictures and `PLAN_KEYS = 16` keys alive at once, which a chain or a chain with a few keyed side branches always does. A graph that needs more, which only a hand-written file with many fan-outs can, and every graph with a join (Parallel, Layer, Splitter and combiner, Outside: a later version) is split by the compiler into several passes with f32 RGB buffers between them (one buffer per value that crosses a split). The compiler is written for splits from the start; this version has the code path and tests it with a graph that needs 9 registers (`docs/23` section 11).
5. **Stops.** A thumbnail of node `k` is the plan compiled to end at `k`'s picture value and then run through the Output; the mask view of `k` is the plan compiled to end at `k`'s `m`. Both name a node, live or dead (a dead node's thumbnail shows what it would make from its own input).

The default graph, Input → Main → Output, is the plan `[Main]`. Nodes numbered on a card are the node's place in this order.

*Readings built in N-03* (`crates/ym-color/src/plan.rs`; `docs/23` section 15): a corrector whose picture input has no link reads the Input's picture (the validator allows such a node and this file did not say what it reads); the Input's picture is the pass's input and takes no register; a value takes a register from the step that makes it to the last step of the pass that reads it, and a step may write into the register its own input leaves, so a chain of any length needs one register; a value that a later pass reads is also written to a buffer when it is made; a pass takes as many steps as fit `PLAN_REGISTERS` and `PLAN_KEYS`, and the next starts with the first step that does not.

### 1.4 Bit-identity of the default graph

With the default graph (the document without `nodes` reads as it: Main with no trim and no masks, linked Input → Main → Output) the plan is `[Main]` and the chain runs Main's C9, C9b and C11 on Main's `grade` (the fields the settings had at the top level before they were node-first, `docs/23` §3), in the order of section 1.1 with steps 1 and 5 skipped, and then C10. For a photo whose curve is the identity or whose look is Neutral this is the sequence of 0.8.2 (a Neutral look and an identity curve are identities, and C10 and C11 commute when either is the identity), so the bytes are equal on the CPU reference. The one case that differs, a curve and a look together, has its own test (section 7). On the device the gates of `docs/17` §12 apply and lavapipe gives the same bytes where the kernel's expressions are unchanged.

### 1.5 The Output

The Output holds C10 (the look and its intensity), C12 (the cine rendering), output sharpening and C13, and is not a node. Its settings are `look`, `look_intensity` and `output_sharpening`. It runs once, after the last node.

## 2. The trims

Both trims are multiplications in linear AWG4. They act first, so the grade after them sees the corrected picture.

### 2.1 Exposure trim

`ev` in stops, −4 to +4 (`TRIM_EXPOSURE_RANGE_EV`), default 0, shown with two decimals and step 0.01. `lin' = lin · 2^ev`, then `encode`. It is exact: the same maths as the Input's exposure (`docs/02` C5–C6), applied to this node's input, with C7's range and toe unchanged (the trim is after C7). A negative `lin` (an out-of-gamut colour) is multiplied like any other, so the trim never clips.

### 2.2 Temperature and tint trim

Temperature and tint are `t` and `n`, each −100 to +100 (`TRIM_TEMPERATURE_RANGE`, `TRIM_TINT_RANGE`), default 0, step 1. They move the white that the picture is balanced for, as the Kelvin and Tint rows of the Input do, and give a von Kries gain in linear AWG4 (a diagonal matrix, green 1).

**The photo's white after the Input.** The Input makes the photo's resolved white neutral: whatever the Kelvin, tint, light preset or Pick white of the photo, its white is `[1, 1, 1]` in AWG4 at the Input's output, and `[1, 1, 1]` in AWG4 is the gamut's white, D65 (x 0.3127, y 0.3290, `docs/02` Gamuts). The trim therefore starts from D65 whatever the Input says. That is the reading of "the photo's resolved white" that this spec uses (`docs/23` section 14, decision 4): a node's gain depends on its two numbers and on nothing else, so the same node pasted on another photo, or kept while the Input's Kelvin changes, renders the same change, and a node's cache key needs no Input state.

**The moved white.** On the CIE 1960 `(u, v)` diagram:

1. **Temperature.** `m₀ = 1e6 / 6504` mired (D65's correlated colour temperature; 153.76 mired). The balanced-for white moves to `m₁ = m₀ − 0.5·t` mired: `t = +100` is 50 mired lower (about 9630 K, a bluer light, so the picture comes out **warmer**, as the Kelvin row does), `t = −100` is 50 mired higher (about 4910 K, a cooler picture). The white's place is the CIE daylight locus at `T₁ = 1e6 / m₁`: `x_D(T)` is the CIE polynomial (4000 K to 7000 K: `−4.6070e9/T³ + 2.9678e6/T² + 0.09911e3/T + 0.244063`; 7000 K to 25000 K: `−2.0064e9/T³ + 1.9018e6/T² + 0.24748e3/T + 0.237040`), `y_D = −3·x_D² + 2.870·x_D − 0.275`, and `(u, v) = (4x, 6y) / (−2x + 12y + 3)`. The range stays inside 4900 K to 9700 K, where the daylight locus is the right one (the 4000 K junction of `docs/02` C3–C4 is never crossed).
2. **The step is a displacement.** The moved white is `W₁ = W₀ + (uv_D(T₁) − uv_D(T₀))` with `W₀` = D65's `(u, v)` and `T₀ = 6504 K`, so that `t = 0` gives `W₀` exactly (the locus polynomial reads D65 about 1.2e-4 off in `y`, and that offset is not used).
3. **Tint.** `n` moves the white by `0.0001·n` in `(u, v)` perpendicular to the locus, at `T₁`: `n = ±100` is ±0.01 Duv. The unit normal `ν` is that of the polyline of `uv_D` at `T₁ ± 1 mired`, pointing **towards green** (the side of the daylight locus on which a light is greener, positive CIE Duv). A positive Tint gains **magenta** in the picture, as on the Input, so the white the picture is balanced for is the greener one: `W₂ = W₁ + 0.0001·n·ν`. For `n < 0` the white moves the other way and the picture gains green.

**The gain.** `(u, v)` to `(x, y) = (3u, 2v) / (2u − 8v + 4)`, `XYZ = (x/y, 1, (1 − x − y)/y)`, then `R(W) = M_XYZ→AWG4 · XYZ(W)` with the matrix of `docs/02` Gamuts. The trim's gain is `d = R(W₀) / R(W₂)` per channel (the colour of a light `W₂` seen by a picture balanced for `W₀`, inverted), normalised to green 1: `d ← d / d_g`. It is a von Kries adaptation done in AWG4 itself.

**Properties** (tests, section 7): `t = n = 0` gives `d = [1, 1, 1]` and the step is skipped; `t > 0` raises red and lowers blue (warmer), `t < 0` the reverse; `n > 0` raises red and blue against green (magenta), `n < 0` the reverse; the red-to-blue ratio is monotone in `t` and the red-and-blue-to-green ratio monotone in `n`; `d_g = 1`; `t = +100` raises the red-to-blue ratio by about 0.49 in log2 (half a stop between red and blue, which is what 50 mired is: a Planck white's blue-to-red ratio changes by 1.44e-2 m K · (1/450 nm − 1/610 nm) · 50e-6 = 0.42 in ln; measured by `crates/ym-color/tests/trims.rs`: 0.4853), and `t = −100` lowers it by the same. (An earlier draft of this file said about −0.17 in log2, which is 17 mired and not 50.) The existing white-point functions `ym_color::wb` (`nudged_cct`, `tinted_white`, `AlexaWhite::locus_white`) work on ALEXA native whites and need the photo's scene light, the class mix and the ARRI matrix at the user's CCT; using them would make a node's gain depend on the Input's state (`docs/23` section 14, decision 4). The profile's `locus.xy` table, the same in all 11 shipped profiles, agrees with the polynomial to within 1.7e-4 in `x` and `y` over 4900 K to 9700 K (measured while writing this spec), so the choice does not change what the Kelvin row and the trim mean.

**The combined gain.** `g = 2^ev · d` is one three-vector per node, computed on the CPU in f64 once per node and given to the kernel as three f32 numbers; the kernel does `encode(decode(v) · g)` and no more.

## 3. How masks combine

A node's mask rows are, in list order, `i = 1 … N`. Each has a kind (section 4), a `mode` (add or subtract), `invert`, `enabled` and `opacity`. Rows with `enabled = false` are absent: they are dropped before anything below.

1. **One mask's value.** The kind gives `s_i` in [0, 1] at the pixel (sections 4 and 5). Then `a_i = o_i · (inv_i ? 1 − s_i : s_i)` with `o_i` the opacity.
2. **The key input.** A corrector has a key input port (`docs/23` section 3.4). When a key link ends on it, the linked node's key output carries that node's final mask `m` at this pixel (step 5), and the key input is an **implicit first region row**: mode add, opacity 1, no invert, `s` = that `m`. When no link ends on it, there is no such row. A node's key output is its `m` whether or not the node is bypassed (a bypassed node's picture is its input, its mask is still made, for the nodes that read it and for the mask view), and a node live only for its key (nothing reads its picture) is run for that `m` alone. (The word *key* names the ports and links; the Colour range and Brightness range rows, the "Keys" group of the Add mask menu, are called **ranges** in this file.)
3. **Regions** are the key input, if any, and the Object, Subject, People, Background, Linear, Radial and Brush rows; **ranges** are the Colour range and Brightness range rows. Each group is folded in list order, the key input first: `acc ← 1 − (1 − acc)(1 − a)` for an add and `acc ← acc · (1 − a)` for a subtract. The first `acc` is 0, except that when the group's first row is a subtract it is 1 (the group then reads "the whole photo, minus this"). The two accumulators are `R` and `K`. *Written out, the first row of a group is taken exactly*: an add gives `a` (not `1 − (1 − 0)(1 − a)`, which is `a` only to rounding) and a subtract `1 − a`, so a node with only a key input has `m` equal to its source's, bit for bit, and a lone Colour range is the range's own `s`.
4. **No group, no mask.** With no region rows (and no key input) `R = 1`; with no range rows `K = 1`; with no rows at all and no key input `m = 1`, the whole photo.
5. **The node's mask** is `m = R · K` (the ranges keep only their part of what the regions and the key input gave), and the node's Invert gives `m ← 1 − m`. This `m` is what the node's **key output** carries to the nodes whose key inputs it feeds, and what the mask view shows. It does not depend on the node's Bypass: a bypassed node still has its mask.

A node with a key input and no rows of its own is therefore `m = m_source`; with the Radial subtracted from it, `m_source · (1 − a)`; with a Colour range, `m_source · K`. Because `m` is made per pixel from this node's `v_in` and the sources' `m`, key links add no spatial step.

The screen rule `1 − (1 − R)(1 − a)` is the owner's choice for overlapping soft edges (2026-11-01): two half-covered pixels give three quarters, nothing exceeds 1, and the order of adds does not matter. A subtract multiplies by `1 − a`, so it cuts exactly its own coverage. A row that selects nothing (an Object mask with no points, a Subject mask the Mac cannot make) is `s = 0`, an enabled row in the fold: with add it adds nothing, so `m` can be 0 and the node changes nothing.

**Cost and evaluation order.** The rows run in list order in the kernel (`MaskOp` records, `docs/23` section 5.4): one loop over the node's rows, any number of them (the settings allow 64), the regions and the ranges folded in two accumulators as they come. Ranges read `v_in`; shapes read the pixel's photo-frame position; raster rows read a plane (section 5). A raster row reads its plane (section 5) at the pixel. A raster row of a kind this machine cannot make yet (Object, Subject, People and Background until N-15 and N-17 to N-19) has no plane and `s = 0`: in a view it is an enabled row in the fold that selects nothing (with Invert on it is `a = o`, as for an Object row with no point), and an export of the photo is refused with `error.kind` `mask` (`docs/10`, `docs/23` section 3.10), never written with the row taken as empty. A chain rendered without planes (the histogram's trace, the pixel readout) gives every raster row `s = 0`, as it gives every shape `s = 0` without a position. A render that cannot tell a pixel's place in the photo (a call that has no position) gives every shape `s = 0`.

**The blend** (section 1.1) is taken for a node that has mask rows, an Invert or a key input (`blends`); a node with none has `m = 1` exactly and keeps its own bits. `m` is made for a node that blends and is not skipped, and for one that is skipped (bypassed, or every step at its identity) only when its key output or the mask view reads it. The blend branches on `m` being exactly 0 (the node's input) or exactly 1 (the node's output) and is `v_in + m (v_c − v_in)` between.

## 4. The shapes and the ranges

All positions are fractions of the photo frame (`docs/23` section 3.6): `(px, py)` is a pixel's place in [0, 1]², and the frame is `W × H` photo pixels with long edge `L_photo`. Lengths are fractions of `L_photo`. For shapes the pixel is taken in an isotropic frame, `p = ((px − cx)·W, (py − cy)·H) / L_photo`, so a circle is a circle on any photo.

**Where the pixel is** (built in N-10). The photo frame is the oriented photo after the lens corrections: the buffer the sampler reads, with the camera's EXIF orientation undone (the frame has no crop, straightening, turn or lens bend in it). An output pixel's place `(px, py)` is the position its centre samples, run back through the sampler's chain: output position, the crop and straightening (`to_canvas`), the lens's bend (`F⁻¹(c / z)`), the division by the canvas size and the user's quarter turns, held to [0, 1]; a pixel the box kernel averages (a shrink of more than 1.25) takes the mean of the places of the sub-samples that fell inside the photo. `SamplerGrid::photo_position` is the f64 reference, and the sampler kernel writes the same two planes beside the pixels (docs/17 section 11a): the colour pass reads them, so a pixel's place is a function of its absolute column and row through the sampler's exact tables and **a tile equals the whole frame bit for bit**. `W`, `H` and `L_photo` are the canvas's with its sides swapped by the user's turns (the oriented photo's size in buffer pixels); `u_px` is the render's scale, and `ε_px = 1/u_px` is the mean of the two step lengths of the output grid in buffer pixels over `L_photo` (a step is one buffer pixel at 100 %, `k` at a reduction by `k`); the lens's zoom, a few percent at most, is not part of it. On the device the photo is placed with the error of the f32 fraction it is written as, about `6e-8` of the frame (3.6e-4 px at 6000 px; measured 1.2e-7 against the f64 reference, `docs/23` section 15), so at Feather 0 and 6000 px a pixel or two of the edge can differ from the CPU's by up to about 1e-3 in `m`.

**The smooth-edge rule.** Every edge, of a shape or of a range, is a smooth ramp at least one output pixel wide, also with Feather 0 (`docs/17` K8: no result depends on a rounding that Metal's fast maths may do differently). In the output grid `u_px` is the number of output pixels along the photo frame's long edge (the render's scale; `docs/23` section 3.6), so one output pixel is `1/u_px` in `L_photo` units; `ε_px = 1/u_px`. A shape's transition is `max(its feather, W)` wide **across the edge, centred on the contour the feather alone has**, with `W = MIN_RAMP_PIXELS · ε_px` and `MIN_RAMP_PIXELS = 1.5`; a range's is `max(its softness band, RANGE_RAMP_MIN)` in code units. A hard edge is therefore a ramp of `W` centred on the edge, the model of a pixel's coverage of a hard edge, and the same mask at two scales differs at an edge by far less than a ramp of the coarser one: the gate of section 7 (largest 0.1, mean 0.005) holds for hard edges too (section 4.2). *History:* the first reading of this section had a Linear half-width of `ε_px` (two pixels, centred) and a Radial ramp of one pixel on the narrower side running inwards from the outline; hard edges then differed by 0.115 to 0.118 (Linear) and 0.52 to 0.61 (Radial) between two scales, over the gate. The lead's decision of 2026-11-02 (`docs/23` section 15) centred both on the edge, one pixel wide (the coverage of a hard edge); at one pixel the gate still failed (0.114 and 0.118), and under the lead's rule to keep whichever width passes best the ramp is 1.5 pixels (a Linear half-width of `0.75 ε_px`, the alternative the lead named: 0.056). The Radial's ramp is also measured across the outline instead of along its narrow axis, which N-10 added to the decision (without it a centred Radial is 0.116 at one pixel and 0.160 at 1.5), for the lead to confirm.

### 4.1 Linear

A band across the photo: `center` `c`, `angle_deg` `θ` (clockwise from the horizontal, 0 = a horizontal line), `feather` `f` in [0, 1] (the transition is `f` of `L_photo` wide). With `n̂ = (−sin θ, cos θ)` (x to the right, y down; for θ = 0 it points down), `s = p · n̂` the signed distance from the centre line and `w = max(f, W)`, `W = MIN_RAMP_PIXELS · ε_px`:

`s_lin = 1 − S((s + w/2) / w)`

It is 1 above the start line (`s ≤ −w/2`), 0 below the end line (`s ≥ w/2`) and 0.5 on the centre line. The mockup's dashed start and end lines are at `s = −w/2` and `s = +w/2`; the solid line is `s = 0`. `n = 1 − s_lin` by the row's Invert. The transition is `w` wide: at Feather 0 it is `W`, 1.5 output pixels, centred on the line.

### 4.2 Radial

An ellipse: `center`, `radii` `(rx, ry)` in `L_photo` units (0.002 to 1), `angle_deg` `θ` (the rotation of the `rx` axis, clockwise), `feather` `f` in [0, 1]. With `(u, v)` the pixel `p` rotated by `−θ` and `d = √((u/rx)² + (v/ry)²)` (1 on the outline), the ramp in units of `d` is

`q = max(f, W · g)`, `g = |∇d| = √((u/rx²)² + (v/ry²)²) / d`, held to [1/max(rx, ry), 1/min(rx, ry)]

(`g` is the number of `d`-units across one unit of `L_photo` at the pixel: 1/r for a circle, running from 1/rx at the ends of the long axis to 1/ry at the ends of the short one, so the ramp is `W` wide in length *everywhere* round the outline), and

`s_rad = 1 − S((d − (1 − f/2 − q/2)) / q)`

(1 for `d` below the ramp, 0 above). The ramp is centred on the contour the feather alone has, `d = 1 − f/2`: with a feather of `W`'s worth or more, `q = f` and the ramp is `[1 − f, 1]` as before (the outline is where the mask reaches 0, the feather extends inwards to the inner ring at `d = 1 − f`, and at `f ≥ 1` the mask is a smooth cone from the centre; the outline and ring are what the page draws), and at Feather 0 it is `W` wide centred on the outline, half inside and half outside it. The kernel clamps `g` as above and floors `d` at 1e-9, so the centre needs no case.

*Edges at two scales* (N-10; the larger render reduced by 4 × 4 against the smaller, a 1200 × 800 photo; `docs/23` section 15 has the table). The edge of a render is `W` of that render, so the render at `k = 4` has an edge four times as wide as the one at `k = 1`; what is compared is a point sample of the wide ramp and a box average of the narrow one. With `W = 1.5 ε_px` the largest difference is 0.046 to 0.056 for a hard Linear (0.056 over ten angles and three offsets), 0.055 to 0.064 for a hard Radial (seven shapes, a circle and a 0.45 × 0.06 sliver among them), the mean at most 6e-4: inside the starting bounds of section 7 (0.1, 0.005). Feathered shapes and the ranges are at most 0.014 (mean 2e-4). The scan that chose `W` (largest over the same placements, a Linear / a Radial): 0.75 px 0.205 / 0.211, **1 px (the coverage of a hard edge by one pixel) 0.114 / 0.118**, 1.25 px 0.037 / 0.040, **1.5 px 0.056 / 0.056** (a Linear at 2 px: 0.118); the Radial with a ramp of `W` along its narrow axis only (the first reading) is 0.116 / 0.160 at 1 / 1.5 px, because the ramp is then 1.67 times as wide at the ends of a 0.25 × 0.15 ellipse's long axis and more as the ellipse is longer.

### 4.3 Colour range

A range on chroma, read from the node's input. `center` `c₀ = (a₀, b₀)` in the plane of `e₁, e₂`, `width` `w` in [0.005, 0.3] (the plane the Colour warp draws reaches 0.30), `softness` `σ` in [0, 1]. With `d = |c(v_in) − c₀|` and `q = max(σ·w, RANGE_RAMP_MIN)`:

`s_col = 1 − S((d − w) / q)` (1 for `d ≤ w`, 0 for `d ≥ w + q`).

The Colour range reads chroma only: it does not look at tone, so a neutral shadow and a neutral highlight have the same `s`. Picking a colour (`sample_chroma`, `docs/23` section 7.6) sets `c₀` to the picked pixel's `c(v_in)` at the node's input.

### 4.4 Brightness range

A range on tone, read from the node's input. `low` ≤ `high` in [0, 1] (LogC4 code values of the luma `L_in`; mid grey is 0.2784, the top code value 1), `softness` `σ` in [0, 1], `q = max(0.25·σ, RANGE_RAMP_MIN)`:

`s_bri = S((L_in − (low − q)) / q) · (1 − S((L_in − high) / q))`

a trapezoid: it rises over `[low − q, low]`, is 1 up to `high` and falls over `[high, high + q]`. A document with `low > high` is read with both set to their mean. Numbers outside [0, 1] are clamped.

`RANGE_RAMP_MIN = 0.02` (about a third of a stop in LogC4; a range's edge is then at most `1.5/0.02 = 75` steep, so a GPU input that differs from the CPU's by `1e-5` moves `m` by at most `7.5e-4`). Measured in N-10 (`docs/23` section 15): the device's `m` is within `1e-5` of the CPU's for every kind, because the inputs agree to `1e-7`.

*Constants for the kernel* (`ym_color::mask::MaskOp::params`, derived once in f64): Linear `cx, cy, sin θ, cos θ, f`; Radial `cx, cy, sin θ, cos θ, rx, ry, f`; Colour range `a₀, b₀, w, q` with `q = max(σ w, RANGE_RAMP_MIN)`; Brightness range `low − q, high, q` with `q = max(0.25 σ, RANGE_RAMP_MIN)`, after `low`, `high` are clamped to [0, 1] and swapped values replaced by their mean. A number that is not a number reads as the field's default. The aspect and `W` (`MIN_RAMP_PIXELS ε_px`) are the render's, not the row's: the kernel takes them from the frame's uniform (`CU.photo`).

### 4.5 The masks' sizes in the UI

The sliders map to the stored numbers as: Feather and Softness 0 to 100 are 0 to 1; Refine edges and Opacity 0 to 100 % are 0 to 1; Grow −100 to 100 is −1 to 1; Width of a Colour range is `0.005 + 0.295·(x/100)²`; Low and High are the code values times 100; a Radial's **Width** and **Height** are the oval's full size as a percent of the long edge, 1 to 200, so the stored radius is half of it (`r = x / 200`), a Brush's **Size** 0 to 100 is `0.001 + 0.249·(x/100)²` of the long edge (the default 0.03 is 34; N-14), and a Linear's or a Radial's **Angle** is in degrees (N-12: the spec gave no rule for the Radial's sizes). The mapping is in `src/screens/tools/maskScales.ts` and tested against the stored ranges.

## 5. Raster masks

Object, Subject, People, Background and Brush are **raster masks**: a picture made once from the photo's pixels (or the person's strokes), kept in memory, and brought to each render's grid. They need the steps below; the shapes and keys need none.

### 5.1 Step by step

1. **The photo-frame raster `S₀`.** A plane of f32 values in [0, 1] over the photo frame (`docs/23` section 3.6), made at the working size of its kind: Object 1024 px on the long edge (SAM's input, section 6), Subject and People the size of Vision's mask, from a 1024 px picture (section 5.3), Background 512 px (the blur map's cells, section 5.4), Brush 2048 px (section 5.5). The plane is **u16 codes** (`code / 65535`; a plane of 2048 x 1365 is 5.6 MB, 2048 x 2048 is 8 MB): `S0` is made in f32 and rounded once, so the CPU and the device read the same numbers. It is kept in a small in-memory cache (8 planes; the device keeps its own copies of the ones it has used) and never on disk, under a key made of what the plane is made from (a hash of a Brush row's strokes and the photo's proportions). It does not depend on the node's grade, Feather, Grow, Opacity, Invert or mode, so changing those re-runs steps 2 to 5 only.
2. **Resample to the output grid.** The output pixel `(i, j)` of the render (a viewport frame, a 100 % tile, a thumbnail or an export tile) maps to a photo-frame position through the same geometry the picture uses (the inverse of crop, straightening, the quarter turn and the lens's bend, `docs/01` *Geometry*), and `S₀` is sampled there. Bilinear when `S₀` is as coarse as the grid or coarser; when the grid is coarser than `S₀` by more than 1.25 the sample is the box-filtered bilinear the picture uses when shrinking. Never Catmull-Rom: a mask must not overshoot. The result is clamped to [0, 1]. Where the position lies outside the photo frame (the empty corner of a straightened photo) the value is 0. *Written out:* `S₀` has `sw x sh` pixels, pixel `i` centred at `i + 0.5`, and `(u, v) = (px sw - 0.5, py sh - 0.5)` for the pixel's place `(px, py)` in the photo frame. With `shrink = max(sw, sh) / u_px` (the pixels of `S₀` under one output pixel), one bilinear sample at `(u, v)` when `shrink <= 1.25` (`SHRINK_BOX_FROM`), else the mean of `n x n` bilinear samples, `n = min(ceil(shrink), 16)`, at the offsets `((a + 0.5) / n - 0.5) shrink` from `(u, v)` along each axis of `S₀` (rows first, `a` from 0), which is a box average of the footprint of the output pixel. The four taps of a bilinear sample are held to the plane, and the place `(px, py)` is the one the sampler gives the pixel (section 4: the mean of its inside sub-samples for the box kernel), so the resample reads nothing of its neighbours.
3. **Grow.** `g ∈ [−1, 1]`. The radius is `r_g = round(|g| · 0.01 · u_px)` output pixels (`GROW_MAX = 0.01` of the long edge at |Grow| = 100). Positive `g` is a grey-scale dilation, negative an erosion, with a **square** structuring element of half-side `r_g` (a separable maximum or minimum; `round` is half away from zero). `r_g = 0` skips the step. A window is cut to the frame (the whole output image): at its edge the maximum is over the pixels that are there. The square is a choice for cost and exactness (section 5.6); it is visible only at |Grow| above about 50, and the feather and the guided filter that follow soften it.
4. **Feather.** `f ∈ [0, 1]`. A Gaussian blur of `σ_f = f · 0.02 · u_px` output pixels (`FEATHER_MAX = 0.02` of the long edge at Feather 100), made as three cascaded box filters whose widths are the standard ones for that variance (sum of the three variances `σ_f²`). `σ_f < 0.5` px skips the step. *The widths* (Wells; Kovesi's "almost Gaussian"): `w = sqrt(4 σ_f² + 1)` rounded down to an odd number `w_l`, `w_u = w_l + 2`, and the first `m = round((12 σ_f² - 3 w_l² - 12 w_l - 9) / (-4 w_l - 4))` of the three boxes are `w_l` wide, the rest `w_u` (`feather_boxes`); a box of width `w` is the mean over `[x - (w - 1)/2, x + (w - 1)/2]`, and its variance is `(w² - 1) / 12`. The summed variance is `σ_f²` to within 7 % from `σ_f = 5` (0.9 % at 36); the sum of the three half-widths is `R_f`. Each box is a horizontal and a vertical pass, the window cut to the frame and divided by the number of samples it kept.
5. **Refine edges (the guided filter).** `ρ ∈ [0, 1]`, default 0.5 (Brush 0). After He, Sun and Tang (2010), with the Input's luma as guide `G`:
   - `r = round(ρ · 0.008 · u_px)` output pixels (`GF_RADIUS_MAX = 0.008` of the long edge at Refine edges 100) and `ε = 10^(−2 − 3ρ)` (1e-2 at 0, 3.2e-4 at 0.5, 1e-5 at 100 %), on `G` in code values 0 to 1.
   - With `P` the mask from step 4, and the mean over the window of half-side `r` written `⟨·⟩` (window clipped at the frame's edge and divided by the number of samples):

     `μ_G = ⟨G⟩`, `μ_P = ⟨P⟩`, `σ²_G = ⟨G²⟩ − μ_G²`, `cov = ⟨G·P⟩ − μ_G·μ_P`
     `a = cov / (σ²_G + ε)`, `b = μ_P − a·μ_G`
     `q = ⟨a⟩ · G + ⟨b⟩`, clamped to [0, 1].
   - **Refine edges 0** (`r = 0`): the step is skipped and the mask is `P` as step 4 made it. **Refine edges 100**: `r` is 0.8 % of the long edge (48 px on a 6000 px photo at full size) and `ε = 1e-5`, so even edges of a few hundredths of a code value in the guide pull the mask's edge to them: hair, a ridge, a window frame. In between `r` grows linearly and `ε` falls by a decade per third of the range. `r < 1` skips the step. At the sizes of a fit view `r` is a few pixels.
   - The guide `G` is the luma `encode(Y(lin))`, clamped to [0, 1], of **the Input's picture** at the output grid (after C8, before any node: section 5.7), so it does not move when a node is graded. It is made for the tile plus its halo before the colour pass. *Which luma:* `encode(Y(lin))` of the linear AWG4 values C7 made, the same number a Brightness range of a node that reads the Input sees (`ColorChain::guide_pixel`; kernel stop 13), 0 for a pixel outside the photo.
   - *Windows and clipping.* A window mean is over the pixels of `[x - r, x + r]` (a horizontal pass, then a vertical one) that lie inside the frame, in ascending order, divided by their number. `a`, `b` and `q` are as written; `ε` is added to the variance as it is, and `q` is clamped to [0, 1].
   - *The Fast Guided Filter* (`r > 16`: `s = ceil(r / 16)`, `r_s = round(r / s)` samples). The guide and the mask are averaged over blocks of `s x s` pixels aligned at **absolute** multiples of `s` (a block at the frame's edge is the mean of the pixels it has), `a` and `b` are found on that grid with a window of `r_s` cells (the means of the guide, the mask, the guide squared and the guide times the mask of the blocks), `⟨a⟩` and `⟨b⟩` are their window means, and they are upsampled bilinearly to the pixel: at the pixel `(x, y)` the coordinate `(x + 0.5) / s - 0.5` on the grid, the four neighbours held to the grid, `a` interpolated along `x` and then `y`; then `q = ⟨a⟩ G + ⟨b⟩` with the full-resolution `G`. The receptive field is `(2 r_s + 3) s` (two windows of `r_s` cells, the block and the interpolation's cell); for `s = 1` it is `2 r`.
   - *Rounding.* On the device the means and the products are f32; `ε` is at least 1e-5 and the guide's variance is the difference of two means in [0, 1], so a `q` differs from the CPU's by a few 1e-5 (section 7).
6. **Quantising.** The result of step 5 (or the last step that ran) is held to [0, 1] and rounded to a u16 code, `floor(v · 65535 + 0.5)` (`quantise`), and this is the plane the colour pass reads, on the CPU and on the device alike: the planes are bytes, not approximations.
7. **Mode, Invert and Opacity** are then applied where the mask is used (section 3, step 1).

The pipeline is run per output grid. A thumbnail of 160 px, a fit view of 1500 px and an export of 6000 px run it at their own `u_px`, with the sizes of steps 3 to 5 scaled to it, so one setting looks the same at every size and the mask's content is the same raster `S₀`.

### 5.2 Receptive field and tiles

Steps 2 to 5 are the only spatial steps of the grade. A tile's output depends on the grid around it by

`H = r_g + R_f + H_gf` output pixels

(the dilation or erosion, the feather's three boxes with `R_f` the sum of their half-widths, and the guided filter's two nested windows, `H_gf = 2 r`, or `(2 r_s + 3) s` when it is subsampled; the resample reads the pixel's own place and nothing of its neighbours), and for several rows the largest `H` of the tile's rows (`RasterSet::halo`). *History:* the first reading of this section had `H = 2 + r_g + R_f + 2 r + 1` and `2 s 16 + s`; the resample needs no pixel of the neighbourhood in this form, and the subsampled form's reach is the one computed here. The tile is computed over itself grown by `H` and cut to the frame, then cut to the tile. Every window fold is a **blocked fold with absolute block origins** (van Herk 1992, Gil and Werman 1993), cut to the frame and to the plane (`docs/17` D4): for a window of half-side `r` the line is cut into blocks of `2r + 1` samples that start at absolute multiples of `2r + 1`; a window is either one block or the end of one and the start of the next, so it is the *suffix* scan of the first joined to the *prefix* scan of the second, `suffix[x - r] + prefix[x + r]` (a box mean's sum, divided by the number of samples the window kept inside the frame and the plane) or `max`, `min` of them (Grow). The scans restart at every block boundary and run in a fixed order (prefixes upwards, suffixes downwards), so a window's value is made from the samples of the window only, in an order that depends on their absolute positions alone: it costs three operations a pixel at any radius and a tile equals the whole frame bit for bit. The blocks of the subsampled form start at absolute multiples of `s` and all positions are absolute, so **a tile equals the whole frame bit for bit** (`docs/17` D5; a halo one pixel short differs, which the tests show). On the device a fold is two dispatches (`SCAN`: one thread for each line and block writes the prefixes and suffixes into two scratch planes, `COMB`: one thread a pixel joins them), on the CPU one pass a row (a column is the transposed row). *History:* the first version summed each window directly in ascending order, a cost of its width a pixel (Feather 100 at 6000 px is boxes of about 240 pixels; 5.1 s a 1024 px tile on lavapipe); the blocked fold costs the same at every radius.

**The grown window and the binding.** The device keeps the f32 planes of the grown window in one scratch buffer (seven planes, 28 bytes a pixel: the mask, a temporary, the two planes of a fold's scans, and three of the guided filter's means) beside the sampler's six-plane image (24), so a window whose grown area passes the design binding (`docs/17` section 4) is not made in one piece. Tiles equal the whole frame, so the cure for an export is a smaller tile (`largest_tile_for`: a quarter smaller at a time, down to 64 px), and for a view it is bands (below).

*What a view's frame is.* The window sums are clipped to the **photo's extent**, the pixels whose centres are inside the final image (`view_frame`), which is the whole output image of an export at the view's scale; the coordinates are those of the extent, whose first pixel is `(0, 0)`, so a 100 % view's blocks are an export's blocks. A pass makes its planes the way an export tile is made: the picture the colour pass makes (the view, or the view grown by the output sharpening's border) is **grown by `H` and cut to the extent**, that wider picture is sampled and guided, the planes are made over it and cut to the part of the colour pass's picture that is inside the extent (`RasterRead`). A zoomed or panned view therefore sees the photo go on past its edge: a pixel near the border has the planes of the export at that scale (within the plane bound, 4e-4; tests: a 100 % view of a region by a feathered dab against the export, and two views panned by a few pixels against each other, on the CPU and on the device), and a pan moves no pixel of the mask. A fit view and a thumbnail show the whole extent, so the wider picture is the extent itself and they are as exact as before; the paper around the photo has no plane (code 0) and is painted by the letterbox. A view magnified above 100 % with output sharpening makes its planes on the 100 % picture it magnifies (the colour pass runs there, `docs/algorithms/04`), at that scale's `H`.

*Views the device cuts into bands.* A band is a view of its own, so its planes are made from the band grown by `H` like any other view's; `band_split` sizes the bands so that the wider picture of every band (the band, the stage's border and `H` on every side) fits the band bytes, which for a chain with raster rows is also capped so that the scratch of the pass fits the design binding (`ViewRoute::new`). The bands are the view in one piece bit for bit (the sums are tile-exact and the positions are the same) and are made on the device; a view whose receptive field alone passes the binding (a zoom of several hundred percent that is not sharpened, with Feather up) is made on the CPU, quietly, like any frame whose buffers do not fit.

### 5.3 Subject and People

On macOS 14 or later, from Apple's Vision framework (`VNGenerateForegroundInstanceMaskRequest` for Subject and `VNGeneratePersonInstanceMaskRequest` for People; `docs/23` section 4.5):

1. The Input's picture, with the Neutral look and no output sharpening (section 5.7), at 1024 px on the long edge, rounded to 8 bits without dither, sRGB, is handed to the request as a `CGImage` in the oriented photo frame.
2. The result is an instance mask. `S₀` is the **union of all its instances** (`allInstances`) at the picture's size, `generateScaledMaskForImage(forInstances:from:)`, converted to f32 in [0, 1]. A photo in which Vision finds no instance gives `S₀ = 0` and the row says "Nothing found."
3. Nothing is trained, tuned or stored from the answers. The same picture on the same Mac gives the same mask (the macOS CI job runs it twice); another macOS version may answer differently (`docs/23` section 12).

### 5.4 Background from blur

From the blur map of lens character (`docs/21` section 7: `ym_ml::bokeh_net` on the lens plane, cached per photo; the classical detector is its fallback). `P(x)` is the probability that the map's cell is out of focus (`P(b ≥ 2·b_min)`), on the cells of the 1024 px plane halved (512 px on the long edge). The row has `level` `λ` in [0, 1] (default 0.5) and `softness` `σ` in [0, 1] (default 0.5):

`S₀ = S((P − (1 − λ) + w/2) / w)`, `w = max(σ, BLUR_RAMP_MIN)`, `BLUR_RAMP_MIN = 0.05`

so `λ = 0.5` puts the edge where the map's own decision falls (`P = 0.5`), a higher level lets more of the photo count as background and a lower one only what is strongly out of focus, and Softness widens the ramp in `P`. The map is the one of the photo's raw group and lens-free picture (it does not move when the lens sliders do: it is made from the raw group, as lens character's, `docs/21` section 3). Nothing is added to the photo where it has no blur: an evenly sharp photo gives `S₀ ≈ 0` (the map says "little out-of-focus background").

*As built (N-15; `ym_core::pipeline::graph::background`, `ym_color::mask_raster::{BlurMap, BackgroundSource, background_s0, background_plane}`).*

- **The map.** `P` is `BokehMap::p` of `ym_ml::bokeh_net` on the lens plane, unchanged: the raw group at `k_L` (`ym_lens::lens_plane_scale_for`, the same plane and the same scale the lens reads, with a lens or without), the cells of the network (2 x 2 pixels of the plane the network saw, about 512 px on the long edge). **It is the lens's map:** a photo has one blur entry in its graph (a viewer) or in its request (an export, made from the CPU reference raw group like the lens's planes, so the bytes of an export never depend on what the viewer's device made), made by whichever of the lens's bokeh reshaping and a Background row needs it first; the other reads it (`StageRuns::blur_maps` counts the network runs: one for both). The lens sliders do not move it. It is made again only when the raw group's parameters change (`RawParams`: the Input's sharpening and noise reduction, as the lens's planes are).
- **The classical fallback** (no model, or the model fails): `ym_lens::classical_blur_map`, the detector of `docs/21` section 7 on the lens plane (with the camera's focus point as the anchor), pooled 2 x 2 from its work plane into cells; `P` of a cell is the mean of the detector's confidence `c` over the pixels it calls out of focus (`b >= 2 b_min`, the network's own definition) and 0 over the others. An evenly sharp plane is all 0. Its cells are the work plane's, pooled 2 x 2 (the lens plane, or its half when the plane's long edge is 1536 px or more), so they are not always 512 across.
- **The frame.** The cells are turned to the photo as the camera oriented it (EXIF orientation 1 to 8, `orient_cells`: a turn of the grid, no value changes) before they are a source; the user's quarter turn, straightening, crop and the lens's bend are the sampler's, as for Brush. `S₀` is the plane of the map's cells (`sw x sh` = the map's width and height), cut by `background_s0` in f64 from the f32 cells and rounded once to u16; the resample of section 5.1 takes it to the output grid. The plane covers the photo frame's whole extent.
- **The keys.** Every map the core makes has a key of its own (a counter), so a plane made from an older map is never read for a new one. The plane cache (process-wide, 16 planes) is keyed by `(map key, Level, Softness)` and holds nothing of Feather, Grow or Refine edges: Level and Softness cut a new plane from the cached cells (no network run), the other three re-run steps 2 to 5 only (the grade plan is made again, the plane is a cache hit). The grade key holds the map's key and the row's five numbers.
- **The ends.** The formula is applied as written, which has two consequences worth knowing: the ramp is centred on the edge, so at Level 20 a cell with `P = 1` is `S(0.9) = 0.97`, not 1; and at Level 100 the edge is at `P = 0`, so a cell with no blur at all is `S₀ = 0.5` and the mask is at least one half everywhere (Level 100 means "everything counts, the blurred part fully"). The default (Level 50, Softness 50) maps `P` up to 0.25 to 0 and from 0.75 to 1; a sharp photo's `P` is under 0.05, so it is empty at every Level up to `1 - w/2`.
- **Tests:** `crates/ym-color/tests/background.rs` (the formula against f64 over `P`, Level and Softness; the default; the ends; the minimum ramp; NaN; the cache), `crates/ym-gpu/tests/gpu_raster_masks.rs` (a Background plane on the device against the CPU's, and tiles bit for bit), `crates/ym-core/tests/mask_background.rs` (the real photos of `testdata/lens-photos` the lens was judged on, the classical map, a sharp photo, the EXIF orientations, the map shared with the lens in both orders, the counters, exports and tiles, determinism, a turned photo), `crates/ym-cli/tests/{render,parity}.rs`.

### 5.5 Brush

The strokes of the row, in list order, drawn into a plane of 2048 px on the long edge (the other side with the photo's proportions, square pixels: 2048 x 1365 for a 3:2 photo), starting at 0. A stroke has `size` `D` (the brush's diameter, 0.001 to 0.25 of `L_photo`), `hardness` `h` (0 to 1), `erase` and the path `points`. In plane pixels the brush has radius `R = max(D · 2048 / 2, 0.5)` and a ramp `ρ = min(max((1 - h) R, 1), R)` wide that ends at the radius; a stamp at distance `d` from its centre (to the pixel's centre) has weight

`w(d) = 1` for `d ≤ R - ρ`, `1 - S((d - (R - ρ)) / ρ)` up to `R`, 0 beyond

(a hard disc at `h = 1` with a ramp of the plane's one pixel, and at least that for every hardness). The path is resampled by arc length: a stamp at the first point and at every `max(0.15 · D · 2048, 0.5)` plane pixels along the polyline, and one at its last point; a single point is one stamp; repeated points are one. A paint stamp makes `S ← 1 - (1 - S)(1 - w)`, an erase stamp `S ← S·(1 - w)`. The plane is made on the CPU in f32 in this order (the geometry in f64: only IEEE operations and a square root, no `sin`, `exp` or `libm` call) and is the same on every machine; the stamps' work is capped (4e9 pixels touched, `BRUSH_WORK_LIMIT`: a file with absurd strokes ends where the budget ends, the same way everywhere), and the result is rounded to u16 (section 5.1, step 1). A stroke's `points` are stored simplified (the page drops the points nearer than `D/8` to the line between their neighbours when it ends the stroke, `docs/23` section 7.6). *Tests* (`crates/ym-color/tests/mask_raster.rs`): a stamp's profile at three hardnesses against the formula, a hard brush's one-pixel ramp, paint then erase and the order of strokes, the screen rule of overlapping strokes, an even bar along a path, the proportions of the plane, determinism and the cache.

### 5.6 Choices and their reasons

- **Order of steps 3 to 5.** Grow acts on the mask as the model made it, Feather then softens the outline the way a person expects ("make it softer"), and the guided filter last pulls the result to the picture's edges, so a feathered Object mask with Refine edges up still ends on the object's edge where the picture has one.
- **Box-based filters.** Every filter of the pass is a (cascade of) box filter or a separable maximum, so it costs a few operations a pixel at any radius, and with absolute block origins it is exactly tile-independent (`docs/17` D4). A true Gaussian or disc would cost its radius in taps a pixel at 24 MP.
- **Photo-relative sizes.** Sizes are fractions of the long edge so a thumbnail, a fit view and an export agree (`docs/23` section 3.6).

### 5.7 What the AI masks look at

SAM, Vision and the blur map read **the photo as the Input renders it**: C1 to C8, the Neutral look, the cine rendering (C12) and no output sharpening, at 1024 px (SAM and Vision) or the blur map's own input. No node grade, no look the person chose and no output sharpening reach them, so grading a node, changing the look or moving a slider of another node never moves a mask, and a photo copied to another photo's settings finds its own objects.

## 6. SAM 2.1 small

Object masks come from Meta's SAM 2.1, the **small** size (checkpoint `sam2.1_hiera_small`, 46 million parameters), run by our own pure-Rust CPU runtime `ym_ml::sam2`. The model's code and checkpoints are Apache-2.0 (`docs/14`). Python is used only to convert the weights once and to write fixtures from Meta's own code (the oracle); nothing in the app or `ym` runs Python (`docs/00`).

### 6.1 What we implement

SAM 2 is built for video, and its image mode uses only a part of it. We implement the image path, and nothing of the video path (no memory attention, no memory encoder, no object pointers, no tracking):

- **The image encoder**: Hiera-S (embedding dimension 96, 1 head at the first stage, stages of 1, 2, 11 and 2 blocks, global attention in blocks 7, 10 and 13, windowed attention elsewhere with the windows of Meta's configuration, query pooling by 2 at the start of stages 2 to 4, channel widths 96, 192, 384, 768 at strides 4, 8, 16, 32) and the FPN neck (256 channels, `fpn_top_down_levels` 2 and 3, nearest-neighbour upsampling, `scalp` 1), as in `sam2.1_hiera_s.yaml`. The neck's sine position encoding is made by Meta's code but only the video path reads it (the decoder uses the prompt encoder's dense encoding), so the runtime does not compute it. Its outputs are the image embedding (64 × 64 × 256, stride 16) plus the learned `no_mem_embed` added to it (`directly_add_no_mem_embed`), and the two high-resolution maps for the decoder (256 × 256 × 32 and 128 × 128 × 64, after the 1 × 1 convolutions `conv_s0` and `conv_s1`). The positional embedding of the trunk (7 × 7 background plus the 8 × 8 window embedding, bicubic to the feature size with PyTorch's kernel `a = −0.75`) is computed once when the weights are loaded.
- **The prompt encoder**: positive and negative point embeddings, the two box-corner embeddings, the random-Fourier positional encoding of coordinates (`(x + 0.5)/1024`), and the dense positional encoding of the 64 × 64 grid. The tokens are, in Meta's order, the box corners (labels 2 and 3), then the points, then **always one padding token** (`not_a_point_embed`): Meta's image predictor hands the box to the encoder as two points, so the encoder never sees a box and always pads. One click is two tokens, a box three. No mask prompt is given: **a mask is a function of the points and the box alone**, so settings that hold only the points reproduce it.
- **The mask decoder**: the two-way transformer (depth 2, 256 dimensions, 8 heads, MLP 2048, downsampling rate 2), the output upscaling with the high-resolution features (`use_high_res_features_in_sam`), the hypernetwork MLPs for 3 + 1 mask tokens, the IoU head (sigmoid) and the object-score head (`pred_obj_scores`). A single positive point is decoded with `multimask_output` on and the mask with the highest predicted IoU is taken; two or more points, or a box, with it off (Meta's own advice for unambiguous prompts), with the dynamic fall-back to the best multimask output where the single mask's stability (the area at logit > 0.05 over the area at logit > −0.05, 1 for an empty mask) is below 0.98 (`dynamic_multimask_via_stability`). *Checked in N-17:* the checkpoint carries no configuration of its own, and 0.98 with delta 0.05 are the values `build_sam2` sets for the image predictor (`apply_postprocessing=True`, its default; the yaml does not name them). They are written into the weights file's header, and the runtime refuses a file that says otherwise.
- **Not implemented**: memory attention, the memory encoder, the video predictor and its propagation, mask prompts, the mask-input path.

### 6.2 Input and preprocessing

`SAM_INPUT_PX = 1024`. The photo, as in section 5.7 (the Input's picture, Neutral look, 8 bits, sRGB), is resized to 1024 × 1024 **without keeping the aspect ratio** (as Meta's `SAM2Transforms`), with an antialiased bilinear resize, then normalised with mean `(0.485, 0.456, 0.406)` and standard deviation `(0.229, 0.224, 0.225)`. The resize is our own: a separable triangle filter whose support is scaled by the shrink factor, in f32, in a fixed order. Point and box coordinates in the photo frame (`x, y` in [0, 1]) are multiplied by 1024 (`transform_coords` of Meta's code). Meta's `Resize` is torchvision's default on a tensor, which is bilinear with antialiasing in the torchvision versions the fixtures record; the fixtures hold both the 8-bit picture and the tensor Meta's code made from it, so the encoder's parity (section 6.5) is measured from identical tensors and our resize has its own bound.

### 6.3 Output and the raster

The decoder gives low-resolution logits of 256 × 256, clamped to ±32 (Meta's clamp). `S₀` of the Object row is `σ(logit)` upsampled bilinearly to 1024 px on the long edge in the photo frame (the 256 × 256 square is stretched back to the photo's aspect), with `σ(x) = 1/(1 + e^{−x})` (`ym_ml::sam2::soft_mask`: half-pixel centres, edges repeated, 1024 px on the long edge and the other side rounded; Meta's own post-processing upsamples the logits and then thresholds, which differs from this only in the band of a pixel or two at the edge). A soft mask goes into section 5.1 as it is. **Nothing found:** the click is refused when the object score is not above 0 or when the mask at logit > 0 covers less than 0.05 % of the photo (`OBJECT_MIN_AREA = 0.0005`); no point is added and no empty mask is kept (`docs/23` section 4.4).

### 6.4 Determinism (as `bokeh_net`)

Everything is f32 in a fixed order, with no fused multiply-add and with `libm` for `exp` (softmax, the sigmoids), `erf` (GELU), `sin` and `cos` (the prompt's Fourier encoding), so the same picture and prompt give the same bits on every run, on any number of threads, and, as far as IEEE 754 goes, on every platform. Matrix products accumulate the bias first and then the inputs in index order, one chain of `f32` multiply-then-add per output element; the register blocking, the cut into tasks (every output row of every product, attention and window is computed by one task, whatever the pool) and the instruction set (the x86-64 build is compiled for the base set and for AVX2, chosen at run time; Apple silicon uses NEON through the same source) decide which chains run side by side and change no bit, because nothing is reassociated. Softmax and layer norm sum in f64 in index order. The weights are read from a safetensors file (f16 stored, widened to f32 on load; `format` and `version` in the header, a SHA-256 checked against `SHA256SUMS`) whose bytes are written deterministically. Window attention and the unfold of the windows follow the order of Meta's code. A model whose header differs from what the runtime implements is refused with an error, never a panic.

### 6.5 The oracle and its gates

- **Fixtures.** `training/sam2/oracle.py` (Python, run by the `models` workflow only, never a dependency of a shipped crate; Meta's `sam2` package at a pinned commit on CPU in f32, with our published f16 weights widened to f32) writes, for 20 public photos (CC0 and public-domain, from Wikimedia Commons, listed with their licences and hashes in `training/sam2/oracle_photos.json`), the 8-bit picture, the 1024² tensor, the encoder's three feature maps, and for each of 3 prompts per photo (one click, a positive and a negative click, one box) the low-resolution logits, the IoU and object scores and the mask. The fixtures are generated data and are not committed when large; `tools/fetch/` makes them.
- **Gates.** Mask IoU of our mask against Meta's at logit > 0, at the 256 × 256 resolution, **at least 0.99** on every photo and prompt; the logits within a stated bound from identical tensors (starting numbers: largest absolute difference 0.05, mean 0.005; the encoder's maps within 2e-3 of their RMS); our resize within 1e-2 of Meta's tensor in the normalised values (mean 1e-3); a second run bit-identical; the runtime's tiles-and-threads independence (1, 2 and 8 threads give equal bits). The encoder's time on the CPU is **measured and recorded** (estimates: 1.5 to 5 s on Apple silicon; the decoder 50 to 150 ms a click) and if the encoder is too slow the fallback is the same encoder on the GPU, still within a stated bound before N-18. A gate that fails is reported, never loosened.
- **Measured in N-17** (`crates/ym-ml/tests/sam2.rs`, 20 photos, 60 prompts, release build; the worst, with the mean where it is stated). Resize against Meta's tensor: largest difference 7.2e-7, mean 4.4e-8 (worst photo). The encoder's three maps from Meta's tensor: RMS of the difference over the map's RMS 3.4e-6 (image embedding), 1.0e-6 (stride 4), 3.6e-6 (stride 8); largest absolute difference 2.5e-5, 1.3e-6, 6.0e-6. The decoder's logits from Meta's maps: largest 2.9e-4, mean 9.2e-5 at worst (2.3e-5 over the 60 prompts); object score 2.7e-5, predicted IoU 8.9e-6. The whole path (our resize, our encoder, our decoder): largest 5.1e-4, mean 1.3e-4 at worst (2.6e-5 over the 60). Mask IoU at logit > 0, 256 × 256: 0.99978 at the smallest from Meta's maps, 1.00000 on the whole path; **the same mask chosen on all 60 prompts**; 20 prompts decoded with the multimask output, and 22 of the 40 single masks were unstable (below 0.98) and fell back to a multimask token, as Meta's did. A second run and 1, 2 and 8 threads give equal bits (preprocess, the three maps and three decodes). **The bounds N-17 proposes in place of the starting numbers** (4 to 14 times the measured worst; the tests assert them; the starting numbers above are met 20 to 2000 times over): resize 1e-5 largest and 1e-6 mean, encoder 5e-5 of the RMS, decoder 3e-3 largest and 5e-4 mean, whole path 5e-3 largest and 5e-4 mean, mask IoU at least 0.999. The fixtures and the weights are fetched by `tools/fetch/sam2.sh --fixtures`; the tests print `SKIPPED` and pass when they are absent (`YM_REQUIRE_SAM2=1` makes that a failure), and need an optimised build (`cargo test -p ym-ml --release --test sam2`): the encoder is 180 billion multiply-adds, so a photo of the gates takes about 11 s in release and about 90 s in the dev profile.
- **Speed, measured in N-17** (`cargo run --release -p ym-ml --example sam2_time`; a 4-core x86-64 Linux machine, Intel Xeon at 2.1 GHz with AVX2, shared with another build job, so the wall times are the best of several runs and the CPU time is the load-free number): the encoder does 11.3 to 11.8 CPU-seconds of work (1 thread: 12.0 s wall); on 4 threads 3.1 s best and 3.6 s median at a quiet moment, 5 to 6.5 s while the neighbour job ran; the trunk is about 98 % of it. A click (prompt encoder and decoder, from kept features) takes 54 to 56 ms best and 70 to 85 ms median on 4 threads; loading the weights 0.5 to 0.6 s; resident memory 360 MB with the model (the model is 154 MB of f32, the kept features 16.8 MB). About a third of the encoder's work is `erf` of the GELU (126 million calls at 20 ns) and `exp` of the softmax (about 240 million at 8 ns, 200 million of them in the three global-attention blocks); a vectorised version of the two functions with the same determinism could save up to a quarter, and is a change of this section and the owner's to agree. On Apple silicon the numbers are not measured yet (M0-H4 style Mac check).

## 7. Tests (`docs/23` section 11 has the gates)

- **Trims.** `ev = 0`, `t = n = 0` are skipped and change no bit; `ev` is `encode(decode(v)·2^ev)` exactly (f64, 1e-13); `d` for the named properties of section 2.2; `t = n = 0` gives `[1, 1, 1]`; the red-to-blue ratio is monotone in `t`, the (red, blue) to green ratio in `n`; `d_g = 1`; `t = +100` and `t = −100` are inverse in the sense `d(t)·d(−t) ≈ [1, 1, 1]` to 5e-3.
- **Node order.** A node with each step on its own equals the same step in the chain it replaced (C9, C9b and C11 of 0.8.2) bit for bit; Main with a curve and Neutral equals 0.8.2; the curve-and-look case differs and is pinned by its own vectors.
- **Combination.** Add and subtract against hand-worked cases (including the lone subtract and a key input as the first row: `m = m_source`, minus a Radial, times a Colour range), screen on overlapping soft edges, the ranges' product, Invert per mask and per node, Opacity, a row with the eye off is absent, an empty Object row gives `m = 0`, no rows gives `m = 1`; the fold is independent of the order of two adds.
- **The plan.** The default graph compiles to `[Main]` in one pass; a chain of 6 nodes to 6 steps in one pass with one register; the order is the topological order with Main first and the ties of section 1.3 (two orders of the same graph in a file give one plan); a graph with a dead branch (a node whose picture output goes nowhere and whose key output is unlinked) leaves that node out of the plan and renders the bytes of the same graph without it; the same branch with its key output linked to a live node's key input puts it in the plan (its mask is used, its picture is not); a graph whose steps need 9 pictures alive compiles to two passes with an f32 buffer between them, and renders the same picture as the one-pass evaluation of the CPU reference within the gate of `docs/23` section 11; a thumbnail stop and a mask-view stop compile to the right prefix of the plan; every graph the validator refuses (a cycle through picture and key links, a link between ports of different types, a second link on an input) never reaches the compiler.
- **Shapes and ranges.** Linear and Radial against an independent f64 evaluation on a grid; the ramp is a ramp of `W` across the edge, centred on it, at Feather 0, at k = 4, 2 and 1 (round the whole outline of an ellipse for a Radial); the value at the centre line is 0.5; ranges against the formulas, the plane's colour at the picked pixel gives 1; GPU against CPU within 2e-4 in LogC4 on the blended output for nodes whose effect is at most 0.25 in LogC4 (the starting bound; a larger `RANGE_RAMP_MIN` is the remedy if it fails, a decision of the owner). Built in N-10: `crates/ym-color/tests/masks.rs` (the formulas, the combination, the key input, the blend, the mask stop, frames with positions) and `crates/ym-gpu/tests/gpu_masks.rs` (every kind over nine geometries, combinations, 64 rows, key links, keys across passes, tiles, positions, the radial on its feature, the two scales, the cost).
- **Raster pass.** Resample, grow, feather and the guided filter against independent f64 references on synthetic masks and pictures (`crates/ym-color/tests/mask_raster.rs`: the sizes at two scales, the resample at a pixel's centre, between pixels and under a shrink, grow and erosion on a point and a square, the feather boxes' variance and an impulse's response, the guided filter against explicit double loops for `s = 1` and for the subsampled form, a step edge with a matching guide returning a step to within 1e-3, a soft mask pulled to the guide's edge); Refine edges 0 and Grow 0 and Feather 0 are exact skips; the guide is the C7 luma code; **tiles equal the whole frame bit for bit** at three tile shapes with halos from `H`, and a halo one pixel short does not, on the CPU and on the device (`crates/ym-gpu/tests/gpu_raster_masks.rs`); the device's planes against the CPU's (below); the same mask at k = 4 and k = 1 agrees after the larger grid is reduced by the same box (largest difference 0.1, mean 0.005, starting bounds, set in N-10 and N-13; the shapes' numbers, hard edges included, are in section 4.2 and `docs/23` section 15); a photo cropped, straightened, turned and bent keeps a Brush dab on the subject (the dab's centroid moves with the dot's within one output pixel); **the blocked folds equal explicit windows** at negative and positive origins, frames cut inside the plane and radii from 0 to wider than the plane, and a window's bits do not depend on where the plane was cut (`crates/ym-color/tests/mask_raster.rs`); **a view sees the photo beyond its edge** (`crates/ym-core/tests/mask_raster.rs`, CPU and device: a 100 % view whose edge is inside a feathered dab against the export of that size, 1 code of the dither at most where the old frame, which ended at the view, differed by 74; views panned by a few pixels against each other) and **the bands of a view are the view in one piece** (0 codes; a retina-size view, 2880 x 1920, with a Brush row is made on the device in bands, 1 code from the CPU's). *Measured in N-13* (lavapipe): the device's planes within 6.1e-5 of the CPU's (4 codes of 65535; most cases 1.5e-5; the starting gate is 4e-4), C13 within 2e-6, the preview against the export 0.031 largest and 7.1e-4 mean (Brush, Grow 0.3, Feather 0.4, Refine edges 0.6, k = 4 against k = 1 of a 1200 x 800 photo), the dab within 0.13 px of its dot through nine geometries.
- **SAM 2.** Section 6.5.
- **Vision.** On a Mac (`mac-gpu.yml`-style job): the two requests give a mask for a synthetic subject and for a rendered person, twice with equal bytes; on an older system or a Mac that cannot run them the probe says no and the kinds are not offered.
