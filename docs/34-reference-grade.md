# 34 · Reference Grade

Reference Grade is a node that matches the grade of a **reference picture**. The person gives it a picture, a film still, a favourite photo or another photo of the same shoot, and the node **fits its own grade** so that this photo, seen through the person's real Output (their look and the cine rendering), looks like the reference. It is for the things a photographer does by eye with a picture beside the screen: "make this look like that". This file covers what it does, where it sits, the settings, the hue curves and the element grades, the scene (what is sky, foliage, water, ground, a building, skin), the fit, how references are read, the kernel change, `ym`, the panel, the tests and gates, the risks, the questions for the owner (answered), the mock-ups and the tasks.

**Status: specified (RG spec 2026-11-24; rewritten to the proof of concept's round 15 by RG-01, 2026-11-25); nothing is built.** The proof of concept (POC) was a Rust example on the branch `refgrade-poc` (CPU only, no UI). It ran fifteen rounds; its last, round 15, was accepted on a held-out set (§16). This file adopts its design. **The owner's decisions (RG-H1, 2026-11-25):**

1. **Grade per element, with a scene segmenter.** Six elements (sky, vegetation, water, ground, built, skin) each get an exposure offset, a hue rotation and a chroma scale; the subject gets an exposure offset only. The segmenter is EoMT small (COCO panoptic): MIT weights and code on a DINOv2 ViT-S backbone (Apache-2.0), 24.0 M parameters, about 48 MB at f16, run by our own Rust runtime and shipped as SAM 2.1 is (§4b.1). Skin comes from MediaPipe Selfie Multiclass, already shipped. The grading network is hand-written, ours and shipped as a text file; it sets targets and gates, and the solver sets the node's settings.
2. **A fit may take several seconds** on a Mac with all cores, with progress and Cancel. Speed work is a task (RG-11); the owner times it (RG-H3).
3. **A quality round on skin and tinted whites before release** (RG-13), judged on a fresh held-out set under the owner's three acceptance rules (§10).
4. **The 18 questions of §14: every recommended default is accepted as written.** Round 15 changed what a few of them mean; §14 says which.

Earlier decisions: 2026-11-23, fit at run time, reference sources are image files and other photos in the filmstrip, hue moves use **a new hue-curve field on the node** (so the colour kernel's node code changes and `diag-alf` must pass before a release). 2026-11-24, "Start the spec. But we want POC of our reference grade approach so I could see how it works before we do full implementation"; then, during the POC, "Grading should be per-element if needed and global" and "We still want a small hand-written model". The research is `research/reference-grade-2026-11-23.md`; **this file wins where they differ** (§15 lists where). The maths is ours: we copy no code and no formula from any tool (`docs/14`).

**In short.**

- A new node kind, `reference_grade`, in the graph of `docs/23`: a numbered node like a corrector, with masks, a key output, a thumbnail and a number. Add node › **Reference Grade**, "Matches the grade of a reference picture." (§9.1). It counts toward the 64 nodes. In a sentence it is "a Reference Grade node".
- **It is a fitted grade.** A run-time fit, in f64 on the CPU, chooses the node's own fields so that the photo, rendered through **the real chain from the node's input to the Output**, reaches the targets a small hand-written network sets from measurements of the photo and the reference (§4b, §5). The fields are a global grade (a luma curve, three channel curves, saturation, two hue curves) and **element grades** (§4a): up to 19 numbers more, each element through a soft mask. The fitted numbers are ordinary settings. The reference is not saved (§3.7).
- **One learned model, for masks only.** EoMT small finds the sky, foliage, water, ground and buildings of the photo and of the reference; MediaPipe finds skin. They make masks and measurements. Every grading decision is in the network file, which is text we wrote. The fit trains nothing.
- **A fit is a search of a few seconds, with progress and Cancel.** The POC measured about 30 s per fit on one Linux thread (§16.7); Mac figures wait for RG-H3.
- **Walls keep the grade from harm.** The objective carries walls against purple turns, skin drift, memory colours (sky, foliage), tinted whites, clipping and colours that turn into other colours (§5.4). Round 15 cut the share of fits with any measured harm from 34.8 % to 15.7 % on held-out photos without losing look (§16.8).
- **Global and element looks only.** A vignette, a graded sky gradient or a different grade in the left half of the frame are not copied (§13). The element grades are the only local part.
- **A new field: hue curves** (§4): `hue_vs_hue` and `hue_vs_sat`, periodic, on the LogC4 chroma-plane hue axis the parametric look uses. A kernel change (§7), made as small as the llvmpipe rule asks.
- **Strength** (0 to 100 %) scales the fitted values from the identity, instantly, with no re-fit (§3.2).
- **Skin protection** (on by default) switches on the two walls that keep skin from moving from the photo's own (§5.6).
- **App and `ym` agree to the bit** (§8): `ym render photo.ARW --reference ref.jpg` fits as the app does, and an export from the app pins the fitted numbers in `ym:Settings`.
- **A new node changes no pixel** until it is fitted or edited, and no existing document changes. `PIPELINE_VERSION` takes the next minor step (§3.8).

## 1. What it does

A photographer has a still from a film, or a photo they love, and wants their own photo to feel like it. Resolve has Shot Match, and Photoshop has Match Color (`research/reference-grade-2026-11-23.md`, *Precedents*). Here:

1. Add node › Reference Grade. It does nothing yet.
2. Choose a reference: an image file, or another photo in the filmstrip.
3. Press **Fit**. After a few seconds, with a progress bar and a Cancel button, the node's Light, Colour, Curve, Hue and Elements values are set. The photo now looks, through the person's own look, like the reference.
4. Lower **Strength** to take part of it. Adjust any tab by hand. Limit it with masks. Export.

### 1.1 What it is, and what it is not

- **It is a node that fits its own grade.** The numbers are the node's corrector fields, its hue curves and its element grades (§3). After a fit the person can read them, move them and undo them, as for any corrector.
- **It is fitted against the person's real Output.** The look (the Signature look, an imported ARRI look or the person's own LUT) and the cine rendering are part of the chain the fit runs, so the match is what the person sees. A grade matched to a reference on a model of the chain would be wrong on their Mac (§2.2).
- **It reads the scene.** It knows where the sky, the foliage, the water, the ground, the buildings and the skin are, in the photo and in the reference, and it grades each on its own as well as the whole (§4a, §4b). That is the part a global grade cannot do: a sky that goes teal while the skin stays natural.
- **It has one learned model and it is only for masks.** Nothing is trained for grading. The segmenter (EoMT small, §4b.1) and MediaPipe make masks and measurements; the rules that turn measurements into targets are a hand-written network of ours (§4b.5), and a solver finds the settings (§5). Nothing is downloaded at run time (`AGENTS.md` rule 7).
- **It is not a LUT copy.** The result is a small set of readable controls, not a table. It cannot reproduce what its parameters cannot express (§12).
- **It is not a transfer of pixels.** It never moves a pixel of the reference into the photo. It compares statistics of two pictures that may show different things (§5.1), which is why it copies global and element looks only.
- **It is not Auto.** Auto resolves the Input's and Main's numbers from one picture and a rule (`docs/19`, `docs/22`); its providers receive one picture and cannot carry a reference (`docs/22` §1 and the research). This node has no Auto and no provider.

### 1.2 How it differs from what exists, and what it reuses

| | What it does | Reference Grade |

| **Colour Uniform** (`docs/32`) | Pulls colours toward one picked colour; has the corrector's tabs after the pull | **Reuses its way of being a corrector-like node with extra fields**, its tabs (Light, Colour and Curve are the same components), its kernel precedent (§7) and its first-release rules. The maths is different: no target, no pull |

The look's curves are not changed and a look still bakes to a cube |
| **The colour warp** (C9b, `docs/02`, `warp.rs`) | Up to 16 Gaussian pins in the chroma plane; radial, not a function of hue angle alone | **Not used by the fit.** The owner chose true hue curves (2026-11-23). The warp stays a tab of the node, as on a corrector |

### 1.3 Photo-to-photo matching

A reference can be another photo in the filmstrip, rendered through that photo's own current settings (§6.4). Matching one photo of a series to another is a strong use. The reference is a **snapshot** of that photo's picture at the moment it was chosen, not a link: if the other photo is graded again later, this node keeps matching the picture it was given (§6.4).

### 2.2 The chain the fit runs, and its dependence on the look

The fit measures the photo **after the real chain**, not the node's own output. The chain, from the node's input, is:

```
node input  →  [the node, with the candidate numbers]  →  every per-pixel step after it  →  the Output: C10 look, C12 cine rendering, C13 encode
```

- **From the node's input.** The part of the plan before the node (the Input, Main, earlier nodes) is rendered **once** per fit, at the analysis size (§5.1), on the CPU reference path as Auto's analysis picture is. Its result is the fixed input of the search. So the fit's input bits are the same in the app and in `ym`.
- **Through the nodes after it and the Output.** Every per-pixel colour step after the node runs for each candidate: correctors, Colour Uniform nodes, mixers, then C10, C12 and C13. An ARRI LogC3-to-Rec.709 look replaces C12 (`docs/02`); the fit follows because it runs the person's actual Output.
- **Spatial nodes after the node are not run** (Film, Blur, Face Refinement): they make their picture before the colour pass and cost far too much to run per candidate. A graph with one after the node shows a note on the card (§9.3). This is a decision of ours, flagged in §14 (question 7).
- **On the CPU reference path, in f64.** The GPU render that follows is held to the parity bounds as for every node (`docs/17` D8).
- **The fit measures the Output as sRGB display codes** (§5.1), whatever space the viewport shows, and the element masks are part of each candidate (§4a.3).

**The fit depends on the Output look.** A grade matched under one look is wrong under another. So **changing the Output's look, its intensity, or anything that replaces C12 makes the fit stale** (§14, question 5):

- The page keeps, for the session only, a fingerprint of the Output's look settings at the moment of the fit. When it differs, the card's status line says **"The look changed since the fit. Fit again to match it with the new look."** and the Fit button reads "Fit again" with an accent. **Nothing is re-fitted by itself** and no number moves: a re-fit takes several seconds and would replace the person's hand edits (§3.3).
- Other changes (an earlier node's exposure, a node after) do not mark the fit stale. The person can press Fit again at any time.
- After the app restarts there is no fingerprint (nothing about the fit is stored, §3.7), so there is no hint: the values are just settings.

### 2.3 The order inside the node

```
w   = hue( C11( C9b( C9( trims(v_in) ) ) ) )                       the corrector's grade, then the hue curves
w'  = element grades of w, in the order sky, vegetation, water, ground, built, skin, subject
out = v_in + m · ( w' − v_in )                                      m: the node's own masks
```

with each element grade, in that order, `w ← w + a_e · (local_e(w) − w)` where `a_e` is the element's soft mask at the pixel (§4a.3) and `local_e` the grade of §4a.2.

- The corrector's steps keep their order whole (`docs/23` §1.3, `ym_color::display::node`). The hue curves are **step 6**, after C11, as the Colour Uniform pull is last in `node_apply` and as the parametric look puts its colour curves after its tone (`docs/08`). Reasons: the fit's tone and channel curves have then done their work and the hue curves refine the colour that is left; and a step that runs last is the shape the kernel's precedent proved safe (§7.2).
- The **element grades are step 7, last, in LogC4**, after the hue step and before the Output (C10 look, C12, C13). This is the order the POC ran (§16): the global grade first, the elements on its result. The seven masks `a_e` do not depend on the node's grade (§4a.3), so a grade never moves its own mask.
- A fixed order keeps the fit's search stages simple (§5.5).

### 3.2 Strength

`strength` scales **what the fit writes**, from the identity, at render time and with no re-fit. For each **fitted field** (`grade.curve.luma`, `.red`, `.green`, `.blue`; `grade.saturation`; `hue_curves.hue_vs_hue`; `hue_curves.hue_vs_sat`; every number of `elements`) and `k = strength / 100`, the render uses the **effective** values:

| Field | Effective value |
|---|---|
| a curve's knot `(x, y)` | `(x, x + k (y − x))` |
| `saturation`, manual `s` | `k s` (saturation is linear in `s`: `λ = 1 + s/100`, `docs/02` C9) |
| `hue_vs_hue` `δ` (degrees) | `k δ` |
| `hue_vs_sat` `σ` (a chroma multiplier) | `1 + k (σ − 1)` |
| an element's `exposure_ev` `e` and `hue_deg` `h` | `k e` and `k h` |
| an element's `chroma` `c` (a multiplier) | `c^k` (a ratio scales as a power, so half of ×1.6 is ×1.26, and Strength 0 is ×1) |

- **Monotone stays monotone.** A knotwise mix of two increasing curves is increasing, so a curve at any Strength is a valid curve.
- **Strength 0 is the identity**, bit for bit, when the node's other fields are at their defaults. **Strength 100 is the stored values.**
- The mix is made in f64 in one function, `ReferenceGradeNode::effective`, which the CPU reference, the cache key and the device's host packing all call. **So the kernel never sees Strength** and needs nothing for it. An element whose effective grade is the identity makes no mask (§4a.3).
- Strength does **not** scale `trim` or the grade fields the fit does not write (Contrast, Brightness, the zones, Colour balance, the warp). They are the person's own numbers (§3.3).
- A hand edit of a fitted field is scaled by Strength like the fit's own value. The tabs show the **stored** numbers; at a Strength below 100 the picture shows less than the numbers say. The Reference card says so in a line under the slider (§9.3).
- The POC scaled its search parameters (a slope in log space, a shift in stops) and not the stored knots; the two agree at Strength 100, where every gate of §10 is measured. Strength is a product control the POC did not exercise (§16.9).

### 3.3 What happens when the person edits a fitted value

**The rule: a fitted value is an ordinary setting. An edit changes that stored value and nothing else.** Strength still scales it. A fit never reads the old numbers: **Fit starts again from the identity** for the fitted fields, so its result depends only on the photo, the reference, the node's other fields and the chain, never on what was there.

- **Fit (and Fit again) overwrites the fitted fields** (§3.2's list) in one patch, one undo step. It leaves everything else alone: `trim`, the other grade fields, the masks, the name, `strength` and `skin_protection`.
- **An edit does not re-fit and does not mark the look stale.** The page keeps a session flag "edited since the fit"; the status line then says "Edited by hand since the fit. Fit again to start over." The flag is cleared by the next fit and is not stored.
- **Undo** restores the earlier numbers, edit or fit.
- **The other fields act as set.** The fit runs the node with its non-fitted fields as they are, so they are part of what the fit sees (a Contrast of +10 the person set earlier is in the chain the fit matches through). A later change of those fields is an ordinary edit.

This is the simplest rule that is consistent: one set of numbers, one dial over the fit's part, no hidden second copy of the "fitted" numbers. It is flagged as question 3 in §14.

### 3.4 Validation and clamping

- Values out of range are **clamped and reported** by path (`4.strength`, `4.hue_curves.hue_vs_sat[1].value`), as `docs/23` §3.5; a value that is **not finite** is `SettingsError::NotFinite` and nothing is applied. A `hue_curves` that is not an object, or a point with a field of another type, is `SettingsError::Parse`.
- Unknown fields are refused: **"A Reference Grade node has no field called “x”."** `trim`, `grade` and the rules of `docs/23` §3.5 hold: ports exist on their kind, an input port has one link, no cycles, unique ids, **at most 64 numbered nodes** counting this one.
- **Hue curve points** (§4.1): at most 16 per curve; `hue_deg` any finite number, read modulo 360 into [0, 360) (**not reported**); two points at the same hue after the reduction are refused ("Two points of a hue curve are at the same hue."); `hue_vs_hue` `value` ±180 and `hue_vs_sat` `value` 0 to 8 (clamped, reported, the parametric look's limits). The list is sorted by hue on read.
- **Element grades** (§4a.1): `elements` must be an object whose members are `sky`, `vegetation`, `water`, `ground`, `built`, `skin` and `subject`; another name is refused ("A Reference Grade node has no element called “x”."). A member or a field left out of a document reads as its default. The subject has `exposure_ev` only; a `hue_deg` or `chroma` on it is an unknown field of that member. `exposure_ev` is held to ±1, `hue_deg` to ± the element's cap (40, 35, 35, 30, 25, 15) and `chroma` to 0.5 to 1.6: clamped and reported by path (`4.elements.sky.hue_deg`); a value that is not finite is `SettingsError::NotFinite`.
- `strength` 0 to 100 is clamped and reported. `skin_protection` must be a bool (a parse error otherwise).

### 3.5 Patches, Reset, Copy and Paste, undo

- **Patches** (`docs/23` §3.7). A node is added by giving its `kind` in `nodes` and removed with `remove`; its links go with it. `SettingsField` gains `HueCurves`, `Elements`, `Strength` and `SkinProtection` (the nodes' group; a change runs the colour pass again and nothing else). The node takes `Trim` and the grade fields as Colour Uniform does (`of_graded_field`, `apply_graded`, `graded_same`, `docs/32` §12a.3): **one place** turns a trim or a grade field into a patch. A patch carries `hue_curves` partially: a curve given replaces that curve, a curve left out stays; it carries `elements` the same way, member by member.
- **The fit is one patch**: `grade.curve`, `grade.saturation`, both hue curves and all seven element members, in one undo step. A slider drag folds into one undo step as every slider.
- **Reset** (`SettingsPatch::reset_node_in`): the node's own fields (`trim`, `grade`, `hue_curves`, `elements`) to their defaults, `strength` to 100 and `skin_protection` to true; name, bypass, masks and links stay. Each card keeps its own Reset (§9).
- **Copy settings and Paste** carry the node like any node. Undo diffs per node.
- **Resolved settings, XMP and DNG.** The node has no Auto, so it is in `ResolvedSettings.settings` as it is, and `ResolvedSettings.values.nodes` has an entry with its trim and grade (as for Colour Uniform, `resolvedGraded`). `ym:Settings` holds the node with its fitted numbers; the DNG bakes it into the pixels like every node (`docs/09`).
- **The JSON Schema** (`crates/ym-core/schema/settings.schema.json`) gains `reference_grade_node` (with `trim` and `grade` as `$ref`s to the corrector's definitions, and `hue_curves`, `hue_point`, `elements`, `element_grade`) and the kind in the `kind` enum. The wire types gain `NodeKind::ReferenceGrade` and `NodeItem::ReferenceGradeNode`; `bindings.ts` is regenerated and the field-name test compares them.

### 3.6 Presets and albums

- **Presets** (`docs/30`). The node is in the **`nodes` group**, which holds every node other than the Input, Main and the Output; no new group. A preset saved from a photo that has the node holds the fitted numbers, `strength`, `skin_protection` and the masks. **Applying it to another photo applies the same numbers; it does not re-fit.** A preset is therefore a recipe of a fixed grade, as a Colour range's centre and a warp's pins are fixed. The person can open the node and Fit again with a reference. The preset table text of `docs/30` §2 gains "Reference Grade nodes" in the list of kinds, and the test that a field in no group fails (`docs/30` §14) covers the new fields. Nothing in the node is a stroke or a click, so `docs/30` §3 (what a preset never holds) is unchanged.
- **Albums** (`docs/31`). An album holds the photo's whole graph as stored, so the node travels in `settings` with no change to the file (version 1 stays). An older build refuses it with "This album was made by a newer version of Yellowmoon. Update to open it." `written_by.pipeline` records the version (§3.8).
- **Older documents** read as they did. A document that holds the node is refused by an older Yellowmoon with the plain unknown-kind sentence (`docs/23` §3.5).

### 3.7 What is saved, and what is never saved

By rule 4 nothing is kept per photo. For this node:

| Saved, as ordinary settings | Never saved |
|---|---|
| the fitted numbers (curves, saturation, hue curves, element grades), `strength`, `skin_protection`, the masks | **the reference picture**, in any form; its pictures; **any statistic computed from it**; **the scene of the photo or of the reference** (label maps, skin plane, soft masks, measures); the fit's objective, seed or status; the Output-look fingerprint of §2.2; the "edited since the fit" flag |
| | **a label of the reference** (file name, path, photo name) |

**Recommended: no label is stored** (§14, question 4). Reasons: (1) `ym:Settings` goes out inside every exported file, and a file name is information about the person's library, and for a film still a hint of what was copied; (2) a path says nothing on another Mac and is private; (3) a label has no effect on any pixel, and a setting that does nothing is noise in documents, presets and albums; (4) it would go stale at the first hand edit. The card shows the reference's name **on screen, for the session only**: the file's name, or "Photo 3 of 7" for a strip photo.

### 3.8 `PIPELINE_VERSION`

No existing graph changes by a bit (a node that is not there runs nothing; §7.3 keeps the kernel's text for every other node identical), so **no golden entry moves**. An older build refuses a graph with a kind it does not know, so `PIPELINE_VERSION` takes **the next minor step** with the node, the rule `docs/01` *pipeline_version* and `docs/23` set when a kind lands. Today's is 0.16.0, so **0.17.0**, set by RG-02 and written to `ym:PipelineVersion`, `ym --version`, `ym doctor`, `ym info` and an album's `written_by` (the snapshots and the tests that assert the number follow). If another change takes 0.17.0 first, the next free minor number is used; the rule does not depend on the value. The golden set gains **one** entry (§10).

## 4. The hue curves

### 4.1 The field

`hue_curves` has two curves, with the parametric look's meaning (`docs/08`, `crates/ym-color/src/look.rs`):

- **`hue_vs_hue`**: a point `{ hue_deg, value }`; `value` is **degrees** (±180) the hue turns; positive turns red towards yellow.
- **`hue_vs_sat`**: a point `{ hue_deg, value }`; `value` multiplies the chroma (0 to 8; 1 is unchanged, 0 is grey).

An empty list is the identity. **One point makes a constant.** At most 16 points. The curve between points is a **monotone cubic (Fritsch–Carlson) spline, periodic with period 360°**: the segment after the last point runs on to the first plus 360°, and every point has two neighbours, so the curve is C1 across 0 and 360 and never overshoots the values given. This is `ym_color::curve`'s `fritsch_carlson_tangents_periodic` and the look's `Spline::new_periodic`, reused.

**The axis** is the LogC4 chroma plane the parametric look, the colour warp, the Colour range and Colour Uniform already use (`docs/32` §3.1):

```
p = v · e1,   q = v · e2         e1 = (1, −½, −½)/√1.5     e2 = (0, 1, −1)/√2     (grade::{E1, E2})
r = hypot(p, q)                  chroma
h = atan2(q, p)                  hue: 0 red, 60 yellow, 120 green, 180 cyan, 240 blue, 300 magenta
m = (v_R + v_G + v_B)/3          the tone, along (1,1,1)
```

### 4.2 The step

```
δ(h)  = hue_vs_hue(h)  in radians          σ(h) = hue_vs_sat(h)
(p', q') = σ(h) · R(δ(h)) · (p, q)         R(δ) the rotation by δ
v' = v + (p' − p) e1 + (q' − q) e2
v'' = v' + (Y(v) − Y(v')) · (1, 1, 1)      Y(x) = 0.2126 x_R + 0.7152 x_G + 0.0722 x_B, the Rec.709 weights on the LogC4 codes
```

- **The luma is restored (the owner, 2026-11-25).** The move scales and rotates `(p, q)`, which are orthogonal to `(1,1,1)`, so the mean `m` of `v'` is the mean of `v` before the restoring step; the step then **restores the Rec.709 luma of the input**, as the parametric look does (`ParametricLook::evaluate` in `crates/ym-color/src/look.rs`, on the LogC4 codes, no decode): `fix = Y709(v) − Y709(v')` with `Y709(x) = 0.2126 x_R + 0.7152 x_G + 0.0722 x_B`, and `v'' = v' + fix · (1, 1, 1)`. It costs three multiply-adds and an add per pixel (two dot products and a shift of the three codes); the kernel needs no decode and no encode (RG-08). So the step moves hue and chroma only and **never changes luma**, to rounding: a test holds it to 1e-12 in f64. The mean `m` then moves by the shift, so a hue move may brighten or darken the channel mean a little; luma is what the eye and the fit's tone terms read. This was question 13 of §14 (the draft kept the mean on a reason, a decode and an encode per pixel, that was wrong); the owner answered "restore luma" and the POC's measured numbers (round 15) are for this step (§15, decision 19).
- **No neutral guard is needed.** The step scales and rotates the vector `(p, q)`, so as `r → 0` the move tends to 0: it is continuous at grey and exact at `r = 0`. A pixel with `r = 0` is returned as it is.
- **The identity is exact.** A node with both curves empty (or Strength 0) runs no hue step (`N_HUE` is not set; §7).
- **The curves are evaluated from baked tables**, one of 1024 f64 samples around the circle for each curve, built from the spline in f64 by one function, with linear interpolation between samples and wrap-around. **The table is the definition**: the CPU reference reads the same table in f64 and the device reads it as f32, so the baking error is not a parity term. The error of the table against the exact spline is measured in RG-02 and stated in `docs/STATUS.md` (§10).

### 4.3 Where else

Only the Reference Grade node has `hue_curves` and `elements` in this version. Correctors and Colour Uniform do not; giving them the field is a later idea (§13), the same work in another kind.

## 4a. The element grades

The element grades are the node's local part: a small grade for each of six kinds of thing in the picture and one for the subject, each acting only where its **soft mask** says that thing is. The POC's round 5 added them to the global grade, and every later round kept them (§16).

### 4a.1 The fields

`elements` has seven members. The first six are the **elements**; the seventh is the **subject**.

| Member | What it is (§4b.2, §4b.3) | `exposure_ev` | `hue_deg` | `chroma` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sky` | sky | −1 to 1, 0 | ±40, 0 | 0.5 to 1.6, 1 |
| `vegetation` | trees, grass, flowers, plants | −1 to 1, 0 | ±35, 0 | 0.5 to 1.6, 1 |
| `water` | sea, river, other water | −1 to 1, 0 | ±35, 0 | 0.5 to 1.6, 1 |
| `ground` | sand, earth, road, rock, mountain, snow | −1 to 1, 0 | ±30, 0 | 0.5 to 1.6, 1 |
| `built` | buildings, walls, roofs; and the surfaces of interiors | −1 to 1, 0 | ±25, 0 | 0.5 to 1.6, 1 |
| `skin` | skin of people (MediaPipe) | −1 to 1, 0 | ±15, 0 | 0.5 to 1.6, 1 |
| `subject` | the person, or the main object | −1 to 1, 0 | – | – |

- **`exposure_ev`** is an exposure offset in stops, in the scene's log space: it adds `exposure_ev · 0.0648` to the three LogC4 codes (0.0648 is the POC's code step of one stop, `V5_STOP_CODE`).
- **`hue_deg`** turns the hue by that many degrees in the chroma plane (positive turns red towards yellow, as `hue_vs_hue`). The range of each element is its **cap**, the largest turn the network lets the fit make for it (§4b.5): a sky may turn 40°, skin 15°.
- **`chroma`** multiplies the chroma, as a ratio (1 is unchanged), in the same plane.
- **The ranges are the fit's own bounds.** The panel's sliders and the validation use the same numbers (§3.4); a hand edit cannot go further than a fit can.
- Defaults are the identity. A member at the identity runs no step and makes no mask.

### 4a.2 The step

For one element, on the pixel's LogC4 triple `v` (the basis of §4.1):

```
m = (v_R + v_G + v_B) / 3        p = v · e1        q = v · e2
(p', q') = chroma · R(hue_deg) · (p, q)
local(v) = (m + exposure_ev · 0.0648) · (1, 1, 1) + p' e1 + q' e2
```

The subject's step is the exposure term alone. The mean `m` moves only by the exposure offset, so a hue or chroma change keeps the tone. As with the hue curves, **no luma restoration** is made here, and the POC did none either (§16.9).

### 4a.3 The soft masks

Each member has a **soft mask** `a_e` per pixel, 0 to 1, made like this:

1. **A binary mask** from the scene: the pixels the segmenter calls that element (§4b.2), skin from MediaPipe (§4b.3), the subject by the rule of §4b.3. At each size the label map of the segmenter's own grid is carried by nearest neighbour (centre-mapped: source index `⌊(x + ½)/W · S⌋`).
2. **A guided filter** (radius `r = max(1, round(0.02 · short side))` px, ε = 1e-3), with the picture's **L\*/100 as the guide**: box means with the window cut at the frame (a mean over the pixels that exist), `mean_I`, `mean_p`, `corr_I`, `corr_Ip`; `var = corr_I − mean_I²`, `cov = corr_Ip − mean_I mean_p`; `a = cov / (var + ε)`, `b = mean_p − a · mean_I`; `q = mean(a) · I + mean(b)`; then `a_e = clamp(q, 0, 1)`. A member whose binary mask is empty has `a_e = 0`. The arithmetic is f32 with f64 box sums, in one fixed order (step 6 says which).
3. **The guide.** The guide is the L\* (CIELAB, D65, of the sRGB display codes) of **the Input's picture as `docs/23` §4.7 renders it**, the same picture the segmenter reads. This is one place where the product departs from the POC, which took the guide from the photo's default render through the person's look (§15, decision 15). **As built** (`ColorChain::scene_guide_pixel`): C1 to C8 of the Input, the cine rendering to **sRGB whatever the output target** (so an export to Display P3 and one to sRGB have the same masks), no node and no look the person chose, rounded to 8-bit codes with no dither, then `L*/100` from the linear Rec.709 luminance of those codes. The reason is the rule of `docs/23` §4.7: grading a node never moves its own mask.
4. **Where and when.** Masks are made **per photo, in the background, at the size they are needed**: the analysis size for a fit, the viewport's size for the viewer and **the full size at render and export** (the radius is a share of the short side, so the same filter at every size). Like the Subject and People masks they follow the machinery of `docs/23` §4.5: **what is held** is the photo's *scene* (the seven elements as bits at the Input picture's grid, 0.7 MB), made once per photo by a worker in the background (the viewer never waits; an export makes its own) and kept in memory for the session only, per photo, keyed by the picture's key, the weights and the rules' version, and dropped with the photo (`docs/23` §3.11): never saved, never written to `ym:Settings`. **The soft masks are made from it at each render's own size** (a fit's analysis picture, the viewer's window, an export's tile) and not kept (§15, decision 39).
5. **Only what is used.** A mask is made only for an element whose effective grade (§3.2) is not the identity, and for a fit, for every element of at least 2 % of the picture. A photo with no element grade at the identity pays nothing.
6. **Tiles.** An export cuts the planes per tile with the filter's halo (`2r + 1` px) as raster masks do (`docs/algorithms/06` §5.2). Bits must not depend on the tile size (§10, `any_graph`). **The arithmetic RG-04 chose is the blocked fold of `docs/algorithms/06` §5.2 that every raster mask already uses** (`ym_color::mask_raster::box_mean`): a window's sum is the *suffix* scan of one block joined to the *prefix* scan of the next, with blocks of `2 r + 1` samples that start at **absolute** multiples of `2 r + 1`, summed in `f64` in a fixed order (prefixes upwards, suffixes downwards) and divided by the number of samples the window kept inside the frame. The scans restart at every block boundary, so a window's value is made from the samples of the window alone, in an order that depends on their absolute positions alone: it does not depend on how the plane was cut. (Integer or prefix-sum arithmetic over the whole row would not do: a prefix sum from the tile's own edge rounds differently from one from the frame's.) The filter nests two windows (`mean(a)` of an `a` that is itself a window mean), so a tile is made over itself grown by `2 r + 1` and cut to the frame (`element_halo`), and **a tile equals the whole frame bit for bit at any tile size**; the planes are not made whole. The `f32` steps between the box means (`var`, `cov`, `a`, `b`, `q`) are per pixel, with no fused multiply-add. The result is stored as a u16 code like every raster plane (`quantise`), so the device (RG-08) reads the same bytes. The export's memory estimate grows by the halo (`2 r + 1` is 161 px at 24 MP). The radius takes the aspect of the **scene's grid** (the photo's, up to the rounding of its sides) so that the scheduler, which knows `u_px` alone, and every tile agree on it (§15, decision 38).
7. **Where the model is missing** (a damaged install): there are no masks, the element step is skipped, the viewer says so and an export is refused (§8).

### 4a.4 What an element grade is not

- It is **not a mask row**. The node's `masks` (`docs/23` §3.3) limit the whole node as for any node: `out = v_in + m (w' − v_in)` (§2.3). The element masks are inside.
- It is not applied where `a_e = 0`, and a pixel in two elements (skin lies inside the person; `built` joins two label groups) takes the steps in the order of §2.3, each by its own mask.
- It does not move geometry and does not change what the segmenter sees.

## 4b. The scene

"The scene" is what the fit knows about a picture beyond its pixels: which pixels are sky, foliage, water, ground, built surfaces, skin and subject, and the measurements made on each. It is made the same way for the photo and for the reference.

### 4b.1 The segmenter

- **Model.** **EoMT small** (Encoder-only Mask Transformer, CVPR 2025), the COCO panoptic checkpoint `tue-mps/coco_panoptic_eomt_small_640_2x`: a plain DINOv2 ViT-S with 4 register tokens and **patch 16** (hidden size 384, 12 blocks, 6 heads, MLP ratio 4, LayerScale; settled by RG-03, see Input), 200 learned queries put in front of the stream before block 9 (the last 3 blocks), two upscaling blocks, a class head of 133 COCO panoptic classes plus "none" and a mask head. **24.0 M parameters** (23,976,079), 96 MB at f32 and **about 48 MB at f16**. Panoptic quality 46.7 on COCO (the authors' model zoo).
- **Licences** (`docs/14`). The EoMT code and weights are **MIT** (Mobile Perception Systems Lab at TU/e; the repository's `LICENSE` and the model card's `license: mit`). The backbone is DINOv2, **Apache-2.0**. The training set is COCO panoptic 2017: its annotations are CC BY 4.0 and its images are under Flickr's terms, noted as for SAM's SA-1B. The DINOv3 variants (`…_dinov3`) carry the DINOv3 licence and are **not used**. Mask2Former, SegFormer and the ADE20K-trained options were rejected for non-commercial terms or cost (`research/` notes of the POC).
- **Runtime.** Our own pure-Rust CPU code, `ym_ml::eomt`, f32, like `sam2` and `depth` (**built by RG-03**). It **reuses the depth model's ViT-S block** (`crates/ym-ml/src/depth/encoder.rs`: LayerNorm, attention, MLP, LayerScale; `pub(crate)` with a constructor from parts, loaded here from this model's names with `q`, `k` and `v` stacked), its transposed convolution and `sam2`'s packed matrix product and resize taps, and adds the patch embedding with its position table, the register tokens, the query tokens in front of the stream from block 9, the depthwise 3 × 3 convolution and the layer norm over channels of the two upscaling blocks, the class and mask heads and the panoptic post-processing. **The attention is never masked:** the model builds a masked attention for the query blocks only when `attn_mask_probs > 0`, and this checkpoint's three values are exactly 0 (the mask was annealed away in training), so inference attends to everything and makes no intermediate prediction; the runtime reads the three values from the file and refuses one that is not 0. No deformable attention and no custom operator.
- **Input.** The Input's picture as `docs/23` §4.7 renders it (Neutral look, no node, no output sharpening), at 1024 px on the long edge as SAM reads it, as 8-bit RGB, **fitted to the model's 640 × 640 as the checkpoint's processor does** (`preprocessor_config.json`, read by RG-03): the size rule of `transformers`' `get_size_with_aspect_ratio(size 640, max 640)` (the long side becomes 640 and the short side `round(640 · short / long)`, the same `f64` operations in the same order; a square picture is 640 × 640), an antialiased bilinear resize in PyTorch's 8-bit integer arithmetic (weights of 22 fractional bits, each of the two passes rounded to 8 bits; equal to PyTorch's on all but 0 to 0.2 % of the pixels, which differ by one level), the result **padded at the bottom and the right with black (0 in 8-bit values)** to 640 × 640, then rescaled by 1/255 and normalised with ImageNet's mean and standard deviation (the pad becomes the normalised 0 of 8 bits, not 0). **The patch size is 16, settled by RG-03 from the checkpoint:** the patch convolution is `[384, 3, 16, 16]` and the position table `embeddings.position_embeddings.weight` has 1600 rows, a 40 × 40 grid for 640 px (the training YAML's `vit_small_patch14_reg4_dinov2` is not what was exported; patch 14 would make a 45 × 45 grid that the table does not fit). The stream is 1605 tokens (class, 4 registers, 1600 patches; the class and register tokens get no position) and 1805 from block 9. The position table is never resized, because the input is always 640 × 640. `export.py` and the runtime refuse any other patch size.
- **Output.** One class label per pixel of a label map at the model's grid, the fitted picture of the Input paragraph (at most 640 × 640; for a 3:2 picture 640 × 427), carried to any size by nearest neighbour (§4a.3; `LabelMap::resized_nearest`); a pixel no segment claims is *unlabelled* (class 133). **It is the panoptic post-processing the POC used, not a per-class semantic merge:** the 160 × 160 mask logits are resized bilinearly to the fitted grid; a query is *kept* when its best class is not "none" and its softmax probability is above 0.8; each pixel goes to the kept query with the largest probability × `sigmoid(mask)`; a query is a *segment* when its mask (`sigmoid ≥ 0.5`) is not empty, they share a pixel, and the pixels it owns are more than 0.8 of its mask's area; the pixels it owns inside its mask are the segment (the stuff classes 80 to 132 that several queries claim are one segment in the reference, which changes no class). `docs/34`'s first draft also named a semantic merge (`softmax(class)[:, :−1] · sigmoid(mask)` summed per class); the POC used the panoptic map, RG-03 builds that, and the semantic merge is not built (it was not needed and costs 133 planes). The class table of §4b.2 is `ym_ml::eomt::{CLASS_NAMES, element_of}` and is tested against the checkpoint's `id2label` (the header of the weights file and the fixture both carry it).
- **Determinism** as `sam2` and `depth`: f32 in a fixed order, no fused multiply-add, `libm` for `exp`, `erf` and `tanh`, every output row computed by one task whatever the pool, so the same picture gives the same labels on every run, on any number of threads and, as far as IEEE 754 goes, on every platform. The app and `ym` give the same masks.
- **Weights and shipping, as SAM 2.1.** `data/models/eomt/` holds `eomt_coco_panoptic_small.f16.safetensors` (about 48 MB; the header carries `format` `yellowmoon-eomt`, `version`, the source URL and SHA-256, the commit of the upstream code and the configuration), `SHA256SUMS`, `MODEL_CARD.md` (`data/models/README.md`) and a `.gitignore` for the weights. They are converted once from the authors' checkpoint (file name and SHA-256 pinned in the script) by `training/eomt/export.py`, which runs in CI (`.github/workflows/models.yml`, job `eomt`; Python is for converting and fixtures only), published as an asset of the Yellowmoon prerelease `models-eomt-small-1` (never "latest", which the updater reads; published 2026-11-25), **fetched by `tools/fetch/eomt.sh` and pinned by SHA-256**, and bundled in the app and next to `ym`. They are **not committed** (`docs/12`). The app is offline and needs no first-run download (`AGENTS.md` rule 7). `NOTICE` carries the MIT and Apache-2.0 notices (item 15; written in RG-01, as the Depth model's item 13 was written by its spec task). The file is 47,978,758 bytes, SHA-256 `1ff3664ebcff247f207031d2bec2496709fca2e4fcd398682f1efcc5f32d0cae`, **byte-identical on every run** (the pin is in `data/models/eomt/SHA256SUMS` and `tools/fetch/eomt.sh` already; the job refuses to publish a file with another hash), and the header carries `format` `yellowmoon-eomt`, `version` 1, the source, the revision, the configuration, the class names and the pre- and post-processing.
- **Parity.** Fixtures from the authors' code (Hugging Face's port of it, `transformers` 5.24.1; `tools/eomt/parity_fixture.py`, CPU, f32, one thread, the f16 weights widened; test tooling only, never run by the app or `ym`), **small and committed** (`crates/ym-ml/tests/data/eomt/`, 260 KB: three pictures made in the script, the original's label maps, and the class logits, mask logits and kept queries of 16 queries per picture), so **no fixture asset is published**; `YM_EOMT_EXTRA_FIXTURE` adds the folder of a `--photos` run for a local check on real photographs. The gates and what was measured are in §10. Without the weights the tests print `SKIPPED` and pass; `YM_REQUIRE_EOMT=1` makes that a failure. The tests of the class table, the post-processing and the determinism (1, 2 and 8 threads, a second run) run without the weights on seeded synthetic ones (`ym_ml::eomt::synthetic_weights`).
- **Cost.** **About 68 GMAC** per picture (`ym_ml::eomt::multiply_adds`), the same for every picture: the input is always 640 × 640 (1605 tokens, 1805 from block 9). **Measured on the Linux test machine (4 logical cores, our CPU code, f32), RG-03:** about **6 s on 1 thread, 3.1 s on 2 and 1.7 s on 4** for the network, under 20 ms for the input and the post-processing (`cargo run --release -p ym-ml --example eomt_time`); the earlier estimate was 1.5 to 2.2 s on 4 cores. **A Linux figure only**: no Apple-silicon timing exists (RG-H3). It runs once per photo, in the background, and once for each reference.

### 4b.3 Skin and the subject

- **Skin** is MediaPipe Selfie Multiclass (`ym_ml::mediapipe`, shipped, deterministic; never Apple Vision, `docs/23` §4.5) classes `BodySkin` and `FaceSkin`, **intersected with `person`**. It runs on each connected component of `person` that covers at least 0.5 % of the picture, on a square crop of the component's box times 1.15; each pixel takes the class of the highest score. It runs only when `person` covers at least 3 % of the picture; otherwise there is no skin. **If the MediaPipe weights are missing** (a damaged install) there is no skin and the status line says so (§9.3); the POC had a colour-rule fallback and the product does not.
- **The subject** is `person` when it covers at least 3 % of the picture; otherwise the `other_thing` pixels inside the central 60 % of the frame (x and y from 0.2 to 0.8) when they cover at least 1 %; otherwise there is no subject.
- **Apple Vision's Subject mask is never used for the subject element** (the owner, 2026-11-25): it is not the same on every Mac, it is absent off macOS, and the fit and the render must see the same subject. The subject is the rule above, made through the same raster-mask machinery as the other elements (§4a.3). A SAM 2.1 refinement of its edge (a box prompt from the segmenter's subject) is considered only in RG-13, and only if the subject's edges show up as a problem (§15, decision 36).
- **The two pictures**: the segmenter and MediaPipe run on the photo (§4b.1) and on the reference's analysis picture (§6.5), each once. Every number below is a measure of one picture with its scene.

### 4b.4 The measures (`X.*`)

Measured in **CIELAB with the D65 white** on the picture's sRGB display codes, clamped to 0 to 1, as the fit's own pictures are (§5.1). The photo `P` is measured on the **8192 sample pixels** of §5.1 rendered with the node at its identity (so after the real chain, not before it); the reference `R` on **all pixels** of its analysis picture. An element that is absent has share 0 and every other value 0. The elements below are the six of §4a.1 (`built` = architecture and interior; `skin` the skin plane).

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| `p2`, `p50`, `p98` | L\* at 2, 50 and 98 % of the pixels' ranks, linear between ranks (position `q · (n − 1)`) |
| `iqr_rel` | `(p75 − p25) / max(p98 − p2, 1)` |
| `logsat` | `ln(max(median C\*, 1))` of the pixels of vegetation, water, ground, built, `other_thing` and `other_stuff` when they are at least 1 % of the picture; otherwise of all pixels that are neither sky nor person (unlabelled pixels count) |
| `tint_{s,m,h}_{a,b}` | the neutral cast: the picture's pixels in three L\* terciles by rank (shadows, mids, highlights); in each, among the pixels of the neutral-capable groups (`unlabelled`, `ground`, `architecture`, `interior`, `other_thing`, `other_stuff`, and `person` minus skin) those with `C* ≤ max(8, the C\* at the 15 % rank of that group's pixels)`; their mean `a*` and `b*` (0 if none) |
| `cast_a`, `cast_b`, `conc` | the frame's cast: mean `a*`, `b*` over the pixels that are not skin and have L\* 15 to 80 (when those are at least 10 % of the picture; else all non-skin pixels; else all); `conc` is the length of that mean over `max(mean C\*, 1)`: 0 when colours cancel, 1 when every pixel has one hue |
| `magenta_share` | the share of the picture with `C* > 8` and **Oklab** hue 300° to 355° |
| `subj`, `sep` | `subj` is 1 when the subject and the background (labelled pixels that are not the subject) each hold at least `max(1 %, 30 samples)`; `sep` is the subject's median L\* minus the background's (0 without a subject) |
| `{e}.share`, `.L`, `.C`, `.logC` | the element's share of the picture; its median L\*; its median C\*; `ln(max(C, 1))` |
| `{e}.h` | the circular mean, in degrees 0 to 360, of the unit vectors `(a*, b*)/C*` of the element's pixels with `C* > 5`; 0 if there are none |
| `s{j}.share`, `.logC`, and its hue | 60° sectors of CIELAB hue centred on 0, 60, … 300 (`j = ⌊((h + 30) mod 360) / 60⌋`): over pixels with `C* > 5`, **not skin**, and **not in a sector-excluded element**. Share of the picture; `ln(max(median C\*, 1))`; the circular mean hue. An element is excluded when both pictures hold it at 2 % or more of the picture (sky, vegetation, water, ground, built; first pass, then measured again) |
| `D.{e}.dh`, `D.s{j}.dh` | `wrap(R.h − P.h)` into (−180°, 180°], 0 if either picture has no hue pixel there |

A group's *hue* in these measures is the circular mean of unit vectors; the walls of §5.4 use another (the angle of the mean Oklab vector).

### 4b.5 The network

A **hand-written feed-forward network** reads these measures of `P` and `R` and writes the grading **targets** (`T.*`) and **gates** (`G.*`). Every weight and bias in it was written by hand; nothing is trained. It makes every grading decision: what to copy from the reference, how far, where to stop, and which gates are open. The objective and the solver (§5) make none.

- **The file.** `data/refgrade/network.ywn`, text, ours, Apache-2.0: round 15's file, which is the POC's v14 file unchanged (432 neurons after expansion, 62 targets and 21 gates; the header of the file lists what each round added and why). It ships in the app and next to `ym`. Its SHA-256 and its expanded text are pinned by a test. **A weight changes only by a spec change with the owner's agreement and a pass of §10's acceptance rules**; it is never changed to fit the owner's own photos (§10, *Data rules*).
- **The grammar** (`.ywn`), one neuron per line, in file order, evaluated in f64:
  - `name = relu( bias + w*node - node + … )` or `name = lin( … )`. `relu(x) = max(0, x)`; `lin(x) = x`. The bias is a number, then terms joined by `+` or `-`; a term is `w*node` or `node` (weight 1). A `node` is an input (§4b.4) or a neuron defined **earlier**. Weights and biases are decimal numbers, a leading `-` allowed.
  - `const NAME key=value key=value …` names a row of constants; `template NAME(var)` … `end` is a block of neuron lines; `apply NAME v1 v2 …` expands the block once per value, with `{var}` replaced by the value and `{key}` replaced from the `const` row named by that value (`-{key}` is minus the constant). Lines starting with `#` and blank lines are ignored.
  - Every neuron named `T.*` or `G.*` is an output.
  - **A file that breaks a rule is refused with the line and the name, never patched**: a node not defined earlier, a template value with a missing constant, a duplicate name (also against an input), an unknown activation, a template without `end`, a term that is not a number times a node.
- **Inputs** (names the network may read): for `X` in `P`, `R`: `X.p2`, `.p50`, `.p98`, `.iqr_rel`, `.logsat`, `.subj`, `.sep`, `.cast_a`, `.cast_b`, `.conc`, `.magenta_share`, `.tint_{s,m,h}_{a,b}`, `X.{e}.{share,L,h,C,logC}` for the six elements, `X.s{j}.{share,logC}` for six sectors; and `D.{e}.dh`, `D.s{j}.dh`.
- **Outputs.** Targets: `T.p2`, `T.p98`, `T.iqr_rel`, `T.p50` (the photo keeps its own median), `T.logsat`, `T.tint_{s,m,h}_{a,b}`, `T.cast_a`, `T.cast_b`, `T.wall_deg` (the hue-drift wall's limit, 25° and up to 45° when unify is open), `T.hl_cmax`, `T.white_a`, `T.white_b`, `T.white_tol`, `T.sep`, `T.s{j}.dh`, `T.s{j}.dlogC`, `T.{e}.h`, `T.{e}.logC`, `T.{e}.off`; and the wall parameters `T.purple_ok`, `T.purple_max_share`, `T.purple_rot_deg`, `T.skin_drift`, `T.skin_dlogC_max`, `T.mem.sky_deg`, `T.mem.vegetation_deg`, `T.turn_deg`, `T.turn_share`, `T.turn_cref`, `T.turn_cap`, `T.turn_full`, `T.clip_extra`. Gates: `G.key_mismatch`, `G.unify`, `G.subj`, `G.s{j}`, `G.{e}.matched`, `G.{e}.hue`.
- **What its parts do, in a sentence each.** *Key mismatch*: a low-key reference (median L\* ≤ 23 to 25) on a high-key photo (≥ 40 to 42) means its exposure relationships are content, not grade; they are halved or dropped. *Floor, ceiling, contrast*: move 0.8, 0.8 and 0.85 of the difference, clipped (±12 L\*, ±0.15). *Saturation*: 0.85 of the log ratio, the ratio held to 0.5 to 1.5. *Tint*: the full difference of the near-neutral cast per band, ±20. *Unify*: opens when the reference's whole frame leans to one colour, and then lets the frame cast and the hue-drift wall move. *Sectors*: the palette of pixels no element covers, gated by both pictures holding 2 to 3 % in a sector. *Elements*: matched when both pictures hold the element at 2 to 3 % (sky also only when lit: median L\* above about 18 to 22 in both, so a black backdrop is not a sky); hue moves 0.85 (sky), 0.75 (vegetation, water), 0.65 (ground), 0.45 (built) and 0.35 (skin) of the difference, up to the caps of §4a.1, inside windows of plausible hue for sky (175° to 285°), vegetation (70° to 180°), water (150° to 285°) and skin (30° to 70°); chroma by a power of the ratio (0.9 to 0.5), exposure relative to the frame. *Purple*: a colour may not turn into purple unless the reference holds it. *Whites*: they may take 0.8 of the reference's own highlight tint, no more. *Memory colours*: sky and foliage hold their hue within 10° and 12°, 45° and 40° when matched.

## 5. The fit

**Every number in this section is the POC's round 15, taken from its code** where the POC's notes and its code differed (§16.3). Nothing here is a design target: it is what was measured to work (§16.8). RG-06 writes it again in Rust under the house rules; the POC's code is not merged. A constant changes only by the rule of §10.

The fit **searches** for the node's numbers. The hand-written network (§4b.5) turns the measures of the photo and the reference into targets and gates; an objective measures how far a candidate's picture is from the targets; walls make harm costly; a staged search moves the numbers until the objective stops falling. The fit makes no grading decision of its own.

### 5.1 Pictures and the sample

| | Photo | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Source | the node's input rendered by the plan (§2.2), once per fit | the loaded reference (§6) |
| Analysis size | **512 px on the long edge** (the deterministic area average of §6.5, in linear light) | the same, never enlarged |
| Pixels read | a **seeded stratified sample of 8192 pixels**, the same for every candidate | **all pixels** of the analysis picture |
| Scene | the label map and skin of §4b, gathered at the 8192 pixels (the soft masks of §4a.3 at the same pixels) | the label map and skin of §4b on the analysis picture |

- **The sample.** A grid of 128 columns by 64 rows over the analysis picture, one pixel per cell: for each cell, row by row, `fx = (gx + u) / 128`, `fy = (gy + u') / 64` with `u`, `u'` the next two draws of **SplitMix64 seeded with `0x5EED_2026_1124`** (`next_u64() >> 11` over 2^53), and the pixel `(⌊fx · W⌋, ⌊fy · H⌋)` clamped to the picture. The seed is a constant: the sample depends on the analysis picture's size and nothing else.
- **The photo's chain-before-the-node is rendered once**; only the node and the steps after it run per candidate (§2.2). `P`, the photo as it stands, is the sample with the node at its identity.
- **Colour.** *(RG-19 planned: from RG-20 the picture is the Display P3 render and every measure below is taken from it, §5.9. The text of this bullet is today's, sRGB.)* Each candidate's pixels go through the Output encoded as **sRGB display codes, clamped to 0 to 1** (the range of an 8-bit file) whatever the viewport's space is, then to linear sRGB, XYZ with the Rec.709 primaries and **CIELAB with the D65 white**, and to Oklab (Ottosson's matrices) for the hue walls. The reference goes to the same space (§6.3). *Clipped white* is any encoded channel at 0.995 or more; *crushed black* is L\* below 2.5.
- **The fit ignores the node's own masks** (`masks`): a reference describes a whole picture, so the grade is fitted as if the masks were 1 everywhere, and the masks then limit where it acts. **It does use the element masks** of §4a.3: they are part of the candidate (§14, question 8).
- **The fit runs the node at Strength 100**; Strength is applied afterwards (§3.2).
- **The statistics are of distributions, not of pixel pairs**: the two pictures may show different things (§12).

### 5.3 The objective: the look terms

The **objective is a weighted sum**; every term is `term(err; scale, dead) = ((max(|err| − dead, 0)) / scale)²`. Measures are those of §4b.4 taken on the candidate (`m.*`); targets are the network's. `w_term` is sky 1.5, vegetation 1.2, water 1.0, ground 1.0, built 0.6, skin 2.0. `√share` is the square root of the photo's own share of the element, `P.{e}.share`.

| Term | Error | Scale | Dead | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| floor | `m.p2 − T.p2` | 2 | 1 | 1.0 |
| ceiling | `m.p98 − T.p98` | 2 | 1 | 1.0 |
| contrast | `m.iqr_rel − T.iqr_rel` | 0.03 | 0.01 | 1.0 |
| anchor | `m.p50 − T.p50` | 1 | 5 | 4.0 |
| tint (6 channels) | `m.tint − T.tint` | 3 | 1 | shadows 1.0, mids 0.7, highlights 1.0 |
| saturation | `m.logsat − T.logsat` | 0.05 | 0.03 | 1.0 |
| frame cast (`a*`, `b*`) | `m.cast − T.cast` | 2 | 1 | 1.2 · `G.unify` |
| sector hue, per open sector `j` | the mean over the sector's pixels (by `P`'s hue) of `wrap(candidate hue − P hue)`, minus `T.s{j}.dh` | 8° | 2° | 0.8 · `G.s{j}` · √(P.s{j}.share) |
| sector chroma | `ln(max(median C\*, 1))` of those pixels minus `P`'s, minus `T.s{j}.dlogC` | 0.08 | 0.03 | 0.8 · `G.s{j}` · √(P.s{j}.share) |
| element hue (the candidate has hue pixels) | `wrap(m.{e}.h − T.{e}.h)` | 10° | 3° | `w_term · √share · G.{e}.hue` |
| element chroma | `m.{e}.logC − T.{e}.logC` | 0.10 | 0.03 | `w_term · √share · G.{e}.matched` |
| element exposure | `(m.{e}.L − m.p50) − T.{e}.off` | 3 | 2 | `w_term · √share · G.{e}.matched` |
| subject | `m.sep − T.sep` | 3 | 2 | 1.0 · `G.subj` |

**The regulariser** is `0.5 · mean(x_global²) + 0.5 · mean(x_local²)` over the 30 global and the 19 local numbers: the fit prefers the smallest grade that reaches the targets.

**The full objective** is the sum of the look terms, the walls of §5.4, the regulariser and `3.0 ·` the clip term of §5.4. **The look-only objective** is the same sum **without the walls and without the clip term** (the regulariser stays); the second search of §5.5 uses it. A candidate the chain refuses (a non-finite result) gets a clip term of 10⁶ and is worse than anything.

### 5.4 The walls

The walls make harm costly. They are not grading decisions: they are what the owner's rule "film grades don't turn content colours into purple" and its like became. They read the network's wall parameters (`T.*`). In every wall *the hue of a group* is the **Oklab hue of the group's mean Oklab (a, b) vector** (`atan2` of the mean `b` and `a`), measured on the pixels of the group in `P` and on the same pixels in the candidate; chroma thresholds stay in CIELAB `C*`. `wrap` folds a difference into (−180°, 180°]. `sq(x) = x²`.

**Groups.** An *element group* is one of the six elements, taken only when the photo's share is at least 2 % and the candidate has pixels in it; *clothing* is `person` minus skin (at least 2 %); a *sector group* is `P`'s pixels of a sector with at least 4 % share; the *whites group* of the hue wall is `P`'s pixels with L\* ≥ 80 and C\* < 10; the *dark colours* are `P`'s non-skin pixels with L\* < 35 and C\* ≥ 2 (at least 2 % of the sample). A group counts in a wall only when the length of `P`'s mean (a\*, b\*) vector over it is at least 2.5. **The skin walls and the whites walls do not use the 2 %**: they exist from the coverage the harms H2 and H4 are tested at, 1 % of the frame, read on the analysis picture (below, *RG-13*).

| Wall | Formula | Constants |
|---|---|---|
| **Hue drift** (per group) | `2 · sq((\|wrap(Δh)\| − limit)₊ / 5)` | `limit = T.wall_deg` (25°, 45° when unify is open); for an element with `G.{e}.hue > 0.5`, `max(T.wall_deg, cap_e + 5)` |
| **Memory colours** (sky, vegetation) | `6 · sq((\|wrap(Δh)\| − T.mem.{sky,vegetation}_deg)₊ / 2)` | limits 10° and 12°, 45° and 40° when matched |
| **Whites: chroma cap** | `3 · sq((C̄ − T.hl_cmax)₊ / 2)` with `C̄` the **mean per-pixel C\*** of the photo's whites **as H4 reads them**: L\* ≥ 80 and C\* < 8, skin included | opposite tints do not cancel; `T.hl_cmax` is the licence's cap (below); the whites walls exist from whites at 1 % of the frame |
| **Whites: direction** | `d` = the **mean per-pixel distance** of those whites' (a\*, b\*) to (`T.white_a`, `T.white_b`); if `d > T.white_tol`: `3 · sq((d − T.white_tol) / 2)` | the target and the tolerance follow the licence (below): the photo's own whites cost nothing |
| **Whites: hue** | the hue-drift wall on the whites group (L\* ≥ 80, C\* < 10), when `P`'s mean-vector C\* there is at least 4 | same coverage |
| **Purple, per group** | in the **Oklab band 300° to 355°**: a group whose candidate hue is in the band adds `3 · sq((\|wrap(Δh)\| − T.purple_rot_deg)₊ / 5)`; a group not in the band at either end but approaching it (within 15° of the band, and nearer than in `P`) adds `1 · sq((15 − dist) / 5) · (1 − T.purple_ok)` | `T.purple_rot_deg` 10°, 45° when the reference holds purple |
| **Purple, pixels** | the share `s` of the sample's non-skin pixels whose candidate C\* ≥ 4, Oklab hue in the band, and either `P` C\* < 2 or a hue turn of at least `T.purple_rot_deg`; if `s > T.purple_max_share`: `6 · sq((s − T.purple_max_share) / 0.01)` | `T.purple_max_share` 1 %, 25 % when the reference holds purple |
| ~~Skin: absolute~~ | **removed (RG-13).** The band (hue 30° to 70°, C\* 12 to 38) asked a photo whose skin is under coloured light to move it into the band; that is H2 by construction | skin under coloured light stays as shot |
| **Skin: hue** (Skin protection on; skin ≥ 1 % of the frame, `P`'s mean-vector C\* ≥ 4) | `12 · sq((\|wrap(Δh)\| − limit)₊ / 3)`, `Δh` the turn of the Oklab mean-vector hue from the photo's own | `limit = min(T.skin_drift, 8°)`: 6°, 8° when the reference's skin is matched with reliable hue; 8° is 0.8 of H2's 10° |
| **Skin: chroma** (on; skin ≥ 1 % of the frame) | `r = ln(C_cand / C_P)` over the mean-vector chroma, **both ways**: `12 · (sq((r − up)₊ / 0.05) + sq((−r − down)₊ / 0.05))` | `up = min(T.skin_dlogC_max, 0.8 ln 1.25)` (ratio at most 1.195); `down = 0.8 ln (1 / 0.55)` (ratio at least 0.620): 0.8 of H2's lines 1.25 and 0.55 |
| **Sky chroma** (sky ≥ 2 %) | `limit = max(1.3 · C_P, exp(T.sky.logC))`; `2 · sq((C_cand − limit)₊ / 2)` over the mean-vector chroma | |
| **Content turn budget** | see below | `T.turn_*` |
| **Clip** | see below | `T.clip_extra` |

**The content turn budget (P2).** A strong colour keeps its hue family; big hue moves belong to weak colours, which is what a cast is. For each non-skin sample pixel with `P` C\* = C ≥ 8, with `Δh` the absolute Oklab hue turn between `P` and the candidate:

```
allowed(C) = clamp( asin( min(1, T.turn_cref · sin(T.turn_deg) / C) ), T.turn_deg, max(T.turn_cap, T.turn_deg) )     (C = 0 gives the cap)
w          = clamp( (Δh − allowed(C)) / (T.turn_full − allowed(C)), 0, 1 )          (a step if T.turn_full ≤ allowed)
s          = Σ w / N                                                                 N: all sample pixels
cost       = 6 · sq((s − T.turn_share) / 0.01)   when s > T.turn_share
```

`T.turn_deg` is 20° (30° when unify is open), `T.turn_share` 0.03, `T.turn_cref` 25, `T.turn_cap` 45°, `T.turn_full` 60° (where a turn is a reversal). The allowed turn is:

| C\* | 8 to 12 | 15 | 20 | 25 and above |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| unify shut | 45° | 35° | 25° | 20° |
| unify open | 45° | 45° | 39° | 30° |

A strong colour turned 30° counts as a quarter of a pixel, 40° as half, 60° as a whole one. A frame where 10 % of strong colours turn 30° costs nothing; a frame where 4 % reverse costs as before.

**The clip term (P5).** A grade does not destroy detail the photo has. With `hi` the share of pixels with any encoded channel ≥ 0.995 and `lo` the share with L\* < 2.5, `p` the photo's own share, `r` the reference's:

```
allow(p, r)  = max( p, min( r, p + T.clip_extra ) )              T.clip_extra = 0.02
over(o, a)   = sq( (o − a − 0.005)₊ / 0.01 )                     the slack is 0.005, CLIP_SCALE is 0.01
clip         = over(hi_cand, allow(hi_P, hi_R)) + over(lo_cand, allow(lo_P, lo_R))
```

The reference may license at most two points of new clipping, never more than the reference itself has: a black night sky is its content, not a licence to crush a daylight photo. The clip term enters the objective at weight 3.0.

### 5.4a RG-13: guards that measure what the harm measures

Round 15's walls missed H2 and H4 in three ways, found on the dev set (`explain`, `tools/eval/refgrade`): a wall that does not exist where the harm is tested (skin 1 to 2 %, whites 1 to 2 % of the sample), a wall on a *place* where the harm is a *move* (the absolute skin band), and a wall that a reference's tint raises above the harm line (`T.hl_cmax` 14 against H4's 8). The changes, all decisions of the owner's rules ("a wall measures what the harm measures", "a wall sits inside its line"), are:

- **Coverage.** The skin walls and the whites walls exist when the photo's **skin is at least 1 % of the frame**, and when its **whites are at least 1 % of the frame**, counted on **every pixel of the analysis picture** (the picture H2 and H4 are measured on), not on the 8192-pixel sample. `FitPhoto::coverage` carries the two shares from where the scene is measured (`ym-core`, `pipeline/fit.rs`); the whites share is counted on the photo with the node at its identity. The walls themselves still read the sample's pixels.
- **Inner limits.** A wall's limit is the harm line pulled in to 0.8 of its distance from no change: hue 8° (H2's line 10°); skin chroma ratio 0.620 to 1.195 (H2's lines 0.55 and 1.25: `exp(0.8 ln 0.55)`, `exp(0.8 ln 1.25)`). These are fixed fractions, not tuned. The network's `T.skin_drift` (6°, 10° when matched) and `T.skin_dlogC_max` (0.12, 0.30 when matched) may widen a limit when the reference's skin is matched, **never past the inner limit**; the chroma fall has no network output and is the inner limit.
- **Skin, relative to the photo.** Two walls (table above), on the move of the skin's mean colour from the **photo's own**: the Oklab mean-vector hue turn, and the ratio of the CIELAB mean-vector chroma, in both directions. The hue wall is tested, as H2 tests it, only when the photo's mean-vector C\* is at least 4. **The absolute skin band is removed.** *Skin under coloured light stays as shot; the owner can reverse this* (it is `Terms::guard_skin` and the constants `SKIN_HUE`, `SKIN_CHROMA` of round 15, in `ym-color` at `476fcc49`).
- **Whites, a licence.** The whites are H4's: L\* ≥ 80 and C\* < 8, skin included, the **mean per-pixel C\*** `p` on the photo and `r` on the reference's whites when they are at least 1 % of its frame, else `r = p` (a reference with no whites of its own licenses nothing beyond the margin). The licence is `allowed = max(p, min(r, p + 3)) + 1`. The H4 line is `max(8, r_own + 4)` with `r_own` the reference's whites' mean C\* over any number of pixels (0 for none), and its inner limit `0.8 ·` that line (6.4 for a reference with no whites). **The cap is `max(p, min(allowed, inner limit))`**: never above the inner limit, never below the photo's own chroma (a wall never asks the photo to lose chroma it has).
- **The whites targets follow the licence** (only where the whites guard exists; elsewhere the network's own stand). `T.hl_cmax` **is** the cap. The white target `(T.white_a, T.white_b)` is moved toward the photo's own whites' mean (a\*, b\*) until it is at most `cap − p` from it, and `T.white_tol` is raised to the direction wall's value at the photo as it is, so the photo's own whites cost nothing and the wall only stops a move away. The highlights band of the `tint` term, `T.tint_h`, is moved toward the photo's own `P.tint_h` until it is at most `cap − p` from it. The network file is unchanged (its hash is pinned); the clamps are applied to its outputs in `Fixed::with_coverage`. "Creamy whites under a creamy reference" (network rule v8) is kept: a reference whose own whites are creamy licenses their chroma, up to the inner limit.
- **Not changed.** The search, the look terms, the other walls, the sample, the seeds. `PIPELINE_VERSION` is not bumped: it is the version of the pixels the processing chain makes, and no render changes; the fit has no version constant of its own. The Linux pins of `crates/ym-core/tests/reference_fit.rs` are taken again (a fit's result changes by design); the macOS pins come from macOS CI.


### 5.5 The search

**Golden section, one parameter at a time, in stages, with fixed counts.** One coordinate step `golden(i, half, n)` searches `[x_i − half, x_i + half]` clipped to [−1, 1]: two interior points at 0.618 and 0.382, then `n` more steps, every evaluation of the full chain on the 8192 pixels; it keeps the **best of all points seen, the start included**, so a step never raises the objective and a tie keeps the start. Nothing needs to be differentiable.

1. **Global stages**, on the full objective, each parameter of the stage in index order, each stage starting from the last:

   | Stage | Parameters | Pass 1 (half, n) | Pass 2 (half, n) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | tone | 0 to 7 | 1.0, 8 | 0.4, 6 |
   | colour per band | 8 to 16 | 1.0, 7 | 0.4, 6 |
   | saturation and `hue_vs_sat` | 17 to 23 | 1.0, 7 | 0.4, 6 |
   | `hue_vs_hue` | 24 to 29 | 1.0, 7 | 0.4, 6 |

2. **The need test.** A matched element (`G.{e}.matched > 0.5`) whose binary mask covers at least 2 % of the picture gets a local grade only if, after the global stages, **its error is over a threshold**: the hue error `|wrap(m.{e}.h − T.{e}.h)|` is more than 6° (and `G.{e}.hue > 0.5`), or the log-chroma error is more than 0.14, or the exposure error `|(m.{e}.L − m.p50) − T.{e}.off|` is more than 4 L\* (and the exposure target is open, `G.{e}.matched − G.key_mismatch > 0.5`). The subject gets its exposure grade when `G.subj > 0.5`, its mask covers at least 2 %, and `|m.sep − T.sep|` is more than 4. **An element that does not pass keeps its parameters at exactly 0 and they are not searched.**
3. **Local stage**, for the needed elements in the order sky, vegetation, water, ground, built, skin, subject, their three parameters (the subject's one): pass (1.0, 7), then pass (0.4, 6).
4. **Joint polish**: the 30 global parameters and the needed local ones, one pass (0.25, 6).

**By count from the stages above** (not measured): one search evaluates about 770 candidates with no local grade, plus about 50 for each needed element and 24 for the polish of its parameters; the fit is three searches (below), so on the order of 2400 candidates, each one the chain on 8192 pixels and a set of measures.

**Two starts, keep the better.** A search from the neutral grade stops in the first basin it reaches, and a grade that carries a strong cast lies far from neutral, behind walls the path must cross. So the fit searches **twice** and keeps the better result:

1. **From neutral**: the search above on the full objective, from `x = 0`.
2. **From the look**: first the same search on the **look-only objective** from `x = 0`; then the search on the **full objective** starting from that result.
3. **Keep the one with the lower full objective. A tie keeps (1).**

This is a property of the search, not of any photo. It cost the POC about five times the time of one search (§16.7) and won on 333 of 728 dev fits and 461 of 962 test fits.

**A result no better than the identity is the identity.** If every parameter is still 0 at the end, the fit writes the identity and the status says "The photo is already close to the reference. Nothing was changed." (§9.3). That is not a failure. **A non-finite value, a segmenter or classifier error that cannot be recovered, or a search that cannot evaluate the chain is a failure** and writes nothing (§9.3).

### 5.6 Skin protection

Off, they are not added; the skin element's own wall as an element group, its grade caps (±15°) and every other wall stay. It is read **at fit time**: changing the switch changes nothing until the next Fit (§9.2).
- **Source: MediaPipe, never Apple Vision** (§4b.3). Apple Vision is not reproducible across Macs (`docs/23` §4.5).
- **No skin found** (no person, or the weights missing): the walls are not added and the status says "No skin was found, so Skin protection did nothing." when the switch is on.
- **RG-13 (skin and whites).** Round 15 left skin (H2, 7.9 % of held-out fits) and tinted whites (H4, 7.8 %) as nearly all of the harm. RG-13 changes the skin and whites walls and the whites targets (below and §15, items 66 to 69); its dev gate and its fresh check are in the round's note and in `docs/STATUS.md`.

### 5.7 Determinism, progress and time

- **CPU, f64 for the measures and the objective, a fixed order of operations, no data-dependent threading.** A candidate's pixels are split in fixed chunks of 256 whatever the thread count, each chunk computed by one task, and every statistic is reduced in pixel order, so 1 and N threads give the same bits (as the CPU chain's tiles do). The skin plane and the segmenter are deterministic. The same photo, reference, settings and version give the same fitted numbers on every run (rule 8). **RG-11 (speed) keeps this and adds to it:** the two independent searches of §5.5 run side by side, each a pure function of the fixed inputs and of its own start, so the result is the one the searches give one after the other; and **no floating-point sum is split across threads** (the measures' passes run side by side, but each sum is taken in pixel order, and a median is the element a sort would put in the middle), so the bits are also those of the single-threaded code RG-06 built, which four pinned fixture fits and the evaluation tool's records show (§19, RG-11).
- **Progress and Cancel.** The fit reports where it is: the scene (segmenter and skin for the photo and the reference, once each and then kept), then "Fitting… 1 of 3", 2 of 3, 3 of 3 and the stage within it. **The two independent searches run side by side (RG-11), so the line counts the stages of both together**: the n-th stage started (of 18) is stage n mod 6 of the (n div 6 + 1)-th third. It never goes back, ends at "3 of 3", and is told from either search, one call at a time. Cancel is checked between candidates, takes effect within a fraction of a second, and **changes nothing**.
- **Time: several seconds on a Mac with all cores is accepted** (the owner, 2026-11-25). The POC measured **about 30 s per fit on one Linux thread** for round 15 and about 6 s for the single search of rounds 11 to 14 (§16.7); those are Linux, single-thread figures and not Mac figures. The segmenter's cost is an estimate (§4b.1). RG-11 is the speed task and RG-H3 the owner's timing. The order of remedies, the quality gate re-run after each: evaluate the candidates across all cores (the two independent searches in parallel, and the pixels of a candidate across chunks), cheaper chain steps for the candidate, the scene cached per photo and per reference, and only then fewer evaluations.

### 5.8 Match brightness (RG-15 planned; RG-16 built; RG-17, RG-18 planned)

**Why.** The fit keeps the photo's own middle brightness (`T.p50` is the photo's median, §4b.5) and moves only its floor, ceiling and contrast toward the reference's; under a key mismatch (§4b.5) it also drops the reference's exposure relationships. That is right when the reference's brightness is its scene (a night still must not darken a beach), and wrong when the person wants the reference's brightness too: a dark overcast photo fitted to a sunny still stays dark, and a bright photo fitted to a dusk still stays bright (the owner, 2026-11-28, on 0.2.5-beta.2). **The owner, 2026-11-28: "Yes, plan the Match brightness slider".** **The plan and its defaults approved, 2026-11-28 (RG-H5): "defaults for brightness are confirmed".**

**What it is.** A slider on the Reference tab, **Match brightness**, 0 to 100 %, **0 by default**, stored as `match_brightness` (§3.1). It is an input of the next fit, like Skin protection: moving it changes no pixel until Fit (§9.3 says so in the status line). **At 0 the fit is today's fit, bit for bit.**

**What a fit does with it.** With `b = match_brightness / 100` above 0:

1. **The gap.** Each picture's brightness is its median L\* as the fit measures it (`P.p50` for the photo with the node at its identity, `R.p50` for the reference, §4b.4), taken to relative luminance `Y` (CIELAB's inverse, `L*` held at 1 or more). The gap in stops is `g = log2(Y_R / Y_P)`, **held to ±1.5 stops**. The move asked for is `Δ = b · g`.
2. **The moved photo `P'`.** The fit finds the exposure shift `d` that, applied to the photo before the node (as the node's own Exposure would be, the rest at the identity), brings the photo's measured median luminance to `Y_P · 2^Δ`: a bisection on `d` in [−2, 2] stops, 16 steps, on the fit's 8192-pixel sample, deterministic; if the target is out of reach the nearer end is taken. `P'` is the sample rendered with that shift. (`d` and `Δ` differ because the Output's curve compresses the ends; the bisection makes the *displayed* middle move by `Δ`.)
3. **`P'` is the photo for the network and the walls.** The network reads `P'`'s measures in place of `P`'s (§4b.4), so every target written relative to the photo (the median it keeps, the floor, ceiling and contrast, the key-mismatch gate, the element offsets, the tints) is made around the moved photo; and the walls of §5.4 that compare a candidate with the photo compare it with `P'`, so the brightness move itself is not counted as harm (a skin chroma ratio, for one, is measured from the moved skin). **The clip term keeps `P` as its baseline**: the move may not clip more than the photo and the reference license (§5.4), so a large lift rolls the highlights off rather than burning them.
4. **The candidates are rendered from the photo as it is**, the node's numbers on top; the move is made by the fitted luma curve (the first knot's ±2 stops and the segment slopes reach ±1.5 stops in the middle), so **Strength scales it** like the rest of the fit (§3.2) and nothing new is stored but the slider.
5. **The key mismatch does not hold the level back.** The move of step 1 is made whatever `G.key_mismatch` says ("if you ask for the brightness, you get it"); the gate is computed on `P'`, so the reference's *element* relationships are still dropped when the moved photo and the reference remain far apart in key.

**What it is not.** Not a second exposure control: the Light tab's Exposure and Main's stay the person's. Not live: the brightness is part of the fit (its walls keep the move from clipping and from turning colours), so a new value needs Fit again, which takes a couple of seconds on a Mac (the owner, 2026-11-28). Not a change of the network file: the move is made outside it, on its inputs, as RG-13 clamps its outputs.

**Notes and `ym`.** After a fit whose gap was held at ±1.5 stops, a note: "The reference is more than 1.5 stops brighter, so the photo was brightened by 1.5 stops." (or "darker … darkened"); §9.3. `ym render --reference … --reference-brightness <0–100>` sets the field; `--json`'s `reference` object gains `brightness: { percent, moved_ev, limited }` (§8).

**As built (RG-16).** `ym_color::refgrade::brightness` (the pure part: `luminance_of_lstar`, `Gap`, `asked_ev`, `solve_shift`, `BrightnessMove`), `measure::median_lightness`, `Fixed::with_moved_photo` and `objective::clip_shares`, and in `ym-core` `ChainCandidates::render_shifted` and `move_photo` in `reference_fit.rs`; `fit_node` runs it only when `match_brightness` is above 0.
- **The field.** A number held to 0 to 100 like Strength (not an integer in the file; the panel's slider will step by 1). Clamped and reported by path (`nodes.<n>.match_brightness`), a non-finite value is `NotFinite`; the patch field `match_brightness`, `SettingsField::MatchBrightness` (a field of the Grade stage group, like Skin protection), Reset to 0, Copy and Paste, presets, albums and the schema all carry it; it is **not** in the render key (it changes no pixel).
- **`P'`.** `P` is the 8192-pixel sample rendered at the node's identity (today's `p_px`). `Δ = b * g` with `g` from `Gap::of_pictures` (the medians of the sample and of every pixel of the reference's analysis picture, `Ranks::of`'s `p50`). The shift `d` is added to the node's own `trim.exposure_ev` for the render (`ChainCandidates::render_shifted`), 16 bisection steps on [-2, 2] stops on the displayed median luminance, then one render for `P'` (17 renders; a cancel is asked before each). A target no shift reaches takes the nearer end; a move of exactly 0 (a gap of 0) runs nothing.
- **Who reads what.** `Fixed::with_moved_photo(net, P, P', ...)` builds the whole fixed part from `P'` (the network's inputs, every target, the key-mismatch gate, the sectors, the groups and licences the walls compare candidates with) and sets only `clip_base` (the shares clipped and crushed that the clip term allows) from `P`. **The skin and whites coverage (§5.4) stays the photo's own** (the analysis picture's every pixel at the identity, `analysis_coverage`), not recomputed on `P'`: the person's photo decides whether there is skin or whites to protect. At 0 `fit_node` calls `Fixed::with_coverage` as before and `clip_base == [p.hi, p.lo25]`, so every bit is today's (the four fixed-fit pins did not move).
- **The outcome.** `FitOutcome::brightness: BrightnessMove { percent, gap_ev (held), asked_ev (`b * g`), shift_ev (`d`), moved_ev, limited }`. `moved_ev` is **the move the displayed median of the sample made**, `log2(Y(P') / Y(P))`, which is `asked_ev` within the bisection's step when the shift is in range; it is what the panel's notes (RG-17) and `ym --json`'s `brightness.moved_ev` say. At 0 it is `BrightnessMove::none()` (all 0, not limited). `limited` is true when the raw gap was beyond ±1.5 stops and `b > 0`.
- **`ym`.** `--reference-brightness <0–100>` (same parser as `--reference-strength`; needs `--reference`) sets the node's field before the fit; `--json`'s `reference.brightness` is `{ "percent", "moved_ev", "limited" }` (`docs/10`).

### 5.9 The fit judges in Display P3 (RG-19 planned; RG-20 build; RG-21 evaluation)

**Why.** The fit judges every picture as sRGB display codes clamped to 0 to 1 (§5.1), while the person looks at the photo in Display P3 on the Mac viewport and may export it in P3. RG-19's diagnosis (the owner, 2026-11-28, on 0.2.5-beta.2: "reds are fitting strange sometimes") measured what that costs, on 30 fits of five dev photos with large red or orange areas against six reference stills, with the same fitted numbers rendered once through the fit's sRGB chain and once through a Display P3 chain:

- **The two pictures are not one picture.** The output rendering is made per output (`display/cine.rs`: the per-channel soft floor and ceiling of step 7 and the output's own primaries), so the P3 render is not a matrix of the sRGB one. At the identity, 14 to 81 % of the strongest reds (the top tenth of the red region by chroma) are outside sRGB in the P3 render and none in the sRGB one.
- **The fit's picture overstates what survives.** For the strongest reds, the chroma ratio after the fit over before, measured as the fit measures it, has median 0.95 (quartiles 0.73 and 1.05). The same numbers shown in P3 give 0.84 (quartiles 0.60 and 0.96). The P3 ratio is the lower one in 30 of 30 pairs, by 0.06 to 0.17 (median 0.12). In log chroma the gap has median −0.15 (range −0.22 to −0.06).
- **The clamp hides what leaves sRGB after the fit.** In 17 of the 30 pairs more than a tenth of the strongest reds leave sRGB once the node is fitted (mean R up to 1.17, G and B down to −0.02, from a look that was inside sRGB at the identity). The clamp then removes a median 1 % of their chroma (at most 7 %) and turns their hue by up to ±3°, and the clip test (§5.1, an encoded channel at 0.995) sees red clipping that P3 does not have (3 to 13 % of the strong reds at the identity in sRGB codes, none in P3 codes).
- **Not found:** a fit that adds chroma to saturated reds for nothing. The fits mostly lower red chroma (median ratio 0.83 on the red region, down to 0.47); the neon look was not reproduced on the dev photos (none holds a comb as strong as the owner's). What was found is that the fit steers by a picture that differs from the one the person sees, by an amount that depends on how strong the colour is.

**The owner chose this remedy, 2026-11-28: "B: judge colour in P3"** ("The fit always judges its results in a Display P3 render, so strong reds are seen as on screen; `ym` and the app stay identical"). Two other remedies of the diagnosis (a guard on the tone curve's effect on skin and a skin guard by face size) were not chosen and are **not** part of this plan.

**What it is.** The picture the fit measures and judges is the Display P3 render. **The output target of the fit is fixed at Display P3**: not the viewport's, not the export's. Why fixed: `ym` and the app must give identical fits for the same inputs (rule 6 of `AGENTS.md`), and the Mac viewport, which is what the owner looks at, is P3. A fit that followed the window's display or the export's space would differ between the app and `ym`, and between two Macs. (`OutputTarget::Viewport` and `DisplayP3` are the same colours, `cine.rs`.) **The consequence is stated, not hidden**: a photo exported to sRGB shows the fit's strong reds clipped to sRGB by the export, as every picture in P3 does; the fit does not try to serve sRGB too.

#### 5.9.1 Which pictures are rendered and measured in P3

**All of them.** One rule: every picture the fit makes, measures or compares is the Display P3 render of the photo, and every colour measure of it is taken from linear Display P3 (clamped to 0 to 1 in P3), so no picture is judged in one space and compared with a picture judged in another.

| Picture | Where made today | Change |
|---|---|---|
| The photo's chain and every candidate | `pipeline/fit.rs` builds the fit's `ViewSpec` with `target: OutputTarget::Srgb` (line 242) and `job.grade(OutputTarget::Srgb, false)` (line 251); `ChainCandidates::render` (`reference_fit.rs`) reads the chain's codes | both become `OutputTarget::DisplayP3`; the codes the chain gives are Display P3 codes (the sRGB curve, `encode.rs`, `OutputSpace::DisplayP3`) |
| `P`, the photo at the node's identity | `p_px` of `fit_node`, the sample through `ChainCandidates::render` | the same call, so it is P3 |
| `P'`, the moved photo (§5.8) | `move_photo` and `render_shifted`; the bisection reads the displayed median luminance of the sample | the same chain, so P3. The luminance is `Y` from CIELAB's `L*`, a colour quantity, so the gap and the stops of §5.8 keep their meaning; the bisection's result changes only as the P3 render's median differs from the sRGB one's |
| The coverage counts | `analysis_coverage` (`fit.rs`): `px_of_codes` over every pixel at the identity | the same call on P3 codes. The 1 % gates read the same quantities (skin from the scene, whites from L\* and C\*) |
| The evaluation picture | `reference_eval_photo` (`eval_scene.rs`, `LabConv::new()`), the evaluation tool | the same chain and `LabConv`, so P3 |
| The scene (segmenter, skin, subject) | the Input's picture | **not changed**: the segmenter and the skin network read sRGB pictures, as they were trained on them, and never the fit's chain |

**`LabConv` (`ym_color::refgrade::measure`).** Today it holds the Rec.709 matrix, clamps the linear value to 0 to 1 per channel, builds CIELAB with the D65 white and Oklab from the clamped linear Rec.709 value, and tests clipping with `srgb_decode(0.995)`. It becomes a converter for **Display P3**:

1. **The matrix** is Display P3's, `DISPLAY_P3.rgb_to_xyz()` (`consts.rs`), the normalised primary matrix for the D65 white, in f64, the same way `REC709.rgb_to_xyz()` is made now. The D65 white of the CIELAB conversion is unchanged.
2. **The clamp** is to 0 to 1 **in linear Display P3**. A colour outside Display P3 is clamped there; a colour outside Rec.709 and inside P3 is not clamped.
3. **Oklab** keeps Ottosson's matrices, which take linear Rec.709. The clamped P3 value is taken to linear Rec.709 by the 3 × 3 matrix `DISPLAY_P3.rgb_to_xyz()` then `REC709.xyz_to_rgb()` (the exact derived matrices, not rounded tables), **not clamped again**, and its cube roots are signed. The Oklab hue and chroma are then those of the colour, whatever space the picture is in; nothing about Oklab's definition changes.
4. **The clip test** keeps its number: an encoded channel at 0.995 or more, on the clamped value, which is `srgb_decode(0.995)` in linear light because Display P3 uses the sRGB curve (`OutputSpace::DisplayP3`). It now means "at the top of the P3 codes of the file the person sees".
5. **`px_of_codes`** decodes with the same curve. Its callers pass P3 codes.
6. **No other path** converts a display value to CIELAB in the fit. `LabConv::new()` is the only constructor; a second constructor for Rec.709 stays for the tests that compare the two spaces, and is not used by the product.

**The reference** is the other input of the fit; see §5.9.2.

#### 5.9.2 The reference

**Today** (§6.2, §6.3, `reference.rs`): the decoders give code values; a matrix/TRC profile, a `cICP` code point or an untagged picture (taken as sRGB) gives **linear sRGB**, clamped to 0 to 1, area-averaged in linear light (§6.5) into the scene picture (long edge 1024 px) and the analysis picture (512 px); a picture from the filmstrip is the photo's display render as 8-bit sRGB codes (`from_snapshot` decodes with the sRGB table); `reference_side` makes its `Px` with `LabConv::px`, and its CIELAB is what the network reads. Reference stills are mostly sRGB JPEGs.

**What changes.**

1. **The reduced pictures hold linear Display P3, clamped to 0 to 1 in P3.** §6.3's conversion (the fixed order of §6.3, item 5) ends in Display P3 instead of sRGB: code values → linear by the curves → XYZ D50 by the profile's matrix → **Bradford to D65 → linear Display P3** (the matrix of `DISPLAY_P3`, derived from its chromaticities in f64). A `cICP` picture goes from its primaries' D65 white to Display P3 directly. An untagged picture is sRGB and goes through the exact sRGB-to-P3 matrix. `ToLinearSrgb` becomes a converter to a named target (`ToLinearDisplay`, with `srgb()` and `display_p3()` targets) and the loader asks for Display P3.
2. **Where it changes nothing.** Every colour an sRGB picture has lies inside Display P3, so the sRGB-to-P3 conversion never clamps, and **the CIELAB of an sRGB reference is the same number as today's** (CIELAB is made from XYZ; the matrices differ but describe the same colour), to f64 rounding of the two matrix products. Two clamps differ in a picture whose colours leave sRGB: the clamp to sRGB removed them, the clamp to P3 keeps what is inside P3.
3. **Where it changes something.** A reference in a wide profile (Display P3 screenshots, our own P3 exports, Adobe RGB, Rec.2020): the colours between the sRGB and the P3 gamut are now kept (they were clamped to sRGB before), and a Rec.2020 or Adobe RGB colour outside P3 is clamped to P3 instead of sRGB. Strong reds and greens in a reference are the visible case.
4. **The filmstrip reference** is the other photo's render **in Display P3** (the same snapshot path, with the P3 target) and is read with the P3 decode. The snapshot is held as 8-bit P3 codes as it is held as 8-bit sRGB codes now; the quantisation is the same.
5. **The scene picture stays for the segmenter in sRGB.** The segmenter and the skin network take sRGB 8-bit codes (`ReducedPicture::srgb8`, `reference.rs`). The reduced picture is in linear P3, so `srgb8` converts P3 to sRGB by the exact matrix and **clamps to sRGB there**, as the scene has always read it. Only the segmenter's input is sRGB; its labels apply to the same pixels.
6. **Where "Lab is device-independent" holds and where it does not.** It holds for the *formulae*: CIELAB and Oklab are functions of the XYZ colour, so a colour measures the same whatever space it came in. It does not hold for (a) the **clamp to 0 to 1**, which is a property of the display space and decides which colours exist in the picture; (b) the **channel clip test** (0.995), which is a property of the codes; (c) **the render**: the P3 render of a photo is not the sRGB render converted (§5.9 *Why*), so P and every candidate are different pictures, not the same ones measured differently; (d) a reference that was **tagged wrongly** or untagged and wide (risk of §12, unchanged).
7. **The reference's size, letterbox and reduction** (§6.5) are unchanged. The area average is of linear P3 values; because the average is linear and the conversion is a matrix, the average of P3 values is the P3 value of the average, except where the P3 clamp acted on single pixels first (a pixel outside P3 is clamped before it is averaged, as pixels outside sRGB are clamped before today).
8. **`ym`** needs no new option. `--json`'s `reference` object keeps its fields; the numbers in it (the measures) are P3's.

**The rule for this task: no new colour threshold, and no constant is tuned.** Each constant below either measures a quantity that does not depend on the display space, and so carries over by definition, or depends on the space and is checked by the evaluation of RG-21 against the gates of §10, with the outcome reported whatever it is. If a gate fails, the constants are **not** changed to pass it (§5.9.7).

#### 5.9.4 The network

**What it reads.** The `P.*` and `R.*` measures and the `D.*` differences of §4b.4 (`network_inputs`, `measure.rs`): the percentiles and the key of `L*`, `logsat`, the tints and the cast, the elements' share, `L*`, hue and `ln C*`, the six sectors' share and `ln C*`, the purple share, and the hue differences. Its weights were set and checked on sRGB-measured pictures (the POC's round 15, §16).

**What moves when the picture changes from sRGB to P3.** Two things, and only the first can move a network input:

1. **The photo's measures** (`P.*`, and `P'`'s): the photo is a different render (§5.9 *Why*). A colour inside sRGB is nearly the same colour in the P3 render; a colour that was soft-clipped by the sRGB render is more saturated, and a few pixels outside sRGB are no longer clamped.
2. **The reference's measures** (`R.*`): for a reference in sRGB (most stills) **none move** (§5.9.2, 2). For a reference in a wide profile, the colours between the gamuts are kept.

**Measured, on the dev set** (`eight dev photos with strong reds, oranges or colours (stage and group portraits with red clothing, a red interior, a red car, a market, a close-up portrait), each rendered once to sRGB and once to Display P3 by an earlier `ym` build (0.2.4, the same look and Output, long edge 640 px, neural steps off), and the measures of §4b.4 that need no scene taken from both pictures with the same code; the elements' measures (which need the segmenter) are not in the table, and the sectors are taken without the elements' exclusions, so every figure is an estimate of the movement, not the network's own input`):

| Group of inputs (units) | Median change | Third quartile | Largest | As a share of the spread between the eight photos: median, third quartile, largest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tone: `p2`, `p50`, `p98`, `iqr_rel` (L\*) | 0.006 | 0.043 | 1.72 | 0.00, 0.02, 0.23 |
| Saturation: `logsat` (ln C\*) | 0.004 | 0.014 | 0.024 | 0.01, 0.02, 0.03 |
| Cast: `cast_a`, `cast_b`, `conc` (a\*, b\*) | 0.147 | 0.528 | 6.66 | 0.01, 0.04, 0.56 |

| Sector chroma: six sectors (ln C\*) | 0.019 | 0.052 | 0.252 | 0.03, 0.11, 0.58 |

- **By picture.** Four of the eight photos move no input by more than a third of its spread (largest 0.09, 0.14, 0.14 and 0.33). Four move one input by about half of it: a sector's chroma (0.52, 0.58), a sector's share (0.52) or the cast (0.56, a red interior of which 30 % of the pixels are outside sRGB in the P3 render: `cast_b` +6.7 and `cast_a` +3.8 a\* and b\* units). On no photo are more than 2 of the 27 inputs above a quarter of their spread.
- **Where the two renders differ.** The mean CIE76 difference between the two renders of the same photo is 0.29 for pixels of sRGB-render C\* below 20 (64 % of the frame), 0.54 for C\* 20 to 50 (18 %) and 2.5 for C\* above 50 (13 %; range 1.8 to 8.3 by photo; 95th percentile 5.2). 2 % to 30 % of a photo's pixels (median 8 %) are outside sRGB in the P3 render. The neutrals, skin and sky do not move; the strongest colours do. The median brightness `p50` moves by at most 0.45 L\*.
- **Not an input, but it moves:** the share of pixels at the top of the codes (`hi`, the clip test): median 0.001 and largest 0.019, as large as its spread between the photos. It is §5.9.3's "the number carries, its reading changes".
- **Limits of this measure.** Eight photos chosen for strong colour are the hard case, so the shares of the spread are upper bounds; the spread is taken over those eight, narrower than over the dev set, so they are over-stated too. The network's own outputs under P3 are measured by RG-21 on the whole dev set with the real measures (§10: *Judged in Display P3 (evaluation)* (1)).

**Decision: the network file is kept as it is.** The inputs that carry the colour of the frame (`logsat`, the tints, the key and the purple share) move by a few hundredths of their spread. The ones that carry the strongest colours (the cast and a sector's chroma and share) move by up to half of it on photos with a large saturated area, and those are the photos the diagnosis was about. A sector's median `C*` rises by a few per cent when the strongest colours stop being clamped: that is a measurement of the picture the person sees, which is what the change is for. The network's outputs are ramps and gates (§4b.5) of these inputs, so a move of this size moves a target by a similar share of its own range, not by a switch, and the walls and the regulariser still bound the fit. **If a later measurement shows the inputs moved by more than the network's own sampling noise on a group** (the move is larger than the spread of that input across the photos of the dev set), **the next step is to ask the owner**, with the table; the network is **not** retrained inside RG-19 to RG-21, because that would be a new learned artefact with its own gates (§4b.5, §16). The clamps of its outputs that RG-13 added (§5.4a) stay in `Fixed::with_coverage`.

#### 5.9.5 Evaluation (RG-21)

**The picture the evaluation measures.** The harms H1 to H6 and the transfers T1 to T3 are measured on **the P3 picture for the baseline and for the new fit alike**, so the two are compared on the picture the person sees and neither is favoured by the space it was judged in. The tool gets it as follows:

1. `ChainCandidates` renders every pixel of the analysis picture through the fit's chain, which is now P3 (§5.9.1), and `LabConv` is the P3 converter: `reference_eval_photo`, the photo's `P` and the fit's picture are P3. This is also how a *new* run is measured.
2. **The baseline's fits are re-measured, not re-fitted.** The baseline is RG-16's fit (sRGB-judged): its stored numbers (the 49 values of each record, which `records.jsonl` keeps as `x`; a baseline run made before this task is repeated once with the old build, with the record gaining `x` if it lacks it) are rendered through the **P3 chain** by `refgrade-eval measure-p3 --records … --out …` (a new command: it renders each record's numbers and writes the harms and the transfers on that picture). The reference's picture is the reference in P3 (§5.9.2).
3. **The audit** (`audit_render`, `run.rs`): renders the fit through the real pipeline whole with the **Display P3 target** (`advisor_picture` gains a target argument) and compares it with the measured picture, as it does for sRGB now; the P3 audit is the check that the judged picture is the shown one.
4. The harm definitions of §10.1 are **unchanged** (lines, groups, hues in Oklab, the 8 and 0.55 and 1.25); only the picture is P3.

**The gates are RG-13's, on the P3 picture** (the owner's three rules, §10.1): over all fits of the dev set, (1) the share of fits with any harm rises by at most 1 point; (2) no single harm rises by more than 1 point (H5, clipping, by at most 2 points, because a strong colour in P3 is judged to clip differently and the clip test's reading changes, §5.9.3); (3) the mean T1, T2 and T3 each fall by no more than 0.01, measured on the fits the baseline left harm-free (the all-fits reading is reported beside it). The dev run is the gate (decision 82); the fresh check follows.

**The reds row** is for a person to read. It uses the 30 red pairs of RG-19's diagnosis (five dev photos with strong reds or oranges, six reference stills) and **a measure that is not made by the construction of the fit**:

- *The strongest colours* are the 2 % of the photo's pixels with the largest Oklab chroma on the P3 picture at the identity, **whatever their hue** (no colour rule; for these photos they are the reds).
- *The viewing gap* of a fit is `ln(C_judged) − ln(C_shown)`, where `C_judged` is the ratio after over before of the mean Oklab chroma of those pixels **measured on the fit's own picture** (the 8192-pixel sample at 512 px, f64, Strength 100, the chain the search used) and `C_shown` is the same ratio **measured on the real render** (`advisor_picture` with the P3 target, the whole pipeline at the photo's size, with the node at the fitted numbers).
- *Why this is not true by construction.* The fit minimises its objective, not this quantity. The two pictures meet only if the judged picture is built by the same chain and the same output rendering as the shown one, and the clamp, the soft clip and the encode agree. It fails if `LabConv` or the target is wrong somewhere, which is what this row exists to catch. At the baseline the gap is large (median −0.15; 30 of 30 negative; −0.22 to −0.06).
- *The gate:* on the 30 red pairs the median viewing gap of the new fit is within ±0.05 and **no pair is below −0.10** (the baseline had 25 of 30 below −0.10). The strong colours' *amount* is not gated: the fit may lower red chroma when the reference asks for it, as the baseline's did (median 0.83).
- *Also reported, not gated:* the chroma ratio of the strongest colours on the P3 picture, before and after, for the baseline and the new fit; the share of strongest pixels outside sRGB before and after; the texture of the strongest colours (the standard deviation of Oklab `L`, after over before).

**The fresh check, and the owner's photos.** After the dev gate, the same two measurements are made **once** on a fresh held-out set (the working rule of §10.1: the previous held-out set is spent), the three rules as above. **The owner's 7 photos are reported separately**, as they were for RG-18: the harms and the viewing gap per photo, not part of any gate, and **nothing is tuned on them**. The owner's own photos stay a held-out check (`docs/32` §2.3).

## 6. References

### 6.1 Image files

- **Formats: JPEG, PNG, TIFF**, 8 or 16 bits per sample, RGB or grey (a grey picture matches as black and white; the status line says so). Decoders: `zune-jpeg` (with its SIMD paths and its error tolerance off, so the pixels do not depend on the CPU and a cut picture is refused rather than filled with grey), `png` and `tiff`, all three already in the lock file; `zune-jpeg` becomes a dependency of `ym-core` (`docs/14`).
- **No HEIC.** libheif is LGPL (`docs/14`, rule 1). HEIC and every other format get the "not supported" error (§9.3), which says to save as JPEG or PNG first. No RAW file is accepted as a reference: a raw's picture depends on its settings; use a photo in the strip, or export it first.
- **Orientation is applied** (the EXIF tag of a JPEG or TIFF, values 1 to 8), because the scene model expects an upright picture (§15, decision 18).
- **A file is only read.** Rule 5 holds: nothing is written, moved or renamed. The file is read once, reduced and let go of (§6.5).

### 6.2 What a reference is read as

An image gives a grid of display-referred code values in its own colour space. §6.3 turns them into display-linear sRGB, the space the fit measures in (§5.1) *(RG-19 planned: Display P3 from RG-20, §5.9.2)*; §6.5 reduces them. **The reference is used at run time and never stored, uploaded or cached** (§6.6).

### 6.3 Colour handling

1. **Untagged files are taken as sRGB** (the sRGB curve and primaries, IEC 61966-2.1). This is a guess and is listed as a risk (§12): a wrongly tagged or untagged picture shifts the whole fit.
2. **Tags that are read**, in this order: an embedded ICC profile (JPEG `APP2`, PNG `iCCP`, TIFF tag 34675); PNG `cICP` (the code points our exports write: sRGB 1/13, Display P3 12/13, Rec.2020; `docs/09`); PNG `sRGB`. The others (`gAMA`, `cHRM`) are not read.
3. **Profiles that are handled: RGB matrix/TRC profiles**: the colorant tags `rXYZ`, `gXYZ`, `bXYZ` (already in the profile connection space, D50) and the curves `rTRC`, `gTRC`, `bTRC` as `curv` (the identity, a gamma or a table) or `para` (types 0 to 4). That covers sRGB, Display P3, Adobe RGB, Rec.2020 and **the "Display" profile macOS puts in screenshots** (the research set had one; RG-05 adds it, and the others, as fixtures). **A profile with a LUT (`A2B0`, `A2B1` or `A2B2`, even when it also has the matrix and curves), a grey, CMYK, Lab or named-colour profile is not handled.**
4. **A profile that cannot be read** (damaged, or not a matrix/TRC kind) is **not an error**: the file is taken as sRGB and the card says so ("The picture's colour profile couldn't be read, so it is taken as sRGB.", §9.3). Stopping would block a picture that is usually fine, and the person sees the note.
5. **The conversion** is in f64, in a fixed order: code values → linear by the curves → XYZ D50 by the matrix → **linear sRGB** (the Bradford adaptation from D50 to D65, then the sRGB matrix), clamped to 0 to 1 as the fit's own pictures are (§5.1). The fit's CIELAB (D65) is made from that. No chromatic adaptation is needed for an ICC profile to reach D50 (its colorants are D50); an untagged picture is already sRGB, and a `cICP` picture goes from its primaries' D65 white to sRGB directly. Colours a wider profile has outside sRGB are clamped.
6. **A 16-bit picture is read at its full precision**; 8-bit pictures are read as they are (no dithering, no smoothing).

**Decision: our own minimal ICC reader, not a crate** (§14, question 10). The reader is a few hundred lines in `ym-color` (the tag table, `XYZ `, `curv`, `para`), in f64 and a fixed order. Why: (1) **determinism**: a general colour-management crate may choose SIMD or f32 paths by CPU, and the fit's bits must not depend on them (rule 8); (2) **scope**: we need exactly matrix/TRC, and refuse the rest; (3) **no new dependency** for `cargo deny` and `docs/14`; (4) our own export already writes such profiles (`ym-export` `icc.rs`: sRGB, Display P3, Rec.2020), so the layout is known and the reader is tested against them. `moxcms` is listed in `docs/14` among the permitted dependencies (MIT/Apache-2.0/BSD) but is **not in the workspace manifest today**; if the owner prefers a crate, it is the candidate, with the determinism check as an acceptance test. The **oracles** of the reader's tests (RG-05): `moxcms` 0.8 as a dev-dependency of `ym-export` (tests only; an independent parser and a different arithmetic), and the closed form (the expected linear sRGB of a fixture computed from its primaries and curve formulas, no profile in between); the bounds are in §19. Neither is a dependency of a shipped crate. **`cICP`:** primaries 1 (Rec.709), 12 (Display P3) and 9 (Rec.2020) with transfer 13 (the sRGB curve) or 1 (read as the pure gamma 2.4 our exports pair it with, `docs/09`), matrix coefficients 0 and full range; any other code points are taken as sRGB with the note (the `png` crate itself refuses a `cICP` with another matrix or a limited range, and then the file is unreadable).

### 6.4 A photo in the filmstrip

- **It is rendered through its own current settings** to a display picture at the scene-picture size (§6.5): the same chain, the same Output, the same C13 space the photo of §5.1 uses, so no profile is involved. The result is a snapshot (§1.3).
- **The picture is held in the core, in memory, for the session**; it is the reduced pictures (§6.5), not the file and not a link. The page asks the core to set it and gets back a thumbnail and a short label.
- **Rules.** The photo being graded cannot be its own reference ("That's the photo you're grading. Choose another."). A photo that is still developing, pending or failed cannot be chosen (its tile is disabled with "This photo isn't ready yet."). The chooser is a mode like Pick colour: Esc ends it.

### 6.5 Letterbox, size and reduction

- **Letterbox removal: uniform bars only.** Rows and columns are examined **from each edge inward**. A row is part of a bar when the **standard deviation of its 8-bit codes (the three channels together) is below 2.0 and their mean is below 16**; a column is tested the same way between the bars already found at the top and bottom (a 16-bit picture is read by its 8-bit codes). The strict run is the lines from the edge up to the first that fails. **A bar is never more than 35 % of a side.** These two thresholds and the 35 % are the POC's (§16.5).
  - **A dark row is not a bar.** Night frames have many dark rows that are picture. The rule needs more than darkness; RG-05 adds, and §16.5 gives the numbers and where each came from: **(1) a step at the bar's inner edge**: within a **ringing margin of 16 lines** after the strict run, the picture's first line is the first whose mean is at least **16 codes** above the line before it (lines in the margin stay under a mean of 32); no such line, no bar. **(2) the ringing margin itself**: JPEG rings next to a hard edge and the last lines of a bar, up to one 4:2:0 block (16 lines), are no longer flat; the bar ends at the step, not at the strict run. **(3) a minimum width**: the bar, margin included, is at least **4 lines and 1 % of the side**. **(4) the 35 % limit is a rejection, not a cut**: a dark run longer than 35 % of a side is not a bar and nothing is removed from that side.
  - A picture in which every line qualifies has no bars removed and then fails the "empty" check below ("There's no picture inside the black bars.").
  - The card says "Black bars were removed from the reference." when it removed any.
  - **Limits** (§16.5): a bar that touches a sky as dark and as flat as itself, or a picture whose first line is under about 16 codes above the bar, is not told from the picture and is left (a few dark lines do the fit no harm); a window box whose side bars dilute the first line's mean is judged on the diluted mean.
- **Size.** After removal, the picture's **short side must be at least *N* = 256 pixels** or the reference is refused ("too small", §9.3; §16.6 says why 256). A picture with no area inside its bars is refused ("empty", §9.3).
- **Reduction.** A deterministic area average (a box filter with exact fractional weights) **in linear light**, in f64, to two pictures: the **scene picture** (long edge at most 1024 px) that the segmenter and MediaPipe read, and the **analysis picture** (long edge at most 512 px) that the fit measures (§5.1). A smaller picture is not enlarged. The core holds both for the session (§6.6), about 8 MB.

### 6.6 Copyright and what is never kept

References are supplied by the person, used at run time and never stored: **not in the settings, not in a preset or an album, not in a log, not in the XMP, not in a cache.** The core holds the reduced pictures and the scene made from them in memory for the session and drops it when the node is deleted, the photo is closed or the app quits. A film still is usually copyrighted; Yellowmoon reads it as the person's own tool does, and keeps no copy. **Evaluation stills are local only** (§10.1): never committed, never cached by CI, never uploaded.

## 7. Rendering

### 7.1 The plan and the CPU reference

- **The plan** (`docs/23` §5.2). The node is a corrector-like per-pixel step with a picture input, a key input and a key output. It fuses into the colour pass like a corrector's grade. `StepKind` gains `ReferenceGrade` (`ym_color::plan`, `pipeline::schedule::step_kind`; their exhaustive matches fail to compile without it): per pixel, reads the picture and the planes of the live elements. A step at its identity (every step at its identity after `effective`, §3.2) is skipped.
- **The CPU reference** is `ColorChain::run_nodes` calling `PreparedNode::apply` (`ym_color::display::node`): the corrector's steps in their order from `effective` values, then `hue::apply` in f64 (§4.2), then the element grades in f64 (§4a.2) through the planes, then the node's mask blend. `color_plan` fills `trim`, `grade`, `warp` and `curves` through `grade_params` as Colour Uniform's does, plus the baked hue tables, the element parameters and the element planes (§4a.3). `nodes_key` (`pipeline/color.rs`) adds the node's stored values and `strength` to the key, or an edit would not invalidate the frame; the element planes' key is the photo's scene key (the segmenter's input and rules version) and the radius.
- **Show mask, thumbnails, histogram, scatter, pixel readout, ancestors, dead nodes**: as a corrector's. The node's thumbnail is the picture after it.

### 7.2 The device: the least change

The colour kernel binds all 8 storage buffers (K3: 8 storage buffers and 1 uniform; `docs/17`). **No binding is added.** The llvmpipe rule (`docs/17`, *A lavapipe miscompile on AMD Zen 4 and 5*, `docs/STATUS.md`: the 2026-11-20 and 2026-11-21 miscompiles of `run_nodes` after small control-flow changes) applies in full: **change `run_nodes`, `node_apply` and `node_mask` as little as possible, prefer host packing, and pass `diag-alf` before a release.** The design:

1. **The hue tables are packed by the host into binding 2**, the curve-table buffer (K3 is full, so nothing new is bound). Today binding 2 holds, in plan order, four 1024-sample `f32` tables for every node that runs a curve, and a record names its first table. **After all of the curve tables**, the host appends **two tables of 1024 `f32`** (`hue_vs_hue` in radians, `hue_vs_sat` as a multiplier) for each live Reference Grade node whose hue curves are not the identity, in plan order. Because they come last, **no existing node's table offset moves and the buffer's bytes for every existing graph are unchanged.** `ChainTables::prepare` writes them from the f64 tables of §4.2, rounded once to `f32`.
2. **One record flag and one row.** A new bit of `ctl.x`, **`N_HUE`**, set by the host for a live node with a non-identity hue step. The first hue table's index is written to a row the node's kind never uses: **`R_HUE` = `R_JOIN + J_IN`, lane x** (the mixers' rows; a Reference Grade node is neither a mixer nor a Colour Uniform node, and `docs/32` §12a.4 already moved the pull's rows there). The constant is written by the host from `offset_of!(NodeRecU, join)`; **the record stays 1328 bytes**, `REC_ROWS` is unchanged, and a test pins the rows and the size.
3. **The fewest new branches.** The kernel gains **one `if` on `N_HUE`** and, inside the new function, **no branch on the data**: the hue is read as `h = select(0.0, atan2(q, p), r > 0.0)` (a `select`, the shape that fixed the Blur node's miscompile), so the step has no `r == 0` branch. The step is: `p`, `q`, `r`; `h`; two table reads with linear interpolation and wrap-around; one `sincos`; the rotation and scale; the add back along `e1` and `e2`; and the luma restoration, **three multiply-adds** for `Y(v)` and again for `Y(v')` and one add to the three codes (the owner's answer to question 13, §4.2). It resembles the Colour Uniform pull (one `atan2`, one `sincos`, one square root; `docs/32` §9), without `exp2` or `log2`.
4. **The shape, in order of preference** (each tried on the same runners; the first that passes is built):
   - **H1 (recommended): an early return, the shape of W1** (`docs/32` §12a.4, `docs/17`). Below the `N_SKIP` return in `node_apply`: `if ((bits & (N_HUE | N_ELEM)) != 0u) { return rg_apply(ni, v_in, bits); }`, and `rg_apply` is a **copy** of `cu_graded_apply` with the pull replaced by the hue step at the end: trims, C9, C9b and C11, then `hue_node`, then the element step (item 5). **The body of `node_apply` below the early return, `run_nodes`, `node_mask` and the mask code are byte-identical to main**, so every other node runs the code it did. This is the shape that passed on all four runners for the graded Colour Uniform node; it costs a second copy of the four steps in WGSL, kept honest by the parity test.
   - **H2 (built): a block last in `node_apply`**, after the Colour Uniform pull, in the shape of `uniform_node`'s block, guarded by `(bits & (N_HUE | N_ELEM)) != 0u` and calling `rg_tail`. Smaller, but it changes `node_apply` itself. H1 failed `diag-alf` (see *As built*); H2 passed.
   - **H3: refuse on the device.** If neither passes, the device refuses a Reference Grade node with non-identity hue curves or element grades (`InvalidInput`, which the render takes as it takes any refusal: the CPU makes the frame and says why, `RenderReport::cpu_reason`). The node, the fit and the Hue tab stay; the owner decides whether a slow node is acceptable. The remedy order is that of the 2026-11-21 round.
5. **The element grades** (§4a). The same early return carries them. For each live node, for each element whose effective grade (§3.2) is not the identity: four `f32` (the exposure shift in LogC4 code, the `cos` and `sin` of the rotation, the chroma ratio; the subject has its one), written by the host into binding 2 **after the hue tables**, so again no existing offset moves; a second record flag, **`N_ELEM`**, in `ctl.x`, and the first block's index in lane y of the row `R_HUE`; the last block of `node_apply` tests `(bits & (N_HUE | N_ELEM))`. An element's soft mask is a **raster plane** of the window (binding 7, `docs/17` §11a, the mechanism of the Brush row), `u16`, quantised by the same rule on the CPU reference so that the two read the same numbers. The step is a short loop inside `elem_node`, in the order of §2.3, whose bound is a host integer, with no branch on pixel data beyond the plane read. **RG-08 settles the packing, the plane table and the record's rows, and states them in `docs/17` §11a;** if a row or a binding cannot be found without changing the record, H3 is the fallback (the device refuses the element step) and the owner is asked.

**As built (RG-08, after the `diag-alf` round of 2026-11-26).** Variant **H2** is built: `node_apply` takes one more argument, `rpix`, for the planes, and has **no early return for the node**; its last block, after the Colour Uniform pull, is `if ((bits & (N_HUE | N_ELEM)) != 0u) { v = rg_tail(ni, v, bits, rpix); }`, and `rg_tail` runs `hue_node` then `elem_node`. A Reference Grade node runs the corrector's four steps in `node_apply`'s own body, as any node does. **Variant H1 (an early return into `rg_apply`, a copy of the four steps) was built first and failed `diag-alf`:** run 45801153156 (commit f84e41f9) had `gpu_uniform` fail 3/3 on AMD EPYC 9V74 and 9V45 (passing on EPYC 7763 and two Intel Xeons), in `a_pull_with_each_grade_step_matches_the_cpu`, F16Native storage only, step "pull and grade" (C10 3.38e-1, C13 9.57e-1, 6,887 pixels moved), and on no other binary. **The cause is the llvmpipe miscompile of 2026-11-20 and 2026-11-21 again, not the shared row:** a Colour Uniform node never has `N_HUE` or `N_ELEM` (the host writes `R_HUE` only for a node with a Reference Grade step, and `the_reference_step_lives_in_one_mixer_row_of_the_record` pins the row), so no Reference Grade datum is read on that path; what changed is the code around it. The failure is a property of the compiled module and the CPU (the same numbers on both AMD types, none on an Intel AVX-512 Xeon, none locally on an Intel Sapphire Rapids host with Mesa 25.2 and LLVM 20, with or without `LP_NATIVE_VECTOR_WIDTH` 128 and 512 and with the host CPU name overridden to `znver4`, `znver5` and `skylake-avx512` by an `LD_PRELOAD` of `llvm::sys::getHostCPUName`). It was **not reproduced locally**; the fix was found by the remedy order of this section and confirmed on the runners. **H2 passed `diag-alf` run 45818984974 (commit 9e3d1f22): every cell 0/3 on all 8 jobs** (`docs/STATUS.md` has the CPU list). The packing, the plane table and the record's row are unchanged by the fix and are in `docs/17` section 11a. The element planes are made **on the device** by the raster pass (the scene's bits uploaded once as u16 planes, the guide by a second colour pass and a small op, the rows' own guided-filter ops), not uploaded from the host: the host has no sampled picture where the export keeps the camera tile on the device. The element planes are made **on the device** by the raster pass (the scene's bits uploaded once as u16 planes, the guide by a second colour pass and a small op, the rows' own guided-filter ops), not uploaded from the host: the host has no sampled picture where the export keeps the camera tile on the device. ### 7.3 Parity and the release gate

- **A CPU f64 reference and a parity test** (`crates/ym-gpu/tests/gpu_reference_grade.rs`): the device against `PreparedNode::apply` at **2e-4 in LogC4** (the bound of `docs/02`), and one 10-bit code after C12 and C13; the identity cases (empty curves, Strength 0, a node with no hue step) **exact**. Cases, on lavapipe and on the Mac GPU CI (Metal, fast-math, K8): `hue_vs_hue` alone, `hue_vs_sat` alone, both, with trims, grade and curves, each element alone and all seven, planes of mask 0, partial and 1, a pixel in two elements, a pixel at `r = 0` and near grey, colours at 0°, 60° and 359.99° (the seam), negative codes, a node after a node, a node with a mask. The measured worst error is written into `docs/STATUS.md`; none is claimed here.
- **K8 (Metal's fast-math).** Nothing is decided by a rounding that changes the result beyond the gate: the `select` on `r > 0` chooses between results that agree to rounding near `r = 0`, because the step scales and rotates a vector of length `r`. The flag, the row and the table index are integers written by the host.
- **`diag-alf` must pass before any release that contains the change.** RG-08 runs the workflow on the four GitHub runner types (AMD EPYC 9V74 and 9V45, EPYC 7763 and the Xeon Platinum 8573C), comparing `main`'s commit with the RG-08 commit, counting the failures of `gpu_alf_look`, `gpu_masks`, `gpu_chain`, the graded Colour Uniform test and the new `gpu_reference_grade` in three runs each. **The gate is 0 failures on every runner**, reported in `docs/STATUS.md` as the earlier rounds were. The Mac GPU CI runs the same suites. A failed gate is reported, never loosened.
- **Speed.** The hue step's cost per megapixel is measured in RG-08 on lavapipe and, as a Mac figure, in RG-H3; the starting budget is the Colour Uniform pull's (`docs/32` §9), which the step resembles. No figure is claimed here.

## 8. `ym`

- **The flags.** `ym render <photo> --reference <file> [--reference-strength <0–100>] [--no-skin-protection] [--reference-node <id>]` and `[--reference-brightness <0–100>]`, which sets `match_brightness` (RG-16, §5.8). The flags set fields of the node and run the fit; they are not new settings. The strength is in percent, as `--intensity <0–100>` is (`docs/10`); it sets `strength`.
- **What the fit does.** `--reference` fits **exactly as the app does** (§5): the same analysis size, loader, ICC reader, letterbox rule, scene model, network, chain and search. A graph with **one** Reference Grade node is fitted; a graph with **none** gets one added after Main (as Add node does in a default graph); with **several**, `--reference-node <id>` is required (exit 2: "The graph has more than one Reference Grade node. Say which with --reference-node."). The fit overwrites the node's fitted fields as Fit again does (§3.3). With several photos on the command line each is fitted to the same reference.
- **Bit-identity.** For **the same reference picture**, the same photo, settings and version, `ym` and the app write the same fitted numbers and so the same bytes (rule 6). **An export from the app pins the fitted values in `ym:Settings`**, like the Auto answers (`docs/19`, `docs/22`), so `ym render photo --settings exported.xmp` (with no `--reference`) renders the same bytes as the app's export. A reference from the filmstrip is a display picture held in memory, not a file, so `ym` cannot take it; the pinned settings reproduce its fit.
- **The scene model.** `ym` runs the segmenter and MediaPipe as the app does (§4b). A graph whose Reference Grade node has element grades, or a `--reference` fit, needs the scene model; without it `ym` stops with `error.kind` `mask` and the sentence "Node 3 has element grades, and the scene model is missing from this install. Reinstall Yellowmoon." (the node's number filled in).
- **As built (RG-09).** The flags are `--reference`, `--reference-strength` (the spec's name; `--strength` was not taken, so as not to be read as the look's), `--no-skin-protection` and `--reference-node`, the last three only with `--reference` (a usage error from the parser otherwise). The node is added by `NodeGraph::insert_reference_grade_after_main` (the rule of `insertAfter` in `src/state/graphOps.ts`: the next id, one unit right of Main, into the link that leads to the Output), and the fit is `Core::reference_choose_file` and `Core::reference_fit`, the commands of the app. The exit codes and the errors are in `docs/10` (*Reference grade*); the report is the `reference` object of `--json`. A reference that cannot be used, a missing file included ("The reference can't be used: this file was not found. Check the path and try again."), is checked **before any photo is opened**; the choice of node is checked per photo, after the settings are applied.
- **The reference is a file only.** Errors have the app's texts (§9.3) after "The reference can't be used:" and exit 2 as an unusable `--settings` file does. `ym render --json` and `ym info --json` print the node in `settings` as the document holds it, with no reference.
- **The help** names the kind in the closing paragraph ("other nodes (correctors, Colour Uniform nodes, Reference Grade nodes, Blur nodes, mixers, …)") and `docs/10` quotes it and gains a *Reference Grade* section and the example of §3.1 as a JSON block that `crates/ym-cli/tests/docs.rs` reads and validates. The snapshot `cli__help_render` changes with it. **RG-02 changes the paragraph and the snapshot with the kind (the docs test compares them); RG-09 adds the section and the example.** Until RG-09 no CLI example of this node is in `docs/10`, so `crates/ym-cli/tests/docs.rs` has no command to run against unbuilt code.
- **Version.** `ym --version`, `ym doctor` and `ym info` print the new pipeline version from RG-02 (§3.8).

### 10.1 The evaluation tool (RG-10)

The POC's evaluation harness is rebuilt in the repository as **`tools/eval/refgrade`** (Rust, a binary that links the core; it never runs in the app or `ym`). It is the measure every change to the network or the objective is judged by. It reads a **local evaluation set of public raw photos and reference stills, never committed** (`testdata/`, git-ignored; its sources are not named in the repository, and the tool takes the set's description from a local `split.json`). It reports **harms** and **look transfer**.

**The set.** `split.json` lists each photo with its raw path, its set (`dev` or `test`) and content tags (people, landscape, city, interiors, hard light, colourful). The POC's set was 130 public photos, 56 in dev and 74 held out in test, each fitted to 13 reference stills (728 and 962 fits). The reference stills are copyrighted pictures the owner and the repository keep local (§6.6); they are used for evaluation only, never committed or cached by CI, never used for any shipped number. **The owner's own photos sit in the held-out set and are never looked at while designing** ("I won't let you tune specifically for my photos"); the tool gives only aggregate numbers on the held-out set, and a seeded random sample of 12 photos (seed `20261124`) for the owner's eyes.

**The working rule** (the owner, 2026-11-24): *no rule targets a photo.* Every change to the network or the objective states a general grading principle ("film grades don't turn content colours into purple"), is found on dev, and is judged on the held-out set. A held-out set used three times (round 15 used the POC's third check) is spent: later rounds are checked on a **fresh** set (RG-13).

**Harms** (per fit, on the full renders of the photo's default and the fit, in display sRGB *(from RG-21, in Display P3, for the baseline and the new fit alike, §5.9.5)*; C\* and L\* in CIELAB, hue in **Oklab**; deliberately different from the walls so a fit cannot pass them just by satisfying its own walls):

| ID | Harm | Flag when |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Purple turn | the share of non-skin pixels with fit C\* ≥ 5 and Oklab hue in 300° to 355°, where the photo's C\* was < 2 or its hue differs by ≥ 15°, is > 2 % of the frame, and the reference's own share (Oklab 300° to 355°, C\* > 8) is < 5 % |
| H2 | Skin colour | skin ≥ 1 % of the frame, and the skin's Oklab mean-vector hue moves by > 10° (only tested when its mean-vector C\* is at least 4), or its mean-vector chroma ratio (fit over photo) is > 1.25 or < 0.55 |
| H3 | Memory colour | sky (or vegetation) ≥ 2 % of the photo and < 2 % of the reference, and its Oklab mean-vector hue moves by > 15° (a sky with median L\* under 20 and a group with mean-vector C\* under 4 are not tested) |
| H4 | Tinted whites | pixels with the photo's L\* ≥ 80 and C\* < 8 cover ≥ 1 % of the frame, and their fit mean C\* is > max(8, the reference's own mean C\* on its whites + 4) |
| H5 | Clipping | the fit's share of L\* > 98, or of L\* < 2, exceeds max(photo share, reference share) by > 3 points |
| H6 | Colour reversal | the share of non-skin pixels with the photo's C\* ≥ 10 whose Oklab hue turns by > 60° is > 3 % of the frame |

**Look transfer** (a fit that does nothing passes every harm check, so how much of the look arrived is measured too):

| ID | Metric | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Colour transfer | `1 − SW(fit, ref) / SW(photo, ref)`; `SW` is the sliced Wasserstein-1 distance between the (a\*, b\*) distributions, 4096 seeded pixel samples per picture, 32 fixed directions. 1 is a matched palette, 0 no change, below 0 further away |
| T2 | Tone transfer | the same form on the key-preserving tone shape `(p98 − p2, (p50 − p2) / (p98 − p2))` of L\*, scaled by 1/50 and 1 before the L1 distance. The key stays with the photo |
| T3 | Cast transfer | `1 − \|cast(fit) − cast(ref)\| / \|cast(photo) − cast(ref)\|`, the cast being the mean (a\*, b\*) of non-skin pixels with L\* 15 to 80 |

Where a denominator is under 1 (under 0.01 for T2), that metric is skipped for the fit. The tool also reports **E2**, the key-preserving CIEDE2000 (`crates/ym-color/src/deltae.rs`), for photos with labels, and the **fit time**.

**The acceptance rules** (the owner's, 2026-11-25), for a new version against a baseline on a held-out set, over all of its fits:

1. The share of fits with any harm **falls** (for RG-10, against the POC's round 15: does not rise; **the owner's decision of 2026-11-26: for the port check the share of fits with any harm is judged like each single harm, so it may rise by at most 1 point**; the mended dev run, 8.2 % to 8.4 %, passes).
2. No single harm's share of fits **rises by more than 1 point**.
3. Mean T1, T2 and T3 each **fall by no more than 0.01, measured on the fits the baseline left harm-free.** Look a baseline got by doing harm is not a loss. The all-fits reading is reported beside it.

If (1) and (2) hold but (3) fails, the round is not accepted as is; the next step is an ablation on dev to find which principle costs transfer, **not** a retune on the held-out set.

**Outputs**: `metrics.json` (per fit: every harm value and flag, T1 to T3, the time), `summary.json` and `summary.md` (the shares of fits flagged per harm and for any, the share of photos with a flagged fit, the means and medians of T1 to T3, a per-reference table so references that cause harm stand out, per-tag tables), the dev grids (references, the previous round, this round), the owner's sample for the held-out set, and `--compare` for two runs. Threads: each fit stays single-threaded and deterministic; the set runs on a pool.

**As built (RG-10).** The tool is the workspace crate `tools/eval/refgrade` (package `refgrade-eval`, binary `refgrade-eval`; test tooling, never shipped, a dependent of `ym-core`; no new dependency). It runs **the product's fit through the core's own API**, not a copy of it: for each photo it opens a core as the app does (the photo's own settings from the session, a private app folder), adds one Reference Grade node between Main and the Output **by the patch the app sends for Add node** (`add_node_patch`: the Output keeps the look the photo opened with; a graph built by hand lost it once, §16.10.1), loads each still with `Core::reference_choose_file` (RG-05's loader), fits with `Core::reference_fit` (RG-06), and undoes the fit's patch before the next reference, so every fit starts from the photo as the person has it. The measures are made on **every pixel of the analysis picture** (512 px on the long edge), not on the fit's sample: `Core::reference_eval_photo` (the fit's own picture, chain, scene and soft masks with the whole frame in place of the 8192 sample pixels) and `Core::reference_measured` (the held reference's pixels and scene, as the fit measures them); the fit's numbers are rendered through the same candidate renderer the search uses (`ChainCandidates`), so a measured picture is the picture the fit judged. The first fit of each photo is also rendered through the real pipeline (`advisor_picture`, whole, 8-bit) and its means of L\*, a\* and b\* and its mean CIE76 distance at matching places are recorded in the record's `audit` field.

- **Commands.** `refgrade-eval run --split split.json --references DIR --out DIR [--set dev|test|all] [--shard i/n] [--threads n] [--only stems] [--max-photos n] [--resume]` writes `records.jsonl` as it goes (one fit per line), `failures.jsonl`, `masks.jsonl`, `progress.txt` and, at the end, the shard's `metrics.json`, `summary.json`, `summary.md` and a `DONE` file; `refgrade-eval merge --out DIR RUN...` joins shards (killed ones too: it reads `records.jsonl`), writes the same three files and the mask summary `masks.md` and `masks.json`; `refgrade-eval compare --baseline A --new B [--set dev] [--out DIR]` prints and writes `compare.md` and `compare.json`, and **exits with 3 when a line fails** (rule 1 read as "does not rise", the reading of RG-10); `refgrade-eval sample --split split.json` lists the seeded 12 held-out photos for the owner; `refgrade-eval renders` writes each photo's default picture as a png for a side-by-side check. Run it as `YELLOWMOON_DATA=... target/release/refgrade-eval ...` (release builds: a fit takes minutes in debug).
- **The records** are the proof of concept's (`photo`, `set`, `tags`, `primary_tag`, `reference`, `fit_seconds`, `harms` with `value` and `flag`, `any_harm`, `T1`, `T2`, `T3`, `search_start`), so a file of round 15 and a file of the product compare directly; the product's adds `objective`, `identity`, `evals`, `scene_seconds` (the photo's scene, once per photo), `prep_seconds` (the fit's own preparation: the photo's picture, the reference's scene and the measures) and `audit`. `fit_seconds` is the whole call of `reference_fit`, the reference's scene included (the core holds a reference per node and the tool clears it after each fit, so every fit pays for it: the cost a person pays for a first fit with a new reference).
- **Shards and determinism.** A shard takes the photos whose position in the chosen list is *i* modulo *n*, with its own core and *t* threads; the fit gives the same bits on any thread count (§5.7), so the shards, the thread count and the order do not change a record. The merge sorts by (set, photo, reference).
- **`compare`** pairs the fits of two files by (photo, reference) and gives the three lines (rule 1 strict and as RG-10 reads it; rule 2 per harm; rule 3 on the baseline's harm-free fits, each transfer averaged over the fits with a value in both runs), the all-fits reading beside it, the harm shares and the flips, the share of fits whose T1, T2, T3 (and T1 with T3) are within 0.02 of the baseline's, the 10 fits that differ most (the larger of the T1 and T3 differences), per-tag aggregates and the times.
- **Mask check** (`masks.jsonl`): the product's class plane at the analysis picture against the label map of the same photo resized to it (nearest), per photo and over all: the equal share of the 10-element map, and for sky, vegetation, water, ground, built and person the agreement of membership and the IoU. The POC's skin and subject masks were made at run time by its own rules and were never cached, so they cannot be compared.
- **Not built.** The dev grids and the owner's grids (the tool writes no pictures; the sample's names are listed, the renders are for a later task); E2 (in the POC it is the cross-content study `--e2` with synthetic grades, not a per-fit record); a per-fit denominator log.
- **Tests** (31 unit tests, no data): each harm on hand-made pictures (the 2 %, 1 %, 3 %, 15°, 10°, 0.55 and 1.25 lines, the dark-sky and grey-skin exemptions, the reference's licences), the transfer metrics (a fit that does nothing gives 0, one that is the reference gives 1, one that goes away goes below 0, the skipped denominators), the seeded sample and the distance, the merge in any order and with a cut line, the three rules on constructed runs (the clean-fits rule against the all-fits reading, a loss of exactly 0.01 passes), the split and the owner's sample, the label resize, the agreement, the sharding; and in `ym-core` the evaluation inputs against the fit's own (`reference_fit.rs`).

**Data rules.**

- **Public data only for choosing constants** (§16). Owner's photos are a held-out check at most, reported and not used to tune (`docs/32` §2.3).
- **The evaluation set and its stills are local only**: never committed, never cached by CI, never uploaded.
- **No measurement of our own is needed**; if a gate seems to need one, use public data and note the limitation in `docs/STATUS.md` (`AGENTS.md`, V1 data rule).

## 12. Risks

| Risk | What happens | What the design does |
|---|---|---|
| **Content mismatch** | A night reference could darken a beach; a reference full of one colour pulls the whole photo | the network keeps the photo's median, matches shape and not level, and drops exposure relations under a key mismatch (§4b.5); the content turn budget and the clip term (§5.4); **Strength** scales the result; the status "Nothing was changed" when the fit does not help |
| **Look dependence** | A fit is valid for one Output look | the fit runs the real chain (§2.2); changing the look marks the fit stale and shows a hint; nothing is re-fitted by itself |
| **Colour tagging of references** | An untagged file is a guess (sRGB); a wrong guess shifts the whole fit | tags are read (§6.3); the note when a profile cannot be read; Strength; the person can see the thumbnail. The POC read 8-bit sRGB only (§16.9) |
| **Fit time** | About 30 s on one Linux thread; unmeasured on a Mac | progress and Cancel, the remedies of §5.7, RG-11, RG-H3 |
| **The scene model is wrong** | A sky called water, foliage called ground, a missing person, a mask that bleeds | a wrong element only changes where a grade acts; the walls watch hue drift, memory colours and skin by measuring the picture, not the label; the hard cases are named in the model card; the guided filter takes the masks to the picture's edges |
| **Skin** | A global grade can push skin toward the reference's cast; skin was 7.9 % of held-out fits flagged in round 15 | the two skin walls and the skin element's caps (§5.4, §4a.1); Strength and the switch; **RG-13 is the quality round on it** |
| **Tinted whites** | White surfaces take the reference's cast; 7.8 % of held-out fits flagged in round 15 | the whites walls (§5.4); RG-13 |
| **Local looks** | Vignettes, a graded sky gradient, split looks in one half cannot be copied | out of scope (§13); the elements cover sky, foliage, water, ground, built, skin and the subject only; the status never promises more; masks and the person's hand cover some |
| **Spatial nodes after the node** | Film, Blur and Face Refinement are not run in the fit | a note on the card; the golden entry has none after the node; question 7 |
| **The kernel change** | llvmpipe has miscompiled small control-flow changes in `run_nodes` | §7: host packing, one `if`, `select`, H1 first, `diag-alf` before release, H3 as the last resort. **No release contains the kernel change until `diag-alf` is clean** |
| **Table resolution** | Hue tables of 1024 samples | the error against the exact spline is measured in RG-02 and stated |
| **Hue step and luma** | The step restores the Rec.709 luma of the LogC4 triple after the move, as the parametric look and the POC's step do (§4.2) | the owner's answer, 2026-11-25: "Restore brightness." (§15, decision 19); closed |
| **Memory** | Seven full-size planes at 24 MP | planes only for live elements, the memory ledger, tiles with a halo (§4a.3) |
| **A hand-written network is not tuned to every picture** | A weight is wrong for a kind of photo | the evaluation tool finds it (§10.1); a weight changes only by a spec change under the acceptance rules; never for the owner's photos |
| **The fit judges a picture the person does not see** | The fit measures sRGB display codes clamped to 0 to 1; the Mac viewport shows Display P3, and for strong reds the two pictures differ (§5.9 *Why*) | RG-19 to RG-21: the fit judges the Display P3 render, fixed, whatever the viewport or the export (§5.9); the evaluation measures both fits on the P3 picture; the network and the constants are kept and checked, not tuned (§5.9.3, §5.9.4) |
| **The held-out set is spent** | Three checks were made on the POC's | later rounds use a fresh set (RG-13) |
| **Reference copyright** | A film still is copyrighted | used at run time, never stored (§6.6); evaluation stills local only |

## 13. Not in this version

- **A learned grading model.** The network is hand-written. A trained predictor, or any model that sets targets or settings, is out; only if the owner asks and with a budget.
- **Spatial or local adaptation beyond the seven element grades** (region-aware fits, a bilateral grid pass, gradients). It would cost a binding, the `diag-alf` gate again and a new pipeline step.
- **Any provider's involvement**: Claude, Antigravity and Apple Intelligence receive no reference and write no numbers here (`docs/22`).
- **HEIC and other formats; RAW files as references; a reference from the web or a URL.**
- **Hue curves and element grades on correctors and Colour Uniform nodes.**
- **Batch matching** (apply one reference to a selection in one step). Each photo is fitted on its own node; a preset or Paste carries the values.
- **Matching inside the node's masks, or a reference region.**
- **Choosing a network weight by looking at the owner's photos** (§10.1).
- **Our own measurements of any kind** (V2).

## 16. POC findings

The POC is the Rust example `crates/ym-core/examples/refgrade_poc.rs` (with `refgrade_poc/eval.rs`) on the branch `refgrade-poc`, built on the real colour chain (`ColorChain`, `ParametricLook`, the looks' library, the MediaPipe segmenter). It ran on the CPU on **Linux**, one thread per fit, on a **local evaluation set of public raw photos and reference stills, never committed** (§10.1). The scene labels were made once by the authors' EoMT checkpoint in Python and cached as label maps the POC read; the POC did not run a segmenter of its own (§16.9). Every number below is the POC's; where this file gives a number the POC did not measure, it says so. **Round numbers** are the POC's: rounds 2 to 5 replaced the first draft's quantile terms by a scene-aware objective and then by the network; rounds 6 to 10 added guards against the harms the owner saw; rounds 11 to 15 are measured on the harms and transfer of §10.1.

### 16.1 The parameter list and the knots

49 normalised parameters, 30 global and 19 local, exactly as §5.2: luma curve at the stops −6, −4, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 4 (8); red, green and blue at the stops −3, 0, 3 (9); saturation (1); `hue_vs_sat` and `hue_vs_hue` at 0°, 60°, … 300° (6 each); per element exposure, hue and chroma (6 × 3); the subject's exposure (1). Ranges: the first luma knot ±2 stops, a luma segment's log slope ±1.1, a channel knot ±1 stop, saturation ±60, `hue_vs_sat` 1 ± 0.7, `hue_vs_hue` ±30°, local exposure ±1 stop, local hue ± the element's cap (40, 35, 35, 30, 25, 15), local chroma ratio 0.5 to 1.6. The regulariser scale is the range itself (parameters are normalised). **The draft's list (30 numbers, a luma curve on fixed scene stops about middle grey, three-knot channel curves, six hue knots) held; the 19 local numbers are new.**

### 16.2 Analysis sizes and sampling

The photo's node picture is made at 1600 px on the long edge and reduced to **512 px** (area average, linear light) for the fit; the **sample is 8192 pixels** (a 128 × 64 grid, one jittered pixel per cell, SplitMix64 seed `0x5EED_2026_1124`); the reference is reduced to **512 px** (never enlarged) and measured on all of its pixels. The area average is a box filter with exact fractional weights, f64 accumulation. The soft masks were made at the fit size and at the 1600 px render size, with the same relative radius.

### 16.3 The objective

Rounds 2 to 5 replaced the first draft's six terms by the network's targets (§4b.5) and the look terms of §5.3; rounds 6 to 15 added the walls of §5.4 one by one, each against a harm the owner or the dev set showed. **Weights, scales and dead bands are in §5.3 and §5.4 and come from the POC's code.** The weights were chosen by hand from development reference stills and the public photos: no weight was changed by looking at the owner's photos or at the held-out stills. A sliced Wasserstein term was not used in the objective (it is the transfer metric T1 of the evaluation, §10.1). The POC's notes and its code differed in a few constants; **§5 follows the code** (wall limits as network outputs, the skin wall weight 12, the single polish pass, the cast measured on L\* 15 to 80 and not on skin).

### 16.4 The optimiser

Golden-section coordinate search, stage table and counts of §5.5, a start-inclusive best-of so a step never raises the objective, the need test for local grades, a joint polish, and round 15's **two starts**, keeping the lower full objective and the neutral search on a tie. No randomness beyond the sample; the seed is the sample's.

### 16.5 Letterbox constants

The POC's rule, from each edge inward: a row (or a column, between the bars found so far) is part of a bar when the **standard deviation of its 8-bit codes is below 2.0 and their mean is below 16**, over the three channels; a bar is at most **35 %** of a side. It was used on reference stills with black bars. **Not covered by the POC:** a minimum bar width, a step test at the bar's inner edge, a JPEG-ringing margin, and night frames with many dark rows. **RG-05 sets them (2026-11-25), from public data and the local evaluation pictures, and `ym_color::letterbox` holds them:**

|---|---|---|
| a bar line's deviation / mean | below **2.0** / below **16** | the POC |
| the longest bar | **35 %** of a side; **a longer dark run is not a bar and nothing is cut from that side** (a cut to 35 % would take picture) | the POC's number; the rejection is RG-05's reading |
| the shortest bar | **4 lines and 1 %** of the side (`max`) | below four lines is a frame edge, not a bar; 1 % of 1080 is 11 lines |
| the ringing margin | **16 lines** after the strict run | JPEG at quality 30 to 95, 4:4:4 and 4:2:0, a bright edge against black at any alignment: the strict run ended **up to 14 lines** before the edge at 4:2:0 and **7** at 4:4:4 (one 8 x 8 block); one 4:2:0 block is 16 |
| the lines in the margin | mean **below 32** | the ripples' mean was at most 11.5 codes (quality 30, a white edge); 4:2:0 colour bleed adds more on a saturated edge |

A bar against a picture whose first line is at least about 16 codes brighter is found |
| a flat dark picture | every line is a bar line: nothing is cut, and the loader says "There's no picture inside the black bars." | §6.5 |

**What it was tested on.** (1) Synthetic pictures in the repository (`ym_color::letterbox` and `crates/ym-core/tests/reference_loading.rs`): a letterbox and a pillarbox and a window box of several widths, trimmed **exactly** (the analysis picture has exactly the picture's size) in PNG, TIFF and JPEG; JPEG at quality 30, 50, 75 and 90, 4:4:4 and 4:2:0, with bar edges at six alignments; **a night frame** (a noisy sky whose mean climbs from 0.3 to 14 over 180 rows, 180 of which pass the POC's two tests, and a lit town), left whole in PNG, JPEG and TIFF and turned a quarter; a dark edge that fades in 24 columns; a flat dark picture; a dark run over 35 %; slivers; a bar next to a picture as dark as itself. (2) **195 local pictures, never committed** (50 stills of five films with no bars, 26 archive stills, 5 more stills, 114 renders of public CC0 raw photographs; short sides 436 to 2160 px; `crates/ym-core/tests/reference_local.rs`, `YM_REFERENCE_PICTURES`): **none loses a line** (0 of 195), where the POC's two thresholds alone would have cut dark runs of up to 277 columns or 193 rows from 29 sides of them. The same 195 with synthetic bars added (12.5 % top and bottom, 8 % left and right, and both) and re-encoded as JPEG at quality 60, 4:2:0: **396 of 585 (68 %) are trimmed to exactly the picture; the 189 that are not keep a bar or two on a side whose picture edge is very dark** (the first lines of a space frame have means under 5 and deviations under 5: there is no step to find). They are left whole, never cut into.

**The limits, kept on purpose.** A reference that is letterboxed and whose picture meets the bar at a sky as dark and as flat as the bar keeps that bar; a window box's side bars dilute the mean of the first line and a dark one can miss the step; a picture whose bars are at code 16 or more (limited-range black) is not under the POC's mean of 16 and is not taken for one. A bar left in the picture is a band of deep shadow in the fit's statistics; the card does not say so, because the rule cannot tell. A dark edge that is cut costs more than a bar that is left (the first is picture, the second is a few per cent of black pixels), and so the rule leans to leaving. RG-10 can measure the fit's own sensitivity to a left bar on the evaluation set and the owner can move the step; it is a constant (`STEP_MEAN_CODES`) and the numbers above are what to move it against.

### 16.6 The minimum reference size

The POC accepted every size and never enlarged one. **N is 256 pixels on the short side**, after the bars are cut (§6.5, RG-05, 2026-11-25). It is set from public data without a fit (the fit is RG-06): the measures the fit takes of a reference are the **distribution** of its CIELAB values (the quantiles of L\* and C\*, the means of a\* and b\*; §5.1, §4b.4), so the question is how far those move when a picture is smaller. On 195 local pictures with a short side of at least 436 px (the set above), each reduced by the area average in linear light to a short side of *s* and compared with the 512 px analysis picture (the 1st, 5th, 25th, 50th, 75th, 95th and 99th percentiles of L\* and C\*, and the means of a\* and b\*; the worst of the 16 statistics of each picture, in L\*, C\* and a\*, b\* units):

| short side *s* | median picture | 95th percentile | worst picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 2.60 | 9.69 | 31.89 |
| 96 | 1.56 | 7.34 | 18.73 |
| 128 | 1.06 | 4.38 | 13.82 |
| 160 | 0.69 | 3.33 | 10.60 |
| 192 | 0.44 | 2.27 | 8.19 |
| **256** | **0.22** | **1.03** | 4.27 |
| 320 | 0.11 | 0.57 | 1.20 |

At 256 the 95th percentile of pictures is within about **one unit** of L\*, C\*, a\* and b\* (the median of the pictures' L\* 50th percentile moves 0.02), the 12-sector hue shares move at most 2.4 points; at 192 the 95th percentile is 2.3 and at 128 it is 4.4, which is the order of the walls' own tolerances (§5.4). Also, the segmenter works on a 640 px grid and reads the scene picture without enlargement: below 256 px its masks come from a picture it must upsample. **This is not the harm-rule criterion of RG-05's first draft** ("the smallest reference whose fits stay inside the harm rules"): that needs the fit, and RG-10 re-runs it on the evaluation set at 256, 192 and 128; the owner may move *N* then (`MIN_SHORT_SIDE_PX`). The script that made the table is a local one-off; the numbers above are its output.
