# 16 · Risks and open decisions

The biggest risk is the ALEXA target itself. Most open decisions are settled by M0 and M1 experiments. Record each decision here with its numbers when it's made.

## Risks

| Risk | Effect | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| ALEXA curve estimate is off by more than 5% | emulated colour drifts, mostly in saturated reds and greens | ensemble spread published; validate against the HdM and CIC 2015 curves if obtainable; swap in measured data if released |
| Far-red tail above 660 nm is unconstrained | skin and red fabrics under tungsten may render wrong | ensemble variants for the tail; targeted review of skin under tungsten in M1-H5 |
| Estimate pulled toward the stills-camera prior | real Sony-to-ALEXA differences understated | prior variants without Sony bodies (already within 2.6% RFSE); compare with ARRI-rendered sample footage offline |
| Light class hard to estimate from RGB alone | wrong map under LEDs and fluorescents | blend maps by class probability; light presets in the Colour tab; as-shot WB prior |
| Metal surface under a transparent Tauri webview | flicker, input routing or resize glitches | M0-06 spike; fallback: native child view |
| wgpu's Apple wide-gamut fixes unreleased in Oct 2026 | colour shifts in Display P3 output | pin the wgpu version; test P3 output in M0 against exported files |
| GPU limits on 50 MP frames | crashes or slow previews on smaller GPUs | tiled f16 pipeline; classical fallback; memory budget from device limits |
| burn API churn | rework on upgrades | ONNX models as the stable artefact; ort as fallback runtime |
| No GPU on CI runners | GPU code untested in CI | lavapipe job for correctness; Metal checks on a Mac before each gate |
| ARRI changes download URLs or objects to automated access | wizard breaks | import from file is the primary path; the wizard opens ARRI's page rather than deep links |
| Some Sony variants decode wrongly (older bodies, cRAW quirks, M/S) | wrong colour or artefacts | every corpus file checked against LibRaw in CI; clear message for anything unverified |
| ARW 6.0 codec: undocumented, hard-coded constants, possible Sony patents | A7 V and A7R VI compressed files don't open, or decode wrongly | ship last behind a feature flag; check against LibRaw master and lightcraft; ask Sony; clear message meanwhile |
| Lossless M/S files aren't Bayer | emulation is approximate | reduced path, flagged in the app |
| Noise estimate off on textured or filtered files | over- or under-smoothing | texture fallback, filtered-file check, bias toward over-estimating, σ jitter in training |
| Other apps render our DNGs differently | the look's tone differs outside Lightroom | look baked into pixels; full-size preview; per-app behaviour documented |
| Training compute unavailable | M2 slips | small models (hours on one GPU); Apple MPS fallback; classical path ships regardless |
| Few openly licensed A1 photos | golden set thin on scene and light variety | three licence tiers (`docs/test-photo-sources.md`); A1 II and A7R IV-family files as stand-ins |
| No controlled captures in V1 | illuminant, noise and lens accuracy on the A1 is validated only indirectly (synthetic, other cameras, oracles) | public-data gates per `docs/13`; noise biased toward over-estimating; captures in V2 tighten the A1 numbers |
| Updater signing key lost or leaked | installed copies can't update, or a third party could ship updates | key generated once, kept outside the repository, backed up in Mykyta's password manager; never logged |
| Yellow Moon Post Production (Northern Ireland) in a neighbouring field | naming dispute in the UK | clearance search before launch |
| Patent US 11948279 | claim against the joint demosaic+denoise network | FTO check before 1.0; classical path as a fallback |
| Patent leads for the raw algorithms (`research/raw-algorithms-prior-art.md` §2.2: Kodak, Epson, Sony, Apple, IBM families; diffraction restoration filters) | the adaptive ratio demosaic is the default and the diffraction and sharpening stages ship | FTO check before 1.0 (M4-H4); the survey records where each piece comes from; Menon stays selectable |
| GPU adaptive ratio demosaic is not bit-identical to the CPU port (f32 against f64 where a direction test ties) | an export on the GPU differs from the CPU fallback by a few isolated samples (worst 1.55e-2 of white on real mosaics) | exports stay byte-identical between the app and `ym` on one machine; golden hashes pin the CPU reference; GPU parity is judged against an f32 emulation of the oracle, not the CPU bound |

- 2026-10-19 · Colour warp: Range keeps its value (pipeline 0.4.4; 0.4.3 on its own branch, merged after the new contrast and saturation). The owner's report: "Colour warp range does not work, and a picked colour, even with range 1, affects most of the image." The cause was the rule that kept one pin from folding the chroma plane: a move longer than 1.5 ranges grew the range to |δ|/1.5, so with Range 1 (0.01) a drag of 0.1 reached 0.067 and most of a photo's colours followed. The fix keeps the range as set and moves each pixel along a flow instead of in one jump: in N steps (N = ⌈1.25 Σ|δ|/(ρ√e)⌉, 1 to 64, one number for the frame) each pin's Gaussian slides from its source by the share of the move the pixel takes, and the pixel follows. Each step is one to one, so one pin still never folds the plane, and its source still lands on its target; a colour three ranges beside or behind the source now moves by at most exp(−4.5) of the move. What a one-to-one map must do with the colours in the move's way it does: those ahead of the source within about a range of its line are swept along to just past the target, and a thin sliver just behind it is stretched along the path; in that sliver the GPU's f32 can differ from the CPU by more than the 2e-4 parity gate for the longest moves at the smallest ranges (`docs/02` C9b, STATUS). A move of up to 1.32 ranges takes one step and renders as before. The CPU reference, the WGSL kernel and the TypeScript port of the plane share the algorithm (`docs/02` C9b).

- 2026-10-20 · Remove from filmstrip, and Undo (the owner: "I also want to remove photos from the film strip."). Removing takes a photo out of the **session**, not off the disk: the filmstrip, picks, caches and edits go (nothing is saved per photo), the file is never touched (rules 4 and 5; a Rust test compares bytes and modification times), and the notice says so ("Removed 3 photos. The files are still on disk."). The lead's decisions, each the simplest reading that is safe: (1) **How**: a right-click menu on each thumbnail (glasscn's `ContextMenu`, taken from its registry like the other glass components) with *Remove from filmstrip* for the photo clicked and, while any photo is picked, *Remove picked photos*; and Delete or Backspace for the photo on screen, ignored in a text box, on a slider, in a menu or dialog, in Crop and Pick white and in the Export panel. No menu-bar item (a ⌫ key equivalent would take the key from text boxes on a Mac). (2) **No multi-selection**: the strip has a current photo and picks, and picks are marks for Export and "Apply to", so Delete removes only the photo on screen and several photos go only through the explicit menu item; a forgotten pick never turns a key press into a bulk removal. (3) **Exports in progress keep the photos they were started with and finish them**; refusing the removal was the alternative. The queue already holds snapshots and survives a closed session (`load_raw` falls back to the file, `wait_for_final_estimates` to the queued estimates), so keeping them needs no new rule and the person is never told no; the one consequence, written down: a job queued while its burst was still developing re-reads the shared estimates from the photos that are left. (4) **Undo is the last removal only**, kept in memory (the core holds the photos with their settings, picks, estimates and places; the page their undo histories) until the next removal or the end of the session, as the owner described; offered as an **Undo button in the notice** (the existing notice bar gains an optional action) and as **⌘Z** while the removal is the last thing done (the undo stack is per photo, so the page compares the number of undo steps with the number at the removal; after a new change ⌘Z undoes that change first). Photos come back at their old places; the photo that was on screen is on screen again unless the person has moved on; a file added again meanwhile stays out so that no file is twice in the strip. (5) **The burst and the pools follow the set**: the median exposure of the burst frames that stay and the pooled noise of their group are made again from the photos that are left (they are functions of the set of photos), those whose estimates moved get `EstimatesChanged` and a new thumbnail, and Undo makes them again with the photos back.
