# 19 · Apple Auto

On Macs where Apple Intelligence's on-device model can look at a photo, that model grades each photo: it sets Auto's exposure, highlights, shadows, black level, contrast and saturation, nudges the estimated white warmer or cooler and greener or more magenta, and colours the shadows and the highlights (the shadows and highlights colour-balance wheels). It is an app setting, on by default where the model is available and absent everywhere else. Off, or on any other computer, Auto is the rule it has always been (`docs/02` Auto exposure, the illuminant estimate, the other values at 0 and the wheels at the centre).

The owner's decision of 2026-10-19 (`docs/16`): "We allow apple-native path for mac platforms and option remains invisible for others. Apple-only automatic exposure, contrast and temperature, saturation - turned on by default - is an app setting, that can be turned off for people who like more control." The white is a nudge of the estimate ("2 - nudge"): the model never picks the kelvin itself. Results may differ between Macs and after macOS updates ("1 - thanks fine"). The owner's direction later that day: "Let's expand number of controls Apple Auto controls to achieve professional or cinematic look depending on a scene." gave prompt version 2 and table version 2 (sections 4 and 5; a menu of named grades was rejected), and the instructions the model reads became the person's to edit in Preferences (section 2). On 2026-10-20 the owner wrote the default instructions himself, a finished base look with no style, which gave prompt version 3 (section 4). The research behind it is `research/scene-understanding-2026-10-19.md`; `docs/18` is the parked `autoset` spec on the branch `claude/autoset` and is not on `main`.

Everything in this file is new after Yellowmoon 0.1.10. Apple Auto does not change `PIPELINE_VERSION` (0.4.2 when it was built, 0.4.4 after the new Contrast and Saturation and the colour warp's fix were merged into its branch): with Apple Auto off the pixels do not change. The table of section 5 is its second version (table 2) and the prompt of section 4 its third (prompt 3, the owner's default instructions), which the owner tunes by eye and in the blind A/B of section 13.

## 1. Where it runs

Apple Auto is **available** when all of these hold, checked in this order; the first that fails is the reason reported (`ym doctor`, `ym info`, the app's preference stays hidden):

| # | Condition | Reason when it fails (`UnavailableReason`, wire name) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A Mac on Apple silicon (`aarch64-apple-darwin`) | `not_a_mac` (Linux, Windows) or `intel_mac` |
| 2 | The binary was built with the macOS 27 SDK or later (section 11) | `built_without_support` |
| 3 | macOS 27 or later at run time | `macos_too_old` |
| 4 | `SystemLanguageModel.default.availability` | `device_not_eligible`, `apple_intelligence_off`, `model_not_ready`, `unavailable` (any other case) |
| 5 | `SystemLanguageModel.default.capabilities.contains(.vision)` | `no_image_input` |

Availability can change while the app runs (Apple Intelligence switched on, the model finishing its download), so it is read again when the app starts, when Preferences opens, when a session opens, when the preferences change and before each export starts (and by the worker before each request: a photo is not failed when Apple Auto stopped being available before its call, it waits for Apple Auto to be back). It is cheap (property reads, no model call); the app's `Core::export_start` and `ym render` read it at the same point, just before the export is queued. macOS downloads the model itself when the user turns Apple Intelligence on; Yellowmoon cannot start that download and adds no network access (hard rule 7).

## 2. The preference

`preferences.json` gets two fields: an eighth, `apple_auto: bool`, default `true` (a file without it reads as `true`), and a ninth, `apple_auto_instructions: string`, the person's own instructions for the model, default `""`: absent, empty or only white space is the default instructions of section 4, and the default text itself is saved as `""`. This is the persistence the owner agreed to on 2026-10-19 ("is an app setting"); `docs/01` §Persistence lists both. They are read on every platform and only act where Apple Auto is available; elsewhere they are kept as they are and ignored.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Place | Preferences, a `Switch` row after Display rendering |
| Shown | only while Apple Auto is available (section 1); hidden otherwise, including in the browser build |
| Title | **Auto with Apple Intelligence** |
| Hint | "Apple's on-device model looks at each photo and grades it: exposure, highlights, shadows, black level, contrast, warmth, tint, saturation and the colours of the shadows and highlights. It runs on this Mac and sends nothing anywhere. Turn it off to use Yellowmoon's own Auto, the same on every computer." |

**The instructions.** Under the switch, shown only while it is on:

| Item | Value |

Only the instructions are the person's: the request line ("Grade this photo.") and the answer's form (the fields, their cases and guides) stay fixed in the Swift (section 4), so other instructions can change what the model answers, never which values it can set or how they reach the picture. The instructions in effect are part of "version" under hard rule 8 (section 7): every report that names the model says whether they are the default or edited, with the first 12 hex digits of their SHA-256 (`instructions`, `instructions_sha256`; `ym_core::apple_auto::InstructionsId`).

`ym` reads the same preferences (section 8), so by default it makes the app's picture on the same Mac; `--apple-auto-instructions FILE` replaces the instructions for one run.

## 3. What the model sees

The model sees **the photo as Auto renders it without Apple**: one sRGB picture per photo, independent of everything the user has set, so that editing a photo never asks a new question.

- The photo's settings at their defaults: exposure, white balance, highlights, shadows, black, contrast, saturation and the shadows and highlights wheels at Auto (the rule: no Apple values), every other field at its default, no crop, no straightening, no quarter turn by the user; the camera's orientation applied, so the picture is upright.
- The look is the built-in Signature at 100 % (the white lift's reference look, `docs/02` C12 step 1b), whatever the photo's look and the default look are; Neutral when the data folder has no Signature file. The display rendering is ours (ACES 2, SDR), whatever Preferences says.
- The reduced raw path (the thumbnail's, `docs/01`) at **768 px on the long edge** (a photo smaller than that is not enlarged), rendered on the **CPU reference path** whatever the GPU policy, so the app and `ym` give the model the same bytes on every Mac; 8-bit sRGB, 3 bytes per pixel, rows top to bottom.

It is rendered when the photo's estimates are first ready and again on Re-run (whose estimates can differ only after a light preset or Pick white, which do not change it, so in practice it is the same picture). It lives in memory until the answer arrives and is then dropped (hard rule 4).

**As built (core).** "The rule" is the photo's **own** estimates: its own exposure after the highlight guard and its own noise estimate, not the burst median or the pooled noise, which depend on which other photos have developed so far; so the picture depends on the photo alone and the app and `ym` send the same bytes whatever order the photos develop in. The pictures come from a renderer of their own (`ym_core::pipeline::AdvisorPictures`) that has no graphics device, no display LUT and only the Signature look (`<data>/looks/builtin/signature.json`, the white lift's reference) and Neutral, so neither the GPU policy, nor Preferences › Display rendering, nor the look library can change them; its caches are dropped after each picture. The development job renders the picture after the thumbnail, while at most 64 pictures (about 1.2 MB each) wait in memory; a photo developed beyond that, or while Apple Auto was off, has its picture rendered by the worker when its turn comes, from the file again (the same bytes). "Not enlarged" and the size: the thumbnail's rule, `round(side × 768 / long side)`.

## 4. What it is asked

**Prompt version 3** (`PROMPT_VERSION = 3` in `ym_core::apple_auto` and in the Swift): the default instructions below are the owner's own text of 2026-10-20, given word for word ("Once everything on master - update this default prompt"). They ask for the best finished photograph the scene allows as the base look, with no style: the shadows' and highlights' colours none and no mood. The request line, the options and the answer's form are those of version 2, unchanged, so the colours stay in the form and other instructions can still ask for them. *Version 2*, the owner's direction of 2026-10-19 (`docs/16`): "Let's expand number of controls Apple Auto controls to achieve professional or cinematic look depending on a scene." The model grades the photo: it sets more controls directly; version 2's default instructions also asked for a cinematic look when the scene invites it and for colours in the shadows and highlights when they serve the mood. A menu of named grades was rejected. *Version 1* asked for four small corrections (exposure, white, contrast, saturation) with "choose keep when the photo already looks right"; on the owner's 10 test photos it answered keep for all four on 8, and its scene line repeated the instructions ("lit with neutral automatic settings"), so it was replaced.

One request per photo, to `SystemLanguageModel.default` through a new `LanguageModelSession` (no tools, no transcript carried from one photo to the next). The bridge never constructs `PrivateCloudComputeLanguageModel` or any other model (hard rule 7; a test reads the Swift source for it). Guardrails are the default.

**Instructions.** The default (`ym_core::apple_auto::DEFAULT_INSTRUCTIONS`), which the person may replace with their own in Preferences (section 2, at most 2000 characters), given here in a block because its line breaks are part of it (1689 characters, 8 lines):

```text
You are a master colorist grading a raw, neutral camera file inside a RAW processor built on an ARRI color pipeline. Your goal is to transform this flat starting point into the best finished, professional photograph the source, light, subject and scene allow, honoring ARRI's signature color science. This is the base look. Add no style: the shadow colour and highlight colour must be none (the light is already correct), and no mood.
1. Analyze & Respect the Scene
Preserve the natural logic of the light. Night scenes stay dark; snow and high-key scenes stay bright; silhouettes stay dark; candlelight and golden hour stay warm. Keep skin tones natural, organic and true to life.
2. Execute a Comprehensive Grade
Do not rely on a single adjustment; a finished grade usually changes several controls, each chosen for what this picture needs:
* Exposure & Tone: set the subject's brightness to guide the eye; brighten a subject that is too dark, hold one that is well lit, never darken a dark photo, and keep night scenes and silhouettes dark. Bring bright skies and whites down for smooth ARRI-style highlight roll-off. Bright sky, clouds, white clothes or lights: highlights darker for roll-off. Shadows brighter only where dark areas hide real detail; leave them when the shadows already show detail. Blacks deeper only if the picture looks hazy or milky; if the blacks are already deep and clean, leave them.
* Contrast: deliberate dynamic range that fits the light: more for flat or hazy light, less for harsh light, with rich midtone detail.
* Color Balance & Density: neutral whites, accurate warmth and tint, fixing casts. Saturation true and dense, neither boosted nor washed out.
```

The instructions are not in the Swift: the core passes the text in effect (the default or the person's own) to `ym_apple_auto_ask` on every call (section 11), and the Swift opens the session with them (`LanguageModelSession(tools: [], instructions: { instructions })`). The request line and the answer's form below stay fixed in the Swift whatever the instructions say.

**Prompt:** "Grade this photo." followed by the picture as an `Attachment` (`CGImage`, orientation up).

**Answer** (guided generation, a `@Generable` type `Answer`, fields generated in this order). Every field but `scene` and `plan` is optional (`var exposure: Exposure?`, …): a field the model leaves empty sets nothing for that control (section 5). The cases with the codes the bridge writes:

**Options:** `GenerationOptions(samplingMode: .greedy, temperature: nil, maximumResponseTokens: 320)` (the macOS 27 SDK's initialiser; `sampling:` is its older spelling; 320 tokens because the answer has two text fields now). Greedy sampling "always produces the same output for a given input", so one Mac with one model gives one answer per picture. Every option is set explicitly, because unset ones may change across OS or model updates.

**An answer cut short.** The answer is streamed (`session.streamResponse(generating: Answer.self, includeSchemaInPrompt: true, options:)` with the same prompt) and the last snapshot's `rawContent` is kept. When the generation stops part way (the token limit, or another error after some fields were produced), the fields filled until then are used and nothing is set for the rest; each value is read from the raw content by its name (`GeneratedContent.value(_:forProperty:)`), so a value cut in the middle of a word, absent or empty, is not set. An answer with no value filled at all is a failure (`bad_answer`), as is any error before a value was filled (with the error's kind). A request that runs out of time (30 s) is a timeout even when some fields were filled: where it stopped depends on the time, and the same photo must give the same answer (section 7).

The Swift types are `Answer`, `Exposure`, `Highlights`, `Shadows`, `Blacks`, `Contrast`, `Warmth`, `Tint`, `Saturation`, `ShadowColour`, `HighlightColour` and `Strength` (both strengths); the schema the model sees may carry their names and the field names, so they are part of the prompt version too, and they keep the spec's British spelling (`shadowColour`) because the model reads them. A test (`crates/ym-apple/src/source_tests.rs`) reads the Swift file and checks the prompt, the options, the fields (and that every value is optional), guides, cases and codes against this section, and that `DEFAULT_INSTRUCTIONS` is the text of the block above, its lines joined with line feeds, and is not in the Swift.

The steps are numbers on the Rust side (`ym_core::apple_auto::AutoSteps`, every field an `Option`): exposure −3 … +3, the other numeric steps −2 … +2 (keep 0); the colours `ShadowColor` and `HighlightColor` (JSON `"none"`, `"teal"`, …) and their strengths 1 … 3. The model's `warmth` is the step `white` in Rust and in every report (the name of version 1, kept to spare the reports and the A/B kit a rename). A strength is kept only with a colour other than none (`AutoSteps::normalised`; a strength is ignored when the colour is none). `scene` is cut to 80 characters and `plan` to 160; the scene is shown only in reports, the plan in reports and on the Automatic card.

## 5. From the answer to the picture

The steps become values through one table (`ym_core::apple_auto::TABLE`, **table version 2**, the owner's to tune by eye):

| Control | −3 | −2 | −1 | 0 | +1 | +2 | +3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exposure, EV added to the rule | −1.0 | −0.6 | −0.3 | 0 | +0.3 | +0.6 | +1.0 |
| Highlights (slider −100 … 100) | | −40 | −20 | 0 | +20 | +40 | |
| Shadows | | −30 | −15 | 0 | +15 | +30 | |
| Black (the model's blacks) | | −30 | −15 | 0 | +15 | +30 | |
| Contrast | | −30 | −15 | 0 | +15 | +30 | |
| Warmth, the picture made warmer by (mired; warmer is a lower mired of the user's white) | | −20 | −10 | 0 | +10 | +20 | |
| Tint (the tint's units, range ±50, positive magenta) | | −10 | −5 | 0 | +5 | +10 | |
| Saturation | | −30 | −15 | 0 | +15 | +30 | |

The signs follow `docs/02` C9 (`crates/ym-color/src/grade.rs`: `v += (x/100) · 1.5 S · bump(L; centre, 1.5 S)`): a positive Highlights or Shadows brightens that zone and a positive Black lifts black, so "darker" and "deeper" are negative values.

**Shadow and highlight colour** set the shadows and highlights colour-balance wheels (C9: `v += 0.03 (a e1 + b e2) zone(L)`, `a` along e1 red↔cyan, `b` along e2 green (+) ↔ blue (−)) to the point `r · (cos, sin)` of the colour's direction at the strength's radius: subtle 0.3, medium 0.55, strong 0.8. Directions `(a, b)`:

| Shadows | Highlights |
|---|---|
| teal (−0.866, −0.5), blue (−0.5, −0.866), green (−0.5, 0.866), amber (0.866, 0.5), magenta (0.5, −0.866), none (0, 0) | warm (0.866, 0.5), gold (0.5, 0.866), cool (−0.5, −0.866), pink (0.866, −0.5), none (0, 0) |

A test renders a neutral grey ramp through C9 with each colour at medium and checks that the offset at the zone's centre (3 stops below or above grey) has the colour's hue within 5° (HSV hue: teal 210°, between cyan and blue; blue 240°; green 120°; amber and warm 30°, orange; magenta 300°; gold 60°, yellow; cool 240°; pink 330°, between magenta and red; `crates/ym-core/tests/apple_auto.rs`, `shadow_and_highlight_colours_take_the_named_hue_through_the_grade`). A colour without a strength sets nothing for its wheel.

**Nothing set.** A step the model left empty (section 4) sets nothing: its Auto value stays exactly what it is without Apple Auto (the rule's exposure, white 0 mired, tint 0, 0 for the sliders, (0, 0) for the wheels). It is not read as keep in reports: `ym info --json` and `ym:AppleAuto` write `null`, and the text row and the Automatic card list only the values that were set. With every step empty the answer is a failure (section 4).

How each value acts, only while its setting is Auto:

- **Exposure.** `EV = EV_rule + ΔEV`, where `EV_rule` is the rule's value after the highlight guard and burst sharing (`docs/02` Auto exposure). The guard is not applied again: the model saw the guarded picture, and a brighter answer is a choice about the subject. The white lift is not re-sized (it is sized at the photo's own rule exposure, as for a burst median). The slider's detent and the Automatic card show `EV`.
- **White (warmth).** In Auto (light source Auto, white balance Auto, no picked white) C3·C4 is the identity: the user's white is the scene's (`docs/02` C3–C4). The nudge moves the user's white along the locus by `m` mired, keeping the scene's tint and class mix: `CCT_user = 1e6 / (1e6 / CCT_scene + m)` with `m = −10 × step` (warmer, a positive step, is a lower mired: a higher Kelvin, so the camera is balanced for a bluer light and the picture is warmer, as a 5500 K setting on a tungsten scene is). `g_user = 1 / w_A(E_user)` with `w_A` the same tinted, class-blended white as the scene's but at `CCT_user`, and the ARRI matrix takes `CCT_user`, as for a manual Kelvin. With `m = 0` the chain is exactly today's Auto (same bits). The Kelvin readout is `CCT_user` rounded to 50 K and the tint is the scene's, so the Automatic card and the Kelvin slider's detent show the white in effect. With a light preset, a picked white or a manual white balance there is no nudge.
  **As built.** The "same white at `CCT_user`" is the scene's tinted, class-blended white moved along the locus: `w_A(E_user) = w_A(E_scene) · w_locus(CCT_user) / w_locus(CCT_scene)` channel by channel (`w_locus` the blackbody/daylight model). For daylight and tungsten light, whose class whites are that model, this is exactly the class-blended white at `CCT_user`; the fluorescent and LED class whites are lists of real lights, not ordered in colour and constant beyond their ends, and evaluating them at `CCT_user` moved the white the wrong way or not at all on every shipped profile (LED at 2700 and 4000 K, fluorescent at 2700 K), so the move of the locus is applied to the scene's own white for every class instead (monotonic and in the right direction for every class, `crates/ym-color/tests/white_nudge.rs`; `docs/02` C3–C4). `CCT_user` is held in 1500–25000 K (the white tables) before the ARRI matrix holds it in 2000–11000 K. The readout's tint is the one that makes `Manual { kelvin, tint }` reproduce the nudged white (Auto's readout formula), which is the scene's tint up to the rounding of the Kelvin for daylight and tungsten light.
- **Tint.** Like the white nudge, only while white balance is Auto (light source Auto, no picked white): the table's tint is added to the scene's own tint, the sum held in −50 … 50, and the user's white takes it as a tint is given to a white (`tinted_white`: red and blue times `2^(−Δt/100)`, green 1; `ym_color::wb::{nudged_tint, nudged_alexa_white}`, `ChainSettings::tint_shift`, `ColorEstimates::tint_shift`). A positive (magenta) nudge takes green out of the picture; the Tint readout moves by the nudge and the Kelvin stays. With a tint nudge of 0 the chain is today's Auto bit for bit, and a warmth nudge without a tint nudge is the white nudge as it was (`crates/ym-color/tests/white_nudge.rs`).
- **Highlights, shadows, black, contrast, saturation and the shadows and highlights wheels** are Auto-or-manual settings (below). Auto is the table's value; without Apple Auto it is 0 or (0, 0), today's default.

**The settings.** `DevelopSettings.contrast`, `.saturation`, `.highlights`, `.shadows` and `.black` are `AutoOr<{ value }>` like exposure: JSON `{ "kind": "auto" }` or `{ "kind": "manual", "value": 12 }`; Auto is the default. A plain number in an older document (a `--settings` file, a golden manifest, a photo's `ym:Settings`) reads as Manual with that value, except 0, which reads as Auto (0 was the default). The range of a manual value stays −100 … 100. The shadows and highlights colour-balance wheels (`color_balance.shadows`, `.highlights`) are `AutoOr<{ point }>`: `{ "kind": "auto" }` or `{ "kind": "manual", "point": [0.1, -0.2] }`; a plain point reads as Manual at that point, except `[0, 0]`, which reads as Auto; the midtones wheel stays a plain point. A patch takes either form. Copy and paste carry Auto as Auto (the photo pasted onto uses its own answer). Re-run puts all seven back to Auto, with exposure and white, while Apple Auto acts on the photo (its state is pending, answered or failed); otherwise Re-run leaves them as they are, as before Apple Auto (decided in review on 2026-10-19: without Apple Auto the card has none of their rows, so Re-run must not reset a value the card does not show). `DevelopSettings::reset_automatic(apple_auto)` takes that from the session (`AppleAuto::acts`). The pinned settings of an export (`ym:Settings`) pin all seven to the numbers and points they resolved to, as they pin Auto exposure; white balance stays Auto there, and the nudges are in `ym:AppleAuto` (section 9). With Apple Auto off or unavailable every new Auto value is 0 or (0, 0), so the picture is today's bit for bit (golden 61 identical, the parity tests byte for byte).

**Bursts.** The rule shares the exposure across a burst (median of the frames' own values). Apple's steps are shared the same way, field by field: each numeric step is the median of the burst's answered frames that filled that field (the lower of the two middle values for an even count), and nothing is set when none did. Colours: the colour most of the answered frames chose (ties: the colour of the earliest frame, in burst order, among the tied ones), with the median strength of the frames that chose it (none: no strength). Frames that failed are left out, and when every frame failed the burst uses the rule. A photo's export therefore waits for its whole burst's answers. A frame that failed takes its burst's steps all the same (its `ym:Auto` is then `apple`, and its `ym:AppleAuto` says it failed and why).

## 6. When it asks, and who waits

- Each session has one Apple Auto worker: one request at a time, in this order: the photo on screen, then the other photos in session order. It starts once a photo's estimates are ready (it needs the picture of section 3) and runs only while the preference is on and Apple Auto is available.
- **The viewer does not wait.** A photo shows with the rule as soon as its estimates are ready; when the answer arrives its Auto values change, the picture and its thumbnail are rendered again (the `estimates_changed` path a burst median already uses), and the Automatic card follows. Until then the card says Apple Intelligence is looking at the photo (section 10).
- **Exports and `ym` wait.** An export's photo waits until it and its burst have an answer or a failure, exactly as it waits for a burst's shared exposure (`docs/01` "App/CLI parity for early exports"), and its turn moves up the worker's order. `ym render` and `ym info` wait for the answers of the photos they develop.
- **Time.** About 1–4 s per photo on Apple silicon (an estimate; measured in section 13): a 500-photo `ym render` takes about 8–33 minutes longer. `--apple-auto off` skips it.
- **Failures.** A request that has not answered after 30 s is abandoned. `rateLimited` and `concurrentRequests` are retried twice, 2 s apart. Any other error (refusal, guardrail, unsupported language, context too large, assets unavailable, a bad answer) fails the photo at once. A failed photo uses the rule for every value and says why (`ym info`, the Automatic card, `ym:AppleAuto`). Re-run asks again.
- **Re-run** recomputes the estimates and asks again; a session that is closed stops its worker and drops the answers.
- **New instructions** (section 2) drop every answer of the session and ask every photo again, the photo on screen first. Each request carries the instructions' generation; an answer or failure that arrives for an older generation is dropped (its photo stays waiting), so a picture never mixes answers to two sets of instructions.
- **Removing photos** (`docs/01` Session, `docs/11` *Removing photos*; merged on 2026-10-20). A photo taken out of the filmstrip is not asked about: it leaves the worker's order and the order of the photos an export waits for, and an answer or a failure that arrives for it, also when it was put back meanwhile, is dropped (the request is tied to the photo's incarnation). Its picture is dropped with it (hard rule 4). The shared steps of a burst are made again from the frames that are left, the way the burst median exposure is, and the frames whose values moved are sent `estimates_changed` and get a new thumbnail; an export waiting for the removed frame, or for the removed photo itself, stops waiting. Undo puts the photo back with its answer or its failure, and it is not asked again; a photo that had none goes back to the worker's order; and the answers of photos that were out when the instructions changed are not brought back (the photo is asked with the new instructions). Closing the session drops all of it.
- **As built.** The order is the photo on screen (the session's current photo), then the photos an export is waiting for in the order the export takes them, each followed by the frames of its burst that have no answer yet (its export waits for them too; `Session::prioritise_apple_auto` adds them, so every caller gets it), then the others in filmstrip order. The 30 s limit is the advisor's: the bridge passes `REQUEST_TIMEOUT_MS` to the Swift call, and the core never abandons a call itself (an advisor that does not return holds the worker, not the app). A call that is running when the session closes is not waited for; its answer is dropped. `ym`'s "waits for the answers" is `Session::wait_idle`, which waits for the worker too. A request is panic-safe as a whole: a panic in the advisor, the picture, storing the result, refreshing the served estimates or the event sink fails that photo with kind `other` and the panic's message (it uses the rule, an export of it does not wait), ends the request, wakes whoever waits and leaves the worker running for the next photo. A worker that cannot be started, or that stops in a panic outside a request, leaves the session as one without an advisor (Apple Auto off and not available, nothing waits for it). An answer that moves a photo's values renders its thumbnail again in the core (`thumbnail_ready`; a photo still developing gets it after its first thumbnail); an answer that changes nothing (every value it set keep or none) changes no pixel and renders nothing, but still sends `estimates_changed` so the Automatic card's line follows. **A known cost:** that thumbnail is rendered from the raw, so an answered photo outside the mosaic cache's window (`MOSAIC_CACHE_LEN`, the photo on screen and its neighbours) is decoded again, about one decode per answered photo whose values moved; and switching the preference off and on again renders the thumbnail of every answered photo again each time (about one decode each), since the photos go back to the rule and then to their answers. Accepted for now: it happens in the background and only when values move. Which Auto acts is decided in this order: no advisor is off; `--apple-auto off` is off; not available is unavailable, whatever the preference says (it is kept and ignored there); the preference off is off unless the run says `on`; else it acts (`ym_core::apple_auto::Activity::decide`).

## 7. Determinism (hard rule 8)

Apple's model is part of "version" under rule 8: the same photo, settings, Yellowmoon version, macOS build, model variant, prompt version and instructions give the same answer and the same bytes on one Mac (greedy sampling, a picture rendered on the CPU). Different Macs or macOS builds may answer differently, which the owner accepted. So every export records which Auto made it (`ym:Auto`, `ym:AppleAuto`), `ym info` and `ym doctor` print the model and macOS build, and two different exports of one file can always be explained.

- **App and `ym`.** On one Mac both read the same preference and send the same picture, so they write the same bytes (hard rule 6). The parity tests and the golden tests run with Apple Auto off (no advisor, or `--apple-auto off`), which gives today's bytes on every machine.
- **The version string.** `Apple Intelligence · <variant display name> · macOS <version> (<build>) · prompt 3 · table 2`, for example `Apple Intelligence · AFM 3 Core · macOS 27.0.1 (27A404) · prompt 3 · table 2`, with ` · edited instructions (<first 12 hex digits of their SHA-256>)` added when the person wrote their own (`AdvisorInfo::version_string`).
- Apple's models are used at runtime only. Their answers never train, label or tune anything of ours (Apple's developer agreements; `docs/16`), and the table's values are chosen by eye, never fitted to Apple answers.

## 11. The bridge (`crates/ym-apple`)

- A new crate, `ym-apple`, depended on by `ym-cli` and the app (never by `ym-core`, the golden tool or the tests of other crates). It implements `ym_core::apple_auto::AutoAdvisor` (section 12) as `AppleAdvisor` and gives one constructor, `ym_apple::system_advisor() -> Arc<dyn AutoAdvisor>`.
- **Swift.** `crates/ym-apple/swift/AppleAuto.swift`: the `@Generable` answer, the prompt, `availability`, `ask` (the instructions come from the core with each call, section 4). It exposes four C functions with `@_cdecl` (C types only), declared in `crates/ym-apple/swift/ym_apple.h` and in Rust with `unsafe extern "C"`:
  - `int32_t ym_apple_auto_availability(void)`: 0 available, else the reason code (section 1, in table order from 3: 3 `macos_too_old`, 4 `device_not_eligible`, 5 `apple_intelligence_off`, 6 `model_not_ready`, 7 `unavailable`, 8 `no_image_input`).
  - `int32_t ym_apple_auto_model(char *buf, size_t len)`: the variant's display name, UTF-8, NUL-terminated (0 on success).
  - `int32_t ym_apple_auto_os(char *buf, size_t len)`: macOS version and build, e.g. `27.0.1 (27A404)`.
  - `int32_t ym_apple_auto_ask(const uint8_t *rgb, uint32_t width, uint32_t height, const char *instructions, uint32_t timeout_ms, YmAppleAnswer *out, char *message, size_t message_len)`: blocks the calling thread until the answer, an error or the timeout; 0 on success, else an error code (1 unavailable, 2 rate limited, 3 timeout, 4 refused, 5 guardrail, 6 unsupported language, 7 context too large, 8 assets unavailable, 9 concurrent requests, 10 bad answer, 11 other) with Apple's message in `message`. `instructions` is the text the model reads (UTF-8, NUL-terminated, not empty: the default or the person's own; `src/abi.rs` turns a NUL inside the text into a space); the Swift copies it before the request starts, as it copies the picture. `YmAppleAnswer { int32_t exposure, highlights, shadows, blacks, contrast, white, tint, saturation, shadow_color, shadow_color_strength, highlight_color, highlight_color_strength; char scene[128]; char plan[256]; }`, the codes of section 4 (`white` is the model's warmth); a field the model left empty is `YM_APPLE_UNSET` (`INT32_MIN`), which the Swift writes into every field before it asks. It checks the availability again first (1 when the model is not available); `timeout_ms` 0 means no limit. The Swift side takes `out` as `void *` and writes the fields at their C offsets (0, 4, … 44, the scene at 48, the plan at 176; 432 bytes), which the header and `src/abi.rs` assert. Strings are cut at a UTF-8 boundary to fit and always NUL-terminated.
- **Build** (`crates/ym-apple/build.rs`, no new dependency): on `aarch64-apple-darwin` with an SDK of version 27 or later (`xcrun --sdk macosx --show-sdk-version`), unless `YM_APPLE_BRIDGE=off`, it compiles the Swift file with `xcrun --sdk macosx swiftc -parse-as-library -emit-library -static -module-name YmAppleAuto -swift-version 5 -O -target arm64-apple-macos14.0` (or the later `MACOSX_DEPLOYMENT_TARGET`, so the Swift never targets an older macOS than the binary) into `OUT_DIR`, links it, links `Foundation` and `CoreGraphics`, adds the Swift runtime library paths that `swiftc -print-target-info` reports and the compiler runtime (`clang_rt.osx`, for `#available`), and sets `cfg(ym_apple_bridge)`. A compile error with such an SDK fails the build (a silent stub would hide a bug). Everywhere else (Linux, Intel, an older SDK, `YM_APPLE_BRIDGE=off`) it compiles nothing and the crate reports `not_a_mac`, `intel_mac` or `built_without_support`; a Mac build without the bridge prints a cargo warning. Inside the Swift file every Foundation Models use is behind `@available(macOS 27.0, *)` or `#available(macOS 27.0, *)`.
- **The weak link.** `FoundationModels` is linked **weakly** (`-weak_framework FoundationModels`, a `LC_LOAD_WEAK_DYLIB`), so the app and `ym` still start on macOS 14 to 26. Cargo has no weak kind of `rustc-link-lib`, and `rustc-link-arg` from a library's build script reaches only that library's own tests and examples, so: the Swift is compiled with `-Xfrontend -disable-autolink-framework -Xfrontend FoundationModels` (the object asks for no strong link); `ym-apple` declares `links = "ym_apple_auto"` and publishes `bridge=1` when it compiled the Swift; and each final binary's build script (`crates/ym-cli/build.rs`, `src-tauri/build.rs`) passes `-Wl,-weak_framework,FoundationModels` when `DEP_YM_APPLE_AUTO_BRIDGE` is `1` (`ym-apple`'s own build script does it for its tests and the probe). A binary that depends on `ym-apple` without that line fails to link instead of linking the framework strongly. The same build scripts add `-Wl,-rpath,/usr/lib/swift`, where macOS keeps the Swift runtime: a binary linked for a macOS older than 12 (rustc's default deployment target is 11.0 when `MACOSX_DEPLOYMENT_TARGET` is not set, as in a plain `cargo build` or `tools/dev/prepare-sidecar.sh`) takes the concurrency runtime by its back-deployment name, `@rpath/libswift_Concurrency.dylib`, and stopped at launch ("no LC_RPATH's found": the first test build of 2026-10-19, whose sidecar `ym` was built without the variable). The build and release workflows also set `MACOSX_DEPLOYMENT_TARGET=14.0`, the app's `minimumSystemVersion`, for every binary, and `apple-bridge.yml` builds `ym` once without it and runs it.
- **Threads.** `ask` runs the async Swift call in a detached `Task` and waits on a semaphore; it is called only from the session's Apple Auto worker thread, never from the main thread.
- **The test advisor.** With `YELLOWMOON_TEST_APPLE_AUTO=fake` in the environment, `system_advisor()` returns `ym_core::apple_auto::FakeAdvisor` on any platform: available, model `Test advisor`, os `test`, steps from the picture's statistics (section 12). It exists for the tests of `ym` and the app on Linux and on Macs without Apple Intelligence, like `YELLOWMOON_GPU`. With `YELLOWMOON_TEST_APPLE_AUTO=none` it returns `NoAdvisor`, never available, for the reason a build without the bridge gives on that platform (`not_a_mac`, `intel_mac`, `built_without_support`): the tests of `ym` that expect the rule run with it, so they pass on a Mac with Apple Intelligence too.
- **CI.** The Swift code needs the macOS 27 SDK, which only the `xcode-27` runner image has (GitHub's, macOS 27 on Apple silicon). `.github/workflows/apple-bridge.yml` builds `ym-apple` and its probe there with the bridge, checks with `nm` that the probe carries the bridge's symbols and with `otool -l` that FoundationModels is a weak load, runs clippy and `ym-apple`'s tests (availability without a crash, the fake, the source checks), runs the probe (with Apple's model and with the fake) and builds the stub with `YM_APPLE_BRIDGE=off`. A second job runs that probe on `macos-14`, `macos-15` and `macos-26`, where it must start and report `macos_too_old`: the proof that the weak link works. Once `ym-cli` and the app depend on `ym-apple`, the job also builds them with the bridge and prints `ym doctor`. On that image macOS 27's dyld refuses the dylibs that Rust 1.97 strips in release ("mis-aligned LINKEDIT string pool": proc macros fail to load, so nothing builds), while 1.97 without stripping and Rust 1.99 work (a print-only step shows it at every run); the workflow therefore sets `CARGO_PROFILE_RELEASE_STRIP` and `CARGO_PROFILE_RELEASE_BUILD_OVERRIDE_STRIP` to `none`, and the release workflows need the same, or a newer pinned Rust, when they move to `xcode-27`. The release and build workflows move to `xcode-27`, so the published app carries the bridge; the x86_64 half of the universal binary gets the stub.

InstructionsId { instructions: Default | Edited, instructions_sha256: String /* 12 hex digits */ }

AdvisorAnswer  { steps: AutoSteps, scene: String, plan: String }
AutoSteps      { exposure: Option<i8> /* -3..=3 */, highlights, shadows, blacks, contrast, white, tint, saturation: Option<i8> /* -2..=2 */,

- `Core::open` takes an optional advisor; the app and `ym` pass `ym_apple::system_advisor()`, every other caller (tests, golden, examples) passes none, which is the same as unavailable.
- `AutoEstimates` gains `apple: AppleAuto` (`Off`, `Unavailable(reason)`, `Pending`, `Answered { answer, info }`, `Failed { kind, message, info }`) and the resolved Auto values `contrast`, `saturation`, `highlights`, `shadows`, `black` (0 without an answer), `balance_shadows`, `balance_highlights` ((0, 0) without an answer), and `white_shift_mired` and `tint_shift` (in `ColorEstimates`, 0 without an answer). The session's `refresh_effective` applies the burst medians of section 5 after the rule's.
- `AutoSummary` (the `estimates` of `ResolvedSettings`) gains those values, `source`, `apple` and `apple_steps`; `exposure_ev`, `kelvin` and `tint` include Apple's values.
- An answered step outside its range, or one the bridge could not decode, fails the photo with `bad_answer`, and so does an answer with no value set (`AutoSteps::any_set`).
- **The fake.** Mean sRGB luma `L` (0…1, Rec.709 weights on the 8-bit values) and its standard deviation `S`: exposure +1 when `L` < 0.30, −1 when `L` > 0.65, else 0; highlights −1 when `L` > 0.5, else not set; shadows +1 when `L` < 0.30, else 0; blacks not set; contrast +1 when `S` < 0.15, else 0; white 0; tint not set; saturation 0; shadow colour teal, subtle, when `S` < 0.15, else none; highlight colour not set; scene "Test picture"; plan "Test plan: " and the first word of the instructions it was given, so the tests see which instructions reached it.
- **As built (the core's API for the CLI and app lanes).**
  - `CoreOptions::advisor: Option<Arc<dyn AutoAdvisor>>` (default `None`) and `CoreOptions::apple_auto: AppleAutoMode` (`from_preference` by default, `on`, `off`); `Core::set_apple_auto_mode(mode)` changes the mode later (for `ym --apple-auto`).   - `Core::apple_auto_status() -> AppleAutoStatus` reads the availability again: `{ "available", "reason", "info": { "model", "os", "instructions", "instructions_sha256" } | null, "preference", "mode", "active", "prompt", "table", "instructions", "instructions_sha256", "own_instructions", "default_instructions", "instructions_max_chars" }`. Without an advisor it is not available and has no reason. `Core::preferences_set` with `apple_auto` or `apple_auto_instructions` applies it at once (`PreferencesPatch::check` refuses instructions over the limit before anything is saved). `CoreOptions::apple_auto_instructions: Option<String>` replaces the preference for a run (`ym --apple-auto-instructions`); `ym_core::apple_auto::{DEFAULT_INSTRUCTIONS, INSTRUCTIONS_MAX_CHARS, effective_instructions, check_instructions}` and `ym_core::preferences::own_instructions` are the rules of section 2.
  - `AutoEstimates` gains `contrast`, `saturation`, `highlights`, `shadows`, `black`, `balance_shadows`, `balance_highlights`, `apple` (the photo's `AppleAuto`) and `apple_steps` (the steps in effect: the photo's or its burst's), with `source()`; `ColorEstimates` gains `white_shift_mired` and `tint_shift`.   - `AutoSummary` (the `estimates` of `ResolvedSettings`, the `auto` object of `ym --json`) gains `contrast`, `saturation`, `source` (`"rule"` or `"apple"`) and `apple` (`{ "state": "off" | "unavailable" | "pending" | "answered" | "failed", … }`: `reason` for unavailable; `answer: { steps, scene }` and `info` for answered; `kind`, `message` and `info` for failed). `AutoFlags` (`auto` of `ResolvedSettings`) gains `contrast`, `saturation`, `highlights`, `shadows`, `black`, `balance_shadows` and `balance_highlights`; `ResolvedValues` holds the values in effect.

## 13. Tests and checks

- **Linux and every Mac (CI):** settings (Auto contrast, saturation, highlights, shadows, black and the two wheels, the old number and point forms, patches, copy and paste, Re-run, pinned settings, the schema); the table and its bounds; the colours' hues through the grade (section 5); the white nudge (`m = 0` gives today's chain bit for bit; ±10 and ±20 mired move the Kelvin readout the right way and the scene white the right way in AWG4; manual white, a preset and Pick white get no nudge) and the tint nudge (0 bit for bit, a magenta nudge takes green out, the readout's tint moves by it); steps left empty (nothing set, `null` in every report, no row on the card, an answer with nothing set fails); burst medians per field and the most frequent colour; the instructions (the preference absent, empty, edited, too long; a change asks every photo again and drops late answers to the old ones; `--apple-auto-instructions`; the reports' `instructions` and digest; the Preferences field, its saving and Reset to default in component tests and the e2e state `35-dialog-preferences-apple-instructions`); the worker's order, the viewer not waiting, exports and `ym` waiting, timeouts, retries and failures, with the fake and with a scripted advisor; the preference switching both ways; `ym render`/`ym info`/`ym doctor` JSON and text with `YELLOWMOON_TEST_APPLE_AUTO=fake`; app–`ym` parity with the fake on; XMP; the Preferences row shown and hidden; the Automatic card's rows and line; golden unchanged (61 identical, Apple Auto off).

- **The blind A/B kit (`tools/eval/ab`, `README.md` there, written for the owner).** The parked `autoset` kit, ported: Python 3 standard library, no network, nothing uploaded. `ab.sh prepare --ym <the ym inside the test build> PHOTOS_DIR` first runs `ym doctor --json` and stops when `apple_auto.available` is false ("Apple Auto is not available on this Mac (<reason in words>). The test needs macOS 27 with Apple Intelligence on."; `--allow-unavailable` is for the kit's tests), then renders each photo with `ym render --cpu --apple-auto on` (`apple`) and `--apple-auto off` (`rule`) at the defaults (the built-in default look, every setting at Auto), JPEG at `--long-edge 2000`, each call with a new empty `YELLOWMOON_HOME`, one photo per call (so no burst sharing) and one Apple render at a time by default (`--apple-jobs`). A photo whose answer failed (or for which Apple Auto was not available) is left out with its reason; a photo whose answer changes nothing (every value it set keep or none), or whose two pictures decode to the same pixels (the JPEGs compared without their metadata), gives no trial; the summary of `prepare` says both counts ("Apple Auto left N of M photos as they were", "Apple Auto failed on K of M photos"). `--with-camera` adds a second comparison, Apple Auto against the camera's embedded JPEG, reported and not gated. Strata are the subfolders of the photos folder.
- **The gate's provisional thresholds** (the old `docs/18` gate 5 numbers, until the owner agrees to them or others): Apple Auto's win share against the rule at least 60 % (ties as half), the Wilson 95 % lower bound above 50 % (ties left out), no stratum below 45 %, and Apple Auto's picture marked "needs a fix" no more often than the rule's. `ab.sh score results.json` prints them as "provisional thresholds, docs/19 §13" with the verdict, and adds what helps tune the table of section 5 and the prompt of section 4: the win share by step direction (brighter or darker, darker or brighter highlights and shadows, deeper or lifted blacks, more or less contrast, warmer or cooler, greener or more magenta, more or less saturation), by the colour given to the shadows or the highlights, for photos with one change only, by step (a colour with its strength), by stratum, how often the model left each field empty, and the lost trials with Apple's scene and plan. **The owner sends back `score.json`** (it carries the numbers, the model and macOS, the photo counts and the diagnostics), or `results.json` and the test's `plan.json`.
