# 19 · Apple Auto

On Macs where Apple Intelligence's on-device model can look at a photo, that model sets four of Auto's values: the exposure, a warmer or cooler nudge of the estimated white, the contrast and the saturation. It is an app setting, on by default where the model is available and absent everywhere else. Off, or on any other computer, Auto is the rule it has always been (`docs/02` Auto exposure, the illuminant estimate, contrast and saturation at 0).

The owner's decision of 2026-10-19 (`docs/16`): "We allow apple-native path for mac platforms and option remains invisible for others. Apple-only automatic exposure, contrast and temperature, saturation - turned on by default - is an app setting, that can be turned off for people who like more control." The white is a nudge of the estimate ("2 - nudge"): the model never picks the kelvin itself. Results may differ between Macs and after macOS updates ("1 - thanks fine"). The research behind it is `research/scene-understanding-2026-10-19.md`; `docs/18` is the parked `autoset` spec on the branch `claude/autoset` and is not on `main`.

Everything in this file is new after Yellowmoon 0.1.10. `PIPELINE_VERSION` stays 0.4.2: with Apple Auto off the pixels do not change. The table of section 5 and the prompt of section 4 are first versions, which the owner tunes by eye and in the blind A/B of section 13.

## 1. Where it runs

Apple Auto is **available** when all of these hold, checked in this order; the first that fails is the reason reported (`ym doctor`, `ym info`, the app's preference stays hidden):

| # | Condition | Reason when it fails (`UnavailableReason`, wire name) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A Mac on Apple silicon (`aarch64-apple-darwin`) | `not_a_mac` (Linux, Windows) or `intel_mac` |
| 2 | The binary was built with the macOS 27 SDK or later (section 11) | `built_without_support` |
| 3 | macOS 27 or later at run time | `macos_too_old` |
| 4 | `SystemLanguageModel.default.availability` | `device_not_eligible`, `apple_intelligence_off`, `model_not_ready`, `unavailable` (any other case) |
| 5 | `SystemLanguageModel.default.capabilities.contains(.vision)` | `no_image_input` |

Availability can change while the app runs (Apple Intelligence switched on, the model finishing its download), so it is read again when the app starts, when Preferences opens, when a session opens, when the preferences change and before each export starts (and by the worker before each request: a photo is not failed when Apple Auto stopped being available before its call, it waits for Apple Auto to be back). It is cheap (property reads, no model call); the app's `Core::export_start` and `ym render` read it at the same point, just before the export is queued. macOS downloads the model itself when the user turns Apple Intelligence on; Yellowmoon cannot start that download and adds no network access (hard rule 7).

## 2. The preference

`preferences.json` gets an eighth field, `apple_auto: bool`, default `true` (a file without it reads as `true`). This is the persistence the owner agreed to on 2026-10-19 ("is an app setting"); `docs/01` §Persistence lists it. It is read on every platform and only acts where Apple Auto is available; elsewhere it is kept as it is and ignored.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Place | Preferences, a `Switch` row after Display rendering |
| Shown | only while Apple Auto is available (section 1); hidden otherwise, including in the browser build |
| Title | **Auto with Apple Intelligence** |
| Hint | "Apple's on-device model looks at each photo and sets its exposure, contrast and saturation, and makes the white warmer or cooler. It runs on this Mac and sends nothing anywhere. Turn it off to use Yellowmoon's own Auto, the same on every computer." |
| Default | on |
| Turning it off | every photo's Auto values go back to the rule at once; the answers already received are kept in memory for the session, so turning it on again does not ask again |
| Turning it on | photos without an answer are asked (section 6) |

`ym` reads the same preference (section 8), so by default it makes the app's picture on the same Mac.

## 3. What the model sees

The model sees **the photo as Auto renders it without Apple**: one sRGB picture per photo, independent of everything the user has set, so that editing a photo never asks a new question.

- The photo's settings at their defaults: exposure, white balance, contrast and saturation at Auto (the rule: no Apple values), every other field at its default, no crop, no straightening, no quarter turn by the user; the camera's orientation applied, so the picture is upright.
- The look is the built-in Signature at 100 % (the white lift's reference look, `docs/02` C12 step 1b), whatever the photo's look and the default look are; Neutral when the data folder has no Signature file. The display rendering is ours (ACES 2, SDR), whatever Preferences says.
- The reduced raw path (the thumbnail's, `docs/01`) at **768 px on the long edge** (a photo smaller than that is not enlarged), rendered on the **CPU reference path** whatever the GPU policy, so the app and `ym` give the model the same bytes on every Mac; 8-bit sRGB, 3 bytes per pixel, rows top to bottom.

It is rendered when the photo's estimates are first ready and again on Re-run (whose estimates can differ only after a light preset or Pick white, which do not change it, so in practice it is the same picture). It lives in memory until the answer arrives and is then dropped (hard rule 4).

**As built (core).** "The rule" is the photo's **own** estimates: its own exposure after the highlight guard and its own noise estimate, not the burst median or the pooled noise, which depend on which other photos have developed so far; so the picture depends on the photo alone and the app and `ym` send the same bytes whatever order the photos develop in. The pictures come from a renderer of their own (`ym_core::pipeline::AdvisorPictures`) that has no graphics device, no display LUT and only the Signature look (`<data>/looks/builtin/signature.json`, the white lift's reference) and Neutral, so neither the GPU policy, nor Preferences › Display rendering, nor the look library can change them; its caches are dropped after each picture. The development job renders the picture after the thumbnail, while at most 64 pictures (about 1.2 MB each) wait in memory; a photo developed beyond that, or while Apple Auto was off, has its picture rendered by the worker when its turn comes, from the file again (the same bytes). "Not enlarged" and the size: the thumbnail's rule, `round(side × 768 / long side)`.

## 4. What it is asked

One request per photo, to `SystemLanguageModel.default` through a new `LanguageModelSession` (no tools, no transcript carried from one photo to the next). The bridge never constructs `PrivateCloudComputeLanguageModel` or any other model (hard rule 7; a test reads the Swift source for it). Guardrails are the default.

**Instructions** (prompt version 1, `APPLE_AUTO_PROMPT_VERSION = 1`):

> You help develop raw photos. You see a photo developed with neutral automatic settings. Suggest the small corrections a careful photographer would make so that it looks its best as a natural, finished picture. Keep what the photographer clearly meant: a night scene stays dark, a bright snowy scene stays bright, a silhouette stays a silhouette, warm candlelight stays warm. Choose keep when the photo already looks right.

**Prompt:** "Look at this photo and choose the corrections." followed by the picture as an `Attachment` (`CGImage`, orientation up).

**Answer** (guided generation, a `@Generable` type, fields generated in this order):

| Field | Type | Guide (`@Guide(description:)`) |
|---|---|---|
| `scene` | `String` | "What the photo shows and how it is lit, in at most eight words." |
| `exposure` | `muchDarker`, `darker`, `slightlyDarker`, `keep`, `slightlyBrighter`, `brighter`, `muchBrighter` | "Brightness of the main subject. Change it only when the subject or the whole photo is clearly too dark or too bright." |
| `white` | `muchCooler`, `cooler`, `keep`, `warmer`, `muchWarmer` | "Colour of the light. Warmer to remove a cold, bluish cast or to bring back golden light; cooler to remove an unwanted yellow or orange cast." |
| `contrast` | `muchLess`, `less`, `keep`, `more`, `muchMore` | "Difference between light and dark tones. More for a flat or hazy photo, less for harsh light with deep shadows." |
| `saturation` | `muchLess`, `less`, `keep`, `more`, `muchMore` | "Strength of the colours. More for dull colours, less for garish colours or skin that looks too red." |

**Options:** `GenerationOptions(samplingMode: .greedy, temperature: nil, maximumResponseTokens: 160)` (the macOS 27 SDK's initialiser; `sampling:` is its older spelling). Greedy sampling "always produces the same output for a given input", so one Mac with one model gives one answer per picture. Every option is set explicitly, because unset ones may change across OS or model updates.

The Swift types are `Answer`, `Exposure`, `White`, `Contrast` and `Saturation`; the schema the model sees may carry their names, so they are part of the prompt version too. A test (`crates/ym-apple/src/source_tests.rs`) reads the Swift file and checks the instructions, the prompt, the options, the fields, guides and cases and the steps below against this section.

The steps are numbers on the Rust side: exposure −3 … +3, the other three −2 … +2 (`muchDarker` −3, `keep` 0, `muchBrighter` +3; `muchCooler` −2, `muchWarmer` +2; `muchLess` −2, `muchMore` +2). `scene` is cut to 80 characters and shown only in reports.

## 5. From the answer to the picture

The steps become values through one table (`ym_core::apple_auto::TABLE`, table version 1, the owner's to tune):

| Step | −3 | −2 | −1 | 0 | +1 | +2 | +3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exposure, EV added to the rule | −1.0 | −0.6 | −0.3 | 0 | +0.3 | +0.6 | +1.0 |
| White, the picture made warmer by (mired) | | −20 | −10 | 0 | +10 | +20 | |
| Contrast (the slider's −100 … 100) | | −30 | −15 | 0 | +15 | +30 | |
| Saturation (the slider's −100 … 100) | | −30 | −15 | 0 | +15 | +30 | |

How each value acts, only while its setting is Auto:

- **Exposure.** `EV = EV_rule + ΔEV`, where `EV_rule` is the rule's value after the highlight guard and burst sharing (`docs/02` Auto exposure). The guard is not applied again: the model saw the guarded picture, and a brighter answer is a choice about the subject. The white lift is not re-sized (it is sized at the photo's own rule exposure, as for a burst median). The slider's detent and the Automatic card show `EV`.
- **White.** In Auto (light source Auto, white balance Auto, no picked white) C3·C4 is the identity: the user's white is the scene's (`docs/02` C3–C4). The nudge moves the user's white along the locus by `m` mired, keeping the scene's tint and class mix: `CCT_user = 1e6 / (1e6 / CCT_scene + m)` with `m = −10 × step` (warmer, a positive step, is a lower mired: a higher Kelvin, so the camera is balanced for a bluer light and the picture is warmer, as a 5500 K setting on a tungsten scene is). `g_user = 1 / w_A(E_user)` with `w_A` the same tinted, class-blended white as the scene's but at `CCT_user`, and the ARRI matrix takes `CCT_user`, as for a manual Kelvin. With `m = 0` the chain is exactly today's Auto (same bits). The Kelvin readout is `CCT_user` rounded to 50 K and the tint is the scene's, so the Automatic card and the Kelvin slider's detent show the white in effect. With a light preset, a picked white or a manual white balance there is no nudge.
  **As built.** The "same white at `CCT_user`" is the scene's tinted, class-blended white moved along the locus: `w_A(E_user) = w_A(E_scene) · w_locus(CCT_user) / w_locus(CCT_scene)` channel by channel (`w_locus` the blackbody/daylight model). For daylight and tungsten light, whose class whites are that model, this is exactly the class-blended white at `CCT_user`; the fluorescent and LED class whites are lists of real lights, not ordered in colour and constant beyond their ends, and evaluating them at `CCT_user` moved the white the wrong way or not at all on every shipped profile (LED at 2700 and 4000 K, fluorescent at 2700 K), so the move of the locus is applied to the scene's own white for every class instead (monotonic and in the right direction for every class, `crates/ym-color/tests/white_nudge.rs`; `docs/02` C3–C4). `CCT_user` is held in 1500–25000 K (the white tables) before the ARRI matrix holds it in 2000–11000 K. The readout's tint is the one that makes `Manual { kelvin, tint }` reproduce the nudged white (Auto's readout formula), which is the scene's tint up to the rounding of the Kelvin for daylight and tungsten light.
- **Contrast and saturation** become Auto-or-manual settings (below). Auto is the table's value; without Apple Auto it is 0, today's default.

**Contrast and saturation settings.** `DevelopSettings.contrast` and `.saturation` change from a number to `AutoOr<{ value }>` like exposure: JSON `{ "kind": "auto" }` or `{ "kind": "manual", "value": 12 }`; Auto is the default. A plain number in an older document (a `--settings` file, a golden manifest, a photo's `ym:Settings`) reads as Manual with that value, except 0, which reads as Auto (0 was the default). The range of a manual value stays −100 … 100. A patch takes either form. Copy and paste carry Auto as Auto (the photo pasted onto uses its own answer). Re-run puts both back to Auto, with exposure and white, while Apple Auto acts on the photo (its state is pending, answered or failed: exactly when the Automatic card shows the Contrast and Saturation rows, section 10); otherwise Re-run leaves them as they are, as before Apple Auto (decided in review on 2026-10-19: without Apple Auto the card has no Contrast or Saturation row, so Re-run must not reset a value the card does not show). `DevelopSettings::reset_automatic(apple_auto)` takes that from the session (`AppleAuto::acts`). The pinned settings of an export (`ym:Settings`) pin Auto contrast and saturation to the numbers they resolved to, as they pin Auto exposure; white balance stays Auto there, and the nudge is in `ym:AppleAuto` (section 9).

**Bursts.** The rule shares the exposure across a burst (median of the frames' own values). Apple's steps are shared the same way, field by field: each of the four steps is the median of the burst's answered frames (the lower of the two middle values for an even count); frames that failed are left out, and when every frame failed the burst uses the rule. A photo's export therefore waits for its whole burst's answers. A frame that failed takes its burst's steps all the same (its `ym:Auto` is then `apple`, and its `ym:AppleAuto` says it failed and why).

## 6. When it asks, and who waits

- Each session has one Apple Auto worker: one request at a time, in this order: the photo on screen, then the other photos in session order. It starts once a photo's estimates are ready (it needs the picture of section 3) and runs only while the preference is on and Apple Auto is available.
- **The viewer does not wait.** A photo shows with the rule as soon as its estimates are ready; when the answer arrives its Auto values change, the picture and its thumbnail are rendered again (the `estimates_changed` path a burst median already uses), and the Automatic card follows. Until then the card says Apple Intelligence is looking at the photo (section 10).
- **Exports and `ym` wait.** An export's photo waits until it and its burst have an answer or a failure, exactly as it waits for a burst's shared exposure (`docs/01` "App/CLI parity for early exports"), and its turn moves up the worker's order. `ym render` and `ym info` wait for the answers of the photos they develop.
- **Time.** About 1–4 s per photo on Apple silicon (an estimate; measured in section 13): a 500-photo `ym render` takes about 8–33 minutes longer. `--apple-auto off` skips it.
- **Failures.** A request that has not answered after 30 s is abandoned. `rateLimited` and `concurrentRequests` are retried twice, 2 s apart. Any other error (refusal, guardrail, unsupported language, context too large, assets unavailable, a bad answer) fails the photo at once. A failed photo uses the rule for all four values and says why (`ym info`, the Automatic card, `ym:AppleAuto`). Re-run asks again.
- **Re-run** recomputes the estimates and asks again; a session that is closed stops its worker and drops the answers.
- **As built.** The order is the photo on screen (the session's current photo), then the photos an export is waiting for in the order the export takes them, each followed by the frames of its burst that have no answer yet (its export waits for them too; `Session::prioritise_apple_auto` adds them, so every caller gets it), then the others in filmstrip order. The 30 s limit is the advisor's: the bridge passes `REQUEST_TIMEOUT_MS` to the Swift call, and the core never abandons a call itself (an advisor that does not return holds the worker, not the app). A call that is running when the session closes is not waited for; its answer is dropped. `ym`'s "waits for the answers" is `Session::wait_idle`, which waits for the worker too. A request is panic-safe as a whole: a panic in the advisor, the picture, storing the result, refreshing the served estimates or the event sink fails that photo with kind `other` and the panic's message (it uses the rule, an export of it does not wait), ends the request, wakes whoever waits and leaves the worker running for the next photo. A worker that cannot be started, or that stops in a panic outside a request, leaves the session as one without an advisor (Apple Auto off and not available, nothing waits for it). An answer that moves a photo's values renders its thumbnail again in the core (`thumbnail_ready`; a photo still developing gets it after its first thumbnail); an answer of keep on every step changes no pixel and renders nothing, but still sends `estimates_changed` so the Automatic card's line follows. **A known cost:** that thumbnail is rendered from the raw, so an answered photo outside the mosaic cache's window (`MOSAIC_CACHE_LEN`, the photo on screen and its neighbours) is decoded again, about one decode per answered photo whose values moved; and switching the preference off and on again renders the thumbnail of every answered photo again each time (about one decode each), since the photos go back to the rule and then to their answers. Accepted for now: it happens in the background and only when values move. Which Auto acts is decided in this order: no advisor is off; `--apple-auto off` is off; not available is unavailable, whatever the preference says (it is kept and ignored there); the preference off is off unless the run says `on`; else it acts (`ym_core::apple_auto::Activity::decide`).

## 7. Determinism (hard rule 8)

Apple's model is part of "version" under rule 8: the same photo, settings, Yellowmoon version, macOS build, model variant and prompt version give the same answer and the same bytes on one Mac (greedy sampling, a picture rendered on the CPU). Different Macs or macOS builds may answer differently, which the owner accepted. So every export records which Auto made it (`ym:Auto`, `ym:AppleAuto`), `ym info` and `ym doctor` print the model and macOS build, and two different exports of one file can always be explained.

- **App and `ym`.** On one Mac both read the same preference and send the same picture, so they write the same bytes (hard rule 6). The parity tests and the golden tests run with Apple Auto off (no advisor, or `--apple-auto off`), which gives today's bytes on every machine.
- **The version string.** `Apple Intelligence · <variant display name> · macOS <version> (<build>) · prompt 1 · table 1`, for example `Apple Intelligence · AFM 3 Core · macOS 27.0.1 (27A404) · prompt 1 · table 1`.
- Apple's models are used at runtime only. Their answers never train, label or tune anything of ours (Apple's developer agreements; `docs/16`), and the table's values are chosen by eye, never fitted to Apple answers.

## 8. The command line

| Option | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `--apple-auto on\|off` | Use Apple Auto for this run. Default: the `apple_auto` preference where Apple Auto is available, off elsewhere. `on` where it is not available prints "Apple Auto is not available on this computer (<reason in words>). Auto uses Yellowmoon's own settings." on stderr and continues (exit code unchanged). |
| `--contrast auto\|N`, `--saturation auto\|N` | `auto` is new; a number is Manual, as before. |

- `ym render --json` per file: `auto_source` (`rule` or `apple`) and, when Apple Auto was on, `apple_auto` (below). Its `auto` object gains `apple`, `contrast`, `saturation` and `source` as `ym info`'s does, and `settings.contrast` and `.saturation` are `{ "kind", "value" }` (section 5) instead of numbers, with Apple Auto off or unavailable too.
- `ym info --json`: `auto` gains `contrast` and `saturation` (the Auto values in effect) and `source`; a new `apple_auto` object: `{ "state": "answered" | "failed" | "off" | "unavailable", "reason": "…" | null, "model": "…", "os": "…", "prompt": 1, "table": 1, "steps": { "exposure": 1, "white": 0, "contrast": 1, "saturation": 0 }, "scene": "…" }` (`steps` and `scene` only when answered). Text: a row **Apple Auto** reading, for example, `Brighter +0.3 EV · contrast +15 · "Backlit portrait at dusk"`, `Off`, `Not available (Apple Intelligence is off)` or `Failed (rate limited); Yellowmoon's own Auto`.
- `ym doctor`: `apple_auto: { "available": bool, "reason": … | null, "model": …, "os": …, "preference": bool, "prompt": 1, "table": 1 }` and a text row.
- Reasons in words: `not_a_mac` "this is not a Mac", `intel_mac` "this Mac has an Intel processor", `built_without_support` "this build of Yellowmoon was made without it", `macos_too_old` "it needs macOS 27 or later", `device_not_eligible` "this Mac can't run Apple Intelligence", `apple_intelligence_off` "Apple Intelligence is off in System Settings", `model_not_ready` "Apple Intelligence is still downloading its model", `no_image_input` "Apple's model on this Mac can't look at pictures", `unavailable` "Apple Intelligence is not available"; failures: `timeout` "no answer within 30 seconds", `rate_limited` "macOS asked Yellowmoon to wait", `refused`, `guardrail` "Apple's safety check stopped it", `unsupported_language`, `context_too_large`, `assets_unavailable`, `bad_answer`, `other` (with Apple's message).

**As built (CLI, `docs/10` Apple Auto).**

- **Options.** `--apple-auto on|off` (any case) on `ym render` and `ym info`, the commands that develop photos; without it `AppleAutoMode::FromPreference`. `ym` hands the core `ym_apple::system_advisor()` as the app does. The notice is a warning (`Warning: Apple Auto is not available on this computer (this is not a Mac). Auto uses Yellowmoon's own settings.`; `--quiet` hides it, like every warning), printed once per run after the core opens, and only for `on`: without the option a computer where Apple Auto cannot run says nothing, as the app hides its row. `ym render` reads the availability again just before it queues the export (section 1, as `Core::export_start` does; `Runtime::apple_auto_notice_before_export`), so the export follows an Apple Auto that came or went during the run; when the run wanted it (`on`, or the preference on without the option), it was available and is not any more, the same notice is printed then, still once per run. `--contrast auto` and `--saturation auto` (any case) put Auto back over a value a `--settings` file set by hand; the `settings` of the JSON pin Auto to the number it resolved to, as for exposure. `ym doctor --bench` and `ym looks bake --preview` give the core no advisor (Apple Auto off): one times the render path, the other renders the Signature look's contact sheet.
- **JSON.** `auto_source` is `rule` or `apple` (`AutoEstimates::source`, what `ym:Auto` records) and comes after `auto`, then `apple_auto`, then `settings`. `apple_auto` always has `state`, `reason`, `model`, `os`, `prompt` and `table`, and `steps` and `scene` when answered; `reason` is the wire name of the unavailable reason or of the failure, else null; `model` and `os` are those of the model that answered or was asked, null for `off`, `unavailable` and `pending` (`pending` only after Ctrl-C: `ym` waits for the answers); `steps` are the photo's own answer, the steps `ym:AppleAuto` writes, while the values in effect can be its burst's. Its keys are sorted, as every value below the top level of the CLI's objects. `ym info` writes it for every developed photo (`null` with `auto` for a file it cannot develop); `ym render` only for a photo Apple Auto acted on (answered, failed or pending). So a run with Apple Auto off or unavailable is not today's JSON plus `auto_source`: in both commands `auto` also gains `apple` (`{ "state": "off" }` or `{ "state": "unavailable", "reason": … }`), `contrast`, `saturation` and `source`, and `settings.contrast` and `settings.saturation` change from a number to `{ "kind": "manual", "value": 0 }` (the number Auto resolved to, pinned as for exposure; `crates/ym-cli/tests/snapshots/render__render_json_shape.snap`), so a script that read them as numbers needs updating (`CHANGELOG.md`).
- **The row of `ym info`** comes after Auto white. It describes the steps in effect, in the order exposure, white, contrast, saturation, only those that are not keep, with the table's numbers: `Brighter +0.3 EV`, `Darker -1.0 EV`, `warmer 10 mired`, `cooler 20 mired`, `contrast +15`, `saturation -30`, joined by ` · ` with the first word capitalised, then the scene in quotes; `No change` when every step is keep. A burst's shared steps that differ from the photo's own answer add ` (shared with its burst)` after the changes. A failure reads `Failed (<failure in words>); Yellowmoon's own Auto`, with the burst's steps instead when the burst has them (`Failed (no answer within 30 seconds); its burst's answer: Darker -0.3 EV`); a failure of kind `other` adds Apple's message (`Failed (an error: <message>)`). The other states read `Off`, `Not available (<reason in words>)` and `Waiting for Apple Intelligence`. The examples of the list above in the words of `UnavailableReason::words` and `FailureKind::words`: `Not available (Apple Intelligence is off in System Settings)`, `Failed (macOS asked Yellowmoon to wait); Yellowmoon's own Auto`.
- **`ym doctor`.** The status is the app's (`AppleAutoStatus`, built from the advisor and the preference without opening a session). The row `Apple Auto` after Render path reads `available · <version string of section 7>`, with ` · turned off in Preferences` when the preference is off, or `not available (<reason in words>)`; the report's first sentence now lists "whether Apple Auto is available". `model` and `os` are null where it is not available.
- **Determinism.** The golden tool renders with `--apple-auto off` (its test stand-in refuses a render without it), and so do `tests/parity.rs`, `tests/parity_gpu.rs`, the JSON snapshots of `ym-cli` and the tools that study Yellowmoon's own Auto or the metadata (`tools/eval/autoexp.py`, `tools/eval/demosaic/blind_look.py`, `tools/oracle/compare_metadata.py`); `tools/readme` and `tools/social-card` do not run `ym`. The parity test's Apple Auto leg gives the core `FakeAdvisor` and `ym` `YELLOWMOON_TEST_APPLE_AUTO=fake` (no option, the preference on in a new app folder) and compares a JPEG, a 16-bit TIFF and a DNG of a folder of two photos, byte for byte, with `ym:Auto` `apple`. `src-tauri/tests/app_cli_parity.rs` runs `ym render` too and needs the same `--apple-auto off` (the app lane's file).

## 9. Export metadata

Two `ym:` properties after `ym:Models`, in this order (`docs/09` table):

| Property | Example |
|---|---|
| `ym:Auto` | `rule` or `apple` (`apple` when the photo's Auto values came from an answer, even if the user then set all four by hand) |
| `ym:AppleAuto` | only when Apple Auto was on for the photo: compact JSON, keys sorted: `{"model":"AFM 3 Core","os":"27.0.1 (27A404)","prompt":1,"result":"answered","scene":"…","steps":{"contrast":1,"exposure":1,"saturation":0,"white":-1},"table":1}`, or `"result":"failed"` with `"reason"` and no steps |

## 10. The app

- **Preferences:** the row of section 2.
- **Automatic card** (Look tab): while Apple Auto is on for the session, two rows between White balance and Noise, **Contrast** (`+15`, `0`, signed with no unit) and **Saturation**, each with the AUTO tag while the setting is Auto and EDITED otherwise; the arrow opens the Look tab's Tone card. Under the rows one muted line: "Set with Apple Intelligence." (answered), "Apple Intelligence is looking at this photo…" (asked or waiting), "Apple Intelligence couldn't look at this photo, so Auto uses Yellowmoon's own settings." (failed). With Apple Auto off or unavailable the card is today's card.
- **Sliders.** The Contrast and Saturation sliders (Look › Tone, Light › Exposure's Contrast, Colour's Saturation) behave like the Exposure slider: the detent and Reset are at the Auto value, a value equal to it is sent as Auto (`manualOrAuto`), any other value as Manual.
- **Re-run** puts contrast and saturation back to Auto with the others while Apple Auto acts on the photo (pending, answered or failed: the card shows their rows); otherwise it leaves them as they are (section 5).
- Nothing else changes: no badge, no extra button, no question at first launch (Apple's guidelines ask to say where AI is used and to offer a way without it: the Preferences row and the card's line do both).
- **As built (app).** `docs/11-ui.md` *Apple Auto* has the details. The shell gives the core `ym_apple::system_advisor()` (`AppConfig::advisor`; the shell's tests give none, or a scripted advisor) and has one more command, `apple_auto_status()` (the core's `AppleAutoStatus`, the availability read again at each call), which Preferences asks each time it opens; the row is shown while `available`. The card's rows and line show while the photo's `estimates.apple.state` is `pending`, `answered` or `failed` (that is, Apple Auto on for the session), so the card needs nothing but the photo's settings. The core's `estimates_changed` (an answer, a failure, the preference changed, a burst's median) reaches the webview as `settings_changed`, and the store reads the photo's settings again. The arrow of Contrast and Saturation stays on the Look tab: it scrolls the Tone card into view and puts the focus on that row's slider. The wire type of `contrast` and `saturation` is `PercentSetting` (`{ kind: "auto" }` | `{ kind: "manual", value }`); a patch on the bridge also takes a plain number, as the core does (0 is Auto). The browser build's mock is not available by default; `?mockAppleAuto=unavailable|pending|answered|failed` plays the other cases (`src/api.mock.ts`). The app's parity tests with `ym` run with Apple Auto off on both sides (no advisor in the app's core, `--apple-auto off` for `ym`).

## 11. The bridge (`crates/ym-apple`)

- A new crate, `ym-apple`, depended on by `ym-cli` and the app (never by `ym-core`, the golden tool or the tests of other crates). It implements `ym_core::apple_auto::AutoAdvisor` (section 12) as `AppleAdvisor` and gives one constructor, `ym_apple::system_advisor() -> Arc<dyn AutoAdvisor>`.
- **Swift.** `crates/ym-apple/swift/AppleAuto.swift`: the `@Generable` answer, the instructions, `availability`, `ask`. It exposes four C functions with `@_cdecl` (C types only), declared in `crates/ym-apple/swift/ym_apple.h` and in Rust with `unsafe extern "C"`:
  - `int32_t ym_apple_auto_availability(void)`: 0 available, else the reason code (section 1, in table order from 3: 3 `macos_too_old`, 4 `device_not_eligible`, 5 `apple_intelligence_off`, 6 `model_not_ready`, 7 `unavailable`, 8 `no_image_input`).
  - `int32_t ym_apple_auto_model(char *buf, size_t len)`: the variant's display name, UTF-8, NUL-terminated (0 on success).
  - `int32_t ym_apple_auto_os(char *buf, size_t len)`: macOS version and build, e.g. `27.0.1 (27A404)`.
  - `int32_t ym_apple_auto_ask(const uint8_t *rgb, uint32_t width, uint32_t height, uint32_t timeout_ms, YmAppleAnswer *out, char *message, size_t message_len)`: blocks the calling thread until the answer, an error or the timeout; 0 on success, else an error code (1 unavailable, 2 rate limited, 3 timeout, 4 refused, 5 guardrail, 6 unsupported language, 7 context too large, 8 assets unavailable, 9 concurrent requests, 10 bad answer, 11 other) with Apple's message in `message`. `YmAppleAnswer { int32_t exposure, white, contrast, saturation; char scene[128]; }`. It checks the availability again first (1 when the model is not available); `timeout_ms` 0 means no limit. The Swift side takes `out` as `void *` and writes the fields at their C offsets (0, 4, 8, 12, the scene at 16; 144 bytes), which the header and `src/abi.rs` assert. Strings are cut at a UTF-8 boundary to fit and always NUL-terminated.
- **Build** (`crates/ym-apple/build.rs`, no new dependency): on `aarch64-apple-darwin` with an SDK of version 27 or later (`xcrun --sdk macosx --show-sdk-version`), unless `YM_APPLE_BRIDGE=off`, it compiles the Swift file with `xcrun --sdk macosx swiftc -parse-as-library -emit-library -static -module-name YmAppleAuto -swift-version 5 -O -target arm64-apple-macos14.0` (or the later `MACOSX_DEPLOYMENT_TARGET`, so the Swift never targets an older macOS than the binary) into `OUT_DIR`, links it, links `Foundation` and `CoreGraphics`, adds the Swift runtime library paths that `swiftc -print-target-info` reports and the compiler runtime (`clang_rt.osx`, for `#available`), and sets `cfg(ym_apple_bridge)`. A compile error with such an SDK fails the build (a silent stub would hide a bug). Everywhere else (Linux, Intel, an older SDK, `YM_APPLE_BRIDGE=off`) it compiles nothing and the crate reports `not_a_mac`, `intel_mac` or `built_without_support`; a Mac build without the bridge prints a cargo warning. Inside the Swift file every Foundation Models use is behind `@available(macOS 27.0, *)` or `#available(macOS 27.0, *)`.
- **The weak link.** `FoundationModels` is linked **weakly** (`-weak_framework FoundationModels`, a `LC_LOAD_WEAK_DYLIB`), so the app and `ym` still start on macOS 14 to 26. Cargo has no weak kind of `rustc-link-lib`, and `rustc-link-arg` from a library's build script reaches only that library's own tests and examples, so: the Swift is compiled with `-Xfrontend -disable-autolink-framework -Xfrontend FoundationModels` (the object asks for no strong link); `ym-apple` declares `links = "ym_apple_auto"` and publishes `bridge=1` when it compiled the Swift; and each final binary's build script (`crates/ym-cli/build.rs`, `src-tauri/build.rs`) passes `-Wl,-weak_framework,FoundationModels` when `DEP_YM_APPLE_AUTO_BRIDGE` is `1` (`ym-apple`'s own build script does it for its tests and the probe). A binary that depends on `ym-apple` without that line fails to link instead of linking the framework strongly.
- **Threads.** `ask` runs the async Swift call in a detached `Task` and waits on a semaphore; it is called only from the session's Apple Auto worker thread, never from the main thread.
- **The test advisor.** With `YELLOWMOON_TEST_APPLE_AUTO=fake` in the environment, `system_advisor()` returns `ym_core::apple_auto::FakeAdvisor` on any platform: available, model `Test advisor`, os `test`, steps from the picture's statistics (section 12). It exists for the tests of `ym` and the app on Linux and on Macs without Apple Intelligence, like `YELLOWMOON_GPU`. With `YELLOWMOON_TEST_APPLE_AUTO=none` it returns `NoAdvisor`, never available, for the reason a build without the bridge gives on that platform (`not_a_mac`, `intel_mac`, `built_without_support`): the tests of `ym` that expect the rule run with it, so they pass on a Mac with Apple Intelligence too.
- **CI.** The Swift code needs the macOS 27 SDK, which only the `xcode-27` runner image has (GitHub's, macOS 27 on Apple silicon). `.github/workflows/apple-bridge.yml` builds `ym-apple` and its probe there with the bridge, checks with `nm` that the probe carries the bridge's symbols and with `otool -l` that FoundationModels is a weak load, runs clippy and `ym-apple`'s tests (availability without a crash, the fake, the source checks), runs the probe (with Apple's model and with the fake) and builds the stub with `YM_APPLE_BRIDGE=off`. A second job runs that probe on `macos-14`, `macos-15` and `macos-26`, where it must start and report `macos_too_old`: the proof that the weak link works. Once `ym-cli` and the app depend on `ym-apple`, the job also builds them with the bridge and prints `ym doctor`. On that image macOS 27's dyld refuses the dylibs that Rust 1.97 strips in release ("mis-aligned LINKEDIT string pool": proc macros fail to load, so nothing builds), while 1.97 without stripping and Rust 1.99 work (a print-only step shows it at every run); the workflow therefore sets `CARGO_PROFILE_RELEASE_STRIP` and `CARGO_PROFILE_RELEASE_BUILD_OVERRIDE_STRIP` to `none`, and the release workflows need the same, or a newer pinned Rust, when they move to `xcode-27`. The release and build workflows move to `xcode-27`, so the published app carries the bridge; the x86_64 half of the universal binary gets the stub.

## 12. The core (`ym_core::apple_auto`)

```text
trait AutoAdvisor: Send + Sync {
    fn availability(&self) -> Availability;                      // Available(AdvisorInfo) | Unavailable(UnavailableReason)
    fn ask(&self, picture: &AdvisorPicture) -> Result<AdvisorAnswer, AdvisorError>;
}
AdvisorInfo    { model: String, os: String }                     // the prompt and table versions are the core's constants
AdvisorPicture { width, height, rgb: Vec<u8> }                  // section 3
AdvisorAnswer  { steps: AutoSteps, scene: String }
AutoSteps      { exposure: i8 /* -3..=3 */, white: i8, contrast: i8, saturation: i8 /* -2..=2 */ }
AdvisorError   { kind: FailureKind, message: String }
FakeAdvisor                                                      // deterministic, for tests
```

- `Core::open` takes an optional advisor; the app and `ym` pass `ym_apple::system_advisor()`, every other caller (tests, golden, examples) passes none, which is the same as unavailable.
- `AutoEstimates` gains `apple: AppleAuto` (`Off`, `Unavailable(reason)`, `Pending`, `Answered { answer, info }`, `Failed { kind, message, info }`) and the resolved Auto values `contrast`, `saturation` (0 without an answer) and `white_shift_mired` (in `ColorEstimates`, 0 without an answer). The session's `refresh_effective` applies the burst medians of section 5 after the rule's.
- `AutoSummary` (the `estimates` of `ResolvedSettings`) gains `contrast`, `saturation`, `source` and `apple`; `exposure_ev`, `kelvin` and `tint` include Apple's values.
- An answered step outside its range, or one the bridge could not decode, fails the photo with `bad_answer`.
- **The fake.** Mean sRGB luma `L` (0…1, Rec.709 weights on the 8-bit values) and its standard deviation `S`: exposure +1 when `L` < 0.30, −1 when `L` > 0.65, else 0; contrast +1 when `S` < 0.15, else 0; white 0; saturation 0; scene "Test picture".
- **As built (the core's API for the CLI and app lanes).**
  - `CoreOptions::advisor: Option<Arc<dyn AutoAdvisor>>` (default `None`) and `CoreOptions::apple_auto: AppleAutoMode` (`from_preference` by default, `on`, `off`); `Core::set_apple_auto_mode(mode)` changes the mode later (for `ym --apple-auto`). The session's part is `SessionEnv::with_apple_auto(AppleAutoEnv { advisor, pictures, preference, mode, retry_pause })`; `Core::open` builds it with `AdvisorPictures` and the preference.
  - `Core::apple_auto_status() -> AppleAutoStatus` reads the availability again: `{ "available", "reason", "info": { "model", "os" } | null, "preference", "mode", "active", "prompt", "table" }`. Without an advisor it is not available and has no reason. `Core::preferences_set` with `apple_auto` applies it at once.
  - `AutoEstimates` gains `contrast`, `saturation`, `apple` (the photo's `AppleAuto`) and `apple_steps` (the steps in effect: the photo's or its burst's), with `source()`; `ColorEstimates` gains `white_shift_mired`. Only the served estimates carry Apple's values; the photo's own stay the rule.
  - `AutoSummary` (the `estimates` of `ResolvedSettings`, the `auto` object of `ym --json`) gains `contrast`, `saturation`, `source` (`"rule"` or `"apple"`) and `apple` (`{ "state": "off" | "unavailable" | "pending" | "answered" | "failed", … }`: `reason` for unavailable; `answer: { steps, scene }` and `info` for answered; `kind`, `message` and `info` for failed). `AutoFlags` (`auto` of `ResolvedSettings`) gains `contrast` and `saturation`; `ResolvedValues.contrast` and `.saturation` are the values in effect.
  - `DevelopSettings.contrast` and `.saturation` are `PercentSetting = AutoOr<PercentValue { value }>`; `SettingsPatch.contrast` and `.saturation` take either form.
  - `Session`: `refresh_apple_auto()`, `set_apple_auto_preference(bool)`, `set_apple_auto_mode(mode)`, `apple_auto_status()`, `apple_auto_active()`, `prioritise_apple_auto(&[PhotoId])` (the export queue calls it; each photo's burst frames without an answer move up with it), `apple_auto_asks()`; `estimates_final` and `wait_idle` wait for the answers while Apple Auto acts.

## 13. Tests and checks

- **Linux and every Mac (CI):** settings (Auto contrast and saturation, the old number forms, patches, copy and paste, Re-run, pinned settings, the schema); the table and its bounds; the white nudge (`m = 0` gives today's chain bit for bit; ±10 and ±20 mired move the Kelvin readout the right way and the scene white the right way in AWG4; manual white, a preset and Pick white get no nudge); burst medians; the worker's order, the viewer not waiting, exports and `ym` waiting, timeouts, retries and failures, with the fake and with a scripted advisor; the preference switching both ways; `ym render`/`ym info`/`ym doctor` JSON and text with `YELLOWMOON_TEST_APPLE_AUTO=fake`; app–`ym` parity with the fake on; XMP; the Preferences row shown and hidden; the Automatic card's rows and line; golden unchanged (61 identical, Apple Auto off).
- **The `xcode-27` runner:** the bridge compiles and links; `ym doctor` runs and reports a reason (a virtual Mac has no Apple Intelligence, so `device_not_eligible` or `apple_intelligence_off` is expected); `ym-apple`'s tests pass.
- **On Mykyta's Mac (step 3, "Waiting on Mykyta"):** the preference appears; an ad-hoc-signed app and `ym` from Terminal may call the model (no entitlement is documented, `docs/12`); answers arrive and are repeatable (the same photo asked twice gives the same steps); the time per photo on his Mac; whether `ym` is rate-limited as a background process; after a request that timed out (30 s), whether the next request meets `rate_limited` or `concurrent_requests` (the abandoned call may still be running inside macOS: the core does not wait for it, and two retries 2 s apart may not be enough); the table's values by eye on contact sheets; then the blind A/B with the A/B kit (`tools/eval/ab`, below): Apple Auto against the rule on fresh photos, stratified, the gate to keep the default on (no stratum below 45 %, a win overall, no rise in photos that need a fix; thresholds need his agreement).
- **The blind A/B kit (`tools/eval/ab`, `README.md` there, written for the owner).** The parked `autoset` kit, ported: Python 3 standard library, no network, nothing uploaded. `ab.sh prepare --ym <the ym inside the test build> PHOTOS_DIR` first runs `ym doctor --json` and stops when `apple_auto.available` is false ("Apple Auto is not available on this Mac (<reason in words>). The test needs macOS 27 with Apple Intelligence on."; `--allow-unavailable` is for the kit's tests), then renders each photo with `ym render --cpu --apple-auto on` (`apple`) and `--apple-auto off` (`rule`) at the defaults (the built-in default look, every setting at Auto), JPEG at `--long-edge 2000`, each call with a new empty `YELLOWMOON_HOME`, one photo per call (so no burst sharing) and one Apple render at a time by default (`--apple-jobs`). From the `apple` render's `--json` it keeps `apple_auto` (state, reason, steps, scene) in `plan.json`, the key, with the model, macOS, prompt and table versions from `ym doctor` and each render's Auto values. A photo whose answer failed (or for which Apple Auto was not available) is left out with its reason; a photo whose steps are all keep, or whose two pictures decode to the same pixels (the JPEGs compared without their metadata), gives no trial; the summary of `prepare` says both counts ("Apple Auto left N of M photos as they were", "Apple Auto failed on K of M photos"). `--with-camera` adds a second comparison, Apple Auto against the camera's embedded JPEG, reported and not gated. The plan and the sides come from a seed (default 20261019); picture paths are folder codes free of variant names; the page keeps the answers in localStorage under the plan id and exports `results.json`; `--resume` keeps the pictures and the `ym` records (`records/`, part of the key) already made and asks Apple again only where its answer failed. Strata are the subfolders of the photos folder.
- **The gate's provisional thresholds** (the old `docs/18` gate 5 numbers, until the owner agrees to them or others): Apple Auto's win share against the rule at least 60 % (ties as half), the Wilson 95 % lower bound above 50 % (ties left out), no stratum below 45 %, and Apple Auto's picture marked "needs a fix" no more often than the rule's. `ab.sh score results.json` prints them as "provisional thresholds, docs/19 §13" with the verdict, and adds what helps tune the table of section 5: the win share by step direction (brighter, darker, warmer, cooler, more or less contrast and saturation), for photos with one change only, by step, by stratum, and the lost trials with Apple's scene. **The owner sends back `score.json`** (it carries the numbers, the model and macOS, the photo counts and the diagnostics), or `results.json` and the test's `plan.json`.
