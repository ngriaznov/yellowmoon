## The dark glass interface

Decided on 2026-10-16 (the owner: "Great, merge C2 and V1 branch to main and build new version"; `docs/16`). It replaces the look of the screens and the controls, not their layout: the same cells in the same places with the same sizes and copy, every control a glasscn component.

- **Dark only.** `index.html` sets `class="dark"`, `data-theme="dark"`, `color-scheme: dark` and paints the root `#0f0f10` inline, so nothing is white before the first style sheet loads. `--paper` of the dark theme in `tokens.css` is that colour. The window behind the page, the letterbox of the core's frames and the window's `backgroundColor` are the same colour (*The native viewport*, below). There is no light theme in the app; the light values of `tokens.css` are the pre-glass design, kept for history.
- **Material.** Panes are glasscn's glass: a translucent dark tint, a thin light rim, a soft shadow, backdrop blur where a pane sits over the aurora. The header, the tool panel and the Export panel are one glass pane each; the cells inside them are separated by 1 px light rims (`--rim`), not by 3 px rules. The filmstrip has a near-opaque surface of its own (no blur), the same neutral grey, a little darker at the foot (`--strip-neutral`).
- **Colour.** *Yellow only as a button accent*: the accent is #FFC20E with black text on it (`--primary`), used by the primary buttons (Open photos, Next, Export), pressed toggles (Before, Crop, Pick white, AUTO) and the selected tab's underline. No large area carries yellow or a tab colour as a tint: the title blocks, the Start block, the wizard rail, the drop zone, tags and notices are neutral glass. Each tab keeps its own accent for what belongs to it (the slider range, a changed value's ring and text, the selected look tile), lightened for the dark ground with black text on it; the tab colours are in `src/styles/glass-c2.css`. A disabled primary button is neutral glass, not faded yellow. Red is for what failed.
- **Ground.** Neutral graphite, the owner's choice of 2026-10-17 (`docs/16`, "like the moon is grey"): a still aurora of three grey blobs on a near-black ground, glasscn's Graphite palette with every hue taken out (`animate={false}`, no running animation under the glass). The frost, the fills and the grey labels have no hue either (glasscn's dark material gives them a faint violet, hue 285, which its `saturate(180%)` frost made visible), so the panes are pure grey and the colour on screen is the photos', the yellow buttons' and the tab accents'. Until 0.1.8 the aurora was cool (deep blue, teal, violet). On the Photos screen the aurora is cut out of the photo cell (`.ym-aurora` is masked by the rectangle the screen reports as `--cell-x/y/w/h`), because with the native viewport the photo is a layer behind the page.
- **Controls.** `Button` (glass for actions, prominent for the primary one, link for text actions), `Toggle` (Before, Crop, Pick white and the AUTO buttons, all `aria-pressed`), line `Tabs`, `SegmentedControl`, `Slider` with `Input`, `Switch`, `Select`, `SearchField`, `DropdownMenu` (Fit; the default look on Start), `Dialog`, `Tooltip`, `Badge`, `Progress`, `Item` (every row of the Automatic card, the queue, the wizard, Preferences and About), `Card` (the drop zone, the wizard tiles) and `Collapsible` (a look library's header). `src/components/` keeps the app's own props, roles and accessible names around them; **a control that glasscn has is never hand-written**, and `e2e/glass.spec.ts` audits all 34 page states for it (the 30 of the dark glass interface, the two of Colour warp and the two of Apple Auto). What stays ours has no glasscn counterpart: the colour wheels, the curve plot, the histogram, look tiles, filmstrip thumbnails, the crop frame, the pick-white layer, the photo cell and the ruled bands of the tool panel (cards, title block, field rows).
- **Tooltips.** A `title` on a `Button` is a glass tooltip (it also shows on a locked button, so that it can say why it is locked) and the button's accessible description (a hidden span and `aria-describedby`). A test reads it with `toHaveAccessibleDescription`, not as a `title` attribute. Every `Button` has the same tree whether or not it has a title (a span for the tooltip around the `<button>`), so a button whose title appears or goes (the wizard's Next, the Export panel's estimate, Import in the LUT dialog) keeps its element, and the keyboard focus stays on it. The tooltip opens on hover only, never on keyboard focus: an open tooltip is Radix's topmost layer and takes the first Esc, so a dialog or a mode (Pick white, Crop) would need a second press. For a keyboard user the reason is the description, and the wizard's footer says it in a note. The same wrapper is on the Update cell (a locked glass button takes no pointer events, so a native `title` on it would never show). A locked `Toggle` (Crop and Pick white while a photo is developing, Pick white while cropping) is muted and inert like a locked `Button`.
- **The filmstrip.** The strip runs the full width of its row; there is no counter cell. One line of muted text in its top inset says where you are, "4 of 12 · 3 picked" ("3 to export" in the Export panel), and describes the strip for assistive technology (`aria-describedby`). Each thumbnail is as tall as the row less the tool panel's inset (24 px) above and below, 3:2, all one size, so they are larger than before (about 124 × 83 px at 1440 × 900, where the pre-glass strip had 60 × 40 px and 90 × 60 for the current one); the current one has a 3 px accent ring. Each thumbnail has glasscn's context menu with Remove from filmstrip (*Removing photos*, below); the thumbnail a menu is open for is ringed.
- **Selection frames are drawn whole (2026-10-20, the owner: "Selection frame is cut off").** The ring of the look in use (a 2 px outline 2 px off the picture: 4 px out), the focus ring (3 px, 2 px off: 5 px out) and the crop's handles are drawn outside the box they belong to, so a box that clips its content must not stand within a ring's width of them. Where one does, it gives the ring room instead of the layout moving: a Look section's body is glasscn's `Collapsible`, which clips so that its height can animate, and it kept the ring of a tile in the first row, the first column or the last column 4 px short, so `.ym-look-group__body` has 8 px of padding on every side and the same as a negative margin (no tile moves); a tab stop that scrolls a tile into the tool panel centres it (the browser's own rule for an element that is partly out of view), so the panel's edge needs nothing; the filmstrip needs nothing more than its 24 px inset and the reveal margin (24 px) it already keeps at both ends; and the crop's handles on the edge of the photo cell are drawn back inside it by their own size (`--crop-handle`, 14 px here) with their focus ring inside them, the frame's focus ring inside the frame. `e2e/selection-frame.spec.ts` measures every ring against everything that clips it (`e2e/helpers/frames.ts`: the ring's outer edge from its outline and shadows, against the padding box of each ancestor that clips, and the window) for each look tile, every photo of the strip from its start to its end, and the crop, at 1440 × 900, 1180 × 760 and 1800 × 1100; a new selected thing that stands near the edge of a clipping box needs its line there.
- **Keyboard, as Radix gives it.** Tabs and segmented controls take the Tab key once and move with the arrows (roving focus); a slider's knob takes the arrows (Shift or Page keys ×10, Home, End); the same keys with ⌘, ⌃ or ⌥ are left to the app (`Slider` stops them before Radix sees them). A menu (`Menu`) opens on the checked item and the arrows wrap. While a dialog or a menu is open Radix hides the rest of the page from assistive technology and stops its pointer events; a dialog gives the focus back to the element that had it when it opened (`Dialog` remembers it; Radix would use a trigger, and these dialogs have none).
- **Local changes to the registry copies** (`src/components/glass/`): `context-menu.tsx` is the registry's file as it is, but for its `"use client"` line (the app has no server components, as the other copies); `search-field.tsx` makes Clear a click target and not a Tab stop (Tab goes from the search box to the first tile, as in a macOS search field), and lets an Esc in an empty field reach the page, so Pick white and Crop still hear it; an Esc that clears text is used up. `Slider` (ours) ignores any pointer button but the primary one: Radix's slider takes a right click on the track as a drag. `slider.tsx` has a `size="sm"` knob, round and 16 px across instead of glasscn's 24 × 36 px capsule, which every `Slider` row uses: the tool panel's track is about 104 px long and the capsule took a third of its travel (the owner's request of 2026-10-17). The tool tabs fill the 46 px tab row, so their labels sit in its middle and the underline on its rim (`glass-c2.css`; glasscn's tab is 32 px high and stacked at the top of the row).
- **Cursor.** A button keeps the arrow; a link-styled one (`LinkButton`, `ResetLink`, a section's text action: glasscn's `link` variant) is a pointing hand while it can be used.
- **Reduced motion** is honoured by glasscn's own `motion-reduce` rules; `global.css` keeps `transition-property: none` for the native viewport's boxes.

Files: `src/styles/glass.css` (Tailwind 4 and glasscn's tokens, keyframes), `shadcn-base.css` (the shadcn base colour tokens glasscn assumes), `glass-c2.css` (the palette, the accent, a colour per tab, the layout and the filmstrip), `glass-c2-pages.css` (Start, the wizard, the modes, the dialogs, the error bar), `legacy.css` (the pre-glass sheets, placed in Tailwind's `base` layer so that glasscn's utilities win; rules of replaced components are gone). ### Colour warp (the Colour tab's third card; the owner's request of 2026-10-17, not mocked)

A chroma warp in the manner of DaVinci Resolve's Color Warper (`docs/02` C9b, `docs/16`). It sits under Colour balance. The owner chose the pins with their own sliders, a card on the Colour tab, and Pick colour on the photo.

- **Card.** Title "Colour warp"; the header's action is the card's Reset (a `ResetLink`, shown while there are pins), which removes every pin: `{ color_warp: { pins: [] } }`.
- **Tools** (one row above the plane, glasscn controls): a `ToggleGroup` of two tools, **Move** (the default) and **Lock**; a `Toggle` **Pick colour** (`aria-pressed`); a glass `Button` **Delete pin**, off while no pin is selected.
- **The plane** (ours, like the wheels and the curve plot): a square as wide as the card's content (352 px in the 400 px panel), centred on neutral, reaching `|c| = 0.30` at the middle of each edge (`WARP_PLANE_EXTENT`). It draws a hue field (each point's colour at a fixed bright grey, faded toward neutral at the centre), faint rings at 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3 and the two axes, a faint scatter of the photo's own colours (`chroma_scatter`, up to 2048 points at the warp's input), and the pins. A moving pin is a 10 px ring at its source, a line, and a 10 px dot at its target filled with the target's hue; a locked pin is a ring with a small square inside; the selected pin has a ring in the tab colour. `+e1` (red) points right and `+e2` (green against blue) up, so red is at 3 o'clock, green near 11 and blue near 7.
- **Pointer.** With Move: press on empty plane adds a moving pin there and the drag moves its target; press on a pin's target dot or source ring selects it and the drag moves its target (the source stays where it was made); a double click on a pin puts its target back on its source. With Lock: a click on empty plane adds a locked pin, a drag moves a locked pin. A click on a pin selects it in either tool. Drags send one patch per animation frame and a final one on release, so a drag is one undo step. Sixteen pins at most: at sixteen a press on empty plane does nothing and the hint says "16 pins at most."
- **Keyboard.** Each pin is a focusable `role="slider"` named "Pin 3" with `aria-valuetext` "moves red toward orange" (or "holds red" for a lock); Tab moves through the pins, the focused pin is the selected one. The arrows move its target (a lock: its position) by 0.002 (Shift ×10), Home puts the target back on the source, Delete or Backspace removes the pin.
- **The selected pin's sliders**, under the plane, the standard `Slider` rows: **Range** 1–25 (`range × 100`, default 4), **Shadows** 0–100 % (default 100 %), **Highlights** 0–100 % (default 100 %), **Pivot** −6…+6 stops (default 0, read `+1.0 EV`), **Exposure** −2…+2 stops (default 0, read `+0.30 EV`). A locked pin shows Range only. With no pin selected the rows are replaced by one line: "Drag a colour to move it. Lock holds a colour in place."
- **Pick colour.** The toggle turns on the same crosshair layer as Pick white (the two are exclusive; Esc ends either). A click asks the core for the chroma of that pixel at the warp's input (`sample_chroma x, y`, CSS pixels from the photo cell's corner, as Pick white) and adds a moving pin there with its target on its source, selected, so the next drag on the plane moves it. The mode stays on until Esc or the toggle, so several colours can be picked in a row. A click on the letterbox does nothing.
- **Copy, paste, undo.** The pins are one setting, `color_warp`, so Copy settings and Paste move them with the rest of the grade, and each finished change is one undo step. They are per photo and in memory only, like every setting.

**As built (2026-10-17).** The card is `ColorWarpCard` (`src/screens/tools/ColorWarpCard.tsx`, the third card of `ColorTab`); the plane is our component `WarpPlane` (`src/components/WarpPlane.tsx`; props `pins`, `onChange(pins, final)`, `selected`, `onSelect(index | null)`, `tool` `"move" | "lock"`, `scatter`), drawn from `src/components/warpMath.ts`, a TypeScript port of `crates/ym-color/src/warp.rs` (the constants are checked against the Rust file by `warpMath.node.test.ts`). The tools are glasscn's `ToggleGroup` (a radio group named "Pin tool"), our `Button` with `pressed` (glasscn's `Toggle`) for Pick colour and a glass `Button` for Delete pin. The selected pin is window state in the store (`warpPin`, `selectWarpPin`; none on another photo, an index past the end counts as none); the scatter is `chroma_scatter`, asked for while the card is mounted and again after frames at most every 200 ms, as the histogram is (`watchChromaScatter`). Pick white and Pick colour share one layer, `PickLayer` (`src/screens/PickLayer.tsx`, formerly `PickWhiteLayer`; class `.ym-pick-layer`), and the Photos screen keeps which of the two is on; `store.pickColor` flushes the viewport input and the patches in flight, asks `sample_chroma` and adds the pin as one undo step. Decisions where the list above is silent:
- *Pointer.* In either tool a press on a pin selects it and a drag moves it: a moving pin's target, a lock as a whole. The drag keeps the offset at which the pin was taken (the dot does not jump to the pointer; taken by its source ring, the target moves by as much as the pointer). The press that adds a pin sends it at once (not final), so the rows show; the release is the final patch, so adding and dragging is one undo step; a press on a pin that does not move sends nothing. Dragged and keyed points stay on the drawn square (`|a|, |b| ≤ 0.30`); a stored point outside it (from the CLI, or Pick colour on a very strong colour, which keeps the sampled chroma) is drawn at the edge. A press focuses the pin it selects (the plane itself takes no focus from the mouse), so Delete and the arrows act on it at once.
- *Sixteen pins.* A press on empty plane, or a Pick colour click, then selects no pin, and the line under the plane reads "16 pins at most." in place of the help line.
- *Keyboard.* Each key is one undo step. Delete or Backspace gives the focus to the pin before (the plane when none is left); Home on a lock does nothing. The focus indicator is the selection ring in the tab colour (3 px with `:focus-visible`), not an outline, which would box the pin's whole line.
- *Accessible values.* `aria-valuenow` is the hue angle of the target (0–359), `aria-valuetext` one of "moves red toward orange", "holds red", "red, not moved", "moves red toward a stronger red" (or "a paler red") when the move stays in one hue, and "grey" within the neutral guard. A hue is named after the nearest of eight angles measured on the plane from pure LogC4 colours: red 0°, yellow 60°, green 120°, cyan 180°, blue 240°, magenta 300°, with orange (30°) and purple (270°) halfway.
- *Hue field.* The grey is LogC4 0.45; the colour is taken to Display P3 and rendered keeping its hue (a colour outside P3 is pulled toward its own luminance, at least 8 % of its largest channel, then the channels are scaled by `1 / (0.6 + max)`), so it rolls off instead of clipping into flat bands; it shows at `0.92 · smoothstep(0, 0.225, |c|)`, fading by 80 % between 0.30 and 0.42. It is drawn once per size (128–384 px, scaled up by the browser) on a Display P3 canvas, sRGB where there is none. The scatter is 1.8 px dots of white at 42 %.
- *Sliders.* Range steps by 1 (0.01 of range), Shadows and Highlights by 1 %, Pivot by 0.1 stop, Exposure by 0.01 stop. The card's Reset also clears the selection.
- *Pick colour.* A click switches to the Colour tab (the mode may outlive it); the mode ends with Esc, the toggle, Pick white (W), Crop, or another photo.
- *The mock.* `src/fixtures/warp.ts` takes a flat sRGB colour to an approximate LogC4 pixel (sRGB to Display P3 to linear AWG4, read as scene-linear, chroma × 3 so a muted mockup colour sits where a camera's would) and back, and the mock's picture runs the TypeScript warp after the light changes and before the look (`adjustScene`), so a pin visibly moves a colour; its scatter is a seeded grid of 1536 points and `sample_chroma` the colour under the click at the warp's input.

### Removing photos (the owner's request of 2026-10-20, not mocked)

The owner: "I also want to remove photos from the film strip." Decisions in `docs/16` (2026-10-20); the core side is `docs/01` §*Removing photos*.

- **What it means.** The photo leaves the session, and with it the filmstrip, the picks, the caches and its edits, because nothing is saved per photo (AGENTS rule 4). **The file on disk is never touched** (rule 5): not deleted, not moved, not renamed, not written. The words say so: "Removed DSC04204. The file is still on disk." and, for several, "Removed 3 photos. The files are still on disk." There is no Trash icon anywhere in this feature.
- **How.** (1) *Right-click a thumbnail* (Control-click, or the Menu key or Shift+F10 on the focused thumbnail): glasscn's `ContextMenu` (`src/components/glass/context-menu.tsx`, from the registry like the other glass components) with **Remove from filmstrip**, which takes out the photo that was clicked, whether or not it is the one on screen, and does not choose it. While any photo is picked the menu also has **Remove picked photos** with the count at its right. (2) *Delete or Backspace* removes the photo on screen. It is ignored in a text box, on a slider's knob, in a menu or a dialog (`usesDeleteKey` in `src/state/shortcuts.ts`: the colour warp's pins, the curve's points and the wheels use the keys themselves), while Crop or Pick white is on, and in the Export panel (Back to photos first). A key held down removes one photo, not the strip (only the last removal can be undone). There is no menu-bar item: a ⌫ key equivalent on a native item would take the key from every text box on a Mac.
- **Selection and Picked.** The filmstrip has no multi-selection: it has the photo on screen and the picks, which are marks for Export and "Apply to", not a selection. So Delete removes only the photo on screen (a forgotten pick must never make a key press remove many photos), and the several-photo removal is the explicit menu item. The menu and the key work on any photo, also one that failed to open (CAN'T OPEN) or is still developing, which stops developing.
- **After a removal.** If the photo on screen was removed, the next photo is shown, or the previous one when the last was removed, with Fit and the camera view off as for any other move; if another photo was removed the photo on screen stays (its place in "4 of 11" moves). If no photo is left the Start screen shows and the notice stays. Keyboard focus that was on the removed thumbnail goes to the photo on screen, so the arrows go on working.
- **The notice and Undo.** The existing notice bar (`ErrorBar`, a glasscn `Item`) says what was removed and has an **Undo** button next to Dismiss. Undo puts the photos back where they were in the filmstrip, with their settings, picks and ⌘Z steps; the photo that was on screen is on screen again unless the person has moved to another photo since; the notice then says "Put back DSC04204." or "Put back 3 photos." (and "1 was already open." for a photo whose file was added again in between, which stays out so that no file is in the strip twice). What is kept is the **last removal only**, in memory (the core keeps the photos, the page their undo histories), until the next removal or the end of the session (⌘W, Start over, or files opened on the Start screen after the last photo was removed, which begin a new one); a second removal replaces it, so Undo is one step, not a history. The notice is replaced by any other notice and goes with Dismiss, but the removal stays undoable.
- **⌘Z.** The undo stack is per photo, so ⌘Z (and Edit › Undo) takes the removal back when it is the last thing done: nothing has been changed since, or what was changed has been undone (the page compares the number of undo steps of all photos with the number it had when the photos left). After a new change ⌘Z undoes that change first, as in any undo stack, and the removal after it. On the Start screen after the last photo was removed, ⌘Z puts the photos back. There is no redo of a removal.
- **Export.** Export panel's strip has no menu and Delete does nothing there. An export that is already running or queued **keeps the photos it was started with and finishes them**, with the settings they had when it was started (its jobs hold snapshots, `docs/01` §*The queue*); removing photos from the filmstrip does not cancel or shorten it. Simpler and safer than refusing: the queue already has to survive a closed session, and the person asked to remove the photos from the strip, not to stop an export.
- **Copy and paste.** Copied settings stay in the store; if the photo they came from is removed, Paste works as before (the settings are a patch, not a reference to the photo).

**As built (2026-10-20).** `AppStore.removePhotos(ids)` waits for the settings patches still in flight to those photos (none may reach a photo that left), calls `remove_photos`, takes the answer's `photo_ids` (the core says which ids were in the session), keeps the removed photos' `PhotoHistory` objects and the history depth in a private `PendingRemoval`, drops their thumbnails (`releaseUrl`), settings, look tiles, senders and gestures, and changes the session with `withoutPhotos` (the page's own entries stay; the snapshot of the answer is older than the events the page has handled since, as for `mergeSession`). `removeCurrentPhoto()` is Delete, `removePickedPhotos()` the menu's second item, `undoRemoval()` the Undo button, `undo()` (⌘Z) tries `removalIsLatest()` first. `AppState.noticeAction` (`"undo-removal"`, cleared by every other notice) makes `ErrorBar` draw the Undo `Button`. `Filmstrip` takes `onRemove` and `onRemovePicked` (no menu without them, and none in `export` mode); the strip has **one** `ContextMenu` around its scroller and not one per thumbnail (hundreds of Radix roots would be redrawn whenever a thumbnail arrives): the thumbnail a menu is about is found from the event's target (`data-photo-id`), a right click between the thumbnails opens nothing, and the one the menu is open for has `data-menu="open"` (the ring). The mock plays the core's rules (`removeNow`, `restoreNow` in `api.mock.ts`: the same successor, places, current photo after Undo, no id used twice). Tests: `src/state/store.remove.test.ts` (23), `src/screens/Photos.remove.test.tsx` (16), `src/components/Filmstrip.test.tsx` (7 on the menu and the focus), `src/state/shortcuts.test.ts`, `src/state/photo.test.ts`, `src/api.mock.remove.test.ts` (12), `e2e/remove.spec.ts` (8).

- **Slider patches:** `onChange(value, final)`. While the thumb is dragged it fires at most once per animation frame with `final = false`, then once with `final = true` on release; keys, typed values, Reset and double click fire once with `final = true`. The same contract holds for `Wheel`, `CurvePlot` and `WarpPlane`. **A drag belongs to the photo it began on** (the owner's report of 2026-10-20, "Look selection and settings"): a control collects input over time (a patch per frame, then the release), and the photo on screen can change between the pointer going down and a frame or the release. So the control keeps the `onChange` of the render the pointer went down in until the gesture ends (`useGestureCallback`, `src/components/hooks.ts`), and every `onChange` that can be a drag sends `store.update(patch, final, rs.photo_id)`, naming the photo its settings were drawn from; the crop session names the photo the crop was started on. Only an input that is handled at once (a key, a click, a typed value) may leave the photo out of `update`, which then means the photo on screen at that moment. A control keeps showing its own value until the controlled value catches up (or one second passes), so a slow reply never makes the thumb jump back.
- **Reset** on an automatic value (Exposure, Kelvin, Tint, Sharpen, Noise reduction) must go back to Auto, not to the number Auto resolves to: pass `onReset`.
- **Wheel keys:** arrows nudge the puck (Shift ×10), Home, Delete or Backspace centre it; exposed as a slider whose value is the strength, with the strength and hue in `aria-valuetext`.
- **Curve:** a press adds a point on the curve at that x, a drag moves it, dragging an inner point more than 14 px off the plot deletes it. The two end points move up and down only and cannot be deleted. They are drawn only while the plot is hovered, focused or the point is selected, so a resting plot looks like the mockup. Keys: Tab through the points, arrows move one code value (Shift ×10), Delete removes, Return on the plot adds a point in the widest gap. Input and Output show the dragged point, else the position under the pointer, else the selected point, else the middle.
- **Focus:** every interactive element gets the global 3 px ring with a 2 px offset, including tabs and segments (the focused cell is lifted so its neighbours do not paint over the ring). The slider draws its own ring because its native input is transparent.
- **Photo cell:** `PhotoCell` carries `data-viewport` and reports `{ x, y, width, height, scale }` through `onRect` on mount, on ResizeObserver callbacks and on window resize. With `native` it draws no `<img>` and is transparent (a native layer draws behind it); with a `frame` (the fallback viewport's picture of the whole cell) it draws that picture at its own size, one picture pixel to one device pixel, on paper; otherwise the photo is an `<img>` letterboxed on paper. The Photos screen chooses from `viewport_info` (see "Wiring to the shell" below); the component's own default for `native` is `isTauri()`.

The webview tells the shell when that changes (`set_menu_state`, sent only on a change; the native menu starts with the three off) and the shell switches the items. `set_menu_state` is a synchronous command, like `viewport_set_rect`: it runs on the main thread in the order the webview sent the calls, so two quick changes cannot overtake each other and leave the menu half switched (`commands.rs` has a compile-time test). A menu action that reaches the webview with a text box focused is done: a menu choice is explicit.
  - **What is copied.** Everything in `DevelopSettings` except `crop`, `orientation` and `white_pick`, which belong to that photo's own geometry and pixels, so a photo keeps its own. The look and its intensity are included. Auto stays Auto (exposure, light preset, white balance, sharpening, noise reduction): each photo keeps its own automatic exposure, white balance, sharpening and noise, and the white lift's offset (a number of stops added to each photo's own automatic amount) is copied as the number. Manual values are copied as they are. **A white balance that came from a picked white** (a white picked while the light preset and the white balance are both Auto) is copied as the manual Kelvin and tint the pick resolved to, since the point means nothing on another photo and the light it found does: the pasted photo's white balance is manual then (EDITED) and not an approximation of the pick (`docs/02`: a manual Kelvin and tint do not render exactly like an Auto white balance, the tint scales the green gain and the Kelvin is rounded to 50 K). A light preset chosen after a pick wins over it, and a manual white balance next to a pick is manual: both are copied as they are. The rules are `SettingsPatch::copied_from` in the core (`docs/01`), not the webview's, and the mock repeats them (`copiedPatch`).
  - **Where it lives.** In the webview store (`AppState.copied`: the photo's name and the patch), for the running app only: never written to disk (hard rule 4: nothing is saved per photo) and never put on the system clipboard. Closing the session keeps it, so settings can be copied from one folder to another; quitting forgets it.
  - **Paste.** Paste (⇧⌘V, the button, the menu) applies to the current photo; Paste Settings to Picked (⌥⇧⌘V, the menu) applies to every picked photo, the current one included when it is picked. One `paste_settings` call for all of them. A photo's own crop, orientation and white pick stay; each photo's change is one undo step in that photo's own history; the settings of the photo on screen when the answer comes (which may not be the one that was when the paste began) are asked for again at once, the settings the mirror held for the others are dropped and asked for when the photo is shown, and the filmstrip thumbnails follow from the core. A photo that is still developing or failed is skipped. **Paste Settings to Picked leaves the photo the settings came from out** when its own white is still a picked one (a Pick white point with the light preset and the white balance both Auto): the paste would swap that Auto white balance for the manual Kelvin and tint that stands for it on other photos, which renders a little differently and marks the photo edited. A source whose white was not picked, or whose white changed since, takes the paste like any photo (that restores it to what was copied). The id of the source is kept for this only while its session is open (`CopiedSettings.sourceId`; the next session numbers its photos again). A crop or a slider drag that is open on a photo when a paste lands is re-based on the pasted settings, so its own undo step, recorded when it ends, does not carry the paste with it. Notices: "Copied the settings of DSC04213." ; "Pasted the settings of DSC04213." for one photo ; "Pasted the settings of DSC04213 to 5 photos." ; with photos left out, "Pasted the settings of DSC04213 to 4 photos. 1 photo was skipped because it isn't developed." (3 photos: "…3 photos were skipped because they aren't developed.") ; "Nothing was pasted: 2 photos aren't developed." ; and, when nothing is copied, nothing is picked, or the source is the only photo picked and its white is a picked one, "Copy the settings of a photo first.", "No photos are picked." and "No other photos are picked." (`pastedNotice` in `store.ts`). "Pasted … to N photos" counts the photos that now have the settings, whether or not anything changed in them (a photo that already had them has an empty step and no undo entry).
  - **Tests.** `crates/ym-core/tests/session.rs` (five: every field but the three copied, Auto stays Auto, a picked white becomes manual Kelvin and tint and the preset and manual cases, skipped photos and a bad patch changing nothing, the undo and redo pair), `src-tauri/src/api/tests.rs` (paste, events, a gone look; opening more files and the counts), `commands.rs` (the IPC names), `menu.rs` (the entries, keys, state and, under `xvfb-run`, the real menu's items switching); `src/state/store.copypaste.test.ts`, `src/screens/Photos.copypaste.test.tsx`, `src/state/shortcuts.test.ts`, `src/state/menu.test.ts`, `src/api.mock.test.ts`; `e2e/copy-paste.spec.ts`.
- **Errors** from any command show in a black bar at the foot of the window until dismissed.

### Shell notes (M3-02, M3-03, M3-04, M3-13)

The Rust side of the interface is `src-tauri` (package `yellowmoon-app`). It is thin: `src/api.rs` has one method per command and no Tauri types, `src/commands.rs` wraps each in a `#[tauri::command]` that runs it on a blocking thread, and everything that crosses the bridge is a type of `src/wire.rs`.

**IPC (M3-02).**
- *Typed bridge.* `specta` + `tauri-specta` generate `src/api/bindings.ts` from the commands and events. `wire.rs` mirrors the core's serde types (the core's types do not derive `specta::Type`) and converts through serde (`wire::convert`); `core_types_convert_without_loss` checks that nothing is lost on the way. The drift check is `cargo test -p yellowmoon-app --bin yellowmoon-app bindings`: it regenerates the file in memory and fails when the committed one differs; `YM_UPDATE_BINDINGS=1` on the same command rewrites the file. Two more tests tie the bridge to the frontend: `field_names_match_the_typescript_contract` and `variants_match_the_typescript_contract` compare every interface and every tagged union of `src/api/types.ts` with the generated types (so `types.ts` stays the hand-written contract, with `export interface` blocks). The TypeScript side is checked by the compiler: `tauriApi` calls the generated `commands` of `src/api/bindings.ts` instead of `invoke("…")`, and `src/api/contract.ts` makes `tsc -b` fail when a payload of `types.ts` and the generated one disagree in the direction it flows, when an event of `ApiEvents` is not a generated event (or the other way round), or when an `Api` method and its command differ in arguments or answer. `every_command_and_event_of_the_frontend_is_in_the_bindings` counted the `invoke("…")` calls of `src/api.ts`; there are none now (it fails with "found 0 commands"), so the test has to shrink to its event half, `every_event_of_the_frontend_is_in_the_bindings`: the commands are `tsc`'s job, and `src/api.test.ts` compares the wire names and argument keys of every method with `bindings.ts`.
- *Errors* are `AppError { scope, message }` (`ErrorScope` of `types.ts`), thrown by the generated functions (`ErrorHandlingMode::Throw`), so a rejected `invoke` is what `isAppError` expects.
- *Differences from `types.ts`*, for whoever wires `api.ts` to the bindings: `SettingsPatch` is the core's patch, a little more generous than `Partial<DevelopSettings>` (nested `decode`, `rolloff`, `color_balance` and `curve` objects and a manual `white_balance`, `sharpen` or `noise_reduction` may leave parts out; `crop: null` and `white_pick: null` clear); `AutoOr<M>` is one enum per setting (`ExposureSetting`, …) with the same JSON; numbers are `number` (the Rust `Num` newtype, because specta would write `number | null` for an `f64`); unit commands return `null`; `MatchKind` has no `forced` variant (only the CLI's `--profile` makes one).
- *Additions* (not in `docs/01`'s original table, not in `types.ts`'s first version; `docs/01` lists them now): commands `viewport_info()` (`native` or `fallback`), `export_busy()`, `restart_app()` and `thumbnails()`; events `export_busy { busy }`, `settings_changed { photo_id }` (sent after a `viewport_input` Pick white click set the photo's `white_pick`, and once for each photo a `paste_settings` changed; ask `get_settings` again, except for the photos the webview is pasting onto, whose changes the paste's answer carries) and `viewport_frame { seq, width, height, sharp, url }` (fallback viewport only; an empty `url` with a size of 0 × 0 means "no picture": remove the image).
- *Events.* `photo_progress`, `thumbnail_ready` (`{ id, url }`: the address of a PNG of at most 240 px that the shell serves on the `ym-frame` scheme, `docs/01` IPC), `render_stats` (after every viewport frame, with the frame's `zoom_percent` and the Fit zoom `fit_percent`), `export_progress`, `looks_changed`, `looks_import_progress` (the copy stage only), `download_found`, `menu_action`, `error` and `update_state` are registered. The shell sends `update_state` never: the updater runs in the webview (`updater.ts` with `tauri-plugin-updater`; `docs/12`), and `capabilities/default.json` allows `updater:default` only. "Update ready" waits for the export queue: the webview knows the run it started, and `export_busy` (command and event) says whether a queue is busy, also after a reload. The restart itself is the app's own command, `restart_app`, not the process plugin's `relaunch` (its permission is not granted and the plugin is not loaded): Tauri's restart cannot be vetoed by the exit handler (`ExitRequestApi::prevent_exit` is ignored for it, so "An export is still running…" would never be asked and the exit would cancel the export and delete its file), so the shell refuses it where it starts, while an export is queued or running (scope `export`, "An export is still running. Restart after it finishes."; the update stays ready and the cell enables when `export_busy` says the queue is idle), and once a restart has begun `export_start` is refused. Tests: `AppApi::begin_restart` against a real export in `src-tauri/src/api/tests.rs`, the updater's reaction in `src/updater.test.ts`.

**Session (M3-03).** `open_paths` takes the dropped or chosen paths (a folder is read one level deep; non-ARW files are ignored; "No ARW files in what you dropped." is an `open` error when nothing is an ARW file). `open_dialog` and `choose_folder` / `choose_look_files` use the system dialogs from Rust (`tauri-plugin-dialog`), starting in the folder remembered in the preferences; the webview does not use the dialog plugin, so `dialog:default` is not in the capabilities. The window accepts drops of files and folders (`dragDropEnabled`; the webview reads the real paths from `onDragDropEvent`). Closing the session (⌘W; the webview asks first when an export runs) stops development and renders but not exports. Quitting with an export running asks "An export is still running…" (Stop the export and quit / Keep exporting), then cancels the queue and deletes the partial file; the close button of the window asks the same question before the window goes (answering "Keep exporting" after the window was gone would leave an app with no window to show the export in). `export_start` does not wait for the session to finish developing and is not refused for photos outside the job: a burst's shared exposure and the pooled noise model depend on other photos, so the core queues the job and each photo's turn waits until the photos *it* depends on (its burst, its noise pool) have their estimates, then reads them (`docs/01` Session, "App/CLI parity for early exports"); `ym render`, which waits for the whole session, and the app write the same bytes, and neither thumbnails, prefetching nor viewport work are waited for. Only photos of the job's scope that have no estimates at all yet are refused: "One photo is still being developed. Wait until it is ready, then export again." / "Some photos are still being developed. Wait until they are ready, then export again." A thumbnail is made once per photo when it is developed; applying a look, changing the Intensity or the crop or the quarter turn of a photo makes a new one (collected for 300 ms, rendered behind the viewport).

**Viewport (M0-06, M3-04).** The webview reports the photo cell with `viewport_set_rect` (CSS pixels of the window; also on resize), the pointer with `viewport_input`, `viewport_set_mode`, `set_before` and `sample_pixel`. The shell keeps the core's `ViewportState` for the current photo and asks the core for a frame (RGBA8, Display P3, the cell's size in device pixels, letterbox in paper) whenever the state, the photo or its settings change. A newer request does not cancel the frame being drawn: that frame finishes and is shown, and the next round starts at once from the newest state (only the pass that makes sharp tiles gives way to a request, and a request that makes the frame wrong, another photo or another cell size, stops the frame too; `docs/01` §Native viewport). When the zoom needs full-resolution tiles that are not ready, a coarse frame goes out at once, the tiles are made at tile priority, and the sharp frame follows (`viewport_frame.sharp`). A new photo goes back to Fit and Before off; a zoom, pan or Before that was still waiting for the photo it was meant for is dropped, the cell's rectangle is kept. Nothing is drawn before the webview's first `viewport_set_rect` (a cell with an area: the controller's initial 1 × 1 cell is not one). A photo that is still developing is drawn as soon as it is ready; one that failed to develop, or is gone, takes the picture away (`viewport_frame` with an empty `url` on the fallback, the hidden layer on the native viewport) instead of leaving the previous photo in the cell. `viewport_set_rect` is a synchronous command (the main thread takes the webview's messages in order; every `async` command runs on a worker thread of its own, and two rectangles of a window being resized that overtook each other would leave the photo at the wrong size). Pick white: a `viewport_input` of kind `pick_white` converts the click through the straightening, the turn and the lens maps to the `white_pick` space (`Renderer::pick_point_at`), sets it with `update_settings` and sends `settings_changed`; a click on the paper does nothing. In the app the click must go to the shell this way and not be converted in the webview: only the core knows the lens crop, the straighten angle and the quarter turn that lie between a point of the cell and the `white_pick` space (the browser mock has no such thing and takes the point of the shown picture). `sample_pixel` fails with a `viewport` error on the paper. `histogram` runs as a thumbnail-priority job, behind the viewport frames it is asked for after.
- *Native (macOS only, `src/native.rs`).* A plain `NSView` is added behind every sibling in the window's content view (below the webview); wgpu makes the `CAMetalLayer` for it and draws each frame as one full-screen triangle sampling the frame texture. The surface is `Bgra8Unorm` with `SurfaceColorSpace::DisplayP3` (wgpu 30 sets the layer's colour space, macOS converts for the display); without that colour space the sink refuses to start. The view follows `viewport_set_rect` (flipped from AppKit's bottom-left origin). It starts hidden, the first frame drawn shows it, and taking the picture away (no photo, a photo that failed) hides it again, so a cell reported while there is no picture does not bring a stale one back. A surface that has no texture to give yet is asked again a few times (a frame is not asked for twice) before the frame is given up on with an error. Needs `transparent: true` and `macOSPrivateApi` (both set in `tauri.conf.json`), the photo cell transparent (`PhotoCell` with `native`) and nothing under it painting either: `PhotoView` sets `data-native-viewport` on the root element (not `data-viewport`, which marks the cell itself) while it shows, which makes the root, body, `#root` and the Photos screen transparent (`screens.css`), and the sink makes the window itself opaque paper (`PAPER_SRGB`, equal to `--paper`; `src/styles/native-viewport.node.test.ts` checks both). It cannot run on Linux; it is type-checked with `cargo check --target aarch64-apple-darwin -p yellowmoon-app`.
- *Fallback (everywhere, `src/viewport.rs`).* Each frame becomes an RGB PNG with the Display P3 ICC profile, kept (the last three) in a `FrameStore` and served at `ym-frame://localhost/<launch>/frame-<seq>.png` (`http://ym-frame.localhost/…` on Windows; `<launch>` is the app's start time in hex milliseconds and a count, `docs/01` IPC); the `viewport_frame` event says when a new one is there and where. The webview shows it as an `<img>` over the cell (CSP `img-src` allows the scheme). It is what Linux development builds and the tests use, and the M0-06 fallback candidate if the Metal layer fails the checklist. It breaks the rule "full images never cross the bridge" (`docs/01`); the decision is in `docs/16`. `viewport_info()` tells the photo cell which of the two is running (`native`: stay transparent; `fallback`: draw the `viewport_frame` image).
- *Manual checklist, native viewport (HUMAN M0-H4; also M3-04).* On a Mac, `npm run tauri dev`, open a folder of ARW files and check: (1) the photo shows in the cell, edge to edge, with the paper-coloured letterbox, and everything else (header, filmstrip, panel) is drawn by the webview over a clean window; (2) no flicker, no white or black flash while a photo develops, when the tab changes, or when a dialog opens; (3) resizing the window keeps the photo glued to the cell (no lag of more than a frame, no stretched frame left behind); (4) full screen (green button and ⌃⌘F), then back; (5) switching Spaces and Mission Control; (6) the window moved between a Display P3 and an sRGB display: the photo looks the same on both as exported files do in Preview (compare a JPEG exported with Display P3); (7) pan and zoom by wheel, drag and double click: the cursor-to-photo mapping is right at Fit, 50 %, 100 % and 200 %, and one frame of input latency is the most there is (the frame-time overlay is `render_stats` in the header); (8) Pick white, the pixel readout with ⌥ held and the crop overlay line up with the photo; (9) Before (`\`) and the look chip; (10) minimise and restore, hide and show; (11) a second display of another scale (Retina and 1×); (12) light and dark appearance of the system (the app is light only); (13) choose a file that shows CAN'T OPEN, then a good one: the cell shows no picture for the first (not the previous photo) and the photo again for the second, without the window being resized; (14) with an export running, the red close button and ⌘Q both ask first, and "Keep exporting" leaves the window open; (15) with an export of a large photo as 16-bit PNG or TIFF writing (a hidden `.yellowmoon-….tmp` file is in the folder), ⌘Q and "Stop the export and quit": the app is gone within a few seconds and the folder holds no `.yellowmoon-….tmp`; (16) open a folder of 500 ARW files (or copy one file 500 times) and press the arrow keys while they develop: keys are on screen within 100 ms (M3-03), and stepping through 20 photos with the Look tab open does not make the app grow by a mosaic for every photo; (17) the wizard on a Mac that has never been asked: import a package and leave "Move to Trash" on; macOS asks whether Yellowmoon may control Finder, with the sentence of `Info.plist`, and the zip is in the Trash afterwards (if the prompt does not come or the zip stays, `trash` should be switched to its `NsFileManager` method, which needs no permission; "Put Back" may then be missing). If any of 1 to 6 fails, switch to the fallback (set `YELLOWMOON_VIEWPORT=fallback` to try it first) and record the reasons in `docs/16`.

**Menus (M3-13).** The bar is `src/menu.rs` (`spec()` is the whole menu as data, tested without a window): Yellowmoon (About Yellowmoon, Preferences… ⌘,, Check for Updates…, Install Command-Line Tool…, the standard Services, Hide and Quit ⌘Q), File (Open Files… ⌘O, Open Folder… ⇧⌘O, Export… ⌘E, Close Session ⌘W), Edit (Undo, Redo, Copy Settings, Paste Settings, Paste Settings to Picked, Pick, Pick All, Clear Picks, and the system's Cut, Copy and Paste for the text boxes), View (Before/After, Fit, 100%, Zoom In ⌘=, Zoom Out ⌘−, Next and Previous Photo, Full Screen), Looks (Import ARRI Looks…, Import .cube…, Make Current Look Default, Show Looks Folder), Window (Minimize, Zoom), Help (Yellowmoon on GitHub, Report a Problem…). Items that need the webview send `menu_action { action }`; the rest are the shell's: *About* is the system's About panel with the version, the Apache-2.0 line, the credits, "This software is based in part on the work of the Independent JPEG Group.", "This product includes DNG technology under license by Adobe." and "Not affiliated with ARRI or Sony. …" (`src/about.rs`; the in-window dialog of Preferences repeats the sentences and needs the IJG one added); *Show Looks Folder* reveals `looks/` of the app folder; *Report a Problem…* asks whether to copy the output of `ym doctor` to the clipboard, copies it if you say yes, and opens the bug report form on GitHub either way; *Yellowmoon on GitHub* opens the repository. ### Wiring to the shell (M3-02, M3-03, M3-04, frontend side)

How the screens use what the shell offers (the shell's side is "Shell notes" above). Decisions where the specs were silent:

- **Commands and types.** `tauriApi` (`src/api.ts`) calls the generated `commands`; a unit command resolves with nothing. `types.ts` stays the contract the screens use and `contract.ts` compares it with the generated file (a type-level check, no run-time code). The CLI is `ym`: the command line preview, the Preferences hint, the mock and every test say `ym render …`. The dev page `components.html` still reproduces the mockup's `yellowmoon render …` text on purpose.
- **Viewport backend.** The store asks `viewport_info()` when it connects and keeps `viewportBackend`: `native`, `fallback`, or `null` (the API has no viewport: the browser mock). The Photos screen draws accordingly: `native` leaves the photo cell transparent and draws nothing in it; `fallback` shows the latest `viewport_frame` (an `<img>` of `ym-frame://…` the size of the cell in device pixels, drawn at that size from the cell's corner, on a paper cell) and takes it away on an empty `url` or when the photo is not ready (the "Still developing." and "This file can't be opened." notes cover the cell); `null` draws the mock's picture. A frame smaller than 16 px a side is no picture: the shell draws a 1 × 1 frame for a cell the webview has not yet placed. An older frame never replaces a newer one, and never brings back a picture that was taken away: the shell numbers a frame before it encodes it and sends the news that the picture is gone from another thread, so a frame can arrive after the clear that was numbered later (the store keeps the newest number heard, clears included). The cell reports its rectangle on mount, on resize and on layout change (`viewport_set_rect`, CSS pixels; `PhotoCell`, ResizeObserver) in every mode, and when the display scale changes (the window dragged to a display of another scale: the cell keeps its size in CSS pixels, not every web view sends `resize` for it, so `PhotoCell` also listens to the media query for the current scale).
- **Input.** Double click is `viewport_input toggle_zoom`, ⌘ or Ctrl and the wheel is `zoom` about the pointer, the wheel and a drag pan once zoomed, ⌥ over the cell reads `sample_pixel` in CSS pixels of the cell (on the paper there is nothing to read and the readout goes), and Pick white is above. The store mirrors the zoom the core is asked for: a double click flips Fit and 100 %, a wheel zoom multiplies the mirror by the same factor at once (from the Fit zoom; capped at 800 %; at or below Fit it is Fit). The header's percentage and the drag to pan follow the mirror; a menu choice (50 %, 100 %, 200 %) sends exact values. The **Fit zoom** is the core's: `render_stats.fit_percent` (and `zoom_percent`) come from the same `ViewportState` the frame was drawn with, so they count the lens crop, the straighten angle, the crop and the quarter turn (`fitZoomOf`). The screen's own figure (`fitZoomPercent`: the cell's device pixels over the final picture's, from the photo's size and its settings) is used only until a frame has reported, and by the mock, which sends `null`; the store drops the zoom figures of the last frame when another photo opens, since the new photo may have another Fit zoom. A new photo goes to Fit with Before off in the mirror, as in the core. How the wheel, the pinch and a drag are collected and sent is in *Photos input and desktop feel* below; since M3-18 the mirror moves once per frame, when a zoom is closed, and not once per wheel event.
- **A fixed zoom at or below the Fit zoom is Fit.** The core draws Fit for it (`ViewportState::zoom` clamps to the Fit zoom, `is_fit` compares with the Fit zoom to 0.01 %), and the screen says the same: on a big display Fit is 62 % for a 24 MP photo, so the menu's "50 %" is Fit, the header reads "Fit 62%", the picture is letterboxed, a drag does not pan, a click on the paper is ignored by Pick white, and ⌘+ goes from Fit straight to 100 % (`showsWhole`, `zoomStep(mode, delta, fit)`: steps at or below the Fit zoom are skipped, so a key press always does something; Z and a double click give Fit again where 100 % is no more than the Fit zoom). ⌘+ and ⌘− step from where the photo is, not from the start of the list: a wheel zoom leaves the zoom anywhere (137 %), and Zoom In then goes to 200 % and Zoom Out to 100 %. The Fit zoom used for these decisions is not rounded (`fitZoomExact`); the header rounds it for display.
- **The pixel readout** asks the core for one reading at a time and, when the last one comes back, for the place the pointer is at then (a reading takes the core a moment, and the last place must not be lost); a reading that comes back after ⌥ was released is not shown.
- **Events.** `thumbnail_ready` fills the filmstrip, `photo_progress` the Start list and the filmstrip's states, `render_stats` the header, `export_progress` the Export panel (the sequence of docs/01 is tested), `settings_changed` makes the store read a photo's settings again (it skips a photo it has not asked about, the one whose Pick white it is waiting for, and the photos it is pasting onto, since the paste's answer says what changed; the core sends it after a Pick white click and once for each photo a paste changed, and the shell sends it for the core's `estimates_changed`: a burst's median moved, Apple Auto answered or failed, or its preference changed), `looks_changed` reloads looks, display LUTs and preferences. The Look tab's tiles ask `look_thumbnail` while they are in view (*Look tiles* below). After a reload of the webview the core does not announce the thumbnails again, so the store asks `thumbnails()` once it has the session back and gets the address of every picture the shell holds.
- **Updates and exports.** The update cell is disabled while the webview's own run is going or the core says an export is busy (`export_busy`, the event, and the command once at connect and again at the click: "Update ready" asks the core at the click and does not restart if an export has begun unheard; the restart itself is the shell's `restart_app` command, which asks again and refuses while an export is queued or running, because a restart Tauri makes cannot be vetoed by the exit handler. The webview takes that refusal (an `AppError` of scope `export`) as "not now": the update stays ready and the cell enables when the export is done).
- **Window.** The header (and the wizard's) has `data-tauri-drag-region`; the folder cell has `deep`, so its text drags too, and buttons never do.
- **About** adds the Independent JPEG Group's sentence to the Adobe notice and the trademark line.
- **Checking it for real.** Playwright drives the mock. `npm run e2e:shell` (`e2e/real/shell-smoke.mjs`; Linux, Xvfb, a built `dist/` and `target/debug/yellowmoon-app`) runs the built frontend inside the real shell: the debug app loads `devUrl`, the script serves `dist/` there with a driver added to `index.html`, drops a folder of ARW files on the window as Tauri's own drag-and-drop event, and checks the Start list, the frames on the `ym-frame` scheme, the thumbnails, a double click, ⌥, a settings change, Pick white, an export to a temporary folder with `export_busy`, a file that is not a raw file, and closing the session. The ARW files are read in place and nothing is written next to them. 35 checks on the nine real files of `testdata/real` on 2026-10-12; the same on the 48 synthetic files. Review of 2026-10-13 added the geometry checks, which need a point off the middle of the photo (the middle of the cell is the middle of the photo whatever is wrong): Pick white lands where the click was on a letterboxed photo, on a photo turned 90°, on a cropped one and on one turned and cropped (the pick is compared with the point worked out independently, within 0.01), a ⌘ wheel zoom keeps the pixel under the pointer, and a drag moves the picture with the pointer (the pixel that was under the start of the drag is under its end, exactly, at 100 %). `YM_SMOKE_SCALE=2` runs the app at a device scale of 2 (`GDK_SCALE`, a larger Xvfb screen), where the frame is twice the cell and a CSS pixel is two device pixels; `YM_SMOKE_ONLY=geometry` runs just the failure-path folder and its Pick white checks. A drop sent before the Start screen has registered its listener is lost, as it would be for a person who drops that fast, so the runner sends the drop again until the screen reacts. 39 of 39 checks pass at scale 1 and at scale 2 on the nine real files.

### Photos input and desktop feel (M3-16 to M3-19, frontend side)

The owner on 2026-10-16: "UI in Yellowmoon is sluggish. I can select UI text, when I pan photo with two fingers on trackpad - all app moves." The cause was in the page, which behaved like a web page: the two-finger pan was not the photo's (the wheel handler was a React `onWheel`, which React registers as passive, so it could not cancel the default action, and WebKit then rubber-banded the whole window), text could be selected by dragging, and every wheel event, every pointer move and every store tick did work. The decisions that fix it (`docs/15` M3-16 to M3-19; the measuring is in the last item):

- **Wheel.** `usePhotoGestures` (`src/components/usePhotoGestures.ts`, used by `PhotoCell` through its `gestures` prop) adds a native `wheel` listener with `{ passive: false }` to the photo cell and calls `preventDefault()` on **every** wheel event over the cell, at Fit, zoomed, in Crop and in Pick white: the gesture is the photo's first, and only then does the handler decide whether to act. ⌘ or ctrl with the wheel zooms about the pointer by `exp(−deltaY / 100)` (ctrl too, because Chromium and some mice send a pinch as ctrl+wheel); the wheel alone pans by the distance the fingers moved when the photo is larger than the cell (`!store.showsWholePhoto()`); at Fit, in Crop and in Pick white it does nothing. There is **no** wheel listener on `document` or `window`: a non-passive one there makes WebKit's scrolling thread ask the busy web process before each scroll step over the whole window, the tool panel included. A cell without `gestures` (the dev page) leaves the wheel to the page.
- **Pinch.** WebKit sends a trackpad pinch as `gesturestart`, `gesturechange` and `gestureend` with a `scale` since the start, not as ctrl+wheel. The cell zooms about the fingers by `scale / previous scale` and cancels all three. `installDesktopFeel` (`src/desktopFeel.ts`, called from `main.tsx`) cancels the same three events on `window`, so a pinch anywhere else never reaches the browser's own zoom. WKWebView has magnification off, so this is a guard, not the only protection. Whether WKWebView delivers the events at all is a check on a Mac.
- **Nothing else scrolls or bounces.** `html`, `body` and `#root` are `overflow: hidden` with `overscroll-behavior: none` (every screen is a fixed grid whose own boxes scroll), the photo cell is `overscroll-behavior: none`, and every box that scrolls on its own (`.ym-tools__scroll`, `.ym-dialog__body`, `.ym-menu__list`, `.ym-wizard__main`, `.ym-develop-list`, `.ym-step-rail`, `.ym-filmstrip__scroll`) is `overscroll-behavior: contain`: it keeps its own end stop and never hands the rest of a gesture to the page. The filmstrip is a row: `overflow-y: hidden`, it scrolls along and never up and down. `src/styles/desktop.node.test.ts` fails when a rule sets `overflow` to `auto` or `scroll` on a selector that is not in that list, so a new scroller cannot forget it. `overscroll-behavior` needs WebKit 16, which every supported macOS has (14 and later ship Safari 17). The dev page (`components.html`) scrolls as a whole and says so in `dev.css`.
- **Coalescing.** Wheel, pinch and drag input goes through `store.queueViewportInput` into a `ViewportInputQueue` (`src/state/viewportInputQueue.ts`): pans add up, zooms about the same point (within a pixel) multiply, a different kind or a moved anchor closes the entry so the order is kept, one `requestAnimationFrame` sends what has been collected, and only one `viewport_input` is in flight (later frames merge into one waiting message). A burst of 100 wheel events is one command. `flushViewportInput` sends at once: when a drag ends (pointer up), and before a click-type input (a double click, Pick white) and before a zoom mode chosen from the menu or a key, so these land after the wheel input that came before them (`viewport_input` is answered on a pool of threads, so two commands can overtake each other: this is why there is only one in flight). `setCurrent` and `closeSession` drop what has not been sent. A refusal is reported once (the error bar) and drops what was waiting. The zoom mirror follows once per closed zoom, so once per frame. `store.viewportInput` is the same input sent now, in order (tests, double click, Pick white).
- **Text selection.** `html` is `user-select: none` (and `-webkit-user-select`, `-webkit-touch-callout: none`) with `cursor: default`. Selectable again, with the prefixed property WebKit needs: fields (`input`, `textarea`, `contenteditable`, with the I-beam), the command line and its example, the folder an export goes to, the template problem, the CLI path hint in Preferences, the About text, the error bar's text, the wizard's errors (the Downloads-folder problem and the failed import), the drop zone's notice ("No ARW files in what you dropped."), the Start screen's error line (only the one with `role="alert"`: the same note class also styles static notes, which stay unselectable), the reason over a photo that can't be opened, the file names and notes of the queues, the Look import wizard's file names and explanations, the `.cube` dialog's file names and reasons, and the Info rows of the RAW tab. The photo chip, the camera line, headings, tags and buttons stay unselectable (selecting there fights the pan and the double click). `img` and `a` are `-webkit-user-drag: none`. The CSS test names every selectable class and checks that the markup still uses it.
- **Context menu.** `installDesktopFeel` cancels `contextmenu` unless the target is editable (a field, `contenteditable`) or selectable text (the nearest ancestor with a `user-select` that is not `auto` says `text`), so WebKit's page menu (Reload, Back) never opens over the app, and Copy and Paste still do where they make sense. The right-click Inspect Element is gone with it; in a debug build the web inspector opens with ⌥⌘I.
- **Cursor.** A button is an arrow, as in a Mac app (`button { cursor: default }`, and every component rule that said `pointer` now says `default`). Only link-styled buttons (`LinkButton`, `ResetLink`) and real links keep the pointing hand; the CSS test lists them. A disabled control keeps the arrow; a field the I-beam; the slider, the wheel, the curve and the crop frame their own cursors.
- **What redraws.** The Photos screen no longer follows `render_stats`, the zoom or the export run: the zoom button (`ZoomMenu`), the status text (`StatusText`) and the Export cell (`ExportCellContainer`) read them themselves, and the screen reads the settings of every photo, the export options and the preferences only while the Export panel is open. The tool tabs and `PhotoView` are `memo` with stable callbacks, so a frame, a wheel zoom or an export tile redraws one button or one span. `export_progress` is applied with the other batched events (`later`, 32 ms). The Look tab reads only the number of photos and picks from the session. Pick white's `letterboxed` is a selector that returns a boolean, and only while it is on.
- **Histogram.** The Light and Curve tabs call `store.watchHistogram()` (ref-counted; Light also asks for the middle readout, which the store keeps as `middleReadout`). It asks once at once, then after `render_stats` at most every 200 ms (a throttle, not a debounce: the plot follows a drag, five times a second, and updates once after the last frame), and nothing while nobody watches. Before, every presented frame made a `histogram` and a `sample_pixel` request that the next frame had to wait for. The plot lags a drag by up to 200 ms.
- **Look tiles.** One `IntersectionObserver` for the whole Look tab (`createTileWatch`); each tile has an `inView` flag that goes both ways. Only the tiles in view ask for a picture when the photo's other settings change, and 400 ms after they stop changing (`THUMBNAIL_SETTLE_MS`; before it was 250). A tile that leaves keeps its picture and asks again when it is back, if the settings changed meanwhile. `LazyLookTile` is `memo` with one `onSelect(id)` for the grid, so a tick of the status line redraws no tile and choosing a look redraws two. `store.lookThumbnail` lets two requests run at once (`LOOK_THUMBNAIL_CONCURRENCY`; the core has one foreground worker and a job is never taken off it, so the next frame of the viewport waits behind at most one tile), newest first, in order within a group asked for together. A request that has not started when the photo's settings change (a new key) or the person moves to another photo is dropped and rejects with `Superseded` (`isSuperseded`): `useLookThumbnail` does not count it as a failure, spend a retry on it or ask again. Sections that are folded draw no tiles and observe nothing.
- **Small things.** `PhotoCell` reads the cell's place from the last report (the observer, the window resize, the display scale) on a pointer move or a wheel event and not from `getBoundingClientRect` each time; a cell with no size yet is measured. The updater tells its listeners when the percentage changes, not at each chunk of the download.
- **Measuring (M3-20, frontend side).** `src/perf.ts` is off unless the page was opened with `?perf` (or `?perf=1`; `?perf=0` says no and wins), the shell set `window.__YM_PERF__ = true` before the page ran (it does when the app is started with `YM_PERF`, which is how the Mac viewport job turns it on: it can add no address and fill no storage), or `localStorage.ymPerf` is set; off, it records nothing, `getApi()` returns the plain api and `perfCount` is an empty function. On, it counts late animation frames (more than 24 ms after the one before), the renders of `Photos`, `LookTab` and `LazyLookTile`, the store's notifications (`store.notify`), the events the api delivers (`event.<name>`) and the calls and round trips of every command, and `window.__ymPerf.snapshot()` returns one JSON object (`reset()` starts again). A React development build with `StrictMode` renders twice, so count in a production build or in the tests. Nothing is sent anywhere: it stays in the page.

Checks that need a Mac (`docs/STATUS.md`, M0-H4): the two-finger pan at Fit and zoomed, over the header, the filmstrip, the panel's end stops, a dialog and the zoom menu moves only the intended content; a pinch over the photo zooms it about the fingers and nothing zooms the page; ⌘ and ctrl with the scroll zoom the photo; the panel's momentum scroll works and its end stop bounces inside the panel only; nothing selects by drag, ⌘A, double click on the photo or triple click in the header, and the command line, the CLI path in Preferences, error text and About do.

```
src/

State lives in Rust; the frontend keeps a mirror for rendering and sends patches. Slider drags send throttled patches (one per animation frame) and a final patch on release.
