# 11 · User interface

This document specifies the app's interface: the screens (Start, Photos, the Export panel, the looks wizard), the tool tabs with their cards and controls, the copy, the states, the keyboard shortcuts and the native viewport. The mockups in `design/mockups/` give layout, size and copy; the look is the dark glass of `design/screenshots-c2/` (every state at 2880 × 1800; the end-to-end suite compares the app with them), with the tokens in `design/tokens.css` and `design/tokens.json`. Where a mockup and the dark glass interface differ, the interface and `design/screenshots-c2/` win. The mockups' sizes (the cells, the 38 px slider row, the 72 × 30 value box, the tab row) hold unless this file says otherwise.

## The dark glass interface

The screens keep the cells, sizes and copy of the mockups; every control is a glasscn component (MIT, a shadcn registry; source in `src/components/glass/`) on Tailwind 4 and Radix, in Figtree throughout.

- **Dark only.** `index.html` sets `class="dark"`, `data-theme="dark"`, `color-scheme: dark` and paints the root `#0f0f10` inline, so nothing is white before the first style sheet loads. `--paper` of the dark theme in `tokens.css` is that colour. The window behind the page, the letterbox of the core's frames and the window's `backgroundColor` are the same colour (*Native viewport* in *Shell notes*). There is no light theme.
- **Material.** Panes are glasscn's glass: a translucent dark tint, a thin light rim, a soft shadow, backdrop blur where a pane sits over the aurora. The header, the tool panel and the Export panel are one glass pane each; the cells inside them are separated by 1 px light rims (`--rim`). The filmstrip has a near-opaque surface of its own (no blur), the same neutral grey, a little darker at the foot (`--strip-neutral`).
- **Colour.** Yellow is only a button accent: #FFC20E with black text on it (`--primary`), used by the primary buttons (Open photos, Next, Export), pressed toggles (Before, Crop, Pick white, AUTO) and the selected tab's underline. No large area carries yellow or a tab colour as a tint: the title blocks, the Start block, the wizard rail, the drop zone, tags and notices are neutral glass. Each tab keeps an accent of its own for what belongs to it (the slider range, a changed value's ring and text, the selected look tile), with black text on it; the seven accents are in `src/styles/glass-c2.css` (Lens is a soft violet, #BD8CFF: black text on it 8.4:1, the colour on the ground 7.6:1 and on a glass pane about 5.3:1, `src/styles/contrast.node.test.ts`). A disabled primary button is neutral glass, not faded yellow. Red is for what failed.
- **Ground.** A still aurora of three neutral grey blobs on a near-black ground (glasscn's Graphite palette with every hue taken out; `animate={false}`, so nothing runs under the glass). The frost, the fills and the grey labels have no hue either, so the panes are pure grey and the colour on screen is the photos', the yellow buttons' and the tab accents'. A grey surround also leaves the photo's colours to be judged against nothing tinted. On the Photos screen the aurora is cut out of the photo cell (`.ym-aurora` is masked by the rectangle the screen reports as `--cell-x/y/w/h`), because with the native viewport the photo is a layer behind the page.
- **Controls.** `Button` (glass for actions, prominent for the primary one, link for text actions), `Toggle` (Before, Crop, Pick white and the AUTO buttons, all `aria-pressed`), line `Tabs`, `SegmentedControl`, `Slider` with `Input`, `Switch`, `Select`, `SearchField`, `DropdownMenu` (Fit; the default look on Start), `Dialog`, `Tooltip`, `Badge`, `Progress`, `Item` (every row of the Automatic card, the queue, the wizard, Preferences and About), `RadioGroup` (the Lens card's lenses, as `Item` rows), `Card` (the drop zone, the wizard tiles) and `Collapsible` (a look library's header). `src/components/` keeps the app's own props, roles and accessible names around them. **A control that glasscn has is never hand-written**; `e2e/glass.spec.ts` audits all 41 page states for it (the 30 of the dark glass interface, the two of Colour warp, the three of Apple Auto, the one of Auto crop, the three of the Lens tab and the two of the Auto providers). What stays ours has no glasscn counterpart: the colour wheels, the curve plot, the histogram, look tiles, filmstrip thumbnails, the crop frame, the pick layer, the photo cell and the ruled bands of the tool panel (cards, title block, field rows).
- **Tooltips.** A `title` on a `Button` is a glass tooltip (it also shows on a locked button, so that it can say why it is locked) and the button's accessible description (a hidden span and `aria-describedby`). A test reads it with `toHaveAccessibleDescription`, not as a `title` attribute. Every `Button` has the same tree whether or not it has a title (a span for the tooltip around the `<button>`), so a button whose title appears or goes (the wizard's Next, the Export panel's estimate, Import in the LUT dialog) keeps its element, and the keyboard focus stays on it. The tooltip opens on hover only, never on keyboard focus: an open tooltip is Radix's topmost layer and takes the first Esc, so a dialog or a mode (Pick white, Crop) would need a second press. For a keyboard user the reason is the description, and the wizard's footer says it in a note. The same wrapper is on the Update cell (a locked glass button takes no pointer events, so a native `title` on it would never show). A locked `Toggle` (Crop and Pick white while a photo is developing, Pick white while cropping) is muted and inert like a locked `Button`.
- **The filmstrip.** The strip runs the full width of its row; there is no counter cell. One line of muted text in its top inset says where you are, "4 of 12 · 3 picked" ("3 to export" in the Export panel), and describes the strip for assistive technology (`aria-describedby`). Each thumbnail is as tall as the row less the tool panel's inset (24 px) above and below, 3:2, all one size (about 124 × 83 px at 1440 × 900); the current one has a 3 px accent ring. Each thumbnail has glasscn's context menu with Remove from filmstrip (*Removing photos*, below); the thumbnail a menu is open for is ringed.
- **Selection frames are drawn whole.** The ring of the look in use (a 2 px outline 2 px off the picture: 4 px out), the focus ring (3 px, 2 px off: 5 px out) and the crop's handles are drawn outside the box they belong to, so a box that clips its content must not stand within a ring's width of them. Where one does, it gives the ring room instead of the layout moving. A Look section's body is glasscn's `Collapsible`, which clips so that its height can animate: `.ym-look-group__body` has 8 px of padding on every side and the same as a negative margin, so no tile moves and the ring of a tile in the first row, the first column or the last column is whole. A tab stop that scrolls a tile into the tool panel centres it when the tile is wholly out of view but aligns it to the panel's edge when it is partly in view (the browser's own rule), so the panel (`.ym-tools__scroll`) has `scroll-padding-block: 8px`, which leaves the aligned tile clear of the edge by more than its ring. (The Look tab ends with the Tone card, which is not enough to scroll a tile of a later row under the panel's top edge, so the test of the partly hidden case at the top uses the first row in the smallest window and checks, before it focuses the tile, that the tile really stands partly above the edge; a second test does the same at the bottom edge in every window. Until the Lens card moved to its own tab, the Look tab was long enough for the first test to use a tile of the second row.) The filmstrip needs nothing more than its 24 px inset and the reveal margin (24 px) it keeps at both ends. The crop's handles on the edge of the photo cell are drawn back inside it by their own size (`--crop-handle`, 14 px) with their focus ring inside them, and the frame's focus ring is inside the frame. `e2e/selection-frame.spec.ts` measures every ring against everything that clips it (`e2e/helpers/frames.ts`: the ring's outer edge from its outline and shadows, against the padding box of each ancestor that clips, and the window) for each look tile, the chosen row of the Lens tab and the focus ring of each of its rows (the row's ring is an inset ring of the tab colour, so nothing of it lies outside the row; the circle's focus ring is 5 px out and the circle stands 14 px in from the row's side; the circle is what the browser scrolls into the panel, and the row's top and bottom stand 9 px beyond it, so the circle has a `scroll-margin-block` of 10 px on top of the panel's scroll padding: without it the row's top edge was cut by 0.6 px when an arrow key scrolled the list up), every photo of the strip from its start to its end, and the crop, at 1440 × 900, 1180 × 760 and 1800 × 1100. A new selected thing that stands near the edge of a clipping box needs its line there.
- **Keyboard, as Radix gives it.** Tabs, segmented controls and the Lens card's list of lenses take the Tab key once and move with the arrows (roving focus; a radio list or a segmented control chooses as the arrows move, a row of tabs selects the tab); a slider's knob takes the arrows (Shift or Page keys ×10, Home, End); the same keys with ⌘, ⌃ or ⌥ are left to the app (`Slider` stops them before Radix sees them). A menu (`Menu`) opens on the checked item and the arrows wrap. While a dialog or a menu is open Radix hides the rest of the page from assistive technology and stops its pointer events; a dialog gives the focus back to the element that had it when it opened (`Dialog` remembers it; Radix would use a trigger, and these dialogs have none).
- **Local changes to the registry copies** (`src/components/glass/`). `context-menu.tsx` is the registry's file as it is, but for its `"use client"` line (the app has no server components, as in the other copies). `search-field.tsx` makes Clear a click target and not a Tab stop (Tab goes from the search box to the first tile, as in a macOS search field), and lets an Esc in an empty field reach the page, so Pick white and Crop still hear it; an Esc that clears text is used up. `Slider` (ours) ignores any pointer button but the primary one: Radix's slider takes a right click on the track as a drag. `slider.tsx` has a `size="sm"` knob, round and 16 px across instead of glasscn's 24 × 36 px capsule, which every `Slider` row uses: the tool panel's track is about 104 px long and the capsule took a third of its travel. The tool tabs fill the 46 px tab row, so their labels sit in its middle and the underline on its rim (`glass-c2.css`; glasscn's tab is 32 px high and stacked at the top of the row).
- **Cursor.** A button keeps the arrow; a link-styled one (`LinkButton`, `ResetLink`, a section's text action: glasscn's `link` variant) is a pointing hand while it can be used.
- **Reduced motion** is honoured by glasscn's own `motion-reduce` rules; under `prefers-reduced-motion` `global.css` also turns transitions off and animations to their end.

Files: `src/styles/glass.css` (Tailwind 4 and glasscn's tokens, keyframes), `shadcn-base.css` (the shadcn base colour tokens glasscn assumes), `glass-c2.css` (the palette, the accent, a colour per tab, the layout and the filmstrip), `glass-c2-pages.css` (Start, the wizard, the modes, the dialogs, the error bar), `legacy.css` (the app's own component sheets, placed in Tailwind's `base` layer so that glasscn's utilities win). Licences: `docs/14`.

## Window

- Tauri window, `titleBarStyle: Overlay`, hidden title. The traffic lights sit over the folder cell; the cell keeps 84 px of left padding there.
- Minimum size 1180 × 760. Default 1440 × 900.
- Dark appearance only. The window is `#0f0f10` from its first moment (`backgroundColor` in `tauri.conf.json`) and `native.rs` sets the same colour on the `NSWindow`, so no white or clear frame shows at launch.

### Start

- **Header.** Moon and "Yellowmoon", sub-line "DROP ARW FILES TO START"; "Open files ⌘O" and "Open folder ⇧⌘O"; the update cell; Export (disabled, title "Open photos to export").
- **Left block.** "Drop Sony ARW files." and the blurb, then a table: Camera "Any Sony camera · every kind of ARW"; Default look (with "Change"); ARRI looks ("Not imported yet" and "Import →", or "87 looks imported").
- **Right block.** A dashed drop zone "Drop files or a folder", "ARW only, as many as you like", with the buttons "Open files…" and "Open folder…". The whole window accepts drops.
- **After a drop** the drop zone becomes "Developing N photos" with a progress bar and a list of files, each tagged Waiting, Developing ("Decoding and demosaicing"), Ready ("Auto exposure, white balance and noise") or Skipped (orange `#FF5A1F`, with the reason, for files that can't be developed). The summary reads "Everything is developed and ready to export." or "N file(s) skipped. Everything else is developed and ready to export." When all are done the zone reads "N photos ready" with "Open photos →" and "Start over". "Open photos →" is available as soon as the first photo is ready ("Available when the first photo is ready" is its title until then); the rest keep developing in the background ("You can start looking while the rest finishes.").
- **With Apple Auto acting** (`docs/19` section 6) the screen waits for Apple's answers too. A photo that is developed but still waits (`PhotoSummary.apple_pending`, kept current by the `apple` stage of `photo_progress`) stays tagged Developing with the note "Graded by Apple Intelligence…" (`APPLE_GRADING_NOTE`; the provider's name, "Claude Code" or "Antigravity", when that one grades, `docs/22` section 12.5). The progress bar counts a photo half done when it is developed and whole when it is graded. Once every photo is developed the title reads "Grading N photos with Apple Intelligence" and the hint "Apple Intelligence is looking at each photo. You can start looking while it finishes." (`APPLE_GRADING_HINT`). "N photos ready" and "Everything is developed and ready to export." come only when no photo waits. "Open photos →" works as soon as the first photo is developed (the photo on screen is asked first). Apple Auto switched off, or found unavailable, ends every wait at once. Tests: `src/screens/Start.test.tsx` (the two "with Apple Auto" cases), `crates/ym-core/tests/apple_auto.rs` (`the_apple_stage_says_which_photos_wait_for_an_answer`, `without_an_advisor_no_photo_waits_and_no_apple_stage_is_sent`).
- **Footer.** Three steps (Open / Adjust, if you want / Export); the current step is highlighted.
- **Non-ARW files** in a drop are ignored and not listed; a drop with only non-ARW files makes the drop zone say "No ARW files in what you dropped." In the app the webview hands every dropped path to `open_paths` and the core, which looks at the disk, does the sorting and gives that sentence (an `open` error that the drop zone shows in place of the error bar): a folder called `Sony.A7C` or `2026.10.04_Lisbon` is a folder, whatever follows its last dot. The browser build only has file names, so there the webview leaves out names that end in another extension (`filterArwPaths`).

### Photos

Grid: columns `minmax(0, 1fr) 400px`; rows `75px auto minmax(124px, 1fr)` (header, photo cell, filmstrip).

**Header cells**, left to right:

1. **Folder cell** (300 px): moon, folder name (24 px, 800; a long name ends in an ellipsis on one line, the full name is the tooltip), and under it "YYYY-MM-DD · 12 PHOTOS" (the capture date of the first photo that has one, then the count; just "12 PHOTOS" when none has a capture time).
2. **Actions:** **Before** (toggles before and after; `\`), **Crop** (C), **Pick white** (W), **Add photos** (⌘O; *Add photos, Copy settings and Paste* below) and **Fit 24% ▾** (the zoom menu: Fit, 50%, 100%, 200%; Z toggles Fit and 100%; the percentage is the Fit zoom of the photo on screen). Right-aligned status text from `render_stats`: "Neural · 42 ms · Display P3" (demosaic path · last render time · output space); below a 1274 px window it is left out so that it never runs over the Export cell.
3. **Update cell**, hidden while idle. "Update available" → "Downloading 42%" → green square "Update ready" (a click restarts; disabled while exporting, tooltip "Restarts after the export finishes") → "Update failed" (the reason on hover). States come from `updater.ts` (phases idle, available, downloading, ready, error). The scheduled check downloads and installs an update it finds by itself, so the cell normally goes from hidden through "Downloading" to "Update ready"; "Update available" shows after a background download failed or after Check for Updates… (`docs/12` §In the app).
4. **Export cell:** "Export ⌘E", or "Export N" when N > 1 photos are picked.

**Photo cell.** Always exactly 3:2 (except in full screen, where it is the whole window: *Full screen*), `width: min(100%, calc((max(100vh, 760px) − 199px) · 1.5))`, centred, with no surround and no hairlines above or below it. The native layer draws here (*Native viewport* in *Shell notes*). Portrait and cropped photos are letterboxed with `--paper`. Overlays:

- Bottom left, a chip with the look and its intensity ("SIGNATURE · 100%" on a photo nobody has touched, since Signature is the default look); hidden in Before mode.
- Bottom right, white text with a soft shadow: "Sony A1 · DSC04204 · ISO 400 · 1/125 · f/2.8".
- Before mode: the Before button is pressed and the photo shows the camera's default rendering, the picture as a Sony camera would produce it by default (its own white balance, colours and tone, with nothing Yellowmoon adds, not even the lens vignetting correction, so the corners are as dark as the camera's own picture leaves them: `docs/02`, *The Before view*), labelled "Before · camera default".
- A pixel readout while hovering with ⌥ held: "R 182 G 164 B 140" (display values).
- Top centre, while the CPU renders the photo, a spinner and "Rendering on the CPU". On a Mac the graphics card normally makes a frame in milliseconds; when the raw stages run on the CPU instead (no usable graphics card, a device error ended the route for the photo or for the app, the device was lost, or a method the device does not run, such as the test-only demosaic switch) a frame, or the 100 % tiles that follow a coarse frame, can take seconds. The note follows `viewport_busy { busy, cpu }` (*Shell notes*): it shows once `busy` and `cpu` have both held for **250 ms**, so a quick frame never flashes it, and goes the moment `busy` is false. It is the cell's glass pill (glasscn `Badge` with the ring `Spinner`, the tint and rim of the Before and look chips, sentence case), 12 px below the top edge and centred, over the crop and pick layers. It takes no pointer events (pan, zoom, double click and the picks work through it), shows in Before and in full screen too, and is a polite `role="status"` outside the picture's `role="img"` (the spinner is `aria-hidden`). Code: `src/screens/RenderingNote.tsx`, in `PhotoCell`'s `notice` slot.

Click selects; ⌘-click or P toggles pick; ←/→ move; horizontal trackpad scroll moves along the strip. Right-click a thumbnail for its menu (Remove from filmstrip, Remove picked photos), and Delete or Backspace takes the photo on screen out of the strip: *Removing photos*.

### Export (panel)

The panel replaces the tool panel and keeps the photo and the filmstrip, which dims the photos that won't be exported. Its title block reads "Export" with "N photos from <folder>. Nothing is saved besides the files you export." The header has "← Back to photos" and "Neural · Display P3"; the caption under the photo leaves out the camera body.

**Rows**, top to bottom:

| Row | Content |
|---|---|
| Photos | This photo / Picked · N / All · N |
| Format | JPEG / TIFF / PNG / DNG, each with its own sub-rows: Quality (JPEG); Bit depth and Compression (TIFF); Bit depth (PNG); the DNG note |
| Colour space | sRGB / Display P3 / Rec.2020, with a note under the choice |
| Size | Full · 8640 px, or Long edge with a slider |
| Save to | the folder and "Change" |
| File name | the template box (`{name}_{look}`) |
| If it exists | Skip / Replace / Keep both |
| Command line | the CLI preview with Copy |
| Estimate | the size estimate |

The footer has the primary "Export N photos" button (56 px) and "Cancel".

**State.** Options, scope and the running export live in the store, not in the panel. Options start from `preferences.export_defaults` and every change is saved back as the default (a drag saves on release; a template that can't be used is never saved). Scope starts as Picked when anything is picked, else This photo, each time the panel opens with no run. "All" counts the photos that can be exported (not those that failed to develop).

**Estimate and command.** `export_estimate` and `cli_preview` are asked again (150 ms after the last change) whenever the job changes. The footer reads "Export N photos" with "≈ 75 MB · ⌘↩" (≈ 1.0 GB from 1000 MB; the time estimate is the button's tooltip). The estimate also carries `adjusted_photos` ("N photos have adjustments this command doesn't include.") and `dng_approximate` (a look with its own display rendering, or ARRI's display rendering chosen in Preferences: the DNG note then adds one sentence, below).

**Colour space notes.** sRGB: "Best for sharing and the web." Display P3: "Wider colours. Use it for recent Macs, iPhones and iPads." Rec.2020: "Very wide colours, for video and HDR work."

**Size.** "Full · 8640 px" is the longest side of the biggest photo in the job. Long edge is a slider from 1000 px to that size in 20 px steps, starting at 3000 px, and remembers its last value; it is disabled for photos hardly bigger than 1000 px.

**Save to.** "Choose…" calls `choose_folder`; "Use default" (shown once a folder was chosen) puts the session's `<source folder> export` back. **File name** is a real text box (`{name}_{look}`) with the first file name previewed under it (made safe as the core makes it: `/ \ : * ? " < > |` and control characters become `_`, leading and trailing dots and spaces are dropped, at most 200 bytes; `{date}` of a photo with no capture time reads `undated`). An explanation shows under it when the template can't be used (`templateProblem`: empty, `/ \ :`, a leading dot, an unknown or unclosed `{token}`, more than 200 characters). With several photos and neither `{name}` nor `{seq}` the panel warns that every photo would get the same name.

**DNG.** The option is always available. A DNG of a look with its own display rendering is made approximately (`docs/09` "What a DNG carries"). When a photo of the job has such a look at an intensity above 0, or ARRI's display rendering is chosen in Preferences (`dng_approximate`), a paragraph under the DNG note says "With a look that includes its own display rendering, the DNG is close to, not the same as, the picture you see." (`ym_export::dng::OWN_DISPLAY_NOTE`; the webview keeps a copy, `OWN_DISPLAY_NOTE` in `ExportOptionsForm.tsx`, and a test in `src-tauri/src/bindings.rs` fails when it drifts). The DNG note has two more paragraphs: the sentence `ym render --help` says about other apps ("Apps other than Adobe's show the stored linear image darker until their own exposure handling is applied.", `ym_export::dng::OTHER_APPS_NOTE`, kept in the webview as `OTHER_APPS_NOTE` and checked the same way), then the Adobe notice and the line that later white balance changes in other apps are approximate (`docs/09` §Legal and §What other apps show).

**Queue.** The running view has the title "Exporting", "1 of 3 done.", a progress bar, a per-file list and the note "Originals are never changed. You can keep working while this runs; an update waits until the export is finished." One row per file: WAITING, a percentage, DONE, SKIPPED (orange: the file existed and "If it exists" is Skip) or FAILED with the reason. The core's `export_progress` names the file under way and the files finished; a failed or skipped file is reported once, as it finishes. Row names start as the panel's prediction and take the core's name as each file is reached (Keep both adds " 2"). The title is Exporting, Exported or Not exported (nothing could be saved); the blurb says "1 of 3 done." or "3 photos saved to Lisbon export." plus what failed or was skipped. Cancel asks the core to stop and returns to the options with "Export stopped. N photos were saved." The finished view reads "Exported", "3 photos saved to Lisbon export." and the note "Originals are never changed. Open the folder or go back to your photos.", with the buttons "Show in Finder" and "Done". Truncated file names carry a tooltip.

*Known limitation.* The folder named in the blurb and the one Show in Finder opens (`ExportRun.destination`) are the panel's prediction (`Save to`), because `export_progress` carries only file names. The core can write elsewhere: `~/Pictures/Yellowmoon export` when `<source folder> export` is not writable, and next to each source folder when the session mixes folders (`default_export_folder` is then null and the panel predicts the fallback). In those cases the finished text and Show in Finder can name a folder the files are not in.

### Crop

The panel replaces the tool panel, with the title block "Crop". One card, Frame, with an AUTO toggle (Auto crop, below) and "Reset" in its header. Rows: Aspect (Original / 1:1 / 4:5 / 16:9 / Free), a Straighten slider −45…45° with a detent at 0 (`crop.angle_deg`) and a "Rotate 90°" button (`orientation`). Footer: "Done" (Return) and "Cancel" (Esc). The viewport shows the whole photo with the crop rectangle (a 2 px ink border with a 2 px paper outer stroke), corner and edge handles (14 px round dots) and thirds lines while dragging. Crop is per photo and in memory only.

- While the panel is open the core is told only the angle and the orientation, with the frame set to the whole photo, so the viewport keeps showing the whole straightened photo (empty corners and all) and the webview draws the frame inside it. Done sends the frame shown (*No border in photos*, below) with the angle and the orientation as one change; Cancel (Esc) puts the old crop and orientation back.
- Return finishes, except on a focused button, which it presses. ←/→ do nothing while cropping; a click on another photo ends the crop without keeping it.
- A frame that is the whole photo at 0° is sent as `crop: null`. Aspect presets follow the shape of the frame (4:5 on a landscape frame is 5:4).
- Handles move with the arrow keys (Shift ×10); the frame moves too.
- A drag belongs to the photo the crop was started on (*Component library*, Slider patches).

**No border in photos** (the owner's decision of 2026-10-22). Straightening never leaves an empty (black) corner in a photo: the frame always lies inside the straightened photo, the way Lightroom keeps a crop to the image.

- **The rule.** The canvas is the photo after its quarter turns, W × H pixels (`canvasOf`). Straightening by θ (clockwise positive) makes the canvas point q show the photo at c + L(q − c), about the centre c = (W/2, H/2), with L(dx, dy) = (cos θ·dx + sin θ·dy, −sin θ·dx + cos θ·dy); a point that lands outside the photo is black. The frame is inside when it lies on the canvas and its four corners land on the photo at least 2 px from its edge (full-resolution pixels, `FIT_MARGIN_PX`; the margin covers the rounding of the reduced views). At 0° it only has to lie on the canvas, so the whole photo at 0° stays exactly the whole photo and is still sent as `crop: null`.
- **The fit.** `fitInside` (`cropMath.ts`) is the core's `fit_inside` (`ym_core::pipeline::geometry`), step for step. The frame is clamped to [0, 1]² (the corner, then the size); at 0° that is all. Otherwise it shrinks about its own centre, keeping its shape in pixels, by as little as puts it inside: each corner moves linearly with the scale, so every bound gives its limit directly. A frame already inside comes back as it is. A frame whose centre lands outside the photo becomes the largest frame of its shape centred on the canvas, fitted the same way.
- **The frame set and the frame shown.** The panel keeps the frame the person set (the base). The frame shown, drawn and sent with Done is the base fitted inside at the current angle. Straighten changes only the angle, so the frame shrinks as the angle grows and grows back to the base towards 0°: the whole photo, then 5°, then 0° again is the whole photo, and Done sends no crop. An aspect, Rotate 90° and Reset set the base as before, and the frame shown is its fit. Auto crop's frame is inside already and is fitted all the same, as a guard; it becomes the base, and turning Auto off puts back the base and the angle from before. A stored crop that pokes outside (an older one, or one from a settings file) opens as the base and is shown fitted; Cancel leaves the stored crop as it was, Done sends the fit.
- **The clamp.** A drag, a handle or an arrow key works on the frame shown; what it asks for is kept inside at the current angle and becomes the base (`constrainFrame`). A frame that is inside is taken as it is, so at 0° nothing changes from before. Otherwise the frame goes from the one shown (inside) as far towards the asked one as it can and stay inside. That is the closed form of a bisection between the two: every corner moves linearly on the way and the inside of the photo is convex, so every bound gives its limit directly. A move or a handle of a Free frame goes across first, then up or down, so a frame pushed into a slanted edge slides along it instead of stopping where it first touches. A handle of a frame with an aspect goes the straight way, which keeps the shape and the opposite corner. The largest frame at an angle can't move or grow, and an arrow key that can't move the frame still makes the frame shown the base.

**Auto crop** (`docs/20-auto-crop.md` §1, the owner's request of 2026-10-22; the core's side is `docs/20`). The Frame card's AUTO (`AutoToggle` with `onOff`, labelled Auto, accessible name "Auto crop", in the header before Reset) switches both ways:

- **On.** It shows pressed at once and the note reads "Finding a frame…". The store asks the core for its proposal at the draft's aspect and orientation (`AppStore.suggestCrop`, `suggest_crop`); the answer sets the draft's straighten angle, told to the core as Straighten tells it, and its frame, the answer's first. The draft from just before the press is kept.
- **While on**, a new aspect or Rotate 90° asks again (the frame jumps to the new answer; the angle does not depend on the aspect). Dragging the frame or a handle, the arrow keys and Straighten turn Auto off and keep what was moved; Reset turns it off and resets as without Auto.
- **Off.** Pressed while on, it puts back the draft from just before it was turned on (frame, angle, aspect and orientation); when the panel opened with Auto on, the whole photo at 0° in the aspect chosen now.
- **Done** sends the crop with `auto: true` while the toggle is on (`draftToCrop`; the whole photo at 0° is still `crop: null`, and with Auto off the field is left out). Cancel is as without Auto. A crop with `auto: true` opens with the toggle on, and the panel asks again to fill the note (the answer is the same, `docs/20` §9).
- **Only the latest request counts.** An answer that arrives after Auto went off, after another aspect or orientation was asked for, or after the panel closed is not used.
- `A` toggles it while the panel is open (*Keyboard shortcuts*); Space and Return press it. It is locked (`aria-disabled`) while the photo is developing, as Crop is.

**The note** is a muted `RowNote` (two while there is an answer) at the foot of the Frame card, in a `role="status"` group so that a screen reader hears it change, shown while Auto is on. While a proposal is on its way (also after a new aspect or Rotate 90°) it is the one line "Finding a frame…". Then the frame, "Cropped to frame the subject." or "No better frame found, so the whole photo is kept." (`keep`), and the angle, "Levelled by 1.4°." (the magnitude, one decimal, as Straighten writes it without the sign), "Already level." or "No horizon or straight lines found, so the photo is not turned." (`unsure`). A request that fails turns Auto off and the note reads "Couldn't suggest a frame for this photo." for 4 s (`AUTO_CROP_FAILED_MS`); nothing goes to the error bar.

Code: `src/screens/crop/` (`CropPanel.tsx` with `AUTO_CROP_LINES` and `autoCropLines`, `CropOverlay.tsx` drawing `CropSession.frame`, `useCropSession.ts` with `CropDraft.auto`, `toggleAuto`, `autoNote` and `shownFrame`, `cropMath.ts` with `canvasOf`, `isInside`, `fitInside` and `constrainFrame`). ### Colour warp

The Colour tab's third card, under Colour balance: a chroma warp in the manner of DaVinci Resolve's Color Warper (`docs/02` C9b). The pins have their own sliders, and Pick colour picks a colour on the photo.

- **Card.** Title "Colour warp"; the header's action is the card's Reset (a `ResetLink`, shown while there are pins), which removes every pin: `{ color_warp: { pins: [] } }` and clears the selection.
- **Tools** (one row above the plane, glasscn controls): a `ToggleGroup` of two tools, **Move** (the default) and **Lock** (a radio group named "Pin tool"); a `Toggle` **Pick colour** (`aria-pressed`); a glass `Button` **Delete pin**, off while no pin is selected.
- **The plane** (ours, like the wheels and the curve plot): a square as wide as the card's content (352 px in the 400 px panel), centred on neutral, reaching `|c| = 0.30` at the middle of each edge (`WARP_PLANE_EXTENT`). It draws a hue field (each point's colour at a fixed bright grey, faded toward neutral at the centre), faint rings at 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3 and the two axes, a faint scatter of the photo's own colours (`chroma_scatter`, up to 2048 points at the warp's input) and the pins. A moving pin is a 10 px ring at its source, a line, and a 10 px dot at its target filled with the target's hue; a locked pin is a ring with a small square inside; the selected pin has a ring in the tab accent. `+e1` (red) points right and `+e2` (green against blue) up, so red is at 3 o'clock, green near 11 and blue near 7.
- **The selected pin's sliders**, under the plane, the standard `Slider` rows:

  | Slider | Range | Default | Step | Reads as |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Range | 1–25 (`range × 100`) | 2 | 1 (0.01 of range) | the number |
  | Shadows | 0–100 % | 100 % | 1 % | percent |
  | Highlights | 0–100 % | 100 % | 1 % | percent |
  | Pivot | −6…+6 stops | 0 | 0.1 stop | `+1.0 EV` |
  | Exposure | −2…+2 stops | 0 | 0.01 stop | `+0.30 EV` |

  A locked pin shows Range only. With no pin selected the rows are replaced by one line: "Drag a colour to move it. Lock holds a colour in place."

**Pointer.**

- With Move: a press on empty plane adds a moving pin there and the drag moves its target; a press on a pin's target dot or source ring selects it and the drag moves its target (the source stays where it was made); a double click on a pin puts its target back on its source.
- With Lock: a click on empty plane adds a locked pin, a drag moves a locked pin as a whole.
- In either tool a press on a pin selects it and a drag moves it. The drag keeps the offset at which the pin was taken (the dot does not jump to the pointer; taken by its source ring, the target moves by as much as the pointer). A press focuses the pin it selects (the plane takes no focus from the mouse), so Delete and the arrows act on it at once.
- The press that adds a pin sends it at once (not final), so the rows show; the release is the final patch, so adding and dragging is one undo step; a press on a pin that does not move sends nothing. Drags send one patch per animation frame and a final one on release.
- Dragged and keyed points stay on the drawn square (`|a|, |b| ≤ 0.30`); a stored point outside it (from the CLI, or Pick colour on a very strong colour, which keeps the sampled chroma) is drawn at the edge.
- Sixteen pins at most: at sixteen a press on empty plane, or a Pick colour click, selects no pin and the line under the plane reads "16 pins at most." in place of the help line.

**Keyboard.** Each pin is a focusable `role="slider"` named "Pin 3"; Tab moves through the pins and the focused pin is the selected one. The arrows move its target (a lock: its position) by 0.002 (Shift ×10), Home puts the target back on the source (nothing on a lock), Delete or Backspace removes the pin and gives the focus to the pin before (the plane when none is left). Each key is one undo step. The focus indicator is the selection ring in the tab accent (3 px with `:focus-visible`), not an outline, which would box the pin's whole line.

**Accessible values.** `aria-valuenow` is the hue angle of the target (0–359). `aria-valuetext` is one of "moves red toward orange", "holds red" (a lock), "red, not moved", "moves red toward a stronger red" (or "a paler red") when the move stays in one hue, and "grey" within the neutral guard. A hue is named after the nearest of eight angles measured on the plane from pure LogC4 colours: red 0°, yellow 60°, green 120°, cyan 180°, blue 240°, magenta 300°, with orange (30°) and purple (270°) halfway.

**Pick colour.** The toggle turns on the same crosshair layer as Pick white (the two are exclusive; Esc ends either). A click asks the core for the chroma of that pixel at the warp's input (`sample_chroma x, y`, CSS pixels from the photo cell's corner, as Pick white) and adds a moving pin there with its target on its source, selected, so the next drag on the plane moves it. The mode stays on until Esc, the toggle, Pick white (W), Crop or another photo, so several colours can be picked in a row. A click switches to the Colour tab (the mode may outlive it); a click on the letterbox does nothing. `store.pickColor` flushes the viewport input and the patches in flight, asks `sample_chroma` and adds the pin as one undo step.

**Hue field.** The grey is LogC4 0.45; the colour is taken to Display P3 and rendered keeping its hue (a colour outside P3 is pulled toward its own luminance, at least 8 % of its largest channel, then the channels are scaled by `1 / (0.6 + max)`), so it rolls off instead of clipping into flat bands. It shows at `0.92 · smoothstep(0, 0.225, |c|)`, fading by 80 % between 0.30 and 0.42. It is drawn once per size (128–384 px, scaled up by the browser) on a Display P3 canvas, sRGB where there is none. The scatter is 1.8 px dots of white at 42 %.

**Copy, paste, undo.** The pins are one setting, `color_warp`, so Copy settings and Paste move them with the rest of the grade, and each finished change is one undo step. They are per photo and in memory only, like every setting.

**Code.** `ColorWarpCard` (`src/screens/tools/ColorWarpCard.tsx`) is the third card of `ColorTab`. The plane is `WarpPlane` (`src/components/WarpPlane.tsx`; props `pins`, `onChange(pins, final)`, `selected`, `onSelect(index | null)`, `tool` `"move" | "lock"`, `scatter`), drawn from `src/components/warpMath.ts`, a TypeScript port of `crates/ym-color/src/warp.rs` (`warpMath.node.test.ts` checks the constants against the Rust file). The selected pin is window state in the store (`warpPin`, `selectWarpPin`; none on another photo, an index past the end counts as none). The scatter is `chroma_scatter`, asked for while the card is mounted and again after frames at most every 200 ms, as the histogram is (`watchChromaScatter`). ### Removing photos

The core side is `docs/01` §*Removing photos*.

- **What it means.** A photo leaves the session, and with it the filmstrip, the picks, the caches and its edits, because nothing is saved per photo (AGENTS rule 4). **The file on disk is never touched** (rule 5): not deleted, not moved, not renamed, not written. The words say so: "Removed DSC04204. The file is still on disk." and, for several, "Removed 3 photos. The files are still on disk." There is no Trash icon anywhere in this feature.
- **How.**
  1. *Right-click a thumbnail* (Control-click, or the Menu key or Shift+F10 on the focused thumbnail) for glasscn's `ContextMenu` (`src/components/glass/context-menu.tsx`) with **Remove from filmstrip**, which takes out the photo that was clicked, whether or not it is the one on screen, and does not choose it. While any photo is picked the menu also has **Remove picked photos** with the count at its right.
  2. *Delete or Backspace* removes the photo on screen. It is ignored in a text box, on a slider's knob, in a menu or a dialog (`usesDeleteKey` in `src/state/shortcuts.ts`: the colour warp's pins, the curve's points and the wheels use the keys themselves), while Crop or Pick white is on, and in the Export panel (Back to photos first). A key held down removes one photo, not the strip.
  There is no menu-bar item: a ⌫ key equivalent on a native item would take the key from every text box on a Mac.
- **Selection and Picked.** The filmstrip has no multi-selection: it has the photo on screen and the picks, which are marks for Export and "Apply to". So Delete removes only the photo on screen (a forgotten pick must never make a key press remove many photos), and the several-photo removal is the explicit menu item. The menu and the key work on any photo, also one that failed to open (CAN'T OPEN) or is still developing, which stops developing.
- **After a removal.** If the photo on screen was removed, the next photo is shown, or the previous one when the last was removed, with Fit and the camera view off as for any other move; if another photo was removed the photo on screen stays (its place in "4 of 11" moves). If no photo is left the Start screen shows and the notice stays. Keyboard focus that was on the removed thumbnail goes to the photo on screen, so the arrows go on working.
- **The notice and Undo.** The notice bar (`ErrorBar`, a glasscn `Item`) says what was removed and has an **Undo** button next to Dismiss. Undo puts the photos back where they were in the filmstrip, with their settings, picks and ⌘Z steps; the photo that was on screen is on screen again unless the person has moved to another photo since. The notice then says "Put back DSC04204." or "Put back 3 photos." (and "1 was already open." for a photo whose file was added again in between, which stays out so that no file is in the strip twice). Only the **last removal** is kept, in memory (the core keeps the photos, the page their undo histories), until the next removal or the end of the session (⌘W, Start over, or files opened on the Start screen after the last photo was removed, which begin a new one); a second removal replaces it, so Undo is one step, not a history. The notice is replaced by any other notice and goes with Dismiss, but the removal stays undoable.
- **⌘Z.** The undo stack is per photo, so ⌘Z (and Edit › Undo) takes the removal back when it is the last thing done: nothing has been changed since, or what was changed has been undone (the page compares the number of undo steps of all photos with the number it had when the photos left). After a new change ⌘Z undoes that change first, then the removal. On the Start screen after the last photo was removed, ⌘Z puts the photos back. There is no redo of a removal.
- **Export.** The Export panel's strip has no menu and Delete does nothing there. An export that is already running or queued keeps the photos it was started with and finishes them, with the settings they had when it was started (its jobs hold snapshots, `docs/01` §*The queue*): removing photos does not cancel or shorten it, because the person asked to remove the photos from the strip, not to stop an export.
- **Copy and paste.** Copied settings stay in the store; if the photo they came from is removed, Paste works as before (the settings are a patch, not a reference to the photo).

**Code.** `AppStore.removePhotos(ids)` waits for the settings patches still in flight to those photos (none may reach a photo that left), calls `remove_photos`, takes the answer's `photo_ids` (the core says which ids were in the session), keeps the removed photos' `PhotoHistory` objects and the history depth in a private `PendingRemoval`, drops their thumbnails (`releaseUrl`), settings, look tiles, senders and gestures, and changes the session with `withoutPhotos` (the page's own entries stay; the snapshot of the answer is older than the events the page has handled since, as for `mergeSession`). `removeCurrentPhoto()` is Delete, `removePickedPhotos()` the menu's second item, `undoRemoval()` the Undo button, and `undo()` (⌘Z) tries `removalIsLatest()` first. `AppState.noticeAction` (`"undo-removal"`, cleared by every other notice) makes `ErrorBar` draw the Undo `Button`. `Filmstrip` takes `onRemove` and `onRemovePicked` (no menu without them, and none in `export` mode). The strip has **one** `ContextMenu` around its scroller and not one per thumbnail (hundreds of Radix roots would be redrawn whenever a thumbnail arrives): the thumbnail a menu is about is found from the event's target (`data-photo-id`), a right click between the thumbnails opens nothing, and the one the menu is open for has `data-menu="open"` (the ring). The mock plays the core's rules (`removeNow`, `restoreNow` in `api.mock.ts`: the same successor, places, current photo after Undo, no id used twice). Tests: `src/state/store.remove.test.ts`, `src/screens/Photos.remove.test.tsx`, `src/components/Filmstrip.test.tsx` (the menu and the focus), `src/state/shortcuts.test.ts`, `src/state/photo.test.ts`, `src/api.mock.remove.test.ts`, `e2e/remove.spec.ts`.

### Full screen

F shows the photo alone, filling the screen.

- **In.** F on the Photos screen hides the header, the filmstrip and the tool panel (the HTML `hidden` attribute, so they are gone for a screen reader too) and the camera line under the photo; the photo cell becomes the whole window (`data-fullscreen="true"` on the screen; the grid is one cell, and the cell is not 3:2 then). The look chip, the Before label and the ⌥ readout stay: they answer keys that still work. The cell reports its new rectangle as after a window resize (the `ResizeObserver` of `PhotoCell`), so the native viewport, the aurora's cut-out and Fit follow it; a fixed zoom stays as it was. In the app the window also goes into macOS full screen (`getCurrentWindow().setFullscreen(true)`), unless it already is (the green button, ⌃⌘F); in a browser and on the mock only the page changes. A control that had the focus and is now hidden gives it up (the page takes it), so the arrows reach the screen. A polite live region says "Full screen. Press F or Esc to leave."
- **Out.** F again, or Esc. Esc first does what it did before: it ends Pick white or Pick colour, then a crop, then closes the Export panel; full screen is the next thing it leaves. The window leaves macOS full screen only if F put it there. Leaving the Photos screen (⌘W, the wizard) ends full screen and puts the window back as well.

**Code.** `shortcutFor` maps F to `full-screen` (`src/state/shortcuts.ts`); `useFullScreen` (`src/screens/useFullScreen.ts`) holds the state, takes the focus off a hidden control, and drives the window through `windowFollower`, which runs the window calls one after another, remembers whether it entered macOS full screen, and ignores a call that fails. `Header` and `Filmstrip` take `hidden`; the layout is in `src/styles/glass-c2.css`. ### Component library

`components.html` (run `npm run dev`, open `/components.html`) shows every component in every state; `/components.html?replica=photos&tab=light` (also `start`, `export`, `wizard`) rebuilds a mockup screen from them, and `e2e/components.spec.ts` checks that each opens without a script error. The real screens are compared with `design/screenshots-c2/`.

- **AUTO in a card header** is a 26 px toggle button (`AutoToggle`: ink while automatic, pressing it restores Auto); the AUTO and EDITED tags on the Automatic rows are the 22 px `Tag`. The Crop panel's AUTO (Auto crop, *Crop*) is the same button with `onOff`, so that it also switches off.
- **Slider patches.** `onChange(value, final)`. While the thumb is dragged it fires at most once per animation frame with `final = false`, then once with `final = true` on release; keys, typed values, Reset and double click fire once with `final = true`. The same contract holds for `Wheel`, `CurvePlot` and `WarpPlane`. **A drag belongs to the photo it began on.** A control collects input over time (a patch per frame, then the release), and the photo on screen can change between the pointer going down and a frame or the release. So the control keeps the `onChange` of the render the pointer went down in until the gesture ends (`useGestureCallback`, `src/components/hooks.ts`), and every `onChange` that can be a drag sends `store.update(patch, final, rs.photo_id)`, naming the photo its settings were drawn from; the crop session names the photo the crop was started on. Only an input that is handled at once (a key, a click, a typed value) may leave the photo out of `update`, which then means the photo on screen at that moment. A control keeps showing its own value until the controlled value catches up (or one second passes), so a slow reply never makes the thumb jump back.
- **Reset** on an automatic value (Exposure, Kelvin, Tint, Sharpening, Noise reduction) goes back to Auto, not to the number Auto resolves to: pass `onReset`.
- **Wheel keys.** Arrows nudge the puck (Shift ×10), Home, Delete or Backspace centre it; it is exposed as a slider whose value is the strength, with the strength and hue in `aria-valuetext`.
- **Curve.** A press adds a point on the curve at that x, a drag moves it, dragging an inner point more than 14 px off the plot deletes it. The two end points move up and down only and cannot be deleted. Points are drawn only while the plot is hovered, focused or the point is selected, so a resting plot is clean. Keys: Tab through the points, arrows move one code value (Shift ×10), Delete removes, Return on the plot adds a point in the widest gap. Input and Output show the dragged point, else the position under the pointer, else the selected point, else the middle. The Curve tab starts with the inner point nearest the middle selected (drawn filled, with the readout naming it).
- **Photo cell.** `PhotoCell` carries `data-viewport` and reports `{ x, y, width, height, scale }` through `onRect` on mount, on ResizeObserver callbacks and on window resize. With `native` it draws no `<img>` and is transparent (a native layer draws behind it); with a `frame` (the fallback viewport's picture of the whole cell) it draws that picture at its own size, one picture pixel to one device pixel, on paper; otherwise the photo is an `<img>` letterboxed on paper. The Photos screen chooses from `viewport_info` (*Wiring to the shell*); the component's own default for `native` is `isTauri()`.
- **Base line height** is `normal`, as in the mockups.

## Screen notes

Start, Photos, the seven tabs, Crop and Pick white are built in `src/screens/`, on a store (`src/state/store.ts`) that mirrors the core and is the only code that calls `Api`. The wizard, the Export panel, Preferences and About are in `src/screens/` too (`ImportLooks.tsx` with `wizard/`, `export/`, `dialogs/`), on pure rules in `src/wizard/model.ts`, `src/export/*.ts` and `src/state/menu.ts`, all tested without a screen. `e2e/export.spec.ts` and `e2e/wizard.spec.ts` compare every mocked state with `design/screenshots-c2/`. This section holds the rules that the screen descriptions above leave out.

### Store, patches and undo

- **Store.** Plain TypeScript with `subscribe`, tested without React; `context.tsx` provides it. Events (`photo_progress`, `thumbnail_ready`, `render_stats`) are applied in batches of about two frames, so 500 photos developing do not draw 2000 times. A status event that arrives before the session snapshot (the core emits while `open_paths` is still answering) is kept and applied when the session arrives.
- **Patches.** `store.update(patch, final)` sends one patch at a time per photo. While one is in flight, later drag patches fold into one waiting patch, so a slow core is never flooded and the last value always arrives. Fields that need no resolving (look, contrast, light preset, wheels, curve, switches, crop, …) show at once; Auto fields wait for the core's reply. A failed patch shows the error and asks the core for the settings again. A read of a photo's settings never overwrites the reply of a patch made after it began (`writes`).
- **Undo and redo** are per photo, in memory, 100 steps (`HISTORY_LIMIT`). An entry holds only the fields one change touched (before and after, as two patches), so changes made by "Apply to" on other photos never get in the way. A whole drag, or a whole crop session, is one step. ⌘Z waits for patches in flight. "Re-run" is a step. A paste is one step in the history of each photo it changed (the core says which fields changed, `paste_settings`), so ⌘Z on a photo takes that photo's paste back and no other.
- **Errors** from any command show in the notice bar at the foot of the window until dismissed. On Photos the bar stops at the tool panel (`.ym-screen[data-screen="photos"] ~ .ym-error-bar { right: var(--panel-w) }`), so that it never covers Paste or the Export panel's footer buttons.

### Auto semantics

A value equal to what Automatic resolves to *is* Automatic: typing +0.30 into Exposure, or dropping the slider on its detent, sends `{ exposure: { kind: "auto" } }` (the same for Kelvin and Tint together, Contrast, Saturation, Sharpening and Noise reduction). Reset on a row of an Auto field sets only that value (and collapses to Auto when it matches); the card's Reset and the AUTO button send Auto. The AUTO button of the Exposure card says whether *exposure* is automatic, not Contrast or Brightness. White balance counts as automatic only when `white_balance` is Auto, `light_source` is Auto and no white was picked (`whiteBalanceState`). The shared logic is in `src/screens/tools/controls.tsx` (`manualOrAuto`, `pointOrAuto`, `PercentSlider`, `KelvinSlider`, `ContrastSlider`, `SaturationSlider`).

### Tool cards

#### Output sharpening

The Output row of the Sharpening card is the standard slider: 0 to 200 %, step 5, the detent and Reset at **100 %**, the value shown as `100%` (`formats.percent`), no unit word, no AUTO tag. It is a plain number: moving it sends `{ output_sharpening: n }`, its own Reset and a double click send `{ output_sharpening: 100 }`, not Auto (`DevelopSettings.output_sharpening`, `docs/01`). Amount and Radius above it are the capture sharpening (Auto, Richardson–Lucy). The card's Reset puts the whole card back: `{ sharpen: Auto, output_sharpening: 100 }`. The viewport, the filmstrip thumbnails and the Look tiles show the sharpened picture as the slider moves, and the exports are made with it; the Before view, the histogram and the pixel readout do not (`docs/01`). Undo and redo take it like any field (one step per drag). There is nothing on the Export panel and no switch: 0 is off. "Apply to" copies the look and its intensity only (`docs/01`), so the amount stays with the photo unless Copy settings and Paste move them, which take every setting but the crop, the orientation and the Pick white point.

#### Range card

The RAW tab's third card (`docs/02` C7) has two sliders, **Highlights** and **Low light**, and a note. Reset sends `{ rolloff: { highlights: 1, low_light: 0.5 } }`. **Highlights** is the highlight range: 0 to 1 in steps of 0.01, the detent and Reset at 1, shown with `formats.fixed2` (`1.00`); it moves where the camera's clipping point lands, from where it was recorded (0) to white (1), and leaves mid grey and everything up to a stop under the clip alone. **Low light** is the toe near black. Under the two sliders a note (`RowNote`), word for word:

"Highlights moves the brightest tones the camera recorded: 0 keeps them where they were, 1 takes them to white. Low light softens the noise near black."

The labels name no camera maker (AGENTS rule 3); the spec and the CLI help say what the range models. Tests: `src/screens/RangeCard.test.tsx` (the card's title, the two sliders, the note, Reset, a drag sends one `rolloff` patch); the note is also compared with `src/screens/tools/RawTab.tsx` by `src/repo.node.test.ts`.

#### White lift

The white lift (`docs/02` C12 step 1b) shows as the Light tab's White card and the Automatic card's White row. It is an automatic setting: a per-photo scene-linear gain on the tones above +1 stop over middle grey, sized from the photo's own highlights when it is opened, that gives a high-key photo a real white (0 for a low-key one).

- *Automatic card.* A row **White** between Exposure and White balance, value `+0.77 EV` (the lift in effect, `values.white_ev`, `formats.ev` and " EV") or `None` when it is 0, the AUTO tag while the offset is 0 and EDITED otherwise, the arrow opening Light (accessible name `White +0.77 EV, Auto. Open Light`).
- *Light tab.* A second card, **White**, between Exposure and High dynamic range, with the AUTO button (accessible name "Auto white": ink while `white_offset_ev` is 0, pressing it sends `{ white_offset_ev: 0 }`), the card's Reset (the same patch) and one slider, **Offset**: −3 to +3 stops in steps of 0.05, the detent and Reset at 0 (the automatic amount), shown as signed stops with `formats.ev` and no unit word (`0.00`, `+0.30`, `−0.50`). It is a plain number, not Auto: moving it sends `{ white_offset_ev: n }` and shows at once. The Automatic card's row follows with the core's reply: `values.white_ev` is the automatic amount plus the offset, never below 0, so an offset of minus the automatic amount, or lower, reads `None` and gives the render of no lift.
- The row is called Offset because the Light tab already has a **White** slider in High dynamic range (the C9 control, −100 to 100); the two are different settings, and the card is named White after the Automatic card's row.
- Re-run on the Automatic card puts the offset back to 0 with exposure, white balance and noise. Undo and redo take it like any field (one step per drag).
- The lift acts only with the built-in ACES 2 display rendering; with an imported ARRI display LUT or a look with its own display rendering the slider still moves but nothing changes in the picture.
- Tests: `src/screens/WhiteCard.test.tsx` (the card, the row, the AUTO semantics, Re-run, undo), `src/repo.node.test.ts` (the Light blurb).

#### Tone card

The Look tab's third card sits directly under the Look card and has three rows in this order: **Temperature**, **Contrast**, **Saturation**. They are the *same settings* as Colour › White balance › Kelvin, Light › Exposure › Contrast and Colour › Colour balance › Saturation (`white_balance`, `contrast`, `saturation`; no field of their own). Both copies are built from the same code (`src/screens/tools/controls.tsx`), read the resolved value and send the same patch through `store.update`, so moving one moves the other, and undo, redo and Reset take them like any field (one undo step per drag).

- Contrast and Saturation: −100…100, step 1, read as `formats.signed`. Each is Auto or a value, like Exposure: the detent and Reset at the photo's Auto value (0 without Apple Auto), see *Apple Auto*.
- **Temperature is the Kelvin control:** 2000–11000 K in steps of 50, the detent and the box showing the photo's Auto Kelvin (`5450 K`). The row's own Reset sends the Auto Kelvin with the current tint and the light preset and picked white cleared (that is Auto while the tint is Auto); dropping on the Auto value sends `white_balance: auto`; moving it on an Auto white balance makes it Manual at that Kelvin with the Auto tint, as a patch with only `kelvin` does. **Tint stays on the Colour tab only.** The labels differ between the tabs (Temperature on Look, Kelvin on Colour) because the person looking at the Look tab has not met the word Kelvin; the value box reads `5450 K` on both.
- The card header has the AUTO button and, after a Pick white, the EDITED tag, exactly as the White balance card; as on the Exposure card, the button speaks for the temperature only (accessible name "Auto temperature"), not for Contrast or Saturation.
- The card's Reset sends `{ white_balance: auto, light_source: auto, white_pick: null, contrast: auto, saturation: auto }` (the tint goes with the white balance, as with the White balance card's Reset).
- Saturation keeps the luminance (`docs/02` C9): more makes colours stronger, less makes them greyer, and neither makes them darker or lighter. Contrast bends the tones around mid grey and keeps black and the top code value.
- Tests: `src/screens/ToneCard.test.tsx` (sync both ways, undo and redo, Auto semantics, Reset) and `e2e/tone.spec.ts`. In every Look state the card lies below the visible part of the panel, so the Look screenshots do not show it.

### Look tab

#### Look tab sections

The Look card has **one section per library**, in this order, each shown only when it has looks:

1. **Built in**
2. **ARRI looks · LogC4**: the 65-point 3D LUTs, or the `.alf4` look files of the same looks
3. **ARRI looks · LogC3**: log-to-log 3D LUTs
4. **ARRI looks · LogC3 to Rec.709**: 3D LUTs that include the display rendering
5. **ARRI looks · LogC3 to Rec.709 · ALF2**: the 261 `.aml` look files, each look in three strengths
6. **Your LUTs**: the person's own `.cube`, `.alf4`, `.alf4c` and `.aml` files, whatever they expect

With ARRI's five Look Files downloads and the person's own files the list can hold about 600 tiles, and the same title ("Film") can appear in several libraries; the sections tell them apart. The core works the library out (`Look::library`, `crates/ym-looks/src/library/model.rs`, from the group, the input and the file format) and sends it with the look as `library`, next to `format` and `lut_size` (`docs/08` §Look model). The call to action keeps its place between Built in and the rest ("ARRI looks", 0, "The free ARRI Look Library is not imported yet.", "Import ARRI looks →") and shows while no ARRI library has a look.

- **Header.** Each header is a disclosure button: a chevron, the label and the count (`87`; `2 of 87` while searching). The chevron is drawn in CSS (a square turned 45°, 2 px strokes with square ends; pointing down when open, right when folded). The accessible name is the label, the count and the action, `ARRI looks · LogC3, 87 looks, collapse` (`expand` when folded), with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` naming the section's body. #### Apply to

The Look card's scope is a mode (This photo / Picked · N / All · N) that **starts on This photo**. It is not remembered between runs: every start begins on This photo. A tile click or the end of an Intensity drag sends `update_settings` for the current photo, then `apply_look(scope, look, intensity)` for This/Picked/All. With the scope at This photo nothing but the photo's own patch is sent. With the scope on Picked or All, a pick on one photo changes the look of those photos by design; it lasts until the app quits or the person chooses This photo. Reset on the card picks the default look at 100 % the same way. "Make default" appears beside Reset when the selected look is not the default.

- **A pick is fixed when it is made.** The photo (`store.chooseLook(look, intensity, photoId)`, which the Look tab passes from the photo it is drawn for), the scope, the look and its strength are read at the click and never again, so a move to another photo before the core has answered neither retargets the pick nor changes how far it is copied.
- **Order.** The copies reach the core in the order the picks were made (`applyAfter`: a copy waits for its photo's own patch and for the copy before it, so a slow pick on photo 1 cannot land after a later pick on photo 2 and leave every photo with the older look).
- **Cache.** Other photos' cached settings are dropped and asked for again when shown. When a copy lands, the mirror keeps the settings of the photo it was picked on (its own reply) and drops all others; the photo on screen when it lands keeps what it had only until it is read again, at once, and reads of settings that were on their way when it landed are asked again (`bulkEpoch`), so the Look tab never shows a look the photo does not have. Paste to Picked fixes its photos before it looks at the source's settings.
- The Look screenshots (`photos-look`, `photos-look-search`, `photos-look-teal`, `photos-before`) show This photo, and in `photos-look-teal` only the photo on screen has a new thumbnail.

#### Look tiles

Thumbnails are requested through `look_thumbnail` when a tile scrolls into view and again 400 ms after the photo's other settings stop changing (`THUMBNAIL_SETTLE_MS`). A missing look is a disabled tile with the note "File missing or changed. Import again."

- **A tile that failed asks again** (`state/useLookThumbnail.ts`, also used by the wizard's Finish tiles; at most twice for one photo, look and settings, then it stays blank). The store keeps one request per photo, look and settings and hands the same one to a tile that comes back, but the core cancels a photo's tiles when the person moves to another photo (`docs/01`, `spawn_look_thumbnail`), so a quick photo A, B, A gave the returning tile a request that then failed. The store drops a failed request from its cache and the tile asks for a new one (not for a refusal of the library, scope `looks`, which asking again cannot change).
- The shell keeps only 256 MiB of tiles (`LOOK_TILE_BUDGET_BYTES`) and answers 404 for the addresses of the photos used least recently, so on an `<img>` error the store forgets that address (`forgetLookThumbnail`) and the tile asks for a new picture.
- One `IntersectionObserver` serves the whole Look tab (`createTileWatch`); each tile has an `inView` flag that goes both ways. Only the tiles in view ask for a picture when the photo's other settings change. A tile that leaves keeps its picture and asks again when it is back, if the settings changed meanwhile. `LazyLookTile` is `memo` with one `onSelect(id)` for the grid, so a tick of the status line redraws no tile and choosing a look redraws two.
- `store.lookThumbnail` lets two requests run at once (`LOOK_THUMBNAIL_CONCURRENCY`; the core has one foreground worker and a job is never taken off it, so the next frame of the viewport waits behind at most one tile), newest first, in order within a group asked for together. A request that has not started when the photo's settings change (a new key) or the person moves to another photo is dropped and rejects with `Superseded` (`isSuperseded`): `useLookThumbnail` does not count it as a failure, spend a retry on it or ask again. Sections that are folded draw no tiles and observe nothing.

### Add photos, Copy settings and Paste

The core adds to an open session (`Session::open`: a file that is open is skipped, the new photos go after the others in path order, the current photo stays); the Photos screen has a header button for it, and a way to move settings between photos.

#### Add photos

A header button after Pick white and before the zoom menu, title "Add photos (⌘O)". It opens the system's dialog for ARW files (`open_dialog("files")`, the same as ⌘O, which adds on Photos; ⇧⌘O adds a folder). Files or a folder **dropped anywhere on the Photos screen** are added the same way (`useWindowDrop`; a yellow 6 px frame around the window shows a drop will be taken; a dialog that is open takes the drop away).

- The button is disabled while a dialog or an open is under way (`AppState.opening`) and is not drawn while the Export panel shows, like Before, Crop and Pick white. A drop on the Export panel is still taken (an export job is a snapshot, `docs/01` ExportQueue, so adding during one is safe) and the Export scope counts (`All · 14`) follow.
- While the new photos develop the filmstrip shows them developing (`photo_progress`); the current photo, its zoom, the other photos' settings and thumbnails do not change.
- Notices (the bar at the foot, on Photos only; Start has its list of photos developing): "Added 3 photos."; "Added 3 photos. 2 were already open." (the second sentence only when something was skipped; "1 was already open."); "These photos are already open." when every chosen file was open; and "No ARW files in what you dropped." for a drop that holds none (Start shows that in its drop zone). The counts come from the core (`SessionState.opened`, `OpenOutcome`); `openedNotice` in `store.ts` makes the words.
- A file listed twice in one drop (a folder and a file inside it) is one file: it is added once and the repeat is not counted as already open. A file that cannot be read still joins the filmstrip as a failed photo and counts as added.
- The shell's answer is a snapshot taken a little before the page handles it, so with a session open it is **merged** into the page's own session (`mergeSession` in `photo.ts`, `adoptOpened` in `store.ts`) and does not replace it: the photos the page has keep their places, status and picks, the current photo stays, and only the photos that are new, the folder name and the default export folder come from the snapshot. Replacing would bring back a status that an event had just changed (a cell stuck on "developing"), a pick, or the photo that was current.
- **Header room.** At 1180 px the header is full: the labels wrap to their longest word (PICK / WHITE) and the status text ends 7 px from the edge of its cell. "Add photos" needs 87 px more, so below a 1274 px window the status text (the render path, time and space, which is a diagnostic) is left out of the Photos tool view (`@media (max-width: 1273px)` in `screens.css`); at 1440 px every label is one line and the status text stays. `e2e/copy-paste.spec.ts` checks 1180 to 1440 px.

#### Copy and paste settings

- **The foot row.** Two header-style buttons in a ruled strip under the cards (`.ym-tools__foot`, a group named "Copy and paste settings"), 55 px, drawn for the Look, Light, Colour, Detail, Curve and RAW tabs (not the Crop and Export panels, which replace the panel). Copy settings is disabled until the photo is developed; Paste is disabled until something was copied, its title then names the photo ("Paste the settings of DSC04213 (⇧⌘V)"; before that "Copy the settings of a photo first (⇧⌘C)").
- **The notice bar** (`ErrorBar`, until dismissed) stops at the tool panel on Photos, so "Copied the settings of DSC04204." never sits over Paste, which is what the next click is.
- **Edit menu.** Copy Settings, Paste Settings and Paste Settings to Picked (`menu_action`: `copy_settings`, `paste_settings`, `paste_settings_to_picked`), between Undo/Redo and Pick, above the system's Cut, Copy and Paste: plain ⌘C and ⌘V stay the system's for the text boxes. The keys ⇧⌘C, ⇧⌘V and ⌥⇧⌘V belong to the webview's key handler, which takes them when focus is not in a text box (`shortcuts.ts`: with ⌥ the key reports "√" or "◊", so ⌥⇧⌘V is told by `code: "KeyV"`), and **the menu items carry no key**, like Undo, Redo and Select All: a native key equivalent that the page leaves unhandled falls through to the menu on macOS, so a menu item bound to these keys would fire inside a text box (⇧⌘V and ⌥⇧⌘V mean "Paste and Match Style" in other apps) and paste settings onto photos. `src-tauri/src/menu.rs` says why and its test keeps these three keys out of the menu.
- **Menu state.** Copy Settings needs a developed current photo, Paste Settings something copied as well, Paste Settings to Picked something copied and a picked photo; all three are off while a dialog (Preferences, About, the close question, the .cube import) is open, as the Photos keys are. The webview tells the shell when that changes (`set_menu_state`, sent only on a change; the native menu starts with the three off) and the shell switches the items. `set_menu_state` is a synchronous command, like `viewport_set_rect`: it runs on the main thread in the order the webview sent the calls, so two quick changes cannot overtake each other and leave the menu half switched (`commands.rs` has a compile-time test). A menu action that reaches the webview with a text box focused is done: a menu choice is explicit.
- **What is copied.** Everything in `DevelopSettings` except `crop`, `orientation` and `white_pick`, which belong to that photo's own geometry and pixels, so a photo keeps its own. The look and its intensity are included. Auto stays Auto (exposure, light preset, white balance, sharpening, noise reduction): each photo keeps its own automatic exposure, white balance, sharpening and noise, and the white lift's offset (a number of stops added to each photo's own automatic amount) is copied as the number. Manual values are copied as they are. **A white balance that came from a picked white** (a white picked while the light preset and the white balance are both Auto) is copied as the manual Kelvin and tint the pick resolved to, since the point means nothing on another photo and the light it found does: the pasted photo's white balance is manual then (EDITED), not an approximation of the pick (`docs/02`: a manual Kelvin and tint do not render exactly like an Auto white balance, because the tint scales the green gain and the Kelvin is rounded to 50 K). A light preset chosen after a pick wins over it, and a manual white balance next to a pick is manual: both are copied as they are. The rules are `SettingsPatch::copied_from` in the core (`docs/01`), not the webview's, and the mock repeats them (`copiedPatch`).
- **Where it lives.** In the webview store (`AppState.copied`: the photo's name and the patch), for the running app only: never written to disk (hard rule 4) and never put on the system clipboard. Closing the session keeps it, so settings can be copied from one folder to another; quitting forgets it.
- **Paste.** Paste (⇧⌘V, the button, the menu) applies to the current photo; Paste Settings to Picked (⌥⇧⌘V, the menu) applies to every picked photo, the current one included when it is picked. One `paste_settings` call covers all of them. A photo's own crop, orientation and white pick stay; each photo's change is one undo step in that photo's own history. The settings of the photo on screen when the answer comes (which may not be the one that was when the paste began) are asked for again at once, the settings the mirror held for the others are dropped and asked for when the photo is shown, and the filmstrip thumbnails follow from the core. A photo that is still developing or failed is skipped. A crop or a slider drag that is open on a photo when a paste lands is re-based on the pasted settings, so its own undo step, recorded when it ends, does not carry the paste with it.
- **Paste Settings to Picked leaves the photo the settings came from out** when its own white is still a picked one (a Pick white point with the light preset and the white balance both Auto): the paste would swap that Auto white balance for the manual Kelvin and tint that stands for it on other photos, which renders a little differently and marks the photo edited. A source whose white was not picked, or whose white changed since, takes the paste like any photo (that restores it to what was copied). The id of the source is kept for this only while its session is open (`CopiedSettings.sourceId`; the next session numbers its photos again).
- **Notices** (`pastedNotice` in `store.ts`): "Copied the settings of DSC04213."; "Pasted the settings of DSC04213." for one photo; "Pasted the settings of DSC04213 to 5 photos."; with photos left out, "Pasted the settings of DSC04213 to 4 photos. 1 photo was skipped because it isn't developed." (3 photos: "…3 photos were skipped because they aren't developed."); "Nothing was pasted: 2 photos aren't developed."; and, when nothing is copied, nothing is picked, or the source is the only photo picked and its white is a picked one, "Copy the settings of a photo first.", "No photos are picked." and "No other photos are picked." "Pasted … to N photos" counts the photos that now have the settings, whether or not anything changed in them (a photo that already had them has an empty step and no undo entry).
- **Tests.** `crates/ym-core/tests/session.rs` (every field but the three copied, Auto stays Auto, a picked white becomes manual Kelvin and tint and the preset and manual cases, skipped photos and a bad patch changing nothing, the undo and redo pair), `src-tauri/src/api/tests.rs` (paste, events, a gone look; opening more files and the counts), `commands.rs` (the IPC names), `menu.rs` (the entries, keys, state and, under `xvfb-run`, the real menu's items switching); `src/state/store.copypaste.test.ts`, `src/screens/Photos.copypaste.test.tsx`, `src/state/shortcuts.test.ts`, `src/state/menu.test.ts`, `src/api.mock.test.ts`; `e2e/copy-paste.spec.ts`.

## Shell notes

The Rust side of the interface is `src-tauri` (package `yellowmoon-app`). It is thin: `src/api.rs` has one method per command and no Tauri types, `src/commands.rs` wraps each in a `#[tauri::command]` that runs it on a blocking thread, and everything that crosses the bridge is a type of `src/wire.rs`.

### IPC (M3-02)

- **Typed bridge.** `specta` and `tauri-specta` generate `src/api/bindings.ts` from the commands and events. `wire.rs` mirrors the core's serde types (the core's types do not derive `specta::Type`) and converts through serde (`wire::convert`); `core_types_convert_without_loss` checks that nothing is lost on the way.
  - The drift check is `cargo test -p yellowmoon-app --bin yellowmoon-app bindings`: it regenerates the file in memory and fails when the committed one differs; `YM_UPDATE_BINDINGS=1` on the same command rewrites the file.
  - `field_names_match_the_typescript_contract` and `variants_match_the_typescript_contract` compare every interface and every tagged union of `src/api/types.ts` with the generated types, so `types.ts` stays the hand-written contract (with `export interface` blocks). `every_event_of_the_frontend_is_in_the_bindings` checks every event of `ApiEvents`.
  - The TypeScript side is checked by the compiler: `tauriApi` calls the generated `commands` of `src/api/bindings.ts`, and `src/api/contract.ts` makes `tsc -b` fail when a payload of `types.ts` and the generated one disagree in the direction it flows, when an event of `ApiEvents` is not a generated event (or the other way round), or when an `Api` method and its command differ in arguments or answer. `src/api.test.ts` compares the wire names and argument keys of every method with `bindings.ts`.
- **Errors** are `AppError { scope, message }` (`ErrorScope` of `types.ts`), thrown by the generated functions (`ErrorHandlingMode::Throw`), so a rejected `invoke` is what `isAppError` expects.
- **Differences from `types.ts`.** `SettingsPatch` is the core's patch, a little more generous than `Partial<DevelopSettings>` (nested `decode`, `rolloff`, `color_balance` and `curve` objects and a manual `white_balance`, `sharpen` or `noise_reduction` may leave parts out; `crop: null` and `white_pick: null` clear). `AutoOr<M>` is one enum per setting (`ExposureSetting`, …) with the same JSON. Numbers are `number` (the Rust `Num` newtype, because specta would write `number | null` for an `f64`). Unit commands return `null`. `MatchKind` has no `forced` variant (only the CLI's `--profile` makes one).
- **Commands and events the screens use** beyond the core's own (`docs/01` §IPC lists them all):
  - Session and viewport: `close_session()`, `viewport_info()` (`native` or `fallback`), `export_busy()`, `restart_app()`, `thumbnails()`, `histogram(photo_id)` → `{ red, green, blue }` bins, `look_thumbnail(photo_id, look_id)` → the address of a PNG on the `ym-frame` scheme (`ym-frame://localhost/<launch>/look-<photo>-<n>.png`), `copy_settings(photo_id)`, `paste_settings(photo_ids, patch)`, `set_menu_state(state)`; `SessionState.opened`; `CameraInfo.model` ("ILCE-1") for the RAW tab's "Detected" row.
  - Wizard, dialogs and system: `auto_status()` and `auto_status_cached()` (`docs/22` section 12.6: `AutoStatus`, the four providers; the first asks every provider to check its availability again, the second reads what they last said and starts no check; neither waits for a program), `looks_recognise(path)`, `looks_display_luts()`, `looks_watch_downloads(enabled)`, `choose_folder(start)`, `choose_look_files()`, `open_url(url)`, `reveal_in_finder(path)`, `trash_path(path)`, `cli_link_status()`, `app_info()`. `looks_import(path, input?)` takes the answer to the generic question and answers with `unchanged` too. `export_estimate` carries `adjusted_photos` and `dng_approximate`; `export_progress` carries `skipped`.
  - Types: `SourceRecognition`, `Recognition`, `PackageId` (the ids of `data/looks/arri-packages.json`; the two look-file ids map onto the 3D-LUT package ids, `PackageId::from_data_id`), `DisplayLut`, `WatchStatus`, `DownloadFound`, `AppInfo`, `CliLinkStatus`, `MenuAction`.
  - The mock has options for every state (`mockDownloads`, `mockDownloadsDenied`, `mockExisting`, `mockExportFails`, `mockExportHold`, `mockImportHold`, `mockPersist`) and the synthetic packages and failure zips of `PACKAGE_ZIPS` and `FAILURE_ZIPS` in `api.mock.ts`.
- **Events.** The registered events are `photo_progress`, `thumbnail_ready` (`{ id, url }`: the address of a PNG of at most 240 px that the shell serves on the `ym-frame` scheme, `docs/01` IPC), `render_stats` (after every viewport frame, with the frame's `zoom_percent` and the Fit zoom `fit_percent`), `export_progress`, `export_busy { busy }`, `looks_changed`, `auto_status_changed` (no data: a provider's availability changed; the store reads `auto_status_cached()`), `looks_import_progress` (the copy stage only), `download_found`, `menu_action`, `error`, `update_state`, `settings_changed { photo_id }`, `viewport_frame` and `viewport_busy`:
  - `settings_changed` is sent after a `viewport_input` Pick white click set the photo's `white_pick`, once for each photo a `paste_settings` changed, and for the core's `estimates_changed` (a burst's median moved, Apple Auto answered or failed, or its preference changed). The webview asks `get_settings` again, except for the photos it is pasting onto, whose changes the paste's answer carries.
  - `viewport_frame { seq, width, height, sharp, url }` is the fallback viewport only; an empty `url` with a size of 0 × 0 means "no picture": remove the image.
  - `viewport_busy { busy, cpu }` is sent by both backends: the viewport worker is making a frame of the photo on screen or the full-resolution tiles a frame needs, from just before it asks the core for the frame until the round is over, whichever way it ends (an RAII guard in `viewport.rs`). `cpu` is `Renderer::raw_on_cpu` as the round starts: the same checks as the route of the raw chain (no device, `RAW_CHAIN` off, a device error ended the route for the photo or the process, the device lost, a demosaic method the device does not run). Only a change is sent; `busy` goes false at the end of every round and when the cell shows no photo. The shell's `Throttle` sends it latest-wins once per 33 ms and drops one that repeats what was sent last, so a round that starts and ends within the period sends nothing new and a slider drag sends a few. The store keeps it as `viewportBusy`; closing the session resets it, as the core's clear does.
  - The shell never sends `update_state`: the updater runs in the webview (`updater.ts` with `tauri-plugin-updater`; `docs/12`), and `capabilities/default.json` allows `updater:default` only.
- **Update and export.** "Update ready" waits for the export queue: the webview knows the run it started, and `export_busy` (command and event) says whether a queue is busy, also after a reload. The restart is the app's own command, `restart_app`, not the process plugin's `relaunch` (its permission is not granted and the plugin is not loaded): Tauri's restart cannot be vetoed by the exit handler (`ExitRequestApi::prevent_exit` is ignored for it, so "An export is still running…" would never be asked and the exit would cancel the export and delete its file). The shell therefore refuses it where it starts, while an export is queued or running (scope `export`, "An export is still running. Restart after it finishes."; the update stays ready and the cell enables when `export_busy` says the queue is idle), and once a restart has begun `export_start` is refused. Tests: `AppApi::begin_restart` against a real export in `src-tauri/src/api/tests.rs`, the updater's reaction in `src/updater.test.ts`.

### Session (M3-03)

`open_paths` takes the dropped or chosen paths (a folder is read one level deep; non-ARW files are ignored; "No ARW files in what you dropped." is an `open` error when nothing is an ARW file). `open_dialog` and `choose_folder` / `choose_look_files` use the system dialogs from Rust (`tauri-plugin-dialog`), starting in the folder remembered in the preferences; the webview does not use the dialog plugin, so `dialog:default` is not in the capabilities. The window accepts drops of files and folders (`dragDropEnabled`; the webview reads the real paths from `onDragDropEvent`).

- Closing the session (⌘W; the webview asks first when an export runs) stops development and renders but not exports.
- Quitting with an export running asks "An export is still running…" (Stop the export and quit / Keep exporting), then cancels the queue and deletes the partial file. The close button of the window asks the same question before the window goes (answering "Keep exporting" after the window was gone would leave an app with no window to show the export in).
- `export_start` does not wait for the session to finish developing and is not refused for photos outside the job: a burst's shared exposure and the pooled noise model depend on other photos, so the core queues the job and each photo's turn waits until the photos *it* depends on (its burst, its noise pool) have their estimates, then reads them (`docs/01` Session, "App/CLI parity for early exports"). `ym render`, which waits for the whole session, and the app write the same bytes; neither thumbnails, prefetching nor viewport work are waited for. Only photos of the job's scope that have no estimates at all yet are refused: "One photo is still being developed. Wait until it is ready, then export again." / "Some photos are still being developed. Wait until they are ready, then export again."
- A thumbnail is made once per photo when it is developed; applying a look, changing the Intensity or the crop or the quarter turn of a photo makes a new one (collected for 300 ms, rendered behind the viewport).

### Viewport (M0-06, M3-04)

The webview reports the photo cell with `viewport_set_rect` (CSS pixels of the window; also on resize), the pointer with `viewport_input`, `viewport_set_mode`, `set_before` and `sample_pixel`. The shell keeps the core's `ViewportState` for the current photo and asks the core for a frame (RGBA8, Display P3, the cell's size in device pixels, letterbox in paper) whenever the state, the photo or its settings change.

- A newer request does not cancel the frame being drawn: that frame finishes and is shown, and the next round starts at once from the newest state (only the pass that makes sharp tiles gives way to a request, and a request that makes the frame wrong, another photo or another cell size, stops the frame too; `docs/01` §Native viewport).
- When the zoom needs full-resolution tiles that are not ready, a coarse frame goes out at once, the tiles are made at tile priority, and the sharp frame follows (`viewport_frame.sharp`).
- A new photo goes back to Fit and Before off; a zoom, pan or Before that was still waiting for the photo it was meant for is dropped, the cell's rectangle is kept. Nothing is drawn before the webview's first `viewport_set_rect` (a cell with an area: the controller's initial 1 × 1 cell is not one).
- A photo that is still developing is drawn as soon as it is ready; one that failed to develop, or is gone, takes the picture away (`viewport_frame` with an empty `url` on the fallback, the hidden layer on the native viewport) instead of leaving the previous photo in the cell.
- `viewport_set_rect` is a synchronous command (the main thread takes the webview's messages in order; every `async` command runs on a worker thread of its own, and two rectangles of a window being resized that overtook each other would leave the photo at the wrong size).
- Pick white goes to the core as a `viewport_input` of kind `pick_white`, which converts the click and sets the pick (*Pick white*); a click on the paper does nothing. The browser mock has no lens crop, straightening or turn and takes the point of the shown picture.
- `sample_pixel` fails with a `viewport` error on the paper. `histogram` runs as a thumbnail-priority job, behind the viewport frames it is asked for after.

#### Native viewport

macOS only (`src-tauri/src/native.rs`). A plain `NSView` is added behind every sibling in the window's content view (below the webview); wgpu makes the `CAMetalLayer` for it and draws each frame as one full-screen triangle sampling the frame texture. The surface is `Bgra8Unorm` with `SurfaceColorSpace::DisplayP3` (wgpu 30 sets the layer's colour space, macOS converts for the display); without that colour space the sink refuses to start.

- The view follows `viewport_set_rect` (flipped from AppKit's bottom-left origin). It starts hidden, the first frame drawn shows it, and taking the picture away (no photo, a photo that failed) hides it again, so a cell reported while there is no picture does not bring a stale one back. A surface that has no texture to give yet is asked again a few times (a frame is not asked for twice) before the frame is given up on with an error.
- It needs `transparent: true` and `macOSPrivateApi` (both set in `tauri.conf.json`), the photo cell transparent (`PhotoCell` with `native`) and nothing under it painting either: `PhotoView` sets `data-native-viewport` on the root element (not `data-viewport`, which marks the cell itself) while it shows, which makes the root, body, `#root` and the Photos screen transparent (`screens.css`), and the sink makes the window itself opaque paper (`PAPER_SRGB`, equal to `--paper`; `src/styles/native-viewport.node.test.ts` checks both).
- It cannot run on Linux; it is type-checked with `cargo check --target aarch64-apple-darwin -p yellowmoon-app`.

#### Fallback viewport

Everywhere, `src-tauri/src/viewport.rs`. Each frame becomes an RGB PNG with the Display P3 ICC profile, kept (the last three) in a `FrameStore` and served at `ym-frame://localhost/<launch>/frame-<seq>.png` (`http://ym-frame.localhost/…` on Windows; `<launch>` is the app's start time in hex milliseconds and a count, `docs/01` IPC); the `viewport_frame` event says when a new one is there and where. The webview shows it as an `<img>` over the cell (CSP `img-src` allows the scheme). It is what Linux development builds and the tests use, and the fallback if the Metal layer fails the manual checklist below. It breaks the rule "full images never cross the bridge" (`docs/01`; the trade-off is in `docs/16`). `viewport_info()` tells the photo cell which of the two is running (`native`: stay transparent; `fallback`: draw the `viewport_frame` image). `YELLOWMOON_VIEWPORT=fallback` selects it.

#### Manual checklist, native viewport (HUMAN M0-H4; also M3-04)

On a Mac, `npm run tauri dev`, open a folder of ARW files and check:

1. The photo shows in the cell, edge to edge, with the paper-coloured letterbox, and everything else (header, filmstrip, panel) is drawn by the webview over a clean window.
2. No flicker, no white or black flash while a photo develops, when the tab changes, or when a dialog opens.
3. Resizing the window keeps the photo glued to the cell (no lag of more than a frame, no stretched frame left behind).
4. Full screen (green button and ⌃⌘F), then back.
5. Switching Spaces and Mission Control.
6. The window moved between a Display P3 and an sRGB display: the photo looks the same on both as exported files do in Preview (compare a JPEG exported with Display P3).
7. Pan and zoom by wheel, drag and double click: the cursor-to-photo mapping is right at Fit, 50 %, 100 % and 200 %, and one frame of input latency is the most there is (the frame-time overlay is `render_stats` in the header).
8. Pick white, the pixel readout with ⌥ held and the crop overlay line up with the photo.
9. Before (`\`) and the look chip.
10. Minimise and restore, hide and show.
11. A second display of another scale (Retina and 1×).
12. Light and dark appearance of the system (the app is dark only).
13. Choose a file that shows CAN'T OPEN, then a good one: the cell shows no picture for the first (not the previous photo) and the photo again for the second, without the window being resized.
14. With an export running, the red close button and ⌘Q both ask first, and "Keep exporting" leaves the window open.
15. With an export of a large photo as 16-bit PNG or TIFF writing (a hidden `.yellowmoon-….tmp` file is in the folder), ⌘Q and "Stop the export and quit": the app is gone within a few seconds and the folder holds no `.yellowmoon-….tmp`.
16. Open a folder of 500 ARW files (or copy one file 500 times) and press the arrow keys while they develop: keys are on screen within 100 ms (M3-03), and stepping through 20 photos with the Look tab open does not make the app grow by a mosaic for every photo.
17. The wizard on a Mac that has never been asked: import a package and leave "Move to Trash" on; macOS asks whether Yellowmoon may control Finder, with the sentence of `Info.plist`, and the zip is in the Trash afterwards. If the prompt does not come or the zip stays, `trash` should be switched to its `NsFileManager` method, which needs no permission ("Put Back" may then be missing).

If any of 1 to 6 fails, switch to the fallback (set `YELLOWMOON_VIEWPORT=fallback` to try it first) and record the reasons in `docs/16`.

### Shell details

- **Install Command-Line Tool…** (`install_cli_link`, `cli_link_status`) links `ym` (`Contents/MacOS/ym`, next to the app's executable) to `/usr/local/bin/ym`. A folder only an administrator can write gets the system's password prompt (`osascript … with administrator privileges`); if the prompt is cancelled or fails, the link goes to `~/.local/bin/ym` and the answer names that path. A different file called `ym` is never replaced. Installed means a working link in `/usr/local/bin`, `/opt/homebrew/bin` (the cask) or `~/.local/bin`.
- **Window.** `titleBarStyle: Overlay` needs a drag region: `data-tauri-drag-region` on the folder cell (`core:window:allow-start-dragging` is granted). The header (and the wizard's) has it; the folder cell has `deep`, so its text drags too, and buttons never do.
- **Log.** `logs/yellowmoon.log` in the app folder, rotated at 5 MB with three files kept; `YELLOWMOON_LOG=debug` for stage timings. File paths are not logged at info level.
- **Development.** `YELLOWMOON_GPU=auto|on|off|require` sets the device policy (`docs/17-gpu.md` section 1; default `auto`: a hardware GPU on any system runs the stages signed off for it, today every stage; a software adapter only with `on` or `require`). `YM_SMOKE=1` on a debug build makes the app check itself four seconds after start and exit (`xvfb-run -a target/debug/yellowmoon-app`).

## Wiring to the shell

How the screens use what the shell offers (the shell's side is *Shell notes*).

- **Commands and types.** `tauriApi` (`src/api.ts`) calls the generated `commands`; a unit command resolves with nothing. `types.ts` stays the contract the screens use and `contract.ts` compares it with the generated file (a type-level check, no run-time code). The CLI is `ym`: the command line preview, the Preferences hint, the mock and every test say `ym render …`. The dev page `components.html` reproduces the mockup's `yellowmoon render …` text on purpose.
- **Viewport backend.** The store asks `viewport_info()` when it connects and keeps `viewportBackend`: `native`, `fallback`, or `null` (the API has no viewport: the browser mock). The Photos screen draws accordingly. `native` leaves the photo cell transparent and draws nothing in it. `fallback` shows the latest `viewport_frame` (an `<img>` of `ym-frame://…` the size of the cell in device pixels, drawn at that size from the cell's corner, on a paper cell) and takes it away on an empty `url` or when the photo is not ready (the "Still developing." and "This file can't be opened." notes cover the cell). `null` draws the mock's picture.
  - A frame smaller than 16 px a side is no picture: the shell draws a 1 × 1 frame for a cell the webview has not yet placed.
  - An older frame never replaces a newer one, and never brings back a picture that was taken away: the shell numbers a frame before it encodes it and sends the news that the picture is gone from another thread, so a frame can arrive after the clear that was numbered later (the store keeps the newest number heard, clears included).
  - The cell reports its rectangle on mount, on resize and on layout change (`viewport_set_rect`, CSS pixels; `PhotoCell`, ResizeObserver) in every mode, and when the display scale changes (the window dragged to a display of another scale: the cell keeps its size in CSS pixels, and not every web view sends `resize` for it, so `PhotoCell` also listens to the media query for the current scale).
- **Input.** Double click is `viewport_input toggle_zoom`, ⌘ or Ctrl and the wheel is `zoom` about the pointer, the wheel and a drag pan once zoomed, ⌥ over the cell reads `sample_pixel` in CSS pixels of the cell (on the paper there is nothing to read and the readout goes), and Pick white is *Pick white*. The store mirrors the zoom the core is asked for: a double click flips Fit and 100 %, a wheel zoom multiplies the mirror by the same factor at once (from the Fit zoom; capped at 800 %; at or below Fit it is Fit). The header's percentage and the drag to pan follow the mirror; a menu choice (50 %, 100 %, 200 %) sends exact values. How the wheel, the pinch and a drag are collected and sent is in *Photos input and desktop feel*; the mirror moves once per frame, when a zoom is closed, and not once per wheel event.
- **The Fit zoom is the core's.** `render_stats.fit_percent` (and `zoom_percent`) come from the same `ViewportState` the frame was drawn with, so they count the lens crop, the straighten angle, the crop and the quarter turn (`fitZoomOf`). The screen's own figure (`fitZoomPercent`: the cell's device pixels over the final picture's, from the photo's size and its settings) is used only until a frame has reported, and by the mock, which sends `null`. The store drops the zoom figures of the last frame when another photo opens, since the new photo may have another Fit zoom.
- **A fixed zoom at or below the Fit zoom is Fit.** The core draws Fit for it (`ViewportState::zoom` clamps to the Fit zoom, `is_fit` compares with the Fit zoom to 0.01 %), and the screen says the same: on a big display Fit is 62 % for a 24 MP photo, so the menu's "50 %" is Fit, the header reads "Fit 62%", the picture is letterboxed, a drag does not pan, a click on the paper is ignored by Pick white, and ⌘+ goes from Fit straight to 100 % (`showsWhole`, `zoomStep(mode, delta, fit)`: steps at or below the Fit zoom are skipped, so a key press always does something; Z and a double click give Fit again where 100 % is no more than the Fit zoom). ⌘+ and ⌘− step from where the photo is, not from the start of the list: a wheel zoom leaves the zoom anywhere (137 %), and Zoom In then goes to 200 % and Zoom Out to 100 %. The Fit zoom used for these decisions is not rounded (`fitZoomExact`); the header rounds it for display.
- **The pixel readout** asks the core for one reading at a time and, when the last one comes back, for the place the pointer is at then (a reading takes the core a moment, and the last place must not be lost); a reading that comes back after ⌥ was released is not shown.
- **Events.** `thumbnail_ready` fills the filmstrip, `photo_progress` the Start list and the filmstrip's states, `render_stats` the header, `export_progress` the Export panel (the sequence of `docs/01` is tested), `viewport_busy` the "Rendering on the CPU" note of the photo cell (the mock sends it only with `?mockCpu=1`, around every frame it "renders", 600 ms divided by `mockSpeed`), `settings_changed` makes the store read a photo's settings again (it skips a photo it has not asked about, the one whose Pick white it is waiting for, and the photos it is pasting onto, since the paste's answer says what changed), and `looks_changed` reloads looks, display LUTs and preferences. The Look tab's tiles ask `look_thumbnail` while they are in view (*Look tab*). After a reload of the webview the core does not announce the thumbnails again, so the store asks `thumbnails()` once it has the session back and gets the address of every picture the shell holds.
- **Updates and exports.** The update cell is disabled while the webview's own run is going or the core says an export is busy (`export_busy`, the event, and the command once at connect and again at the click: "Update ready" asks the core at the click and does not restart if an export has begun unheard). The webview takes the shell's refusal of `restart_app` (*Shell notes*, IPC; an `AppError` of scope `export`) as "not now": the update stays ready and the cell enables when the export is done.
- **Checking it for real.** Playwright drives the mock. `npm run e2e:shell` (`e2e/real/shell-smoke.mjs`; Linux, Xvfb, a built `dist/` and `target/debug/yellowmoon-app`) runs the built frontend inside the real shell: the debug app loads `devUrl`, the script serves `dist/` there with a driver added to `index.html`, drops a folder of ARW files on the window as Tauri's own drag-and-drop event, and checks the Start list, the frames on the `ym-frame` scheme, the thumbnails, a double click, ⌥, a settings change, Pick white, an export to a temporary folder with `export_busy`, a file that is not a raw file, and closing the session. The ARW files are read in place and nothing is written next to them.
  - The geometry checks need a point off the middle of the photo (the middle of the cell is the middle of the photo whatever is wrong): Pick white lands where the click was on a letterboxed photo, on a photo turned 90°, on a cropped one and on one turned and cropped (the pick is compared with the point worked out independently, within 0.01), a ⌘ wheel zoom keeps the pixel under the pointer, and a drag moves the picture with the pointer (the pixel that was under the start of the drag is under its end, exactly, at 100 %).
  - `YM_SMOKE_SCALE=2` runs the app at a device scale of 2 (`GDK_SCALE`, a larger Xvfb screen), where the frame is twice the cell and a CSS pixel is two device pixels; `YM_SMOKE_ONLY=geometry` runs just the failure-path folder and its Pick white checks. A drop sent before the Start screen has registered its listener is lost, as it would be for a person who drops that fast, so the runner sends the drop again until the screen reacts.
  - Measured: 39 of 39 checks pass at scale 1 and at scale 2 on the nine real files of `testdata/real`.

## Photos input and desktop feel

The page behaves like a desktop app, not a web page: a two-finger pan on the photo moves the photo and nothing else, text does not select by dragging, and wheel events, pointer moves and store ticks do as little work as they can (`docs/15` M3-16 to M3-19).

- **Wheel.** `usePhotoGestures` (`src/components/usePhotoGestures.ts`, used by `PhotoCell` through its `gestures` prop) adds a native `wheel` listener with `{ passive: false }` to the photo cell and calls `preventDefault()` on **every** wheel event over the cell, at Fit, zoomed, in Crop and in Pick white: the gesture is the photo's first, and only then does the handler decide whether to act. (A React `onWheel` is registered as passive and cannot cancel the default action, and WebKit would rubber-band the whole window.) ⌘ or ctrl with the wheel zooms about the pointer by `exp(−deltaY / 100)` (ctrl too, because Chromium and some mice send a pinch as ctrl+wheel); the wheel alone pans by the distance the fingers moved when the photo is larger than the cell (`!store.showsWholePhoto()`); at Fit, in Crop and in Pick white it does nothing. There is **no** wheel listener on `document` or `window`: a non-passive one there makes WebKit's scrolling thread ask the busy web process before each scroll step over the whole window, the tool panel included. A cell without `gestures` (the dev page) leaves the wheel to the page.
- **Pinch.** WebKit sends a trackpad pinch as `gesturestart`, `gesturechange` and `gestureend` with a `scale` since the start, not as ctrl+wheel. The cell zooms about the fingers by `scale / previous scale` and cancels all three. `installDesktopFeel` (`src/desktopFeel.ts`, called from `main.tsx`) cancels the same three events on `window`, so a pinch anywhere else never reaches the browser's own zoom. WKWebView has magnification off, so this is a guard, not the only protection.
- **Nothing else scrolls or bounces.** `html`, `body` and `#root` are `overflow: hidden` with `overscroll-behavior: none` (every screen is a fixed grid whose own boxes scroll), the photo cell is `overscroll-behavior: none`, and every box that scrolls on its own (`.ym-tools__scroll`, `.ym-dialog__body`, `.ym-menu__list`, `.ym-wizard__main`, `.ym-develop-list`, `.ym-step-rail`, `.ym-filmstrip__scroll`) is `overscroll-behavior: contain`: it keeps its own end stop and never hands the rest of a gesture to the page. The filmstrip is a row: `overflow-y: hidden`, it scrolls along and never up and down. `src/styles/desktop.node.test.ts` fails when a rule sets `overflow` to `auto` or `scroll` on a selector that is not in that list, so a new scroller cannot forget it. `overscroll-behavior` needs WebKit 16, which every supported macOS has (14 and later ship Safari 17). The dev page (`components.html`) scrolls as a whole and says so in `dev.css`.
- **Coalescing.** Wheel, pinch and drag input goes through `store.queueViewportInput` into a `ViewportInputQueue` (`src/state/viewportInputQueue.ts`): pans add up, zooms about the same point (within a pixel) multiply, a different kind or a moved anchor closes the entry so the order is kept, one `requestAnimationFrame` sends what has been collected, and only one `viewport_input` is in flight (later frames merge into one waiting message). A burst of 100 wheel events is one command. `flushViewportInput` sends at once: when a drag ends (pointer up), and before a click-type input (a double click, Pick white) and before a zoom mode chosen from the menu or a key, so these land after the wheel input that came before them (`viewport_input` is answered on a pool of threads, so two commands can overtake each other: this is why there is only one in flight). `setCurrent` and `closeSession` drop what has not been sent. A refusal is reported once (the notice bar) and drops what was waiting. The zoom mirror follows once per closed zoom, so once per frame. `store.viewportInput` is the same input sent now, in order (tests, double click, Pick white).
- **Text selection.** `html` is `user-select: none` (and `-webkit-user-select`, `-webkit-touch-callout: none`) with `cursor: default`. Selectable again, with the prefixed property WebKit needs: fields (`input`, `textarea`, `contenteditable`, with the I-beam), the command line and its example, the folder an export goes to, the template problem, the CLI path hint in Preferences, the About text, the notice bar's text, the wizard's errors (the Downloads-folder problem and the failed import), the drop zone's notice ("No ARW files in what you dropped."), the Start screen's error line (only the one with `role="alert"`: the same note class also styles static notes, which stay unselectable), the reason over a photo that can't be opened, the file names and notes of the queues, the Look import wizard's file names and explanations, the `.cube` dialog's file names and reasons, and the Info rows of the RAW tab. The photo chip, the camera line, headings, tags and buttons stay unselectable (selecting there fights the pan and the double click). `img` and `a` are `-webkit-user-drag: none`. The CSS test names every selectable class and checks that the markup still uses it.
- **Context menu.** `installDesktopFeel` cancels `contextmenu` unless the target is editable (a field, `contenteditable`) or selectable text (the nearest ancestor with a `user-select` that is not `auto` says `text`), so WebKit's page menu (Reload, Back) never opens over the app, and Copy and Paste still do where they make sense. In a debug build the web inspector opens with ⌥⌘I.
- **Cursor.** A button is an arrow, as in a Mac app (`button { cursor: default }`). Only link-styled buttons (`LinkButton`, `ResetLink`) and real links keep the pointing hand; the CSS test lists them. A disabled control keeps the arrow; a field the I-beam; the slider, the wheel, the curve and the crop frame their own cursors.
- **What redraws.** The Photos screen does not follow `render_stats`, the zoom or the export run: the zoom button (`ZoomMenu`), the status text (`StatusText`) and the Export cell (`ExportCellContainer`) read them themselves, and the screen reads the settings of every photo, the export options and the preferences only while the Export panel is open. The tool tabs and `PhotoView` are `memo` with stable callbacks, so a frame, a wheel zoom or an export tile redraws one button or one span. `export_progress` is applied with the other batched events (`later`, 32 ms). The Look tab reads only the number of photos and picks from the session. Pick white's `letterboxed` is a selector that returns a boolean, and only while it is on. Look tiles: *Look tab*.
- **Histogram.** The Light and Curve tabs call `store.watchHistogram()` (ref-counted; Light also asks for the middle readout, which the store keeps as `middleReadout`). It asks once at once, then after `render_stats` at most every 200 ms (a throttle, not a debounce: the plot follows a drag, five times a second, and updates once after the last frame), and nothing while nobody watches. The plot lags a drag by up to 200 ms.
- **Small things.** `PhotoCell` reads the cell's place from the last report (the observer, the window resize, the display scale) on a pointer move or a wheel event and not from `getBoundingClientRect` each time; a cell with no size yet is measured. The updater tells its listeners when the percentage changes, not at each chunk of the download.
- **Measuring (M3-20).** `src/perf.ts` is off unless the page was opened with `?perf` (or `?perf=1`; `?perf=0` says no and wins), the shell set `window.__YM_PERF__ = true` before the page ran (it does when the app is started with `YM_PERF`, which is how the Mac viewport job turns it on: it can add no address and fill no storage), or `localStorage.ymPerf` is set; off, it records nothing, `getApi()` returns the plain api and `perfCount` is an empty function. On, it counts late animation frames (more than 24 ms after the one before), the renders of `Photos`, `LookTab` and `LazyLookTile`, the store's notifications (`store.notify`), the events the api delivers (`event.<name>`) and the calls and round trips of every command, and `window.__ymPerf.snapshot()` returns one JSON object (`reset()` starts again). A React development build with `StrictMode` renders twice, so count in a production build or in the tests. Nothing is sent anywhere: it stays in the page.

Checks that need a Mac (M0-H4): the two-finger pan at Fit and zoomed, over the header, the filmstrip, the panel's end stops, a dialog and the zoom menu moves only the intended content; a pinch over the photo zooms it about the fingers and nothing zooms the page (whether WKWebView delivers the gesture events at all is part of this check); ⌘ and ctrl with the scroll zoom the photo; the panel's momentum scroll works and its end stop bounces inside the panel only; nothing selects by drag, ⌘A, double click on the photo or triple click in the header, and the command line, the CLI path in Preferences, error text and About do.

## Comparison with the mockups and the references

**Method.** Each mockup page can be rendered in the state of its screenshot (`window.__inst.setState`, see `design/README.md`). `e2e/helpers/domDump.ts` lists every visible element with its own text, border or background (position, size, font size, weight, tracking, colour), and `compareScreen` writes that list when `YM_DOM_DUMP=1`, so a 1 px shift, a wrong weight or a missing word shows up where a screenshot threshold lets it through. Captures, reports and dumps go to `node_modules/.cache/yellowmoon-e2e/` (Playwright empties `test-results/` at the start of every run). The mockup's `min-height: 760px` and photo-cell formula must hold at 1180 × 760, 1280 × 800, 1512 × 982, 1920 × 1080 and 2560 × 1440.

**Tolerance.** `toHaveScreenshot` tolerates 0.35 % of the pixels by Playwright's own count (anti-aliasing costs at most 0.25 %), with a budget per state in `e2e/helpers/compare.ts` where the difference is on purpose. Every wait is on a condition (all photos developed before "Open photos →", every visible look tile loaded, the filmstrip's thumbnails replaced, the export held with `mockExportHold` and released with `flush()`), not on time. `e2e/global-setup.ts` loads both pages once so the first test does not pay for the dev server's cold start. Limits are 60 s a test and 10 s an `expect`, for a busy machine.

**Where the app differs from the mockups** (the mockups are not edited):

- **Before.** `design/mockups/photos.html` and `design/source/Bold.dc.html` read "Before · camera log" and show the flat log picture; the app shows the camera default, labelled "Before · camera default". The dev replica `src/dev/PhotosReplica.tsx` keeps the mockup's label and picture so that `e2e/components.spec.ts` can still compare it with the mockup.
- **Fit label.** The mockup's "Fit 34%" is made up; the app's is the cell's device width over the photo's (2080 / 8640 = 24 %).
- **Pictures.** The photo and the thumbnails are SVG and PNG pictures where the mockup draws CSS shapes, so anti-aliased edges differ.
- **Tool panel.** The mockup draws six tabs in a six-column row (`repeat(6, …)`); the app has a seventh, Lens, second in the bar (its cards are in *Lens card*), and a seven-column row. The Automatic card has the White row (and, with Apple Auto, its rows); the Look tab has the Tone card; the Light tab has the White card; the Sharpening card has the Output row; the RAW tab's Range card. The panel ends in the Copy settings and Paste foot row, and the header has Add photos.
- **Light tab.** The histogram and the pixel readout come from the photo (a flat-colour mock photo has three peaks); the mockup's are made-up numbers.
- **Curve tab.** A histogram behind the plot, which this document asks for; the spline is the C11 Fritsch–Carlson curve, not the mockup's hand-drawn Bézier, so the ends differ.
- **Look tab.** "Make default" appears beside Reset when the selected look is not the default; "Import .cube…" sits beside "Import ARRI looks →". The mockup draws two groups, "Built in" and one "ARRI looks · imported" (its data has only the seven LogC4 names), with a plain header row and no fold; the app's heading for those looks is "ARRI looks · LogC4", every header is a button with a chevron, and further sections follow when more libraries are imported. The scope of Apply to starts on This photo where the mockup draws All.
- **Export.** The DNG state has the other-apps sentence, Adobe's notice and the white-balance caveat (`docs/09` §Legal). While an export runs the update cell is disabled, where the mockup draws it normal.
- **Wizard.** The real file name and size where the mockup says "Look Library zip · 181 MB" (a name and a size on two lines); step 2 waits for the zip, so Next is disabled and the footer line has no "For this mock, it is already here."; Cancel and Back are greyed while files are copied.

**Accessibility.** `e2e/a11y.spec.ts` walks the accessibility tree of Start, every tab, Crop (and Crop with Auto crop on), Pick white, the zoom menu, the Export panel in each format and while it runs, the wizard, Preferences and About, and fails on any button, slider, tab, switch, radio, text box, radio group, tab list or dialog without a name. Each screen has one `main` (the photo cell on Photos, the drop area on Start, the wizard's main) and one `banner`, and Photos has the folder name as its `h1`. The keyboard alone gets from Start through Open files, Open photos, the tab bar (←/→), a slider (arrows, Shift, Home, ⌘Z), ⌘E and the Export button to Done; a segmented group, a switch, the search box, the look tiles, a typed value (Return, Esc), the wizard and a dialog (focus trapped, Esc, focus returns) work without the mouse; every stop of the Tab order of Start and of the Look tab has the 3 px ring. The tab cell's ring has a 2 px paper gap, so the ring stays on a bounded edge over a title block of the same colour. `src/styles/contrast.node.test.ts` checks the tokens of `design/tokens.css`: text on every tab colour 4.5:1 or better, ink on the brand yellow and the status colours, muted text on paper, and the focus ring 3:1 against paper, ink and the brand yellow. Nothing inside a title block is interactive, so the ring never has to cross one.

## Copy rules

Short, plain sentences that say what happens. British spelling in user-facing text. No exclamation marks. Errors say what went wrong and what to do. Name ARRI and Sony only factually ("Imports ARRI Look Library files"). Never call the output "ARRI" or "ALEXA" footage; say "cinema colour" or "ALEXA-style colour" only in help text, never in the product name.

## Frontend structure

```
src/
  main.tsx, App.tsx              screen routing (Start, Photos, ImportLooks), menus → actions
  api.ts / api.mock.ts           typed Tauri commands (generated types) and a browser mock with fixture data
  updater.ts                     as in plugout (phases idle/available/downloading/ready/error)
  state/                         the store (a mirror of the core), shortcuts, history, menu, look labels, viewport input queue
  screens/                       Start, Photos, Header, PhotoView; tools/ (the seven tabs), crop/, export/, wizard/, dialogs/
  components/                    see Components
  components/glass/              glasscn's components (MIT; LICENSE inside), changed only for types and the adaptations in this file
  styles/tokens.css              from design/tokens.css; fonts/ with Figtree woff2 + OFL.txt
  styles/glass*.css, legacy.css  Tailwind 4 + glasscn tokens, the C2 palette and layout, the app's component sheets in the base layer
```

glasscn (Radix, Tailwind 4, class-variance-authority, lucide-react) is the component and CSS framework; the app's own sheets (`src/styles/legacy.css`) sit in Tailwind's `base` layer. State lives in Rust; the frontend keeps a mirror for rendering and sends patches. Slider drags send throttled patches (one per animation frame) and a final patch on release.

## Tests

- **Components.** Vitest and Testing Library (keyboard, ARIA, reset behaviour).
- **End to end.** Playwright against `api.mock.ts` (no Tauri): the flows Start → drop → Photos → change look → Export → finished; the wizard happy path; error states (unsupported file, missing look). Screenshot comparisons against `design/screenshots-c2/` at 1440 × 900 (2×) with a tolerance, for the 28 states there (Start, the tabs, Before, the Look states, Colour warp (`photos-color-warp.png`), the Look tab with Apple Auto's answer (`photos-look-apple-auto.png`), Preferences with every provider listed and the consent dialog (`photos-preferences-providers.png`, `photos-preferences-consent.png`), the Export panel in each state, the wizard steps; `e2e/screenshots.spec.ts`, `export.spec.ts`, `wizard.spec.ts`; budgets in `e2e/helpers/compare.ts`). The references are the app's own pictures, taken on the mock API with the browser flags of `playwright.config.ts`; `npx playwright test --update-snapshots` retakes them and a person looks at the result before it is committed.
- **Glass and viewport.** `e2e/glass.spec.ts` audits the 41 page states for controls of ours where glasscn has one, and checks the keys, the pointer and the ARIA state of the controls; `e2e/viewport.spec.ts` checks that nothing the page paints reaches the photo cell.
- **Desktop feel.** `src/styles/desktop.node.test.ts` reads the CSS (the root locked, every scroller contained, selection off with its exceptions, arrow and hand cursors, no wheel listener on `document`). Vitest covers `usePhotoGestures`, `ViewportInputQueue`, the store's input queue, histogram throttle and tile limiter (`store.viewport.test.ts`), the redraw counts of the Photos screen (`Photos.render.test.tsx`, with `perfCount` replaced by a counter) and the look tiles with a driven `IntersectionObserver` (`LookTab.tiles.test.tsx`). `e2e/desktop-feel.spec.ts` covers what only a browser has (`defaultPrevented` on the photo cell and not on the tool panel, the panel scrolling with the mouse wheel, 60 wheel events as at most two pans with the summed distance, a synthetic pinch, selection, the page menu, cursors, `?perf`).
- **Rendering on the CPU.** `src/screens/RenderingNote.test.tsx` (fake timers: nothing before 250 ms, shown after, gone at once when `busy` goes false, never with `cpu` false; outside the picture's `role="img"`, in Before too), `src/state/store.backend.test.ts` (`viewport_busy` sets `viewportBusy`; the mock with and without `cpu`), `e2e/cpu-indicator.spec.ts` (`?mockCpu=1`: a slider and another photo show it at the top centre and it goes, a double click on it reaches the photo, Before and full screen; without it the words never appear). The shell: `events.rs` (the throttle's merging), `viewport.rs` and `api/tests.rs` (a round on a renderer without a device sends `{ busy: true, cpu: true }` then `{ busy: false, cpu: true }`, a zoom's tile pass included, and a closed session ends not busy). The core: `Renderer::raw_on_cpu` has a unit test without a device, `tests/render_gpu.rs` with one, and with the fault injection a photo whose route an error ended and a lost device.
- **Native viewport.** The manual checklist on real hardware (*Shell notes*, resize, full screen, Spaces, display change between P3 and sRGB monitors).
