## Current milestone

M0–M3 were built in parallel in one session (2026-10-12), each task implemented and then independently reviewed. Integration is done: ym-core (session, render pipeline, export queue), the `ym` CLI, the Tauri app, the frontend wired to it, the golden runner and dngfit. A review pass followed, and on 2026-10-13 the raw-algorithm work (below) changed the R6 and sharpening defaults. M4 hasn't started.

Two facts shape every number below:

- **No Mac.** The agent ran on Linux x86_64 (4 cores). GPU code was checked on Mesa lavapipe (software Vulkan); Metal, the native viewport, timings on M1/M3 and the release/updater flow need a Mac (M0-H4).
- **No CC0 corpus.** rawdb.dnglab.org and raw.pixls.us were unreachable, so the decoder, golden and noise gates run on (a) a physically based synthetic ARW set with ALEXA ground truth and (b) nine real ARW files from a public GitHub test-image repository, used for local evaluation only (never committed). The CC0 gates are blocked, not passed.

## UI performance: the shell (M3-17 and M3-20, 2026-10-16, shell side)

The same report as above (the diagnosis: 13 findings, no Mac). The shell half is in, with tests: `docs/01` §Native viewport (*Which request stops which render*, *The `ym-frame` handler and its cache*, *Event coalescing*) and `docs/13` §Performance logging and the viewport job describe it.

- **The viewport finishes the frame it is drawing (finding 2).** Every input and every settings patch used to cancel the render in flight, and a cancelled render yields no frame, so once a render took longer than the gap between two inputs (8 ms for a wheel, 16 ms for a slider) nothing reached the screen until the hand stopped. Now `Shared::request` stops only the sharp-tile pass (`Flight::Tiles`), the frame being drawn (`Flight::Frame`) finishes and is shown, and the next round starts at once from the newest state. `request_now` (another photo or none, which includes the session closing; a cell of another size or scale) stops the frame too. `snapshot_synced` (Pick white, the ⌥ readout) now waits for the next round to apply what is pending, up to one frame, and still gives up after 1.5 s (tested under a stream of pans).
- **`ym-frame` off the main thread, cached (finding 8).** The scheme is an asynchronous protocol: the main thread hands the path to a blocking thread and gets the answer back (test: another thread holds the tiles' mutex, the handler returns at once, the answer arrives after the release). Addresses are `ym-frame://localhost/<launch>/…` with the launch id made from the start time, and a thumbnail or look tile that exists is served `Cache-Control: private, max-age=31536000, immutable`; frames and 404s stay `no-store`. - **`render_stats` and `photo_progress` coalesced (finding 8).** At most one `render_stats` and one batch of `photo_progress` per 33 ms from one thread; `render_stats` latest-wins; a photo's `done` or `error` event is never dropped or reordered (a property test over 300 random sequences). ## Contrast and Saturation, a new design (pipeline 0.4.3, 2026-10-19)

You asked for a new contrast and saturation and an independent design; this is the lead's design (`docs/02` C9, `docs/16`), with your three answers of 2026-10-19 after the first contact sheets built in. Branch `claude/grade-contrast-saturation`.

- **Contrast** is a tone curve on the pixel's luma, added to the three channels as one offset: the old slope at mid grey (`2^(0.6·c/100)`), but black and the top code value stay fixed and the curve bends toward them (slope `1/k` there, straight lines beyond), so blacks stay black. **It now adds part of the colour a curve on each channel would** (your answer 2): the offset result's chroma is scaled, its hue kept, half way (`β = CONTRAST_COLOR = 0.5`) toward the chroma length of the same curve on each LogC4 channel; the mean of the channels does not move, neutrals stay neutral, and the factor stays between 0.83 and 1.26.
- **Saturation** scales the chroma about the luminance in linear AWG4, so a colour is neither darker nor lighter at any setting: −100 is the grey of the same luminance; above 0 muted colours get the full factor `F = 1 + 1.5·s/100` and strong ones less, and no channel is pushed below 0. You confirmed it (answer 3); it replaces the subtractive saturation of 2026-10-13. **Boosted** (answer 1): `SATURATION_BOOST` 1.0 → 1.5, measured on your three photos at +60: α 1.2 … 2.0 in steps of 0.1 gave a mean chroma gain of +61.7, +65.9, +69.8, **+73.6**, +77.2, +80.7, +84.1, +87.3, +90.4 % against the old control's +72.9 % (+84.6, +64.9, +69.3), so 1.5. Looks (the ASC CDL saturation of C10) are unchanged, so the Signature look renders as before.
- **Numbers.** CPU reference f64 (`ym_color::grade`, `contrast_pixel`), 37 tests in `crates/ym-color/tests/grade.rs` (fixed points, slopes, increasing and C¹; the colour step keeps the hue and the mean and scales by the formula's `f` for random pixels, `c` and `β`, `β = 0` is the offset, `β = 1` the per-channel length, neutrals untouched; luminance, hue, no channel below 0, monotone in `s`; Contrast 0 and Saturation 0 byte-identical to a copy of the 0.4.2 grade). - **Where the design was silent, and one edge.** A blue or violet whose red and green are nearly equal can have every channel above its luminance (the blue weight of AWG4 `Y` is negative), where `r < 0` and `r′/r` would grow without bound as `Y` goes to 0; those pixels get the plain factor `F`. A continuous variant, if you see it, is `λ = 1 + (F − 1)·Y/min` for `r < 0`. A single channel of a coloured pixel is no longer promised to move monotonically with Contrast (the colour step scales the chroma); the mean of the channels and every channel of a neutral are.

## Colour warp: Range keeps its value (pipeline 0.4.4, the owner's report of 2026-10-19)

"Colour warp range does not work, and a picked colour, even with range 1, affects most of the image." A move longer than 1.5 ranges grew the pin's range to |δ|/1.5, so the slider only mattered for short moves. Now the range stays as set and each pixel follows a flow of N steps (N = ⌈1.25 Σ|δ|/(ρ√e)⌉, 1 to 64) while the pins' Gaussians slide toward their targets (`docs/02` C9b, `docs/16`). CPU reference, WGSL kernel and the plane's TypeScript share it.

- **What holds.** A single pin's source lands on its target (1e-12); a colour three ranges beside or behind the source moves by at most exp(−4.5) of the move; one pin never folds the plane (45 pins up to |δ| = 1 at range 0.01: every mapped cell keeps its orientation, smallest Jacobian 7.1e-5); a partial tonal weight or a lock's hold moves the source by that share of the move; a move of up to 1.32 ranges takes one step and renders as before. The golden set has no pins: 61 identical.
- **What a long, narrow move does.** Colours ahead of the source within about a range of the move's line are swept along to just past the target; a sliver behind it (about 2ρ²/|δ| long) is stretched along the path.
- **Parity gate (failed at the extreme, reported).** In that sliver the flow magnifies rounding by up to |δ|²/(2ρ²). On lavapipe a move of 0.48 at range 0.01 gives C9b 7.2e-5 (the CI test), 0.68 gives 9.3e-5, 0.87 gives 2.0e-4, at the 2e-4 gate; a C port under clang fast-math with every pin long and narrow gives 2.7e-4 between fast and strict f32 and 3.3e-4 between f32 and f64 in the sliver. So moves longer than about 0.85 at range 0.01 break the C9b gate in those pixels (about 0.005 of a stop). Everything else: C9b 4.2e-7 to 9.1e-7 (measured again on 0.4.4, with the new contrast and saturation).
- **Checks** (Linux, 2026-10-19, branch `claude/fix-colour-warp-range`): `cargo fmt --check`; `cargo clippy -p ym-color -p ym-gpu -p ym-core --all-targets -D warnings`; the tests of ym-color (584), ym-gpu's library, shader and colour tests on lavapipe (`gpu_color` 24, `gpu_tail`, `gpu_chain`, `gpu_color_*`), ym-core's library, `render_api`, `render_graph`, `session` and `settings`, ym-cli's `cli`, `info`, `render` and `parity`; `npm test` (1581; two tests of the README screenshot tool timed out under load and pass alone); `npm run build`; Playwright `colour-warp.spec.ts` (5), the `photos-color-warp` screenshot (0.000 % differ) and the two warp audit states; golden 61 identical (`ym` debug, `--cpu`).

## Questions for Mykyta

1. **Sensor-map gate with estimated lights (docs/02, M1-06).** As worded it can't pass: any white-balance error dominates (1° ≈ 1.2–1.9 ΔE00, while the gate needs the white right to about 0.3°). Proposal in docs/02: gate the estimator by angular error only, and state the estimated-light check as the map-only increase (≤ +0.2 median, ≤ +0.7 p95 — measured +0.15 / +0.69, so these thresholds are post-hoc). Accept, or another wording?
2. **GPU storage precision.** The spec says f16 tiles. f16 storage alone can exceed the 1e-3 spike gate on white-noise data (up to 6e-3) and the 1-code DRT gate on 3–4 of 898 extreme vectors; f32 storage meets both and doubles GPU memory (50 MP peak ~0.28 → ~0.56 GiB, gate 1.5 GiB). Options: f32 by default; keep f16 and accept a data-dependent gate; f16 only when memory-bound.
3. **16-bit TIFF default.** On a real 24 MP frame 16-bit LZW was 26 % larger than uncompressed and 38 % larger than ZIP. Switch the default to ZIP?
4. **Spec error in M1-07.** docs/15 says "3200 K under daylight renders warmer than Auto"; physically it renders bluer. The test checks the ALEXA-maths amount with the correct sign. OK to fix the wording?
5. **M1-02 tolerance.** "Runtime values match to 1e-6 on a test grid" holds on grid; off grid the 10 K tables reach 5e-6 (blackbody) and 4e-4 (daylight, because colour-science rounds the D-series M1/M2 coefficients). Accept these tolerances, or use a 1 K blackbody grid (1.4e-7) and unrounded daylight?
6. **Robust as-shot prior for the illuminant.** A two-component prior that discounts a wrong white-balance preset improves p90 4.93 → 4.16° and wrong-preset scenes 5.9 → 3.1°, but the worst case rises 22 → 33°. Adopt (needs a retrain)?
7. **Tint readout near 4000 K.** The blackbody and daylight locus branches meet with a 0.04 log-chroma gap, so small magenta tints on ~4100 K lights read with the wrong sign. Fix with one Planckian readout locus or a blend over 3500–4500 K (changes `profile.json`)?
8. **IJG licence.** JPEG export uses `jpeg-encoder`, whose forward DCT is IJG-licensed (permissive, needs "This software is based in part on the work of the Independent JPEG Group." in docs and About). It's allowed in `deny.toml` now; please confirm.

## Known issues

- Colour warp (2026-10-19): the stepped flow stretches a thin sliver of colours just behind the source of a long, narrow move along its path; there the GPU (f32) and the CPU (f64) differ by up to about 3e-4 in LogC4 for moves longer than about 0.85 at range 0.01, over the 2e-4 parity gate (Colour warp section above). A floor on the range relative to the move (for example |δ|/20) would bound the stretch; it is not done, since the fix was to keep the range exactly.
- Output sharpening's halo limiter (2026-10-17; fixed 2026-10-18): its rank counts flipped where a neighbour ties the centre pixel within a rounding error, so the device (f32) and the CPU (f64) differed by a few codes at a handful of pixels of a sharpened picture, up to 118 codes at chart patch edges. Since pipeline 0.4.1 the limiter is continuous (section "Output sharpening: a continuous halo limiter"). Three parity tests still carry an exception for it (Highlight range section, "Test bounds widened"), to be tightened after a Metal run.
- Dark glass interface (2026-10-16): three behaviours come from Radix and differ from the pre-glass controls. A modal menu traps Tab (it does not close the menu), and the first Esc on a locked button whose tooltip is open closes the tooltip and the second the dialog; tabs, segmented controls and groups take Tab once and move with the arrows (roving focus), so the Tab stops of a screen are fewer. The dev page's replicas of the mockups are no longer compared with them (the screens are compared with `design/screenshots-c2/`). The references were taken on one machine: a CI runner may need a budget for the glass's blur (see the References bullet above). `README.md` (`docs/images/readme/`) shows the pre-glass interface.
