# Status

The current state of Yellowmoon: release, milestone, what is built, gate results, open items and the environment the agent works in. Specs in `docs/` hold the technical detail; this page summarises and links.

## Project

- **Repository:** github.com/ngriaznov/yellowmoon, on the owner's personal account like plugout.
- **Domain:** not given yet. `docs/12-distribution.md` and the app have no domain-dependent setting.
- **Licence:** Apache-2.0 (`docs/14-licensing.md`).
- **What it is:** a free, open-source macOS raw processor for Sony ARW files. It renders each photo the way an ARRI ALEXA (ALEV III sensor) would, through spectral sensor emulation, a LogC4 / ACES 2 colour pipeline and small learned models, with very few controls. Rust crates under `crates/` hold all image processing; a Tauri + React app and the `ym` CLI are the two front ends.
- **Workflow:** work is on `main`; a version bump on `main` publishes a release (`docs/12-distribution.md`).

**Cube++ check of the light estimator (2026-10-23, the owner: "yes, set up the Cube++ check").** The M1-06 real-photo gate had been blocked since M1 because zenodo.org is unreachable from the agents' cloud containers; GitHub's runners reach it, so it now runs in `.github/workflows/cubepp.yml` (a push to `m1-06-cubepp`, or by hand once on main). It takes only SimpleCube++ (2 GB of the 200 GB record, CC BY 4.0, pinned SHA-256) and runs every shipped model, histogram only (no as-shot value, no brightness: stricter than the app), on the test split. The Canon photos are mapped to each profile by a least-squares fit between the rawtoaces Canon EOS 600D curves and the profile's curves (neutral whites within 0.1° from 2856 to 6500 K), so the check measures the estimator on real image statistics, not a Sony body. **It passes on all 11 profiles: median 1.60 to 2.09° against the 3° gate.** The estimator never trained on a real photo, so the train split is unseen too: 1.99° on 1,772 photos (A7R IV). By the dataset's labels, outdoor daylight photos read 1.6 to 1.7°, indoor 2.7°, artificial light 2.5°, blurred photos 3.9°. The ground truth of outdoor daylight photos sits between the Canon curves' D55 and D65 whites, so the curves' raw scale is not off. **For scale, the classical estimators on the same previews (A7R IV, test split): white patch median 1.27° (p90 8.2°), shades of grey 1.58° (9.5°), grey world 2.15° (8.4°), grey edge 3.29° (9.6°).** Ours is far steadier (p90 5.5°) but its typical error is about 0.8° above white patch's. **The offset (run 5):** on the train split the estimate reads the light 5.4 % redder and 2.4 % less blue than the truth (median log-chroma du −0.054, dv +0.024; white patch's is a quarter of that), so a photo would render slightly cool. Subtracting the train split's offset on the test split takes the median from 2.09 to 1.55° and p90 from 5.5 to 5.0°, but only for daylight (outdoor 1.71 → 1.35°): indoor and artificial light get worse (2.68 → 3.19°, 2.53 → 3.15°), so the offset depends on the light and a constant cannot remove it. And the offset is the size of the few-per-cent channel-scale errors the published curves are known to carry (`docs/03`), here the Canon EOS 600D curves that map these photos, so this Canon-based check cannot say how much of it a Sony body would see. Averaging the estimate with white patch in log chroma gives a median of 1.44° (test) and 1.47° (train) at about the same tail (p90 6.2° and 5.5°, p95 8.8° and 7.1°). **Conclusion:** no retrain; the estimator passes and is the steadiest of the five. Two cheap options if the owner wants a better typical case, both to be judged on Sony photos (the A/B kit) before shipping, because Cube++ is Canon: a white-patch cue blended into the estimate, or, for indoor colour, more artificial lights in the synthetic scenes (a CPU retrain).

**GPU path.** `Auto` uses hardware adapters only and the CPU for software adapters (lavapipe, WARP). A device error re-renders the whole photo on the CPU, never a mix of tiles, and golden hashes pin the CPU reference. On the device: the raw front (R1 to R5b, R9, sigma plane, fit-view group), R6 to R8, the sampler, the colour chain with looks, output sharpening with C13, magnified views above 100 % and the export tail in slabs of at most 128 MiB. Metal compiles with fast-math, so kernels that must match the CPU pin every product and sum behind an OR barrier (`docs/17-gpu.md` section 13).

*Not on the device yet.* The CPU keeps decode and the preview estimates, the R6 test-switch methods (Menon, LMMSE, bilinear, log-guided), Lossless M/S, histogram counts, the DNG white point, the full-chain R5 offsets and every encoder (`docs/17-gpu.md` section 11); a device JPEG front end would be revisited only if a Mac shows DCT and Huffman coding above about 2 s per 50 MP. The neighbour raw prefetch (`Renderer::spawn_raw_prefetch`) exists but the app does not call it, and the device warm-up is one job a viewport job cannot pass. If the viewport's counters show contention on a Mac, the follow-ups are presenting on its own thread, `AutoNoVsync` or `Mailbox` present modes, pan and zoom applied without waiting for the core, a `viewport_input_batch` command, and `QOS_CLASS_UTILITY` for the decode, thumbnail and export threads. An opt-in upstream option to switch Metal's fast-math off would remove the kernel workarounds.

- **Export tiles of the synthetic lights scene differed by tile size (found 2026-11-02 by N-15; fixed 2026-11-02, golden updated).** On `synthetic` lights scenes (640 × 480, discs at 0.45 of the range), CPU exports made with tiles of 100, 128 and 256 px differed from the whole frame in 8 pixels (25 values, at most 5.6e-9) with no mask at all; Refine edges amplified it through its guide. **Cause:** the first plane that differed was R7's false-colour stage (`false_color::suppress`, its colour guided filter `detail::chroma_guided_filter`): its `filters::box_mean` kept a running sum along each row and column (add one sample, take one off), so the rounding of every sum it had passed through stayed in it, and on squares and products that span many powers of two (a bright light on a dark ground) that residue depends on where the sum began, which is a different place in every tile (`docs/17-gpu.md` D4 forbids exactly this). - **Colour warp, long narrow moves.** The stepped flow stretches a thin sliver of colours behind the source of a long, narrow move; there the GPU (f32) and CPU (f64) differ by up to about 3e-4 in LogC4 for moves longer than about 0.85 at range 0.01, over the 2e-4 gate. A floor on the range relative to the move (for example |δ|/20) would bound the stretch.
- **Saturation near deep blues.** Positive saturation jumps from the full factor to 1 across Y = 0 for deep blues (blue LEDs), a possible contour in a blue LED gradient at high saturation. A continuous variant is `λ = 1 + (F − 1)·Y/min` for `r < 0`.

- **White and estimators.** Day, Shade and Tungsten presets carry the 3.5 to 6 % difference between the Weta curves' white and the camera ColorMatrix white (A7 III, A7 IV). The illuminant's synthetic brightness range stops at Bv 13 (real sunlit files read 14.3 to 14.6) and LED and fluorescent recognition is weak on independent chart scenes. The Kelvin readout rounded to 50 K leaves up to 1.6 % red/blue imbalance below 2300 K. The white lift has no local variant and no burst rule, and its row stays visible with an imported ARRI display LUT, where it does nothing.
- **Shared matrices.** 37 of the 44 bodies in `data/cameras/color-matrices.json` fall into 14 groups that carry one published matrix (sensor siblings such as the A7R III and A7R IIIA, the A1 and A1 II); that is right only where the sensor is the same and could not be verified body by body. The A7 V and A7R VI use the A7R IV matrix.
