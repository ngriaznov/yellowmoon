# Ship Nikon first, X-Trans last

Yellowmoon can add Nikon and Fujifilm inside its current rules, but only for a short list of bodies with their own colour, and only after the owner lifts "Non-Sony raw files" from the out-of-scope list (`docs/00-overview.md`, about line 62). The colour data is the easy part: the rawtoaces-data commit the project already pins (`e9b8503`, 52 Apache-2.0 curves) holds **11 Nikon and 3 Fujifilm curves** (Nikon Z f, D850, D810, D800E, D5300, D3300 and five legacy DSLRs; Fujifilm X-T3, X-T4 and GFX 100), and the fit code is make-agnostic ([rawtoaces-data](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/rawtoaces-data/tree/main/data/camera)). The cost sits in three places: a Sony-only decoder crate with no format seam, two codecs that exist publicly only in copyleft code (Nikon's Huffman NEF and Fujifilm's compressed RAF), and a pipeline that assumes a 2×2 Bayer pattern from the decoder to the demosaic network. Nikon's High Efficiency raw (intoPIX TicoRAW) has no open decoder and carries asserted patents, so it should be detected and refused, exactly as ARW 6.0 is today. The recommended order is a multi-maker refactor, then classic NEF, then Fujifilm Bayer RAF (GFX), then X-Trans, which is the largest single item. Every body without a curve should fall back to a same-maker profile, not the A7R IV: on the public curves, same-maker bodies differ by about 1 % and cross-maker bodies by 4–7 %. Twelve decisions belong to the owner; the four that gate the work are scope, the fallback rule, Nikon's white-balance cipher tables, and the clean-room process for the Fuji codec.

Two warnings on the evidence. The four research notes disagree on whether any Fujifilm curve exists; this report follows the note that cloned rawtoaces-data on 2026-11-11 and read the files. And all effort sizes below are estimates relative to the existing ARW work, since no implementer has published time figures.

## The Sony assumption is spread across decoder, registry, pipeline and copy

**`ym-raw` is a Sony decoder, not a raw decoder with a Sony back end.** Its entry points are free functions, its probe returns `RawError::NotArw` for anything without Sony evidence (`crates/ym-raw/src/metadata.rs:384-400`), and `DecodeKind` lists only Sony compression variants (`crates/ym-raw/src/layout.rs:293-350`). The maker note reader, the SR2 cipher, black and white levels, white balance, crop tags and lens tags are all Sony-specific. Three parts are reusable as they stand: the TIFF walker (both byte orders), the EXIF reader, and a general lossless-JPEG decoder (`docs/04-raw-decoding.md:110`). The first job is therefore a per-maker dispatch that does not exist yet, plus new `DecodeKind` variants and a neutral error (`not_arw` is part of the CLI JSON contract, `docs/10-cli.md:164`).

**The output type fits Bayer NEF and Bayer RAF, and nothing else.** `Layout` has two variants, `Bayer` with a 2×2 `CfaPattern` and `Ycbcr` (`crates/ym-raw/src/layout.rs:271-289`), and `RawImage` carries `black` and `white` as `[f32; 4]`. All four Bayer phases are supported end to end, so a Nikon or GFX mosaic passes through the pipeline unchanged once it is decoded. X-Trans does not: the 2×2 assumption is in the GPU `Cfa` type (`crates/ym-gpu/src/cpu.rs:153-181`), raw stages R1 to R6 and seven WGSL kernels, highlight reconstruction on 2×2 superpixels (`docs/05-raw-processing.md:49`), mosaic-domain CA correction, the binned preview path, the noise model's four planes (`crates/ym-noise/src/model.rs:8`) and the learned demosaic's four packed planes (`docs/07-ml-models.md:58`). The default demosaic spec says "Non-Bayer patterns: Not supported" (`docs/algorithms/01-adaptive-ratio-demosaic.md:502`). This audit rests on types, specs and grep hits; the kernels were not read line by line.

**Profiles are make-agnostic in maths and Sony-keyed in plumbing.** The registry keys bodies by Sony's maker-note model ID with no `make` field; schemas pin `sony/Sony_…_380_780_5.json` paths and `.ARW` white-scale sources (`crates/ym-profiles/schema/profile.schema.json:27`, `registry.schema.json:95`); `ProfileStore::detect` takes a `u16` model ID and sends any non-Sony Make to the A7R IV default (`crates/ym-profiles/src/detect.rs:240-281`). Each profile also needs its own illuminant model (11 ship today, trained on synthetic scenes; `docs/07-ml-models.md:31`), and the Before view needs a per-body D65 matrix and a camera tone curve, the latter fitted to Sony's JPEG engine. The project's own Canon/Nikon research (2026-10-19) already ran the toolchain on Nikon curves and found the fit, tables and checks need no change (`research/canon-nikon-curves-2026-10-19.md:141`). It was not run on Fujifilm curves.

**The rest is a long, shallow list.** "arw" is hard-coded at five entry points (core session, CLI inputs, CLI preview, the Tauri open dialog, the frontend drop filter). Noise priors and gain regimes are keyed by Sony model ID, and `BodyClass::from_model` matches only Sony model strings (`crates/ym-noise/src/priors.rs:205-248`). Lens correction reads only Sony tags 0x7032, 0x7035 and 0x7037. The synthetic-file writer (`testgen.rs`, 2,906 lines), seven fuzz targets, the oracle and the fetch scripts are ARW-only. UI copy, the README, the cask and the About sentence all say Sony. The synthetic test tooling will cost roughly as much per format as the decoder itself.

## Classic NEF is tractable; High Efficiency is not

**NEF is TIFF-based and Bayer, so it lands on the existing shape.** The raw IFD uses compression 1 (uncompressed), 32769 (packed with padding) or 34713 (Nikon compressed) ([libopenraw NEF notes](https://libopenraw.pages.freedesktop.org/formats/nef)). The decoding parameters live in the Nikon maker note, which ExifTool's tag tables document: `NEFCompression` (0x0093), the linearisation table (0x0096), `BlackLevel` (0x003d), `CropArea` (0x0045), `NEFBitDepth` (0x0e22) and white balance in `WB_RBLevels` (0x000c) and the versioned `ColorBalance` block (0x0097) ([ExifTool Nikon tags](https://exiftool.org/TagNames/Nikon.html)). The compressed codec is one entropy coder with table variants: a lossy encoding through a curve stored in the file, then non-adaptive Huffman coding, with lossless files skipping the curve ([Claff, NEF Compression](https://photonstophotos.net/NikonInfo/NEF_Compression.htm)). Lossy tables are 1:1 in the shadows and quantise highlights only. Some 14-bit files switch Huffman table at a mid-image "split" row, a case that needed a specially hunted sample in other decoders ([pixls.us](https://discuss.pixls.us/t/nikon-a-specific-raw-sample-wanted/5483/9)).

**No permissive description of the byte layout exists.** ExifTool marks 0x0096 as an opaque blob, and libopenraw's page defers to dcraw; the predictor layout, the six Huffman tables and the split offset are expressed only in code. The notes' account of them (predict from two columns left, four vertical predictors, split value at byte 562) is from memory and unverified, with one corroborating hit in a Rust crate of unchecked licence ([bioformats nikon.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/bioformats/0.1.3/source/src/tiff/nikon.rs)). The clean-room route is the one hard rule 1 already describes: read copyleft code for understanding, write a prose description, implement from the prose, and compare bit-exactly against LibRaw and rawler in `tools/oracle`. The codec is integer-exact, so oracle equality is a complete test. No maintained MIT, BSD or Apache NEF decoder was found; the licences of the copyleft decoders named in the notes were not re-fetched and need confirming before they enter `docs/14`.

**White balance has an encryption problem.** ExifTool states that ColorBalance is "encrypted for most camera models" and that SerialNumber and ShutterCount are keys ([ExifTool Nikon tags](https://exiftool.org/TagNames/Nikon.html)). The project's earlier research found the cipher's two 256-byte tables publicly only in GPL or Artistic-licence code and flagged whether `docs/14` rule 8 ("numeric facts may come from any source") covers them (`research/canon-nikon-curves-2026-10-19.md:123, 135`). `WB_RBLevels` is in the clear and should give as-shot white, but that was inferred, not checked on files. The details of the cipher in the notes are from memory and unverified. White level is not stored in most NEFs (from memory, unverified); other decoders carry per-camera tables that are LGPL data and cannot be copied, so the values would have to be derived from public samples.

**High Efficiency should be refused with a plain sentence.** `NEFCompression` 13 and 14 are intoPIX TicoRAW, a wavelet codec inside the NEF container ([Photography Life](https://photographylife.com/nikon-he-versus-lossless-compressed-nef-files)). intoPIX says TicoRAW is "covered by one or more claims of patent" and publishes no licence terms ([intoPIX](https://www.intopix.com/ticoraw)). No open decoder was found: darktable lists HE as unsupported, RawTherapee and ART fail on it, and a community reverse-engineering effort in April and May 2026 reached a viewable but wrong image and paused without publishing code ([pixls.us](https://discuss.pixls.us/t/nikon-he-decoder/56955?page=3)). The Z8, Z9 and Z6III offer only lossless, HE★ and HE, with no uncompressed option ([Nikon Z8 specifications](https://onlinemanual.nikonimglib.com/z8/en/specifications_364.html), [Z6III manual](https://onlinemanual.nikonimglib.com/z6III/da/psm_raw_recording_133.html)), so a refused HE file is a real loss on the newest bodies. How large is unknown: no statistic on HE usage was found, and factory defaults per body were not confirmed.

Three workarounds exist, and none is free. Apple's system decoder is legally clean, but it returns demosaiced RGB, varies with the macOS version, and cannot run in Linux CI, which breaks hard rules 6 and 8; whether macOS 26.4 decodes HE at all rests on one enthusiast site and conflicting readings of Apple's support page ([Apple Support 122870](https://support.apple.com/en-us/122870)). That Apple's API exposes no mosaic data is from memory. Adobe DNG Converter turns HE into a mosaic DNG, but Yellowmoon reads no DNG today. An intoPIX licence is a [HUMAN] enquiry. The ARW 6.0 precedent applies directly: detect, refuse, gate on an owner email (`docs/04-raw-decoding.md:131-136`). Small NEF (reported from memory as YCbCr on some DSLRs, not mosaic data), NRW and the D1-era oddities should be refused too.

## RAF parses cleanly, but its codec exists only in LGPL code

**The container is fully checkable on CC0 files.** The Fuji note parsed the headers of raw.pixls.us samples directly, without third-party code. A RAF starts with a big-endian header (`FUJIFILMCCD-RAW `, model, then three offset and length pairs at byte 84 for the JPEG, a tag block and the CFA block), with the compression mode at byte 108: 0 uncompressed, 2 lossless, 3 lossy ([X-H2 sample](https://raw.pixls.us/data/Fujifilm/X-H2/Fuji-X-H2-Compressed-Raw.RAF), [ExifTool FujiFilm tags](https://exiftool.org/TagNames/FujiFilm.html)). The CFA block is itself a little-endian TIFF whose single entry points to a private IFD holding dimensions, bit depth, strip offset, black level, white balance and lens curves; this was true even on the 2012 X-Pro1 ([X-Pro1 sample](https://raw.pixls.us/data/Fujifilm/X-Pro1/DSCF2131.RAF)). So the existing TIFF reader is reusable; the new parts are the outer header, the big-endian tag block and a JPEG APP1 walker to reach the maker note.

**The traps are per-file geometry.** On the X-Pro2, the uncompressed and lossless files of one camera have different raw sizes, crop origins and CFA phase ([uncompressed](https://raw.pixls.us/data/Fujifilm/X-Pro2/_DSF3050.RAF), [lossless](https://raw.pixls.us/data/Fujifilm/X-Pro2/_DSF3051.RAF)). Black level has 36 values on X-Trans and 4 on Bayer. Uncompressed data is 12-bit packed on the X-Pro1 and little-endian 16-bit containers on later bodies. GFX files are 14 or 16 bits per file. White balance was in the private IFD (0xf00e) in all eight files parsed, not in the tag dcraw reads. Which of the two chroma codes in the 36-byte X-Trans layout is red cannot be derived from counts and must be confirmed against an oracle. White level was in no tag found. Fujifilm's DR200 and DR400 modes underexpose by one or two stops, so a decoder must read `RawExposureBias` (0x9650) or `DevelopmentDynamicRange` and compensate ([pixls.us](https://discuss.pixls.us/t/need-help-figuring-out-x-t1-default-dt-processing/32136/9)); the baseline of −0.72 EV was measured on three bodies but its meaning is undocumented.

**The compressed codec is the main legal and schedule risk for Fuji.** Lossless and lossy share one block-structured adaptive codec with a 16-byte stream header, which the note decoded on samples ([X-T5 lossless](https://raw.pixls.us/data/Fujifilm/X-T5/DSCF0021.RAF)). Every implementation found descends from Alexey Danilchenko's 2016 code: LibRaw (LGPL-2.1 or CDDL-1.0), rawspeed (LGPL-2.1+) and rawler (LGPL-2.1) ([LibRaw source header](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LibRaw/LibRaw/master/src/decoders/fuji_compressed.cpp), [rawler Cargo.toml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dnglab/dnglab/main/rawler/Cargo.toml)). dcraw has no compressed-RAF decoder, and no permissive implementation or written specification turned up. LibRaw's CDDL option does not help: CDDL is file-level copyleft, and the ASF policy bars such source from Apache releases ([ASF policy](https://www.apache.org/legal/resolved.html)). The header fields are format facts; the entropy coder and its adaptive tables are closer to algorithm. A defensible process has two passes: one writes a prose specification, a separate one implements only from that prose, with bit-exact oracle comparison. A 2020 prose write-up of the lossless algorithm is referenced on pixls.us but was not located. A search found no Fujifilm patent on the codec, which is not a freedom-to-operate opinion; the codec has shipped in LGPL libraries since 2016 without a reported assertion.

**Uncompressed-only support would not be enough.** Lossy mode appears in samples from the X-T4 onward (X-S20, X-H2, X-H2S, X-T5, X100VI, GFX100 II), and an X-T5 file is 82 MB uncompressed against 41 MB lossless and 27 MB lossy ([raw.pixls.us X-T5](https://raw.pixls.us/data/Fujifilm/X-T5/)), so most users of current bodies will shoot compressed. The exclusion list is clear: Super CCD, EXR and SR FinePix bodies and the 2/3-inch compacts have other layouts and should be refused.

## X-Trans needs its own raw front end

**One 6×6 layout covers every generation.** The pattern bytes are identical from the X-Pro1 to the X-T5: 20 green, 8 red and 8 blue per tile, with the phase read per file ([X-T5 sample](https://raw.pixls.us/data/Fujifilm/X-T5/DSCF0022.RAF)). Demosaic code is therefore body-independent. GFX bodies are Bayer (measured on the GFX100 II); that the X-A, X-T100 and X-T200 lines are Bayer is from memory, with one source for the X-A1.

There are three demosaic routes, and they are not exclusive.

**A clean classical demosaic is the necessary base.** Markesteijn's algorithm is what darktable and RawTherapee use, with 3-pass recommended ([RawPedia](https://rawpedia.rawtherapee.com/Demosaicing)). It originates in dcraw, outside the block dcraw marks RESTRICTED, so dcraw's own terms call it "free for all uses" ([dcraw.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ncruces/dcraw/master/dcraw.c)). Hard rule 1 still names dcraw as never-copy, so it needs either an independent implementation from prose or an explicit owner exception. The project's conventions require a CPU reference for every GPU kernel anyway, so some classical X-Trans demosaic must exist whichever route wins. Non-Markesteijn academic methods were not surveyed.

**A learned demosaic is plausible and its training data is free.** Gharbi et al.'s joint demosaic and denoise code is MIT and had an X-Trans model ([demosaicnet LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mgharbi/demosaicnet/master/LICENSE), [demosaicnet_caffe](https://github.com/mgharbi/demosaicnet_caffe)). X-Trans mosaics can be synthesised from clean linear RGB by applying the 6×6 mask, which is how Gharbi's model and a 2026 hobby project were trained ([pixls.us X-veon](https://discuss.pixls.us/t/x-veon-a-better-demosaic-for-x-trans-100-less-worms/56196)), so the existing PixelShift200 targets can be reused with no captures. The Bayer network cannot be reused: packing 2×2 into four half-resolution planes has no X-Trans equivalent, and the alternatives (full-resolution input with mask channels, or 36 planes at one-sixth resolution) are design options, not findings. RawNIND offers 677 real X-Trans raws with clean and noisy pairs, but under CC BY-SA 4.0 ([Dataverse record](https://dataverse.uclouvain.be/api/datasets/:persistentId/?persistentId=doi:10.14428/DVN/DEQCIM)); `docs/14` rule 5 lists CC BY-SA as an allowed source for shipped weights, so the owner's call is only whether to take on share-alike questions for weights, or keep RawNIND for validation. Two 2026 projects claim to beat Markesteijn with very small models, on visual evidence only. The Bayer `jdd` network itself has no trained weights yet (`docs/07-ml-models.md:14`), so a learned X-Trans model queues behind that.

**A staged entry lowers the first cost.** The pipeline already accepts demosaiced camera RGB for Sony's reduced-size files (`Layout::Ycbcr`; `docs/04-raw-decoding.md:124-127`). An X-Trans demosaic that outputs camera RGB could join there first, giving correct colour early at the price of no mosaic-domain highlight reconstruction, no mosaic CA correction and no joint denoise. This is an inference from the notes, not a spec proposal. The full version needs a 6×6 `Layout` variant, 36-position levels, X-Trans counterparts of R1 to R6, the binned preview and the noise estimator's plane model, plus parity and golden tests.

**The quality bar is public and specific.** Fuji users know Adobe's old failure by name: "worms" and watercolour smearing in fine detail, fixed by Adobe's ML Enhance Details at a speed cost ([Fujirumors](https://www.fujirumors.com/lightroom-enhanced-details-for-fujifilm-x-trans-files-tested-fuji-users-beware-wormy-artifacts-fixed)). Matching Markesteijn 3-pass is the open-source baseline. Purple grid artefacts from masked phase-detect pixels in backlit shots affect X-Trans II and III under any algorithm ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2017/02/23/fuji-cameras-produce-strange-purple-flaregrid-artifact/)) and should ship as a documented limitation.

## Fourteen bodies have shippable curves; the rest need a same-maker default

**rawtoaces-data is the only shippable source, and it already includes Fujifilm.** The spectral note cloned the repository on 2026-11-11 at `e9b8503`, the same commit `tools/fetch/spectral.sh` pins for its 52-curve check. Every camera file is Apache-2.0, 380–780 nm at 5 nm. The X-T3 and X-T4 files came in with the Weta import from physlight on 2025-09-16; the GFX 100 was added on 2026-04-09 ([commit cf6452c](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/rawtoaces-data/commit/cf6452c3ce44112f6cf3f1c2d7bf6381a4c90638), [commit 145e771](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/rawtoaces-data/commit/145e7713e8c892f76ef069fb6f92275f9d61de4a)). Two of the notes say no Fujifilm curve is documented; they did not have the files, and the direct reading wins. The X-Trans files carry plain R, G, B curves, which is sufficient: the CFA layout affects demosaicing, not per-channel response.

| Maker | Bodies with a shippable measured curve | Source | Notable bodies without one |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nikon | Z f, D850, D810, D800E, D5300, D3300 | Weta | Z5, Z5II, Z6, Z6II, Z6III, Z7, Z7II, Z8, Z9, Z30, Z50, Z50II, Zfc, D780, D750, D500 |
| Nikon (legacy) | D70, D200, D700, D7000 (AMPAS); D5100 (NPL) | AMPAS, NPL | |

**Nothing else can ship.** camspec is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/3245883)); Glenn Butcher's DIY Nikon Z 6 curve is all-rights-reserved plus CC BY-NC-SA ([ssf-data](https://github.com/butcherg/ssf-data)); the Solomatov and Akkaynak predictions for about a thousand cameras are unlicensed and are estimates from Adobe matrices, not measurements ([repository](https://github.com/COLOR-Lab-Eilat/Spectral-sensitivity-estimation)). No measured Fujifilm curve was found anywhere else. This is a negative result from the sources inspected; several collections were not individually checked.

**Shared silicon does not mean shared colour.** The spectral note compared the Weta curves with its own quick metric (per-channel scaled RMS difference over peak, mean of R, G, B; not a standard measure). Same-maker pairs sit near 1 %: X-T3 against X-T4 at 0.6 %, the Bayer GFX 100 against the X-Trans X-T3 at 1.0 %, D810 against D800E at 1.1 %. Cross-maker pairs on related Sony silicon sit far higher: X-T3 against A7R IV at 5.7 %, GFX 100 against A7R IV at 6.3 %, Z f against A7 III at 3.8 %, D850 against A7R III at 6.8 %. Fujifilm's red channel peaks 10 nm later than Sony's and keeps more response at 700 nm. In rendered terms, the earlier research found the D850 at 0.34 median ΔE00 with its own curves and 2.09 on the A7R IV stand-in, and the Z f at 0.27 against 0.81 (`research/canon-nikon-curves-2026-10-19.md:67-70`; run on profiles before 1.2.0 and not re-run). Sensor part attributions come from rumour sites that contradict each other and are unreliable either way.

**The fallback order should be own curve, strict same-maker sibling, same-maker default, and only then the A7R IV.** For Fujifilm the evidence for a make default is three bodies within 1 % across two sensor formats and both CFA types; the X-Trans 5 bodies are a newer generation than anything measured, so the default is an extrapolation there and should be labelled as an estimate. For Nikon the picture is weaker: under the existing 0.02 matrix rule no Z body qualifies as a sibling of the Z f (Z6III 0.041, Z8 0.10), and the D850 is an outlier even among Nikons (4.6 % from the Z f). The best single Nikon map in the earlier research was the D5300's. rawtoaces-data's maintainer asserts Z6 II → Z f as "same sensor" ([aliases.json](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/rawtoaces-data/blob/main/data/aliases.json)); the DIY Z 6 curve sits 3.0 % from the Z f, inside that rig's own 4 % disagreement with another measurement, so the alias is plausible but unproven. Two checks have not been run for Fujifilm and are small jobs with `training/other_makes/check.py`: the matrix-sibling test and the sensor-map gate. The gate matters: in the earlier research the Z f and D850 failed it on some held-out lights, and thresholds change only with the owner's agreement.

**Matrix-derived curves add nothing.** Estimating curves from Adobe's matrices has a published median error near 8 % ([arXiv 2304.11549](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.11549v2)), and the note measured those predictions at 6–7 % against the Weta files: no better than a cross-maker stand-in and far worse than a same-maker one. The published matrix stays useful for what the project already does with it, the sibling test and the Before view. More measured bodies can only come from Weta, which is a [HUMAN] email.

## Noise needs no new data; lens correction needs new readers

**The noise estimator is maker-neutral by design.** It fits each photo's model from its own pixels with priors from public files, and stores nothing (`docs/06-noise.md:7`). No per-body prior file ships even for Sony yet, so Nikon and Fujifilm Bayer files start level with most Sony bodies on the wide prior. The work is plumbing: maker-qualified keys (Sony model IDs are not unique across makers), a body class derived from sensor size instead of Sony model strings, and gain-switch ISOs as numbers. Ready-made tables exist but should not ship: photonstophotos is all rights reserved ([photonstophotos.net](https://www.photonstophotos.net/)), and darktable's profiles for 69 Nikon and 43 Fujifilm models sit in a GPL project ([noiseprofiles.json](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/blob/master/data/noiseprofiles.json)). Taking them as "numeric facts" under rule 8 is arguable but they are large compiled tables; the wide prior is the safer default. Known hazards are unverified on files: older Nikons clip black to zero, some bodies scale or filter raw data, and whether RAF files expose masked pixels is unconfirmed. X-Trans needs the estimator's plane extraction rewritten for the 6×6 pattern. The learned denoiser, once trained, will have seen only Sony A7R III data, and no gate measures that shift.

**Both makers embed lens corrections, in undocumented forms.** Nikon Z files carry three radial distortion coefficients and three vignetting coefficients in a private block (SubIFD1 tag 0xc7d5), plus on/off flags in the maker note; ExifTool names the fields but not the polynomial form, and lists no chromatic aberration data ([ExifTool Nikon tags](https://exiftool.org/TagNames/Nikon.html)). F-mount DSLR files carry no coefficients (from memory, unverified). Fujifilm stores sampled radial curves for distortion, CA and vignetting in its private IFD (0xf00b, 0xf00f, 0xf010); counts were measured (19 to 31 rationals), but the knot layout and units are an arithmetic guess. Both need confirming against darktable-cli as a separate-process oracle, which the project already uses for Sony. The alternative, lensfun, is ruled out today by spec ("No Lensfun") and its library by licence; its database is CC BY-SA 3.0 (from a search summary, not the upstream README), shippable only unmodified as aggregated data with an independent implementation of the lens models. Shipping the first release with the existing "No lens data in this file" state is honest and cheap, except for Z lenses flagged "On (Required)", which are designed around software correction.

## A five-phase plan, and the decisions only the owner can make

Adding either maker contradicts the current specs, so by AGENTS.md no agent may start from the milestones list; phase 0 begins with the owner's answers. Sizes are this report's estimates relative to the ARW decoder (`ym-raw` holds roughly 8,000 lines of container, metadata and decoder code plus a 2,900-line test generator).

| Phase | Content | What users get | Size | Blocking decisions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Multi-maker groundwork | Spec changes; format dispatch in `ym-raw`; `make` in registry, schemas and detection; one shared list of extensions; neutral errors and copy; maker-qualified noise keys; run the profile checks on the 3 Fuji curves | Nothing visible | Medium, mostly renaming | 1, 2, 3 |
| 1. Classic NEF | Nikon maker note; uncompressed, lossless and lossy Huffman; levels, crop, WB from 0x000c (cipher if allowed); refuse HE, small NEF, NRW; profiles and illuminant models for the 11 measured bodies; Nikon default; synthetic NEF writer, fuzzing, oracle | All DSLR and pre-EXPEED 7 Z files, lossless files from Z8, Z9, Z f, Z6III; own colour on Z f, D850, D810, D800E, D5300, D3300 | Medium, about one ARW decoder | 4, 5, 6 |
| 2. Fujifilm Bayer RAF | RAF container; uncompressed; clean-room compressed codec (lossless and lossy); DR compensation; GFX 100 profile, Fuji default | GFX and Bayer X bodies; own colour on GFX 100 | Medium to large; the codec dominates, and clean-room work roughly doubles its calendar time | 7 |
| 3. X-Trans | Stage A: classical demosaic to camera RGB joining the reduced-file entry. Stage B: 6×6 layout through R1–R6, preview, noise, GPU kernels and parity tests | Every X-Trans body; own colour on X-T3 and X-T4 | Large, the biggest item | 8 |
| 4. Follow-ups | Embedded lens readers for both makers; learned X-Trans demosaic; an HE route if the owner picks one; more curves from Weta | Parity with the Sony feature set | Medium each | 5, 9, 10 |

Nikon goes first because it needs no pipeline change and proves the refactor on the cheapest format. Fujifilm's colour story for current bodies is actually the stronger one, with a well-supported make default, so the owner could reasonably swap phases 1 and 2 if Fuji users matter more; X-Trans stays last either way because it depends on the RAF decoder. Test files are not a constraint for either maker: raw.pixls.us is CC0 for new samples with SHA-256 lists and covers every Nikon compression and bit-depth combination by policy, and 79 Fujifilm folders including all three modes for recent bodies ([raw.pixls.us](https://raw.pixls.us/), [rawspeed sample archive](https://darktable-org.github.io/rawspeed/ReferenceSampleArchive.html)). The X-T50, X-M5, X-E5 and GFX100S II have no samples there and would ship as untested.

The owner's decisions, in the order they block work:

1. **Scope and timing.** Whether other makers enter V1, a V1.x or V2, and the rewrite of the product line in `docs/00` and AGENTS.md ("V1 relies on available measurements for Sony cameras"). The data rule's substance already allows published curves from any maker.
2. **The fallback rule.** Replace "same-sensor sibling or the A7R IV" with a same-maker default, choose each maker's default body, and decide whether to accept rawtoaces-data's Z6 II → Z f alias, which the 0.02 matrix rule rejects.
3. **The sensor-map gate.** What to do with measured bodies that fail it on some lights (Z f and D850 in the earlier research; Fujifilm untested): a per-make threshold, a richer map, or shipping them as estimates.
4. **Nikon's cipher tables.** Whether the two 256-byte tables count as numeric facts under rule 8. If not, white balance comes from `WB_RBLevels` only, which must first be verified on files.
5. **High Efficiency.** Refuse (recommended), add a DNG reader, permit a labelled Apple-decoder path outside the determinism rules, or email intoPIX. A hands-on Mac test of Apple's HE support is an owner task.
6. **Clean-room process for NEF.** Accept a prose-then-implement process for the Huffman tables, which are documented only in copyleft code.
7. **Clean-room process and patent comfort for the Fuji codec.** Same process, larger algorithm, no patent search of substance behind it.
8. **X-Trans demosaic.** Independent reimplementation or an explicit exception for dcraw's unrestricted Markesteijn code; whether stage A is an acceptable first release.
9. **Training data.** Whether RawNIND (CC BY-SA 4.0) may train shipped weights or only validate them.
10. **Lens data.** Embedded readers per maker (recommended), no correction, or revisiting "No Lensfun" for the CC BY-SA database.
11. **Exclusions and copy.** The refused families (small NEF, NRW, Super CCD, EXR), the trademark sentence in About, and whether Fujifilm cameras sitting beside Fujifilm film-stock names in the Film node needs different wording.
12. **[HUMAN] outreach.** An email to Weta about Z6 II, Z8, Z9 and X-Trans 5 bodies. physlight's "more cameras" issue has been open since 2021 without a commitment.

## Conclusion

The earlier Canon and Nikon research concluded that curves are the small part of the job and decoders the large one. That holds, with two changes. Fujifilm turns out to be better placed on colour than Nikon: three measured bodies that agree with each other across formats make a credible make-wide default, while Nikon's one measured Z body has no passing sibling. And the binding constraint differs per maker. For Nikon it is a codec nobody may legally open on the newest bodies; for Fujifilm it is engineering volume, in a codec that must be rebuilt from prose and a mosaic the pipeline has never seen.

The cross-maker measurements also change what "fallback" should mean in this project. A same-sensor sibling from another maker is no better than a generic default, so the registry should be organised by maker first and sensor second. That is a one-line rule change with a large effect on how honest the camera card can be, and it is worth settling before any decoder is written.
